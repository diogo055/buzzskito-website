# Selling US pest-control calls from buzzskito.ca: pilot plan

October 30, 2026. Research only: nothing was signed up for, submitted or changed. The appendix holds the tables, pages and sources.

## Recommendation

**Run a small, cheap test on buzzskito.ca, and stop counting on calls as the big earner.** Apply to **Lead Smart Inc** first, with **CallScaler** as the backup.

Lead Smart is the only network I found that publishes its pest rate (its own figure: US$42.50 median per call of 90 seconds or more), says publishers outside the US can join, and allows SEO sites. It is free, with no contract. Two cautions: it pays monthly with a US$100 minimum, and two publishers claim it withheld money (unverified).

## What the research changed

- **Little traffic.** The 31 test pages got 172 US search visits in October, 108 in the last week. Two pages made up two-thirds. Pages about hiring a pro got almost none.
- **"1 in 100" looks far too high.** Our Call button got 3.9 taps per 1,000 views on service pages and 0.2 on articles. I expect **about 1 paid call per 1,000 US visits (range 0.2 to 3.5)**.
- **Small money.** At about US$35 a call, that adds about 5 Canadian cents a visit to today's 3-5 cents. The test itself should earn about US$40. At the July peak on .ca I estimate CA$200-850 a month, not the roadmap's CA$12,800. Calls will not carry the $10,000 goal.

## Your three questions

1. **Does any network object to a .ca site, a Canadian company, or only some American readers?** I found no written rule against any of the three, so there is nothing to quote. Most publisher contracts are not public, so the answer is mostly "not stated; ask them" (Appendix A).
2. **Does this need buzzskito.com?** No. Same pages, same domain, no move. The cost of staying: Americans click us half as often as Canadians do (0.50% vs 1.07% in October). The domain is not proven to be why.
3. **What about Canadian visitors?** Everyone sees the same box. Nothing checks the visitor's country, which is the lesson of the July Amazon rejection. About 1 view in 5 on these pages is Canadian, so the box says "US only". A Canadian who dials may not connect. If the call connects, it is not paid. Pages read mostly by Canadians are out of the test.

## The box

A plain box under the byline, on US guides only. No fixed bar, form or callback.

> **Would you rather talk to a pest control company?** This line connects readers in the US with an independent local company. BuzzSkito does not treat bed bugs and has no US service.
> **[ Call 1-8XX-XXX-XXXX ]**
> Paid referral: we are paid when you call. We don't choose, check or guarantee the company. Ask for its state pest control license before you hire. Calls may be recorded. US only; this line does not serve Canada. Best reached weekdays, 8am to 5pm Central.

Use one phone number per pest group if the network allows it, otherwise one. Not one per state, because the network finds the caller's location. No swapping by visitor.

- December speaks only for rodents, roaches and bed bugs, pest by pest. Termites and wasps get almost no visits now. Re-read termites by Mar 29 and wasps in June. Never multiply a December total.

## D. Rules

I am not a lawyer. This is what I could read, and it goes to the lawyer as a list of questions.

**FTC.** The Endorsement Guides and FAQ say a paid connection that readers would not expect must be disclosed clearly and close to the recommendation. "Paid link" right next to a link should be adequate. "Affiliate link" alone or a plain button is not. The FAQ also says US law applies when it is foreseeable that US consumers are affected, so being Canadian is no shield. Our wording sits directly under the button and says "Paid referral: we are paid when you call."

**TCPA.** The statute restricts a person's ability "to make any call" by autodialer or recorded voice, and defines a telephone solicitation as "the initiation of a telephone call". With a plain phone link the reader makes the call, so the consent rules should not arise. I found no ruling on this exact setup, so the lawyer should confirm.
- **Keep the test outside it:** no form, no "call me back" button, no texts from anyone. eLocal and Service Direct both bar SMS.
- **If a form is ever added:** it needs prior express written consent. The FCC's one-to-one rule was struck down in January 2025 and later removed, but clear written consent is still required, and some states add their own rules.

**Call recording.** Networks record calls, and California requires consent from all parties. Clear notice at the start of the call is the usual fix (law firm summaries). CallScaler's terms put this on the user. The box says calls may be recorded, and we ask the network to play a notice on every call.

**State pest-control law.** These are the laws I found readily, not a survey. All three are about pest control generally. I found no case or regulator statement applying them to a site selling calls, and large lead companies openly sell calls in these states. A law firm search summary mentioned lead-generation listings as a Florida violation, but I could not open the page.
- **Florida:** "It is unlawful to solicit, practice, perform, or advertise in pest control except as provided by this chapter" (s. 482.191). Pest control is defined to include "The advertisement of, the solicitation of, or the acceptance of remuneration for" pest control work (s. 482.021(22)(e)). Violations are a second-degree misdemeanor, with civil penalties under s. 482.165.
- **Texas:** a person is in the business if they advertise or solicit "the person's performance" of pest services for compensation (Occupations Code 1951.003). That wording points at advertising your own work. The Department of Agriculture's page lists advertising such services as licensed activity.
- **California:** it is unlawful to advertise the business of structural pest control without a license, and unlawful for an unlicensed person to offer an opinion or recommendation on the need for the work (Business and Professions Code 8550(a), (d)). This is why the box never says "you need a pro". Ask whether our articles' general advice raises the same question.
- **Scale:** these three states are about a fifth of US views on these guides (CA 11%, TX 5%, FL 4%). If the lawyer wants a state left out, the network blocks it on the phone line and the box says so in words. The website never checks where the reader is.

**Mediavine.** Its agreement (version 3.0, May 13, 2025) defines ads to include in-content ads, text ads and other promotions. It gives Mediavine exclusive rights to control and represent all ads, and bars the publisher from selling or negotiating ads with third parties.
- **No carve-out:** I found no exception for affiliate links or sponsors in the contract. Outside guides say those are fine (third party), but that is not the contract.
- **Direct deals:** its help center says ads sold directly to a brand go through Mediavine Direct, and a direct-sold ad outside it is not allowed. A paid call box is not a brand's display ad, but this is the rule it could fall under, so we ask.
- **Set-Up Period:** for 90 days from the first ad, you may not change the ad placement Mediavine sets.
- **Beside ads:** I saw no rule about a non-ad box next to an ad. I read about 400,000 of the legal page's 837,000 characters, which repeat older versions of the agreement and add privacy notices and transparency reports. Schedule C and the Publisher Policies that the agreement names were not in what I read. The box uses `.not-prose`, which your draft note asks Mediavine to keep ads away from.
- **Why it blocks the test:** all 31 pages are ad pages, and Mediavine is the income the roadmap counts on.

- Both groups are frozen from go-live to the read: no photos, links in or out, Amazon edits or title edits. If an edit cannot wait, it goes on both groups.
- Page releases must pick other old guides as link sources.
- Mediavine's launch hits both groups, so the hold-back absorbs it.
- This test touches no domain, no state page and no hreflang, so it has no dependency on buzzskito.com.

**What gets counted.**
- From the network each week: calls, connected calls, calls of 90 seconds or more, paid calls, rejection reasons, out-of-area calls, by number.
- From GA4: taps per page, and US search visits by landing page (session medium "organic", country United States).
- Against the hold-back: engaged time and pages per visit, plus ad income per visit if Mediavine shows it.

**Why search visits.** GA4's US numbers include robots. Ashburn, VA shows 108 sessions averaging 4 seconds, The Dalles, OR 40 averaging 2 seconds, Boardman, OR 31 averaging 1 second. "Direct" US sessions were 782 of 3,497, only 18% engaged, against 52% for Google search.

**What the hold-back can catch.** These groups are small, and groups of about 10 pages normally swing 20-40% against each other (photo read). So it catches a disaster, not a 10% dip. Amazon clicks are too few to read: 4 US clicks in October across all 43 pages.

**How to read December for summer.**
- **What December can show.** October US search visits on the box pages: rodents 73, cockroaches 68, bed bugs 20, wildlife 10, termites 1, wasps 0. So December speaks for rodents and roaches, a little for bed bugs, and not at all for termites or wasps.
- **The seasons.** CallScaler says rodent calls rise in fall and winter, termite calls in the spring swarm, mosquito and tick calls May to September, and bed bugs stay steady with spikes after travel (network marketing, not data). Lead Smart's pest share of connected calls rose from 17.1% in July to 27.3% in September (network). A month-by-month winter curve is not public.
- **The rule.** Keep the rate per pest and use it for that pest only. A December zero on termites means nothing. Re-read termites by Mar 29 and wasps in June.
- **Holidays.** Leave out Dec 24 to Jan 1, because buyers and callers both thin out.
- **Payout.** Record the average payout each month, since it moves with buyer demand (network). Rate and payout are separate questions.
- **Which pests qualify.** Marketcall's listed pest offer bars bed bugs and wasps. If our network does too, those pages show no paid calls for a reason that has nothing to do with readers. Ask first. Renters: not stated; ask them. One listing accepts apartments (third party).
- **My judgment, not data:** roach and bed bug readers rent more often, so December may understate what homeowner pests do in summer.

## G. How I got the numbers

**No public benchmark exists for calls per visit from information pages: not public.** So this is an estimate built from our own data.

| Step | Low | Middle | High | Basis |

**Compared with today.** "3-5 cents a visit" is about CA$30-50 per 1,000 visits. The middle case about doubles that. The call income is on top of ads and Amazon, not instead of them.

**The visits in the test.** 100 to 300 US search visits a week (108 in the last week of October) over 5.7 counted weeks: Nov 23 to Jan 10, minus Dec 24 to Jan 1. That gives 570 to 1,700, with a middle of about 1,100. A later start moves the read.

**The July visits.** The goal is 71,417 Google visitors. I assume 30-40% are American (22.5% in October, rising). Of US search visits on Oct 22-28, 18% landed on the 88 guides on these topics (200 of 1,115). That gives about 4,000-5,000 visits, which is generous because not all 88 pass the screen. Summer traffic may shift toward mosquito pages, and new pages may add to this. It is rough. The roadmap's CA$12,800 needs about 260 calls a month. At 1 per 1,000 that is 260,000 visits to these pages.

**Sense checks.**
- Service Direct's own example uses a 12% call rate on landing pages built for paid search (network). Those readers are looking for a company. Ours are mostly looking for an answer.
- Service sites are quoted at 2-4% calls plus forms (third party, marketing). Our service pages' 3.9 taps per 1,000 is well below that, and it was off season. There is no event data before Sep 16, so a July number does not exist yet.
- Only about 36% of US views on the 88 guides fall Monday to Friday, 8am-5pm Central (assuming GA4 reports Eastern time). Lead Smart says 82% of connected calls arrive in those hours and 85.9% on weekdays (network, all trades). The low case allows for this.

**What each result would look like at 1,000 visits**

| If the true rate is | Stop (0-1 paid) | Wait (2-4) | Go (5+) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 per 1,000 | 98% | 2% | 0% |
| 1 per 1,000 | 74% | 26% | 0.4% |
| 2 per 1,000 | 41% | 54% | 5% |
| 3.5 per 1,000 | 14% | 59% | 28% |
| 10 per 1,000 (roadmap) | 0% | 3% | 97% |

So January can clearly tell the roadmap's 1 in 100 from everything else. It cannot tell 1 from 3.5 per 1,000. That takes about 2,500 visits: then 6 or more paid calls means go and 2 or fewer means stop. At 1 per 1,000, 6 or more happens 4% of the time. At 3.5 per 1,000, 87% of the time. At 140-200 visits a week, 2,500 visits arrive between late February and Mar 29. I set the re-read at Mar 29 because termite swarm season will have started by then. If visits are below 1,000 on Jan 11, there is no verdict yet.

**Caveats.**
- Two pages (vamoose-for-mice, bugs-that-look-like-cockroaches) are two-thirds of visits. A vamoose reader is shopping for a product, so I report those two separately.
- Visits grew 22, 42, 108 a week in October, and those pages are new. The next few weeks may not keep that pace.
- A reader on a Canadian site may trust a US phone line less. This test cannot measure that.

**The real lever, if calls work.** The pages that bring calls should be pages about hiring. mice-exterminator, mice-removal and termite-damage rank but get almost no US visits. If the test goes well, the next question is which hiring searches we can win, not how many more boxes to add.
