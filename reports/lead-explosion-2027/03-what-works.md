# What works for local service companies, and what it means for 2,000 leads

Research date: Oct 30, 2026. Research only. Nothing was changed in any account. Nothing was signed up for or submitted. All Google Ads numbers are published figures, not from the owner's account. This is not legal advice.

**How sources are graded.** Numbers in [brackets] point to the source list at the end.

| Grade | What it means |
|---|---|

| Google | Google's own help pages |
| Law | A statute or a regulator |
| Filing | A franchise disclosure document. A legal filing, but franchisor-reported and not audited. I read these through summary sites, not the filing itself |
| Vendor | A company selling marketing or software. Sometimes real data, always with a motive |
| Forum | Owners' posts. Anecdote |
| In-house | BuzzSkito's own Hub, GA4 or Search Console numbers |

Every lead range in this report is **my estimate**. Nobody has published a forecast for a GTA mosquito company.

## 1. The answer

**Is 2,000 leads in Apr-Jun 2027 reachable?** Only just, and only if all of this is true:

- Phone calls count as leads. Form leads alone will not get there.
- Every lever below works at the same time.
- The owner spends about **CA$111,000 in about 13 weeks**: about CA$92,000 on ads and print, and about CA$19,000 on sales help.

Search cannot carry it. All search combined looks good for roughly 300 to 1,400 leads (section 5). The rest must come from Meta ads and neighbours, and those are the least proven.

**Can he serve 2,000 leads?** It depends on the close rate. This assumes 15 stops a day per van.

| Close rate on 2,000 leads | New customers | With about 45 returning | Vans needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9% (recorded today) | 180 | 225 | 1 |
| 16% (my all-out mix) | 319 | 364 | 1.6 to 1.8 |
| 20% | 400 | 445 | about 2 |
| 30% | 600 | 645 | about 3 |

So if selling improves as it should, 2,000 leads is **more than two vans can serve**. It is also about 22 leads a day. That is about one full-time person even with the AI first call, and about two without it (section 6).

**The biggest finding is not about leads.** Mosquito Squad's franchise filing discloses close rate across 80 franchisees: **47% average, 33% in the bottom quarter, 15% at the very lowest** [9]. A later filing shows 48% [10]. BuzzSkito's recorded rate is 9%. The definitions differ (section 2), so the true gap is smaller. But it is too large to be only definitions.

Going from 9% to 18% gives the same customers as doubling every lead source. It costs far less.

**So I would change the goal.** The number that matters is about 365 new customers. With about 45 returning, that nearly fills two vans.

| Close rate | Leads needed for 365 new customers |
|---|---|
| 9% (recorded) | 4,056 |
| 15% | 2,433 |
| 20% | 1,825 |
| 25% | 1,460 |
| 30% | 1,217 |
| 47% (Mosquito Squad average) | 777 |

**What I would do:** aim for about 1,350 leads and about CA$57,000, for about 290 customers (about 1.3 vans). Then scale toward 2,000 only if the April tests show a paying customer costs under about CA$700.

**Three inputs need checking before anyone spends money:**

1. **Stops a day.** The brief says 15. The June 2026 operations notes and the Oct 23 Hub plan both say 5 a day, across 7 zones from Niagara to the far east. At 5 a day, one van holds about 77 customers and two vans about 154. Everything here assumes 15. That only works if stops cluster, which is one more reason to want street deals.
2. **Close rate.** The 9% is measured on all leads, including the 38% not clearly in the GTA. On clearly GTA leads it is about 14%. If some customers came by phone and were never logged as leads, the true rate on all leads is different again. In September, 21 of 54 matched customers still showed "quoted" or "new" in the Hub. So the real number is not known.
3. **Definitions.** Mosquito Squad counts a lead as "a new contact entered into the CRM" and a sale as "at least one treatment" [9]. Its one-off jobs count as sales. Its staff may also filter contacts before entering them. BuzzSkito's bar is higher, because its average customer is a season plan. Mosquito Squad's average customer is US$660 (about CA$937), so the prices are not far apart.

These do not simply add up. LSA, Google Ads, the profile and page rankings all compete for the same searches. In a 2018 vendor test, adding LSAs took about 7 points of clicks from organic and about 3.5 from the map pack [22].

Pre-sale is not in the table. It moves leads earlier rather than creating them. It does fill routes and bring cash. Seen in the market: 5% to 15% off for paying up front [43]. BuzzSkito's better version is a bonus spray, about CA$8 of product instead of CA$61-183 off a CA$1,222 plan. If Feb-Mar leads count as spring, add 20 to 80.

### Why each lever sits where it does

**1. Phone-first selling.**
- Mosquito Squad's close rates are the anchor [9]. So is the in-house gap: leads with a phone number became customers at 16.5%, against 3.4% without one. Popup leads closed at 6.3%, form leads at 14.7%.
- In September, 15% of leads had never received a quote email. Median reply, when sent, was 0.7 hours. So speed is decent, but coverage has holes.
- Cost is my estimate: about 17 weeks at about CA$25 an hour, plus the AI at about US$0.11-0.15 a minute.

**2. Count and answer every call.**
- At 10 pest and lawn companies, 3 in 4 organic leads were calls [23] (Vendor, its own clients). BuzzSkito's site pushes forms and a mosquito plan is not an emergency, so I assumed 20% to 50%.
- Only 52% of inbound home-service calls get answered, rising to 73% for calls over 30 seconds [30]. Vendor data, and CallRail reports a much lower missed rate of 14%, but it shows what is at stake.
- Counting changes the number, not the business. Only the calls that were missed and now get answered are truly new.

**3. Local Services Ads.**
- Best paid fit: pay per lead, mostly calls, and rank depends on rating, review count and response time [3].
- A 5.0 rating helps, and "higher quality profiles may also pay lower costs per lead" [3].
- BuzzSkito treats only mosquitoes and ticks. Turn off every other job type, or it pays for mouse and ant calls. An agency says 25-40% of billable calls are off-target [24].
- Since Oct 1, 2026, a missed call in business hours can be charged as a lead if the caller holds about 20 seconds [34a]. Missed calls also hurt rank [3]. So the AI must answer the phone before LSA goes live.

**4. Neighbour system.** How the range was built:

| Piece | Volume | Response assumed | Leads | Cost (CA$) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lawn signs on customers' lawns | 100-200 signs | 0.2-1 lead per sign, May-Jun | 20-200 | 1,000-3,000 |
| Hangers on 6 homes around each new customer | 600-1,200 | 1-3% | 6-36 | 500-1,000 |
| Area drop on the best streets | 15,000 doors | 0.2-1% | 30-150 | 5,000-11,000 |
| Referrals paid in sprays | 60-100 customers | 15-40% refer | 10-40 | under 500 |
| Street deals: 5 homes, each gets a bonus spray | 5-15 deals | 3-5 homes each | 15-75 | small |

All response rates are my judgement from weak sources (section 7, question 6). The reason it ranks this high is route density: more stops on one street lowers the cost of every stop and makes 15 a day possible.

**5. Meta lead ads.** The only channel that can reach people who are not already searching, so the only one that can scale past the search limit. The catch is closing. In-house, popup leads closed at 6.3% and no-phone leads at 3.4%, and Meta leads are likely similar unless called fast.

**6. Past leads.** About 370 unsold leads from Apr-Aug 2026. Under Canada's anti-spam law, an inquiry gives implied consent for only six months [13]. Leads from May run out through November. Leads from June run out through December. A person can send a short note now, inside the window, asking if they want a spring reminder. A yes is express consent, which does not run out. **This is the only lever where waiting loses something permanently.** Customers get two years from their last purchase [13].

**7. Profile and reviews.** Biggest free source of leads at the 10 companies studied [23] (Vendor), but it only reaches so far from its address (section 7, question 2).

**8. Google Ads search.** Published cost per lead is high, and search demand caps it. It can still be worth a small test. Published mosquito ads convert at 2.45% [29], while BuzzSkito's service pages get about one lead per 6 to 10 GTA visits (In-house). Paid visitors may behave differently, so this needs a real test. Any test is the owner's call in his own account.

**9. Page rankings.** Nine of our addresses compete for "mosquito control toronto". The March date in the current plan is too late for May.

**10-11.** Small and unproven. Do them only if there is time left.

## 4. Paid channels: the arithmetic

Cost per customer = cost per lead / close rate. Worth of a customer: about CA$1,000 in the first season. CA$1,222 is the revenue.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meta, home improvement (LocaliQ) [26] | 42.95 | 61 | 678 | 407 | 305 | 203 |
| Meta, pest pilot (Pesty) [32] | 47.12 | 67 | 743 | 446 | 335 | 223 |
| LSA, all trades (SearchLight) [27] | 53.00 | 75 | 836 | 502 | 376 | 251 |
| LSA, pest, only 2 campaigns (99 Calls) [28] | 61.65 | 88 | 973 | 584 | 438 | 292 |
| Google search, home services (LocaliQ) [25] | 90.92 | 129 | 1,435 | 861 | 646 | 430 |
| LSA, pest (Coalmarch) [24] | 96.00 | 136 | 1,515 | 909 | 682 | 454 |
| Google search, mosquito and tick (Evergrow) [29] | 97.71 | 139 | 1,542 | 925 | 694 | 462 |
| All marketing, mosquito (Mosquito Squad filing) [9] | 142.00 | 202 | 2,240 | 1,344 | 1,008 | 672 |
| Google search, pest (Coalmarch) [24] | 148.00 | 210 | 2,335 | 1,401 | 1,051 | 701 |

**What CA$10,000 of ads buys at those published prices.** About CA$222 of every CA$1,222 goes to running the stops. So CA$10,000 of ads pays for itself in the first season only if it brings in about **CA$12,200 of revenue**.

| Source | Leads | Customers at 9% | Revenue at 9% | Customers at 20% | Revenue at 20% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meta, CA$61 a lead | 164 | 15 | 18,000 | 33 | 40,100 |
| LSA, CA$88 a lead | 114 | 10 | 12,500 | 23 | 27,800 |
| LSA, CA$136 a lead | 74 | 7 | 8,100 | 15 | 18,000 |
| Google search, CA$139 a lead | 72 | 6 | 7,900 | 14 | 17,600 |
| Google search, CA$210 a lead | 48 | 4 | 5,200 | 10 | 11,600 |

**What this says:**

- **At the recorded 9%, Google Ads and the higher LSA figures lose money in the first season.** Each customer costs CA$1,435 to CA$2,335, against about CA$1,000 of worth.
- **Meta looks cheap at 9%, but its leads are unlikely to close at 9%.** At 5%, a CA$61 lead means a CA$1,220 customer.
- **At 20% to 30%, nearly everything works.** This is why close rate is lever 1.
- **The real-world anchor is about CA$460.** Mosquito Squad's average cost per sale is US$326 (about CA$463), with quarters from US$138 to US$561 [9]. That is at 47% close, on a definition that counts single treatments.
- **All of this is US data.** No published cost per lead for pest or mosquito in Canada was found on any channel.
- **Second-season value changes the picture but not the cash.** Renewal is 68-70% at Mosquito Squad [9, 10] and 76% at Mosquito Joe [11]. At those rates a customer lasts about 3 to 4 seasons. BuzzSkito's own renewal rate is unknown. And the spending happens before the money comes back.

## 5. Why search alone cannot do it

**In-house check, read-only from Search Console.** Canada, Apr-Jun 2026. Service-type searches that showed BuzzSkito came to about **7,000 impressions**: about 5,300 with "mosquito control", about 930 with "mosquito spray", and under 1,000 for tick services. The largest were:

| Search | Impressions | Position |
|---|---|---|
| mosquito control | 718 | 7.6 |
| mosquito control near me | 632 | 9.2 |
| mosquito control toronto | 615 | 15.4 |
| mosquito control vaughan | 585 | 7.0 |

The big mosquito searches are do-it-yourself products, not buyers: "mosquito bits" had 7,839 and "mosquito dunks canada" 6,418. Any ads must exclude these.

These numbers undercount. Search Console hides rare searches, leaves out Google Maps, and a page-two result gets seen less often. My guess is the real number is 2 to 4 times higher, so **15,000 to 30,000 service searches in the three months**. No public GTA figure exists.

**The arithmetic (my estimate):**
- 15,000-30,000 searches, about 75% ending in a click, BuzzSkito winning 25-40% of clicks across map, organic, LSA and ads, gives 2,800-9,000 clicks.
- At 10-16% leads per visit, that is **280 to 1,440 leads from all of search combined.**

**The page is not the leak.** In May and June 2026, GTA visits to service pages and the homepage came to 1,583, against 255 leads. That is 16% on all leads, or about 10% on clearly GTA leads. Home improvement landing pages have a 2.6% median and 19.5% at the top quarter [35] (Vendor). GA4 undercounts visits, so treat this as rough. Adding 1,600 leads at those rates would take 10,000 to 16,000 more visits, 6 to 10 times the service-page traffic of May and June 2026.

**A sanity check from the filing (my arithmetic).** An average Mosquito Squad territory in 2022: US$451,658 in sales at US$660 a customer is about 684 customers. With 68% renewal and 14% growth, about 276 are new. At 47% close that is about 590 leads a year, at about US$83,000 of marketing [9]. So 2,000 leads in 13 weeks is more than **three times what an average US territory seems to get in a whole year.** The GTA is bigger than one territory, so this is a warning, not a limit.

### Question 2. Google Business Profile and the map pack

**Clicks.** A 2018 test with 5,500 people in San Francisco, without LSAs on the page: organic 50.8%, map pack 32.3%, paid 14.6%. With LSAs: organic 43.9%, pack 28.8%, LSA 13.8%, all paid 25.3% [22]. Vendor test, old, one city, no phone split. No better public test found.

**What Google says.** Rank rests on relevance, distance and prominence. "More reviews and positive ratings can help." "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking" [7].

**What moves rankings.** Whitespark's 2026 survey of 47 experts, 187 factors, published Nov 6, 2025 [19]. Expert opinion, not measurement.

| Factor | Rank for map pack | Rank for conversion |
|---|---|---|
| Primary category | 1 | |
| Distance from searcher | 2 | 3 |
| Keywords in business name | 3 | |
| Address in the city searched | 4 | |
| Open at time of search | 5 | 6 |
| High Google rating | 6 | 1 |
| Additional categories | 8 | |
| Number of Google reviews with text | 9 | 5 |
| Recency of reviews | 11 | 7 |
| Photo and video quality | 45 | 11 |
| Number of photos | 96 | |
| Frequency of posts | 148 | |

**Posts rank near the bottom, so stop spending time on them.** Photos matter more for winning the click than for ranking. The often-quoted "42% more direction requests from photos" has no named source [50].

**Reviews.**

| Finding | Number | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Reviews held by pest control companies in the map pack | Lowest tenth 18, **median 265**, top tenth 1,708 [20] | Vendor, 50M US results. Correlation only |
| Rating held by pest control companies in the map pack | Lowest tenth 4.6, median 4.9 [20] | Vendor |
| Metro vs rural | Metro needs about 1.5-2 times the reviews [20] | Vendor |
| Consumers who will only use 4+ stars | 68% [21] | Vendor survey, 1,002 US adults, Feb 2026 |
| Won't use a business with under 20 reviews | 47% [21] | Vendor survey |
| Look for reviews from the last three months | 74% [21] | Vendor survey |
| Expect the owner to reply | 89% [21] | Vendor survey |
| Ranking jump at 10 reviews, little from more after that | Small study, 2022 [48] | Vendor |
| Fresh reviews lifted rank | Small study [48] | Vendor |

**What this means for BuzzSkito:**
- **200 reviews is not a magic line.** It is below the 265 median, and metros run higher. Mosquito Man has 900+, LawnSavers 1,070+. Expect reviews to help LSA rank and winning the click more than map rank.
- **A seasonal business has a recency gap.** By May, the newest review could be seven months old, and 74% of readers look at the last three months. Ask all 2026 customers in March and April.
- **Do not pay for reviews.** Google bans any discount or free service in exchange for a review. It also bans asking only happy customers [8]. A bonus spray for a review could get reviews removed and the profile restricted.
- **The profile cannot cover 19 cities.** In a study of about 1,000 law firms, 56% ranked first at their own address, and average rank fell to about 20 by mile 3 in the Miami example [34]. Law firms, not pest companies, but the shape fits Google's own statement. The profile is strong in Mississauga. Other cities need LSA, ads and pages. A fake address is not an option.
- **Set real hours.** "Open at time of search" scores high on both lists. Once the AI answers the phone, longer hours may be honest. Only list them if they are true.
- **Check the category.** Confirm the primary category and additional categories. Top of the list and free.

## 11. Rules and legal flags

Everything here keeps the owner's rule: **a person presses Send on every message, except the AI first call and the 48-hour follow-up.**

- **Google Ads:** research only. We change nothing in his account. Google Ads and LSA choices above are his.
- **Anti-spam (CASL):** implied consent lasts six months after an inquiry and two years after a purchase [13]. Applies to email and texts. Check the current form wording: if a lead gave express consent, that does not expire.
- **AI calls:** automated sales calls need express consent in advance, and an existing relationship is not an exemption [14]. The CRTC opened a review of these rules in June 2026 [14a]. Add a consent line to the quote form, and to any Meta form, before spring. **Get a lawyer's view on this one.** Calling hours and do-not-call rules are in the Hub plan.
- **Reviews:** no reward for a review, no asking only happy customers [8].
- **Pesticide signs:** Ontario's notice signs must match the ministry's drawings [15]. I could not confirm whether a company name or ad may be added. So keep them untouched and use a separate lawn sign, with the customer's OK. Check city sign by-laws, and respect "no flyers" signs.
- **Ad wording:** the claim rules apply to ads, signs and hangers too. No "safe", no "Health Canada approved".
