# ChatGPT Shopping Skips Publishers; Google Still Pays

There is a real opportunity, but it is small, and it runs through only one of the two doors the question imagines. **ChatGPT's shopping features do not send shoppers to review sites.** Its product cards, "Shop at" buttons, buyer's guides and ads are built from retailers' own product data and send buyers to Walmart, Target, Best Buy and brand stores. OpenAI pays publishers nothing, the feed-powered shopping is officially live only in the U.S., and **Amazon blocks OpenAI's crawlers on both amazon.com and amazon.ca**, so none of ChatGPT's own buy buttons carries BuzzSkito's Amazon tag. **The open door is the source link (the "citation") in ChatGPT's written answers.** A reader who clicks one lands on a BuzzSkito page, sees its ads and uses its Amazon links exactly like a Google visitor, and the site is already cited: 118 times across 64 pages in Ahrefs' ChatGPT sample, about twice bettertermite.com's 57. The catch is volume. All AI assistants together sent **0.28% of website visits** in March 2026, about one for every 99 from Google ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/)), and most ChatGPT clicks now land on homepages, which on buzzskito.ca carry no ads and no Amazon links. On the project's own numbers, a strong ChatGPT showing is worth **roughly $4 to $40 a month at the July 2027 peak, against a goal of $5,066 for that month**, although a single GTA spray customer sent by ChatGPT ($549 to $2,049 a season) would outweigh a year of it. The rumor that Google is "massively pushing out" OpenAI is mostly overstated: Google refused OpenAI direct access to its index, made scraping harder and sued a scraper OpenAI reportedly used, but it never named OpenAI, lost the core of that lawsuit in July 2026, and on October 21, 2026 agreed to sell OpenAI's models on Google Cloud ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/21/openai-google-cloud-ai-models)). Because the work that wins on Google is the same work that earns ChatGPT citations, the plan already underway stays the plan, plus three cheap fixes (re-verify the site with Bing, correct Bing's wrong business listing, check ChatGPT's answers to GTA homeowners each month) and a short list of tempting spends to skip.

## ChatGPT's shopping tools send buyers to Walmart, not to review sites

ChatGPT assembled its shopping features in quick steps. It began showing products for shopping questions in September 2025 and launched in-chat buying ("Instant Checkout," built with Stripe) on September 29, 2025 ([OpenAI release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). "Shopping research," which asks the shopper a few questions and writes a personalized buyer's guide, followed on November 24, 2025 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-shopping-research/)), and virtual try-on and saved favourites came on October 1, 2026 ([OpenAI release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). In-chat buying disappointed: Walmart reported those purchases converted at one-third the rate of shoppers who clicked through to Walmart.com ([martech.org](https://martech.org/walmart-says-chatgpt-checkout-converted-3x-worse-than-its-own-website/)), and in March 2026 OpenAI conceded the feature "did not offer the level of flexibility that we aspire to provide," sent purchases back to merchants' own checkouts and refocused on "product discovery" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)). Discovery runs on **product feeds**, files in which retailers send OpenAI their products, prices and stock. Target, Sephora, Nordstrom, Lowe's, Best Buy, The Home Depot and Wayfair are named partners, Walmart runs its own app inside ChatGPT, and every Shopify store is connected automatically ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)). OpenAI's merchant page is plain about geography: **"Shopping is currently live in the U.S. and will expand to additional regions over time"** ([chatgpt.com/merchants](https://chatgpt.com/merchants/)). Labelled ads under answers are a separate product; they reached Canadian users of the Free and Go plans on April 16, 2026 and 89 countries by October 21, 2026, when OpenAI put ChatGPT at 1.2 billion weekly users ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-26-new-countries/)).

How ChatGPT picks products leaves no seat for a review site at the moment of purchase. OpenAI says product results are "selected independently by ChatGPT and are not ads," are built from "structured metadata from first-party and third-party providers (e.g., price, product description)," and list sellers by "availability, price, quality, and whether they are the maker or primary seller of that item" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)). Only a merchant has those things. Independent trackers show the tilt toward big merchants growing: on July 10, 2026 the share of recommendations built from merchant feeds jumped from **8% to 62%**, and the ten biggest merchants' share of product references rose from **22.5% to 41.8%** ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/chatgpt-shopping-results-lean-hard-on-product-feeds/589000/)). The "Shop at" buttons that appeared under recommended products from October 10, 2026 pointed **56% of the time to big-box retailers** (a group that includes Amazon) and about 5% of the time to the brand's own store, in one early and small sample ([Wislr](https://www.wislr.com/articles/chatgpt-shop-at-buttons-big-box/)). A study of ChatGPT shopping citations found that "Walmart consistently appears as the top buy-link destination, with Target behind it" ([Azoma via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/26/3351631/0/en/what-sources-does-chatgpt-cite-for-products-azoma-publishes-the-data-and-explains-how-brands-get-recommended.html)). OpenAI's own pitch casts the product as a replacement for the affiliate page: shopping on the web "often means jumping between tabs, reading the same 'best of' lists," and "ChatGPT solves that" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)).

Nor is there a way to be paid for the shopping a BuzzSkito page informs. **OpenAI runs no affiliate, commission or revenue-share program for publishers.** Asked in June 2026 whether OpenAI would share ad revenue with the publishers whose content appears next to ads, its vice-president of media partnerships, Varun Shetty, answered "Not at this point"; only large media groups such as News Corp, Hearst and Condé Nast hold licensing deals, on undisclosed terms ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/openai-not-planning-to-share-advertising-revenue-with-publishers/)). Merchants pay OpenAI nothing on unpaid recommendations ("There are no fees on purchases that start in ChatGPT"), so no commission exists to share ([chatgpt.com/merchants](https://chatgpt.com/merchants/)). OpenAI earns from ads instead, priced like search ads, with a suggested starting bid of **$3 to $5 a click** ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chatgpt-ads-manager-now-supports-product-feeds-after-checkout-is-killed/)).

Amazon has taken itself out of ChatGPT, which matters more to BuzzSkito than any OpenAI feature. On October 21, 2026, **both amazon.com and amazon.ca block OpenAI's search crawler (OAI-SearchBot), its browsing agent (ChatGPT-User) and its training crawler (GPTBot)** in the rules files that tell crawlers what they may read ([amazon.com robots.txt](https://www.amazon.com/robots.txt); [amazon.ca robots.txt](https://www.amazon.ca/robots.txt)), and in reporters' tests ChatGPT answered "I can't browse or display live product listings from Amazon" ([Digiday/Modern Retail](https://digiday.com/media-buying/openais-new-chatgpt-shopping-tool-promises-in-depth-research-just-not-for-amazon-products/)). Amazon's $50 billion investment in OpenAI in February 2026 did not lift the block ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/20/open-ai-agentic-shopping-etsy-shopify-walmart-amazon.html)), and Amazon is pushing its own assistant, renamed Alexa for Shopping on May 13, 2026 and offered only in the U.S. ([About Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-rufus)). Two consequences follow. A shopper who finishes the whole trip inside ChatGPT is steered toward Walmart, Target or Best Buy, and any ChatGPT button that does reach Amazon carries no BuzzSkito tag. But a ChatGPT reader who clicks through to a BuzzSkito page and then clicks its tagged Amazon link earns the site exactly what a Google reader does, because Amazon credits the tagged click and what the shopper buys within 24 hours, whoever sent the reader ([Amazon.ca Associates](https://associates.amazon.ca/help/node/topic/G9SMD8TQHFJ7728F)); the research found no report of ChatGPT stripping tags or of Amazon treating these visits differently. And because ChatGPT cannot read Amazon, pages that describe Amazon-sold products are one of the few ways those products reach its answers, an opening that is plausible but unmeasured.

The U.S.-only rollout tilts slightly in BuzzSkito's favour at home. A Canadian ChatGPT user is officially outside the feed-powered shopping experience and so more often gets a written answer with sources, the format in which a publisher can win the click; the U.S. guides with their amazon.com picks face the full experience, where retailers' buttons compete for the same reader. No OpenAI page says exactly what Canadian users see today, and this research ran no test prompts, so treat that as a reasoned expectation rather than a measurement. One more signal matters with Mediavine ads planned for December 1, 2026 ([goal-2027.md](../data/god-build/goal-2027.md)): shopping research is "trained to avoid sites that may be low quality, spammy or overly commercial," and Isa Fulford, who leads OpenAI's Deep Research and ChatGPT agent teams, defined low-quality sources as "sites where there are pop-up ads" ([Digiday/Modern Retail](https://digiday.com/media-buying/openais-new-chatgpt-shopping-tool-promises-in-depth-research-just-not-for-amazon-products/)). That is one interview quote, not a published rule, but it costs nothing to respect.

## Citations are the one open door, and BuzzSkito already gets through it

Publishers still feature heavily as sources, even in shopping answers. A study of millions of ChatGPT shopping citations from the second quarter of 2026 counted **41% "earned media"** (publisher reviews and lists), 37% retailers, 19% user posts and 3% brand sites ([Azoma via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/26/3351631/0/en/what-sources-does-chatgpt-cite-for-products-azoma-publishes-the-data-and-explains-how-brands-get-recommended.html)). A September 2026 sample of 17,524 AI answers found that **61% of ChatGPT's "best product" answers cited at least one affiliate review site** ([Suff Digital](https://www.suffdigital.com/resources/data-studies/ai-product-answers-affiliate-sites)), and Reddit alone supplied about a third of shopping citations in a June 2026 sample ([Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-shopping-end-to-end-breakdown)). In BuzzSkito's niche, Bing's AI answers on October 21, 2026 cited This Old House, Bob Vila, Business Insider, Healthline, wikiHow and PestSource for "best roach killer" and "how to get rid of mice" ([Bing](https://www.bing.com/search?q=best+roach+killer); [Bing](https://www.bing.com/search?q=how+to+get+rid+of+mice)); ChatGPT itself was not queried. Those big home publishers usually claim hands-on product testing, which BuzzSkito's own rules forbid it to claim, so its edge has to come from what they lack: whether a product is legal in Canada (PMRA registration), label and active-ingredient facts, government and university extension data, and separate regional editions.

BuzzSkito is already in the game. Ahrefs' tracking, pulled on October 21, 2026, finds **3,959 AI citations of 321 buzzskito.ca pages**, set against bettertermite.com, one of the comparable sites the build plan studies ([Ahrefs API pull](https://docs.ahrefs.com/docs/api/reference/introduction)):

| Where the citation appeared | buzzskito.ca: citations (pages) | bettertermite.com: citations (pages) |
|---|---|---|
| All AI platforms | 3,959 (321) | 20,383 (776) |
| Google AI Overviews (keyword sample) | 2,737 (262) | 18,481 (731) |
| Perplexity | 471 (133) | 829 (160) |
| **ChatGPT** | **118 (64)** | **57 (36)** |
| Copilot | 93 (49) | 29 (21) |

BuzzSkito already beats bettertermite.com in ChatGPT and Copilot. Where bettertermite.com leads, by about seven to one, is Google's AI Overviews (the AI summary at the top of Google results), whose citations follow Google rankings, so **the citation gap is a Google-ranking gap, not a formatting gap**. Ahrefs samples each platform at a different size, so compare the two sites within a row, not platforms within a column.

Technically, nothing is left to fix for ChatGPT. Every AI search crawler, OpenAI's included, receives the full page in under half a second; pages arrive as readable text before any scripts run, with sources, visible dates, tables and FAQs; and no setting stops an assistant from quoting them ([buzzskito.ca/robots.txt](https://buzzskito.ca/robots.txt)). That matters because OpenAI says sites that block its search crawler "will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers" ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots)). One correction for the project's notes: CLAUDE.md still says the site blocks GPTBot and CCBot, but both have been allowed since the owner's decision of July 16, 2026 ([app/robots.ts](../app/robots.ts)). GPTBot only gathers training data, and OpenAI says "Each setting is independent of the others," so the choice has no bearing on whether ChatGPT search cites the site; leave it and correct the note.

The evidence on what earns citations points to habits that also win on Google. ChatGPT breaks a question into several narrower searches, about 4.7 on average in one test run through OpenAI's developer API ([AI+Automation](https://aiplusautomation.com/blog/bing-replication-chatgpt-citations)), which is why only **8% of its citations rank in Google's top 10 for the original question** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-search-overlap/)). A page that answers one specific question (a look-alike, a sign, an "X vs Y," a state or province) is what those narrower searches find, and that is how the build already works. AI-cited pages are about **26% newer** than Google's results, and ChatGPT is the most freshness-minded assistant ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/do-ai-assistants-prefer-to-cite-fresh-content/)); about **44% of ChatGPT citations come from the first 30% of a page** ([Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/studies-reveal-ai-citation-clues)); and adding statistics, quotations and named sources raised visibility by up to 40% in a laboratory benchmark ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.09735)). The strongest predictor sits off the site: across 75,000 brands, mentions in YouTube videos (correlation about 0.74) and on other websites (0.66 to 0.71) tracked AI visibility far more closely than backlinks (0.22), though correlation does not prove cause ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-brand-visibility-correlations)).

The popular extras have no evidence behind them. Across about 300,000 domains, having an llms.txt file made no measurable difference to citations ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/llms-txt-shows-no-clear-effect-on-ai-citations-based-on-300k-domains/)), and Google says "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). In a controlled test of 1,885 pages, adding schema markup lifted ChatGPT citations by 2.2%, statistically indistinguishable from zero ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/)), and a study of 10,000 local-business sites found no meaningful relationship between page speed and AI citations ([Insites](https://insites.com/blog/does-website-speed-matter-for-ai-search-we-tested-it-against-10000-sites/)). Writing aimed at machines now carries a penalty risk: Bing's February 2026 guidelines name "Artificially Engineered Language" as abuse and warn that "large-scale content generated without oversight, quality control, or editorial review" may be dropped from the index ([Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/bing-adds-geo-to-official-guidelines-expands-ai-abuse-definitions/568442/)). At 40 AI-drafted pages a week, the site's honest authorship notice, second fact-check pass and genuine "Reviewed by" lines are exactly the evidence of oversight that wording looks for.

The one real technical weakness is Bing. Bing is only one of several sources ChatGPT uses (newer studies match 8% to 27% of its citations to Bing results), but it is the foundation of Microsoft's Copilot and of Web IQ, a Bing-based service that press coverage says ChatGPT uses in part ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/microsoft-releases-web-iq-powered-by-bing-but-designed-for-how-ai-agents-search-479194)). buzzskito.ca has no direct Bing verification: `/BingSiteAuth.xml` returns "not found" and the homepage has no Bing verification tag ([buzzskito.ca](https://buzzskito.ca/BingSiteAuth.xml)). Bing has refused the site's instant new-page notices (IndexNow) since at least September 2026 ([project note, Sep 14, 2026](file:///C:/Users/buzzs/.claude/projects/C--Users-buzzs-buzzskito-website/memory/reference_bing_indexnow_healthy.md)), and in a small spot check, tick pages released October 16 that Google had indexed by October 19 were still missing from Bing on October 21, while Bing still listed an old address that now redirects. Bing's business panel also describes BuzzSkito as **"Milton · Open 24 hours"**, while the site gives a Mississauga base and Monday-Friday 8am-6pm, Saturday 9am-4pm hours ([Bing](https://www.bing.com/search?q=buzzskito); [buzzskito.ca/llms.txt](https://buzzskito.ca/llms.txt)). Fixing verification also unlocks Bing's free AI Performance report, which lists Copilot citations, the cited pages and the phrases the AI searched with ([Bing Webmaster Blog](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview)). No equivalent exists for ChatGPT, which shares citation data only with publishers that license content to OpenAI ([Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/bings-improved-ai-performance-section)).

## At the 2027 peak, ChatGPT's clicks are worth $4 to $40 a month

ChatGPT is enormous (OpenAI says users ask it 50 million shopping questions a day) ([Digiday/Modern Retail](https://digiday.com/media-buying/openais-new-chatgpt-shopping-tool-promises-in-depth-research-just-not-for-amazon-products/)), but it is built to answer inside the chat, and the traffic it sends out is a sliver of Google's. In Ahrefs' panel of 74,752 websites, all AI assistants together sent 3.5 million visits in March 2026, **0.28% of all traffic**, against 345 million from Google; ChatGPT's 2.7 million works out to **one visit for every 128 from Google** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/)). ChatGPT's share hit a record 0.32% of all website traffic in May 2026, after it made brand names clickable ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/chatgpt-referral-traffic-may-2026/)), while in Chartbeat's mostly-news network AI referrals were 0.01% of traffic and fell during 2026 for all five assistants measured ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/seo/chartbeat-google-search-publisher-traffic-down-40-percent/)): the channel is small and unstable. A citation rarely becomes a visit. People clicked a link inside Google's AI summaries on **1% of visits** ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)), and when AI assistants mentioned Ahrefs they linked to it only about 10% of the time ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-chatbot-traffic/)).

Where ChatGPT's clicks land thins the affiliate case further. Since brand names became clickable in May 2026, about **62% of ChatGPT's clicks to websites go to homepages**, up from 26-29% before ([Similarweb](https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/gen-ai-stats/)). BuzzSkito's homepage is its quote page: Mediavine's script runs there with every ad switched off, and it has no Amazon links ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md); [app/page.tsx](../app/page.tsx)). For ad and Amazon income a homepage click earns nothing; for the spray business it is the best possible landing.

The project's own numbers put a ceiling on the ad and Amazon side. The table takes the goal for Google visitors in July 2027, the busiest month planned, and applies the panel ratios above with the site's measured earnings.

Even the generous column, which assumes every article visitor behaves like a product-page reader and clicks to Amazon 50% more often than usual, comes to **under 1% of the July goal**. Visitor quality does not rescue it. Retailers see AI-referred shoppers convert 60% better than other visitors ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/08/19/adobe-ai-referral-traffic-data-july-2026/)), but nobody publishes the equivalent for affiliate or ad-supported sites, and a better rate on a sliver is still a sliver.

The exception is the service side. One GTA customer is worth **$549 to $2,049 a season** ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)), more than a full year of the generous column, and ChatGPT's habit of sending brand clicks to homepages suits a business whose homepage takes quote requests. OpenAI said in June 2026 that its updated model makes "shopping and local business queries" more useful by bringing together "product recommendations, business information, and images" ([OpenAI release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). Whether ChatGPT names BuzzSkito when a Mississauga homeowner asks who sprays for mosquitoes has never been checked: this research submitted no prompts and did not study what drives ChatGPT's local recommendations. It is a five-minute test the owner can run himself, in a private, signed-out browser window, because ChatGPT tailors answers to a signed-in user's saved Memory ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11128490-shopping-with-chatgpt-search)).

The AI that moves BuzzSkito's income most is Google's own. In a randomized experiment with 1,065 U.S. users in January and February 2026, Google's AI Overviews **cut clicks to websites by 38%** on the searches where they appeared and raised the share of searches ending with no click from 54% to 72% ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-cut-organic-clicks-38-field-study-finds/573145/)). Pew found that searches starting with a question word got an AI summary 60% of the time, against 8% for one- or two-word searches ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)), which puts pest identification and "how do I get rid of" questions in the exposed group; product searches, where 80% of BuzzSkito's Amazon clicks happen ([reverse-engineering-2026-10-20.md](../data/amazon/reverse-engineering-2026-10-20.md)), are probably less exposed, though nobody has measured pest product searches specifically. Once an Overview appears, being cited in it beats being absent: cited brands got about 35% more organic clicks in one vendor's data ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overview-ctr-fell-61-but-clicks-didnt-collapse/572993/)). About 83% of BuzzSkito's tracked AI citations already sit in Google's AI products (2,957 in AI Overviews, 222 in Gemini and 97 in AI Mode, out of 3,959), partly because Ahrefs samples Google most heavily ([Ahrefs API pull](https://docs.ahrefs.com/docs/api/reference/introduction)). **"Getting cited by AI" is worth most as a defence of Google traffic, not as new traffic from ChatGPT.**

## Google is out-competing OpenAI, not locking it out

The rumor blends documented events with motives nobody has shown. Here is the record as of October 21, 2026.

| "Google is shutting OpenAI out." | A U.S. court ordered Google to share search-index data with "qualified competitors," which Google is appealing ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-antitrust-ruling-what-the-remedies-mean-for-search-seo-and-ai-assistants/555086/)); ChatGPT still appears to draw on Google-derived results through data vendors ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/chatgpt-built-its-own-search-index)); and on October 21, 2026 Google Cloud agreed to sell OpenAI's models ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/21/openai-google-cloud-ai-models)). | **False** |

In short, the rumor is **true as competition, unproven as a targeted campaign and false as exclusion**. For BuzzSkito the effect is indirect. ChatGPT blends several sources: what researchers read as OpenAI's own index (nicknamed "Labrador," a reading OpenAI has not confirmed), Microsoft's Bing-based Web IQ, and Google-derived results bought from scraping vendors ([Peec AI](https://peec.ai/blog/chatgpt-built-its-own-search-index)); one study matched more than 83% of ChatGPT's shopping-carousel products to Google Shopping's top results ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/new-finding-chatgpt-sources-83-of-its-carousel-products-from-google-shopping-via-shopping-query-fan-outs-470723)). Ranking on Google therefore still feeds ChatGPT. If Google's campaign against scrapers eventually succeeds, ChatGPT would have to lean harder on its own crawler, which the site already welcomes, and on Bing, where the site's verification appears to have lapsed, so the Bing fix below doubles as cheap insurance against the rumor coming true. Gemini's rise does not call for separate work either. ChatGPT still sent about **80% of AI chatbot clicks to websites** in September 2026 ([Statcounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/ai-chatbot-market-share)), and Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode cite only pages that are in Google's own index, with "no additional requirements" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)), which the existing plan already targets.

## Five moves ranked by income, and six traps to skip

Ranked by expected effect on income over the next twelve months, the moves come out as below. The first is the existing plan, on purpose: the biggest income risk in this whole topic is distraction.

The Bing and ChatGPT checks need about half an hour of the owner's time and one small code change. In Bing Webmaster Tools, check whether buzzskito.ca is listed and how it was verified, then download the BingSiteAuth.xml verification file; a Claude session puts that file in the site's public folder and deploys it, and pressing Verify gives the site an ownership record that no longer depends on the Google Search Console link, which Bing says must be reconnected whenever it breaks ([Bing Webmaster Blog](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/september-2019/Import-sites-from-Search-Console-to-Bing-Webmaster-Tools)). Then submit `https://buzzskito.ca/sitemap.xml`, check on the IndexNow page that the next deploy's notices are accepted, and open AI Performance. In Bing Places for Business, correct the town and hours behind "Milton · Open 24 hours." If Bing still refuses the notices after verification, contact Bing Webmaster support before generating a new key, since the project's own notes warn against changing the key casually. For the ChatGPT check, open a private browser window, go to chatgpt.com without signing in, ask the questions a GTA homeowner would ask ("Who is the best mosquito control company in Mississauga?", "How much does mosquito spraying cost in Oakville?", "Is BuzzSkito any good?"), and write down which businesses it names and what it says about BuzzSkito; any wrong fact points to the listing that needs fixing.

## Conclusion

The question pictured ChatGPT shopping as a new road to the affiliate page; the evidence shows a product built to replace that page. OpenAI hands the purchase to merchants, earns from ads and pays publishers nothing, and an Amazon affiliate sits in the weakest seat of all because Amazon has walked out of ChatGPT. What remains is the citation, a by-product of the same specific, sourced, fresh pages that win on Google. The more useful finding is that AI pulls BuzzSkito's two businesses in opposite directions: the information pages lose clicks to AI answers, mostly Google's own Overviews on question-style searches, while ChatGPT's switch to sending brand clicks to homepages favours a local service whose homepage is its quote page. That makes AI citations a defence of Google traffic rather than a new income line, and it turns ChatGPT's real upside into a question about whether it recommends BuzzSkito the spray company each spring, which costs ten minutes a month to watch.

Four events would justify reopening the question: OpenAI announcing any revenue share or affiliate payment for publishers, ChatGPT shopping launching in Canada, Amazon unblocking OpenAI's crawlers, or the monthly check showing ChatGPT above about 1% of the site's visits. As of October 21, 2026, none is in sight.
