## 0. The plan on one page

**Why we're doing this.** The buying pages (best rat trap, bed bug spray, cockroach gel bait and the others) sit at Google positions 7-10. October's content upgrades moved them about 0.1 on average. Only about 4 real websites link to buzzskito.ca: The Epoch Times, CityNews, homeowner.ca and knowanimals.com (the Oct 21 count). None of them links to a buying page. Answering journalists' questions is the cheapest honest way to earn links from news and lifestyle sites.

**Top 3 services (all free):**
1. **Source of Sources (SOS).** Up to 3 emails a day. It had the highest share of followed links of any platform measured.
2. **HARO (Help a Reporter Out).** 3 emails a day, free, with no limit on how many you answer.
3. **Qwoted, free plan.** The best outlets of any platform, but only 2 pitches a month on the free plan.

Also worth setting up: **Connectively's free plan**, which gives 3 answers a month.

**First 3 steps (this week, about 35 minutes, once):**
1. **Get the basics ready.** Take a real, recent photo of yourself. Make sure your LinkedIn shows the same name, the title "Co-owner, BuzzSkito Mosquito & Tick Control" and the same photo. HARO and Connectively check LinkedIn, and Qwoted approves each account by hand.
2. **Subscribe to the two free email lists.** SOS: fill in the form at sourceofsources.com. HARO: click "Sign Up" at helpareporter.com. Use the inbox you read every weekday.
3. **Create the free Qwoted profile.** Go to app.qwoted.com/users/sign_up, paste the bio from section 3, add your photo and topics, and skip every upgrade. Then tell Claude "platforms done".

**The weekly routine:** 30 minutes, in two parts:
- a 2-minute scan of the newest HARO and SOS emails each weekday at about 1 p.m.;
- two 10-minute answers a week, when a question fits.

The full routine is in section 6.

**What to expect.**
- Realistic case: just under 1 new linking site a month in winter, and 1-1.7 a month from April to June, when the tick and mosquito questions arrive. The cautious case is about a third of that.
- By Aug 31, 2027: about **4 (cautious) to 11 (realistic)** new real linking sites. Roughly half will be followed links, and 1-3 will come from big outlets.
- That would take buzzskito.ca from 4 real linking sites to roughly 8-15. The working is in section 10.

**Two honest catches:**
1. **The links will mostly not land on the buying pages.** Journalists link the homepage, or the tick, mosquito and data page that fits their story. They will not link the rat-trap or bed-bug-spray guides, because you should not be quoted as an exterminator of rats or bed bugs. The links raise the whole site's trust slowly. On their own they are unlikely to move the rodent, bed bug and cockroach buying pages from 7-10 within a few months. The realistic path to a buying page is a gear roundup inside your own lane: mosquito dunks, repellents, tick removal tools and bug zappers.
2. **Write every answer in your own words.**
   - Qwoted's rules forbid AI-written pitches (Sep 22, 2026), and accounts get disabled for it.
   - Across Featured's three products (Featured, HARO and Connectively), about 1 in 3 human-written pitches was picked, against 1 in 10 fully AI-written ones.
   - On HARO, about 35% of journalists block AI-flagged pitches altogether.
   - So Claude finds the questions, gives you the facts and checks your draft. You write the words.

## 5. Six sample answers (models; send them in your own words)

**Read this first.** Claude wrote these answers. Each one is 100-200 words, and every fact comes from Health Canada, the Public Health Agency of Canada (PHAC), the CDC, a university or the Ontario government. Use them as a checklist of facts and as a model of shape and length. When a real question comes in, write the answer in your own words, aimed at that question. Never paste them into Qwoted: AI-written pitches break its rules. On HARO, SOS and Connectively, AI-written pitches are picked far less often.

### Sample 1: Mice moving indoors in winter

**Typical request (example):** "Home writer looking for pest pros: how do I keep mice out of my house this winter?"

> Mice move in when nights turn cold, so close the gaps they use. University of California pest specialists note that house mice often enter homes in autumn as night temperatures drop, and the CDC says a mouse fits through a hole the width of a pencil, about 6 mm. Health Canada adds that even the gap under a worn door threshold is enough. Walk around the outside of the house and check where pipes and vents pass through the wall, along the foundation and around doors. Pack small holes with steel wool and seal it in with caulk; foam, plastic or wood alone won't stop a mouse. If mice are already inside, Health Canada calls snap traps easy to use and very effective when placed well, about one per metre along the walls, and says poison bait must sit in a tamper-proof station wherever children or pets can reach it. And never sweep or vacuum droppings: the Public Health Agency of Canada says to spray them with one part bleach to nine parts water, let it soak 10 minutes, then wipe up with gloves on.

- **Sources:**
  - CDC, How to Seal Up to Prevent Rodents (pencil width, 6 mm; steel wool and caulk);
  - Health Canada, Rats and mice (worn thresholds; snap traps; 1 trap per metre along walls; tamper-proof bait stations; never sweep dry droppings);
  - UC IPM, House Mouse (Jul 2025: mice enter in autumn; foam, plastic and wood don't work);
  - PHAC, Hantavirus prevention (Jul 29, 2026: 1 part bleach to 9 parts water, 10-minute soak, gloves).
- **Link to offer:** https://buzzskito.ca/blog/how-to-keep-mice-out-of-your-house-winter. Fix its "dime" line first (section 11).
- **Your own line:** usually none, because BuzzSkito doesn't treat mice. Say plainly that BuzzSkito is a mosquito and tick company that publishes free pest guides.

### Sample 2: Bed bugs while travelling

**Typical request (example):** "Travel writer: how do travellers avoid bringing bed bugs home from a hotel?"

> Check the room before you unpack. Health Canada's advice is to leave your suitcase on a hard surface such as the bathroom tile, never on the bed or a soft chair, and to inspect first: lift each corner of the mattress and look along the seams and tufts, behind the headboard, at the bed frame and at the luggage stand where the straps attach. University of Kentucky entomologists say the area behind the headboard is often the first place bed bugs settle in hotels, and that you're looking for the bugs themselves, their shed skins and blackish spots of droppings. If you see signs, ask the front desk for another room that isn't next to that one, or stay somewhere else. During the stay, keep your clothes in the suitcase on the stand rather than in drawers. When you get home, Health Canada says to wash all clothing in hot water whether you wore it or not, dry anything that can't be washed on the highest heat for 30 minutes, and vacuum the suitcase. The CDC says bed bugs are not known to spread disease, though the bites itch and cost sleep.

- **Sources:**
  - Health Canada, Bedbugs: how do I avoid them when travelling? (all luggage, inspection, room-change and return-home steps);
  - University of Kentucky ENTFACT-636 (revised March 2026: behind the headboard; bugs, shed skins, blackish fecal spots);
  - CDC, About Bed Bugs (Apr 2024: not known to spread disease; itching and sleep loss).
- **Link to offer:** https://buzzskito.ca/blog/bed-bugs-in-hotels
- **Your own line:** none. As with sample 1, this is general prevention, not a service BuzzSkito offers.

### Sample 3: Tick season and Lyme disease in Ontario

**Typical request (example):** "Canadian outlet: when does tick season start in Ontario, and how worried should people be about Lyme disease?"

> Sooner than most people expect. The Public Health Agency of Canada says you're most likely to meet ticks in spring, summer and fall, but they can be active whenever the temperature is consistently above freezing and there's no snow on the ground, so a mild spell in November or March counts. Reported Lyme disease keeps climbing: Canada recorded 4,785 cases in 2023 and 5,809 in 2024, and PHAC's preliminary count for 2025 is 7,105. Ontario reported 2,369 cases in 2024, the most of any province. Most cases start in summer: 28% of 2024 cases began in June, 26% in July and 13% in August. The habit that protects you is the tick check. PHAC says infected ticks usually need to be attached for at least 24 hours to pass on the bacteria, so shower within two hours of coming inside and check your scalp, ears, armpits, waist, belly button, groin, behind your knees and between your toes, plus your kids and pets. If you find one, pull it straight out with clean, fine-point tweezers and upload a photo to eTick.ca to have it identified.

- **Sources:**
  - PHAC, Prevention of Lyme disease (Jul 3, 2026: seasons, "consistently above freezing", 24 hours);
  - PHAC, Lyme disease surveillance (2023: 4,785; 2024: 5,809; 2025 preliminary: 7,105);
  - PHAC annual tick-borne disease report (updated Jan 16, 2026: Ontario 2,369; onset June 28%, July 26%, August 13%);
  - PHAC Lyme prevention toolkit (shower within two hours; daily checks of children and pets);
  - PHAC, Top 10 tick hiding spots poster;
  - PHAC, How to remove a tick (tweezers; eTick photo upload).
- **Caution:** PHAC notes that case-definition changes in 2016 and 2024 affect comparisons between years. If a journalist asks "why the rise?", say that, and don't claim a cause.
- **Link to offer:** https://buzzskito.ca/lyme-disease-canada-statistics for the numbers, or https://buzzskito.ca/blog/tick-season-ontario-when-are-ticks-active for the timing. Offer one, whichever fits the story.
- **Your own line (optional, only if true):** where on GTA properties your team most often finds ticks.

### Sample 4: DIY mosquito control that works

**Typical request (example):** "Home and garden writer: what DIY mosquito control actually works, and what's a waste of money?"

> Start with water, because every mosquito starts there. The CDC's advice is to walk the yard once a week and empty and scrub, turn over, cover or throw out anything that holds water: buckets, planters, toys, tires, pools, birdbaths, flowerpot saucers and trash containers. Cover rain barrels tightly or screen them with fine mesh, and for larger water you can't dump or cover, such as an ornamental pond, use a larvicide exactly as the label directs. On your skin, use a registered repellent: Health Canada says adults and children over 12 can use DEET up to 30%, and icaridin is another option for anyone older than six months. What doesn't work well? Health Canada lists bug zappers, electronic and ultrasonic devices, citrosa plants, odour-baited traps and repellent wristbands among products that don't protect well against bites. When University of Delaware researchers counted 13,789 insects killed by backyard bug zappers, only 31, about 0.2%, were biting flies such as mosquitoes.

- **Sources:**
  - CDC, Mosquito Control at Home (Apr 2024: weekly water check and its list; rain barrels; larvicides for water that can't be dumped);
  - Health Canada, Personal insect repellents (DEET up to 30% for adults and children over 12; icaridin not under 6 months; the "Products that do not protect well against biting insects" list);
  - Iowa State University Extension's summary of Frick and Tallamy, University of Delaware (31 of 13,789, 0.22%).
- **Link to offer:**
  - Canadian outlet: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-mosquitoes-in-yard-ontario
  - US outlet: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-mosquitoes
  - A story about gadgets: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/bug-zappers-canada-do-they-work, a buying guide in your lane.
- **Your own line (optional, only if true):** the breeding spot you find most often in GTA yards.

### Sample 5: Wasps in late summer

**Typical request (example):** "Why are wasps so aggressive in late summer, and how do I keep them away from my patio?"

> It's the time of year, not your patio. University of Kentucky entomologists explain that by late summer yellowjacket colonies are near full size, with huge numbers of workers out foraging, and as insect prey gets scarce they scavenge for sweets like fruit, soft drinks and ice cream. That's why they find your drink. Keep food and drinks covered until you eat, check cans and cups before you sip, wipe up spills, keep garbage in tightly closed containers and don't leave pet food out. Health Canada adds one people forget: don't walk barefoot on the lawn in late summer, when wasps are most abundant and active. Workers away from the nest seldom sting unless provoked; the nest is where the trouble is. If a nest isn't a hazard where it is, Health Canada suggests leaving it until November or December, and the University of Kentucky notes that colonies die off in late autumn and old nests aren't reused. If an active nest has to go, Health Canada recommends professional help. And if a sting brings unusual swelling, dizziness or trouble breathing, get medical help right away.

- **Sources:**
  - University of Kentucky ENTFACT-620 (revised March 2026: late-summer colonies, scavenging for sweets, covered food and drinks, foragers seldom aggressive, die-off, nests not reused);
  - Health Canada, Wasps (barefoot in late summer, garbage and pet food, leave the nest until November or December, professional removal, seek care for severe reactions).
- **Link to offer:** https://buzzskito.ca/blog/yellow-jackets
- **Your own line:** none. BuzzSkito doesn't remove wasp nests, so don't imply it does.

### Sample 6: Choosing a pest control company

**Typical request (example):** "What should homeowners ask before hiring a pest control company?"

> Ask for proof first, then for the plan. In Ontario, a business that applies pesticides for a fee needs a provincial operator licence, a licensed exterminator in charge, insurance, and a current-year ministry sticker on every vehicle that carries or applies pesticides, so ask for the licence number and look at the truck. Next, ask which product they'll use and its PCP number. Health Canada says every pesticide registered for use in Canada carries a Pest Control Product registration number on its label, and suggests asking for an itemized invoice that lists the name and registration number of each pesticide used. A good company identifies the pest before it treats. University of California IPM advice is to contact at least three companies, expect a written diagnosis that names the pest, ask for an estimate of the total cost and ask about long-term solutions, not just the first treatment. Penn State Extension's red flags: a "secret formula" or "more potent" pesticide, and pressure to decide on the spot. You never have to sign on the first call.

- **Sources:**
  - Ontario, Guide to pesticide licensing (updated Feb 1, 2024: operator licence for extermination for a fee, licensed exterminator in charge, insurance, current-year vehicle sticker);
  - Health Canada, Pesticides FAQ ("All pesticide products that are registered for use in Canada have a ... (PCP) registration number on the label");
  - Health Canada, Information on pesticide use for landlords and building managers (Oct 2024: itemized invoice with name and registration number);
  - UC IPM, Hiring a Pest Control Company (Aug 2023: at least three companies, written diagnosis and identification, total cost, long-term solutions);
  - Penn State Extension, Choosing a Qualified Pest Management or Lawn Care Company (secret formula; pressure; "You do not have to decide on the phone").
- **Link to offer:** none. A journalist won't link a company's own page in a "how to choose a company" piece. If they ask about costs, offer https://buzzskito.ca/pest-control-cost-canada.
- **Your own line (optional):** "For yard mosquito or tick service, ask how many visits a season you get and what happens if it rains after a visit."

## 9. What to avoid

**Google's link-spam rules.** These are quoted from its spam policies. Bought links are also banned by the owner.
- **"Buying or selling links for ranking purposes."** This includes "Exchanging money for links, or posts that contain links", "Exchanging goods or services for links" and "Sending someone a product in exchange for them writing about it and including a link". For BuzzSkito that means no free spray, discount or gift in return for a link.
- **"Excessive link exchanges ('Link to me and I'll link to you')"** and partner pages that exist only to link to each other.
- **"Using automated programs or services to create links to your site".**
- **"Advertorials or native advertising where payment is received for articles that include links that pass ranking credit".** Any sponsorship we ever pay for must carry `rel="sponsored"`.
- **"Low-quality directory or bookmark site links"**, and **"Creating low-value content primarily for the purposes of manipulating linking and ranking signals"**. That covers guest-post farms and private blog networks.
- **Site reputation abuse.** Don't pay to publish in a big site's "partner" or sponsored section to borrow its ranking.

**The platforms' own rules.**
- SOS removes you for one off-topic reply.
- HARO bans accounts for sending too many pitches.
- Qwoted disables accounts for AI-written pitches.
- Connectively forbids stuffing keywords into your name or link text, and asks you to answer only where you have real expertise.

**BuzzSkito's own rules.**
- Never claim to treat mice, bed bugs, cockroaches or wasps.
- None of these phrases: "Health Canada approved", "safe for kids and pets", "prevents Lyme disease".
- No testing claims, and no prices except our own.
- Tick plans are 5 sprays a season.
- Don't reuse the April 2026 outreach drafts: they carry the wrong name and false claims.
- Never reply to the link sellers who email after any outreach.
- No "guaranteed placement" services, no "pay to be listed" best-of lists, no editing Wikipedia about ourselves and no "scholarship" pages.

### What these links would do for rankings

- **Google treats links as one signal among several.**
  - Its "How Search Works" page says one quality signal is whether "other prominent websites link or refer to the content".
  - Google's Gary Illyes said in April 2024 that "we need very few links to rank pages", and later partly walked it back.
  - In 2023 he said links were no longer a top-3 ranking signal.
  - So links help, but nobody, Google included, publishes a rate at which links turn into positions.
- **Nofollow links still count for something.** Since 2019 Google treats `nofollow` as "hints ... for ranking purposes", so a nofollow mention in a big newspaper is not worthless.
- **Linked pages tend to get traffic.** Ahrefs finds a positive correlation between the number of linking websites and search traffic, and says plainly that correlation is not causation. 96.55% of all pages get no Google traffic at all.
- **Where these links will land.** So far, CityNews linked the homepage, The Epoch Times four tick posts, homeowner.ca the pricing page, and knowanimals.com the bugs-that-look-like-ticks page. None linked a buying page. Strength from the homepage reaches the buying pages only through the header menu (Mosquito Gear, Tick Gear, Bed Bug Control, Mouse & Rat Control), and that is a weak, indirect path.
- **Honest expectation for the rodent, bed bug and cockroach buying pages at positions 7-10:** no measurable change from this channel on its own over the next few months. The best odds are:
  1. tick, mosquito and data pages that are already at positions 5-9 and get a direct followed link from a relevant site;
  2. the mosquito and tick buying guides that you offer in gear roundups.

  Earlier research found that bettertermite.com grew from about 1,000 to 56,000 visits a month while its links stayed flat (`data/god-build/bettertermite-teardown.md`). Links are upside, not the engine.
- **What else you get:**
  - GTA customers from local press.
  - Real people quoted in real news, which builds trust.
  - Mentions in AI assistants' answers. Featured's own 2026 audit (vendor data) found 64.5% of AI citations went to editorial coverage, against 1.7% to brands' own sites.

### How we'll measure (Ahrefs is cancelled)

1. **Monthly:** Alex reads Search Console's Links report ("Top linking sites"). It has no API, and takes 2 minutes.
2. **Every published piece:** Claude checks the page code for our link, its `rel` value and the page it points to.
3. **Every page that gains a followed link:** compare its Search Console position and impressions for the 28 days before the link with days 15-42 after it, against 5 similar pages that gained none. This is the Oct 21 method. With a handful of links it gives a signal, not proof.
4. **Decision points:**

   - **Jun 1, 2027:** if 6 or more pieces have been published and you have the time, consider Qwoted Pro (US$1,188 a year) for the summer. Otherwise stay free.
