# Canadian Amazon plan: CA$10,000 isn't in reach yet, 3-5x is

**Verdict:** the Canadian market is big enough, but the visitors aren't there. Nothing I measured reaches CA$10,000 a month from Amazon.ca on this site, and CA$100,000 is out of reach. A realistic goal is **about CA$1,300-2,400 a month at the July 2027 peak**, against about CA$420 in October. That is my estimate, and it needs the steps below to work. All rates are from August to October 2026, so no start-of-season rate exists yet.

You are right on two things. Amazon pays on almost anything bought within 24 hours, so the pest market doesn't cap you. And more product pages do add money, more than I told you.

## What each target takes

One Amazon click earns about 21-22 cents. One Canadian view of a product page earns about 10 cents.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CA$3,000 | 14,500 | 27,500 | 5x |
| CA$10,000 | 48,400 | 91,700 | 16x |
| CA$100,000 | 484,000 | 917,000 | 164x |

## The market is not the limit

- **Reports:** none covers Canadian consumer pest products properly. The big headline numbers (US$858M-1.6B) are paywalled, and one vendor's own split says about 78% of its figure is business buyers.
- **My estimate:** Canadians spend about CA$310M a year on consumer pest products (CA$178-578M), and about CA$29M of that is on Amazon.ca (CA$6-113M). About two-thirds of the middle figure rests on assumptions ([market notes](../research_notes/Canadian%20Amazon%20earnings%20growth%20plan/canadian_market_size.md)).
- **Why it doesn't cap you:** CA$100,000 a month needs about CA$23M a year in orders, which is most of that CA$29M. But Amazon.ca sells roughly CA$35-51B a year in total, and you earn on whatever is in the cart.
- **The real limit:** how many Canadians reach your pages and click.

## Why the visitors are hard to get

Canadians do search a lot: very roughly 400,000 searches a month on the terms measured, about 160,000 core pest and 240,000 appliances and sprayers. That is good to a factor of two at best ([demand map](../research_notes/Canadian%20Amazon%20earnings%20growth%20plan/canadian_demand_map.md)).

Google keeps most of those clicks away from sites like ours. On 22 Canadian buying searches ([results notes](../research_notes/Canadian%20Amazon%20earnings%20growth%20plan/canadian_product_serps.md)):

- An AI answer and a product grid appeared on all 22.
- Amazon and big retailers held 49% of top-5 spots.
- The first blue link sat a median 1,050 pixels down the page.

**This is the key finding: the wording of the search matters enormously.**

| Search | Our position | Clicks per 100 showings |
|---|---:|---:|
| best mosquito repellent canada | 3.2 | 9.2 |
| mosquito repellent | 11.0 | 0.2 |
| best wasp spray canada | 2.1 | 7.7 |
| wasp spray | 8.4 | 0.3 |

**But those "best" searches are small.** Across the Jun 8-Aug 30 peak, all 125 "best ... canada" searches we showed for made 6,585 showings and 202 clicks, 3.3% of Canadian clicks. Only one passed 1,000 showings in those 84 days, and "best mosquito repellent canada" made about 31 clicks a month. So they are a good target, not a flood.

Rivals are thin too. There is no Canadian Wirecutter, and we already outrank it on "best wasp spray canada". By Similarweb's rough Canada ranking, we beat every Canada-focused product site I checked except one built around "made in Canada".

**Even winning the top of Google has a ceiling** ([yield notes](../research_notes/Canadian%20Amazon%20earnings%20growth%20plan/product_yields.md)):

## Would a lot more product pages do it?

Partly. I told you a median page earns $0.16 a month. **That was too low**, because the old number came from a data pull that misses most Canadian clicks. Re-measured with Analytics:

| A product page built now | Winter, per month | Peak, per month | Per year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Average | CA$1.70-1.90 | CA$3.60-4.60 | About CA$30 |
| Typical (median) | CA$0.44 | CA$0.83-1.25 | About CA$8 |

So 200 pages add about CA$210-910 a month at peak if yields hold, which is real money but not five figures. The top of the 1,000-page row would need about 840,000 more showings a month, against 125,429 seen in August, so don't count on it.

Later pages should also earn less, because the best topics are taken. Of the 32 new page ideas found, only about 8 have clear demand. So build in tested batches, not all at once. The existing 174 product pages also have the biggest gap: "mouse trap" sits at position 32 on our site, with demand of roughly 10,000-30,000 searches a month in November.

### What 500 more "best ... canada" pages would add

The closest comparison is the 73 "best" pages already on the site. Over August to October they earned about CA$149 a month together: CA$2.04 a page on average and CA$1.09 for a typical page, with only one above CA$10. They drew about 24 sessions a page a month, about 90% Canadian.

| 500 pages, once ranked (my estimate) | Amazon, winter | Amazon, peak | Ads, winter | Ads, peak | Total, winter | Total, peak |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low: half a typical page | CA$110 | CA$265 | CA$25 | CA$100 | CA$135 | CA$365 |
| Middle: a typical page | CA$220 | CA$530 | CA$50 | CA$200 | CA$270 | CA$730 |
| High: an average page | CA$855 | CA$2,275 | CA$120 | CA$390 | CA$975 | CA$2,665 |

- **Ads** assume CA$9-15 per 1,000 sessions in winter and about CA$20 at peak. They are estimates until the first Mediavine month, and ads could reduce Amazon clicks, which is unmeasured.
- **The high case** needs about 3.4 times all of August's product-page views, from pages on weaker topics, so I would not plan on it. Only 45 "best" product phrases reached 100 showings at the peak, so 500 pages means going well beyond pests. Demand we don't show for yet can't be seen here, so unseen categories could do better.
- **The middle case** is about CA$5,400 a year, about what 4-5 spray customers bring in. A Google penalty on the shared domain would cost far more, which is why the owner paused 71 pages a day in July.

**Recommendation:** build about 50 first, on winter and year-round topics with real demand. Read them in mid-January, then build 100-200 for summer by March if a typical page earns about CA$1 a month and nothing else on the site falls.

| Aug 2027 | Read real yields, then decide whether to scale pages | You | | |

The real testing is the one thing rivals can't copy: a licensed technician in real yards every day. Top pages on "best dehumidifier canada" show their own measurements, and pages with 517 and 1,104 words outrank ours, so more words won't help. It doesn't pay back from Amazon alone (a mouse trap test might add about CA$6 per four months in clicks), so it has to earn its place through photos, video and press. And we can only claim tests that really happened.

**The only route I can see to five figures** is to go wider than pests: a Canadian buying guide for home and outdoor products, since no real Canadian Wirecutter exists. That is my reading, and nobody has documented it working. It would take years, and a pest brand may not be believed on heaters or air purifiers. I would only test it with dehumidifiers and steam cleaners first, and decide after August 2027.

## What I would not do

- **Paid ads:** Meta costs about 90 cents a click in Canada, and a view earns 10 cents on average and about 46 on the best pages.
- **Levanta:** it pays only on the linked product and needs about 22% of items to be that product. We are at about 8%, so it loses money on most pages. This replaces my earlier +CA$40-125.
- **Chasing bare product words:** retailers hold those pages.
- **Creator Connections:** it appears to be US-only.
- **TikTok and Reels as a direct Amazon driver:** use them only for reach.

## Risks

- **Amazon's rules:** in April 2026 videos on Amazon's own pages stopped earning on other items. If that reached normal links, you would lose about 90% of your items.
- **Commission cuts:** Canada pays 6% on Home against 3% in the US. If Canada fell to the US rate, income would drop about 42%.
- **Google:** HouseFresh, a real testing site, reports losing 91% of its Google visitors in the March 2024 update. AI answers may also take more clicks.
- **What "forget everything else" costs:** CA$120,000 a year from Amazon equals about 131 extra Standard Season spray customers. One customer is worth about 1.5-2 months of today's Amazon income.

## What this research could not see

- No winter or June-July money data exists, and the results pages are one day, on desktop, in the off-season.
- Reddit, Facebook groups and many forums couldn't be read, so "no documented case" means none in what could be read.
- Health Canada's full sales reports couldn't be read, so the newest consumer split is 2016.
- Product-by-product legal checks are not done. Every chemical product needs its registration number checked before a page recommends it.
- Whether we can actually rank for most head terms is untested.
- One researcher opened an Amazon.ca help page once, by mistake, against our rule. Nothing cites it.
