# Product and registration findings from the audit

While refreshing the product hubs and broadening guides, the editors checked the products those pages name against the manufacturer or the regulator. **Nothing was removed or changed on the strength of these findings**: taking a product or a buy link off a page is your decision. Items marked ACTION are the ones most worth your time.

## carpenter-ants-canada

- **Bonide Termite and Carpenter Ant Killer**: Named in plain text only (no link, price or rating) as a WSU example of a product legal in Washington; active ingredient permethrin
  - Evidence: WSU PestSense carpenter ants fact sheet lists 'Bonide Termite and Carpenter Ant Killer Conc' with permethrin. I shortened the name.
- **Combat Source Kill Max**: Named in plain text only as a WSU example; active ingredient fipronil
  - Evidence: WSU lists 'Combat Source Kill Max A2 Fast Killing Formula' with fipronil. The page does not say what form the product takes, so I did not call it a bait.
- **Ortho Bug B Gon Insect Killer**: Named in plain text only as a WSU example; active ingredients bifenthrin and zeta-cypermethrin
  - Evidence: WSU lists 'Orthro Bug B Gon Insect Killer/Lawns and Gardens R-T-Spray' (their typo). I wrote 'Ortho' and shortened the name. It is a lawn-and-garden spray, so I kept it out of the 'sprays applied into the nest' bullet.
- **US borate liquid bait brands (e.g. Terro)**: Deliberately not named in the new US material
  - Evidence: I could not confirm from a primary source that a US borate bait label lists carpenter ants, so the text tells readers to confirm the label names carpenter ants instead.

## bed-bug-spray-canada

- **EcoRaider**: Named in plain text only (no link, price or rating)
  - Evidence: Singh, Wang & Cooper 2014 (Rutgers, J. Econ. Entomol. 107(6)): over 90% nymph mortality in direct spray and forced-exposure residual assays; 87% egg mortality by direct spray; significantly less effective than Temprid SC and Demand CS in the choice-exposure residual assay.
- **Bed Bug Patrol**: Named in plain text only
  - Evidence: Same Rutgers paper: the only other product of the eleven to exceed 90% nymph mortality; little effect on eggs.
- **Temprid SC and Demand CS**: Named in plain text only, as the conventional comparison insecticides in the Rutgers study
  - Evidence: Rutgers paper Table 1 and abstract. No claim added about whether either is sold to consumers.
- **Crossfire, Temprid FX, Bedlam Plus, Transport GHP**: No new claims added
  - Evidence: I did not assert any specific product's EPA registration; the existing Canadian text about these is untouched.

## what-kills-bed-bugs-instantly-canada

- **CimeXa (silica gel dust)**: Named in US extension guidance; no Canadian registration found under that name
  - Evidence: University of Kentucky ENTFACT-636 says silica gel marketed as CimeXa can be very effective if applied correctly; Texas A&M ENT-3012 names Tri-Die and CimeXa as silica aerogel trade names. Health Canada label search for 'cimexa' (registered, domestic + commercial) returned 0 records on 2026-10-07. I did NOT claim it is in the EPA consumer search tool (one extraction showed EPA Reg. 73079-12 listed as 'SiliCide' from Rockwell Labs; unconfirmed, not used).
- **Steri-Fab (contact spray)**: In EPA consumer search tool; no Canadian registration found under that name
  - Evidence: EPA tool results row (two separate extractions agreed): Steri-Fab, Noble Pine Products, EPA Reg. 397-13, actives phenothrin + quats + isopropyl alcohol, marked Mattress and Crack/Surface/Void. Texas A&M says SteriFab or Bedlam kill only on contact. Health Canada search for 'sterifab' and 'steri-fab' returned 0 records. I did not print the reg number or the full ingredient list on the page, only 'the pyrethroid phenothrin among its active ingredients'.
- **Hot Shot bed bug sprays**: US consumer pyrethroid sprays; no Canadian registration found under that name
  - Evidence: Rutgers study (Singh, Wang, Cooper 2014, J. Econ. Entomol. 107(6)) names Hot Shot Bed Bug & Flea aerosol and ready-to-use spray as commonly used consumer products and says pyrethroid sprays are not very effective at eradicating bed bugs due to resistance. EPA tool also lists Hot Shot Bed Bug & Flea Home Insect Killer (permethrin). Health Canada search for 'hot shot' returned 0 records. No active ingredients stated on the page because the study's formulation and the EPA tool's differ.
- **EcoRaider and Bed Bug Patrol (plant-oil sprays)**: FIFRA 25(b)-exempt US products; no Canadian registration found under those names
  - Evidence: Rutgers study abstract: of 11 non-synthetic products only these two caused >=90% nymph mortality in direct spray and forced-exposure residual assays; EcoRaider direct spray caused 87% egg mortality; others had little effect on eggs; further testing in naturally infested environments needed. Paper text says such products are exempt from normal registration under FIFRA Section 25b. Health Canada searches for 'ecoraider' and 'bed bug patrol' returned 0 records. Not verified: whether EcoRaider is now sold under another name.
- **Bedlam and Temprid**: Named in US sources but DO have Canadian registrations
  - Evidence: Health Canada label search returned BEDLAM INSECTICIDE (Reg. 30075, Sumitomo Biorational) and TEMPRID SC (Reg. 32524) plus TEMPRID READYSPRAY (Reg. 32523), all REGISTERED. I therefore excluded them from the 'not on Canadian shelves' list and added one sentence saying the search does return registrations under those names. Marketing class (domestic vs commercial) not verified.
- **Crossfire**: Checked, not used on the page
  - Evidence: Health Canada search for 'crossfire' returned 0 records, but I found no primary US source naming it, so it is not mentioned.

## hub-cockroach-control

- **ACTION** **Boric acid dust / powder (domestic class)**: Cancelled for home use in Canada; three existing guides still recommend it
  - Evidence: Health Canada, 'Changes to the registration of boron pesticides' (canada.ca, modified 2025-10-07): stop using domestic dust and granular products, and solutions except in sealed bait stations or localized gel treatments, as of July 22, 2019; sale prohibited as of July 22, 2018; decision RVD2016-01. best-cockroach-killer-canada (a 'Best Budget Add-On' pick with a buy link), how-to-get-rid-of-cockroaches-canada and advion-cockroach-gel-bait all still describe boric acid powder as a current consumer product. best-cockroach-spray-canada already has it right.
- **Boric acid bait stations and gels (domestic class)**: Still permitted for consumers
  - Evidence: Same Health Canada page: domestic bait stations and gel formulations remain, with the label line 'Apply/use only in areas inaccessible to children and pets.'
- **ACTION** **Advion Cockroach Gel Bait (indoxacarb)**: Registration status not verified; existing guides disagree
  - Evidence: best-cockroach-killer-canada says commercial class in Canada; best-cockroach-spray-canada says not registered in any class (zero label-search records, names VANECTO Reg. No. 35686 instead); the Advion and how-to guides say typically US-labelled stock. I could not run a name search on Health Canada's label database, so the hub only says what all of them agree on: US-labelled stock, check for a PCP number.
- **Enclosed cockroach bait stations (domestic class)**: Presented as the consumer route, no brand named in the hub
  - Evidence: Health Canada cockroach page (modified 2024-03-13) lists bait stations in tamper-proof containers and says to always look for a PCP number on the label. Rutgers FS1322: 'The most effective formula is bait.'
- **Total-release foggers**: Described as not recommended
  - Evidence: University of Kentucky ENTFACT-614: a treatment 'NOT recommended for cockroaches', 'seldom are effective against household pests', ingredients 'tend to be repellent'. Rutgers FS1322: not effective for cockroach control. EPA fogger safety page: propellants typically flammable, improper use may cause fire or explosion.
- **Diatomaceous earth insect dust**: Mentioned once as the registered dust alternative, attributed to the spray guide
  - Evidence: best-cockroach-spray-canada FAQ; Health Canada's cockroach page also lists it. Health Canada calls it 'non-toxic to humans and pets'; I did not repeat that claim.
