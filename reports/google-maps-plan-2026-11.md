## 3. Your Google Business Profile: what to do (you do these)

Off-season is the right time. A change that triggers Google's re-verification shouldn't happen in May.

1. **Hours.** Set the hours someone really answers the phone. The listing now says "Open 24 hours". That matters even more once the pay-per-lead ads run, because Google charges for missed calls during the hours you list.
2. **Business name.** Google's rule: the name must be your "real-world name, as used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery, and as known to customers."
   - The profile shows "BuzzSkito Mosquito Control". Our website calls the company "BuzzSkito Mosquito & Tick Control".
   - **If** that full name is on your truck, invoices and registration, use it on the profile. "Tick Control" in the name helps the "tick control" searches the listing already gets.
   - If the real name is just "BuzzSkito", leave the profile name as it is. Adding words that aren't your name breaks Google's rules and can get the listing suspended.
3. **Categories.**
   - Keep "Pest control service" as the main one.
   - Type "mosquito" and "tick" into the category box to see whether Google offers anything more specific. I couldn't confirm one exists.
   - Don't add unrelated categories like "Lawn care service" just for the keywords. Google says categories must describe what the business is.
4. **Service areas.** List all 19 cities. Google allows up to 20 and says the area should be within about 2 hours' drive, which ours is. Durham stays out.
5. **Services, with prices:**
   - mosquito barrier spray from $99 on a standard lot;
   - season plans: Basic $549, Standard $994, Exclusive $2,049;
   - tick control $597 a season, or $497 added to a mosquito plan.
6. **Website link with a tracking tag.** Paste this as the website: `https://buzzskito.ca/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`.
   - It's standard practice and Google doesn't forbid it.
   - From then on, Search Console and Google Analytics show exactly what the map listing brings, separate from everything else.
   - If the profile offers an appointment or booking link, use `https://buzzskito.ca/free-yard-assessment?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp-booking`.
7. **Photos.** Real job photos only, JPG or PNG, about 720 × 720 or bigger. Add a few every week from May to September.
8. **Posts.** One a week in season. Google archives posts after six months, and you can now schedule them ahead.
9. **Reviews.** Ask every customer after every visit. Text them the review link, or print the QR code on invoices.
   - To get the link and QR code, use a computer, not a phone: go to business.google.com, then Read Reviews, then Get more reviews.
   - Google bans: offering anything in return, asking only happy customers, asking for reviews on the spot, quotas for your technicians, and asking people to mention specific things like the technician's name or the service.
   - Reply to every review, short and personal.
   - Since July 2025 these same reviews also count for the pay-per-lead ads.
10. **Questions & Answers is gone.** Google removed it from Maps in December 2025, so there's nothing to do there.
11. **Same name, address and phone everywhere.** Check Yellow Pages, Yelp, HomeStars, the BBB, Facebook, Apple Maps and Bing Places. Each should match the Business Profile exactly.

How Google ranks the map box, in its own words: results are "mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity", and prominence includes "how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have." "There's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking."

## 4. The competitors' "virtual offices"

**Google's rules:**
- "If your business rents a physical mailing address but doesn't operate out of that location, also known as a virtual office, that location isn't eligible for a Business Profile."
- "Service-area businesses can't list a 'virtual' office unless that office is staffed during business hours."
- A coworking office only counts with permanent signage, staff there during business hours, and customers received there.

**What I found (public listings, Nov 7):**
- **Mosquito Man Oakville, 690 Dorval Dr, Suite 200.** The building rents coworking desks and "virtual office" addresses through several operators. A psychotherapy clinic also gives Suite 200 as its address.
- **Mosquito Man Brampton, 16 Rutherford Rd S, Unit 203.** Listed as a coworking space offering desks, private offices and virtual-office mail handling.
- **Mosquito Man Mississauga ("Plaza 3…").** I couldn't confirm the full address yet.
- All three listings say "Open 24 hours", which is unlikely for a staffed office.

**How to report them (you, from your own Google account):**
1. **Suggest an edit.** In Google Maps, open the listing, then Suggest an edit, then "Place is closed or not here", then "Doesn't exist here".
2. **Business Redressal Complaint Form:** https://support.google.com/business/contact/business_redressal_form. Choose "Address", paste the Maps links, attach the evidence, and quote the exact address that's wrong.

**Expect little.** Google promises no action and no updates. Reports from other businesses say it can take months and works only sometimes. It costs nothing to try. A franchisee with a real staffed office and a sign is allowed, so the case has to show the address is only a mailbox or virtual office.

**If you want, I'll put the evidence together** (links and screenshots of each building's virtual-office listings) so you can submit it in a few minutes.

**Never copy them.** If Google finds a fake address on our listing, it suspends it, and that listing is where most of our customers come from.

## 5. Pay-per-lead ads (Google Local Services Ads), starting in March

**What it is.** The "Sponsored · Pest control technicians" box at the very top, where Mosquito Man shows in every city.
- You pay only for real leads: answered calls, missed calls longer than 20 seconds during your listed hours, messages and booking requests.
- You set a weekly budget. Leads that turn out to be junk are usually credited back automatically within about 30 days.
- The badge is now called "Google Verified". It replaced "Google Guaranteed" and "Google Screened" in October 2025.

**Timing.**
- Verification takes 3–4 weeks on average after the documents go in, and Google's bidding needs about 2 weeks to settle.
- **I've set a reminder for Monday, Feb 1, 2027** to start the application with you, so the ads are live in March.
- In 2027 Google is moving Canadian accounts into Google Ads. Screens may look different from older guides, and budgets become daily instead of weekly.

**Have these ready:**
- [ ] Ontario pesticide Operator Licence and each owner's Exterminator Licence: numbers, expiry dates, PDFs.
- [ ] Your business registration, with the name matching the Business Profile.
- [ ] A signed certificate of insurance in the exact business name, covering general and professional liability. Google shows its minimum only on the upload screen; your operator licence already requires at least $1 million of liability cover.
- [ ] Every owner's ID, for Google's free criminal background check: you and Nick, as the two owners. For pest control, Google checks the business and the owners, not each technician.
- [ ] Access as owner or manager of the Business Profile, by the person signing up.
- [ ] Real job photos, 640 × 640 or bigger, with **no phone numbers visible** anywhere, including on the van or uniforms.
- [ ] The service list (mosquito, tick), the cities, and the real hours you answer the phone.
- [ ] A weekly budget you're comfortable testing. We'll set it together and check the cost per lead against $1,222 per customer.

**What ranks you higher inside that box:**
- your rating and number of reviews;
- answering calls fast (missed calls hurt);
- good photos;
- completed checks;
