# Earn Google's trust before multiplying pages

**No: more images, and more pages built to hold them, are neither risk-free nor a route to 100,000 image-search visits a month.** That target is **66 times the site's current 1,530 image clicks per 30 days** and about the entire estimated traffic of a pest-content peer such as Better Termite. The sites that really have six-figure image traffic either sell the picture itself or run a crowd of identifiers, while Bugwood, an expert archive of roughly 300,000 insect and plant photos, draws an estimated 14,000 visits a month. The owner is half right about AI: no 2026 study shows AI answers taking Images-tab clicks, but none measures them, and the proven threat is Google itself, which has moved pest pictures into AI Overviews, put ads and save buttons into Images, routed camera searches into AI answers and, on Nov 3, announced Search switched off by default for K-12 student accounts while **about 55% of the site's image clicks look like US school computers**. The risk has already cost money: on Nov 2 Google's ad review rated the site "low-value content", so Mediavine now runs it on Journey without Google's ad demand at roughly 60-75% of the pay, and at goal traffic next May and June display ads would earn about **CA$0.6-1.6K a month against a goal line of CA$1.9-2.6K**. That makes page quality the largest income lever available: getting Google's demand back, which can be requested again from about May 2027, is worth roughly **CA$400-500 a month at goal traffic, against CA$45-90 a month for the whole Images channel** in July 2027. Google names no safe publishing pace, but every 2025-2026 collapse paired fast growth with commodity pages and fell three to six months after the peak, which describes the site's recent run of about 60 AI-drafted, unreviewed pages a week; the safe ceiling is **about 20-25 new pages a week, never more than 10 in a day**, each with real demand, something only BuzzSkito has and a human read, with the rest of the effort spent on upgrades to existing pages, Lens, a free Bing fix and cheap Discover groundwork, while Pinterest earns only a small test and AI assistants send almost nothing.

## 100,000 image visits would take 66 times today's traffic

The site's own October data is the best evidence anywhere on this question, and it supports the owner's instinct about photos while undercutting his hope about volume. Pages with no photos sat near zero in image search until photos arrived, then reached **about 270-330 image impressions and 0.25 clicks per page per weekday within days**, while pages still waiting for photos stayed flat through the same weeks, so later pages served as the control for earlier ones ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)). But photos work as a gate, not a dial: **7.5% of pages with no images appear in image search at all, against 95% of pages with three to five**, and past about six to eight images per page, clicks stop rising. Topic matters more than count, since with the same 6-10 images a spider page earns 1.33 image clicks a weekday and a state page 0.06. The gain also stays inside image search: ten pages that received photos and nothing else earned 174 ordinary web clicks against 176 expected ([photo read](../data/god-build/photo-upgrade-read-2026-10-29.md)). Counting weekends, the late-October run rate was **about 1,530 image clicks per 30 days on about 924,000 impressions**, roughly 14% of the site's Google clicks ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)).

| The owner's belief | Verdict | The deciding evidence |
|---|---|---|
| More images, and more pages to hold them, carry no risk | **Wrong** | Google's spam policy names sites that compile other people's images without added value, and Google's ad review already rated the site "low-value content" on Nov 2 |
| Image search can reach 100,000 visits a month, as other sites have | **Not at this size** | The big image sites sell the picture or run a crowd. No content site of 800-1,000 pages is documented anywhere near it |
| AI answers take web clicks but cannot take image clicks | **Half right** | Unmeasured rather than disproven. Google's own redesigns and its new school-account policy are the larger, proven threats |
| Image traffic is a big income unlock | **Small in money** | About 1 Canadian cent a visit on Journey: about CA$15 a month today, about CA$45-90 in July 2027 |

The owner is right that such sites exist. What separates them from BuzzSkito is not the number of images but what the visitor gets.

Bugwood settles the question. It holds hundreds of times more expert photos than BuzzSkito's ledger, yet draws an estimated 14,000 visits a month, and even ten times that estimate would not support the idea that a large photo archive pulls traffic by itself ([named-site notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/named_sites_wins_and_losses.md)). A likely reason, inferred rather than measured, is that its images are licensed for reuse and appear on many extension and teaching sites, and Google tends to show only one or a few hosts for near-identical images. BuzzSkito draws on the same kind of public pools: of the **829 photos in its ledger, 446 come from iNaturalist, 218 from Wikimedia Commons, 161 from CDC and 4 from the National Park Service** ([other-channels notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/other_visual_channels.md)), so more of the same supply runs into the same ceiling. The encouraging flip side is that the site already earns far more per photo than the big archives (image clicks here against all visits there, so the measures differ), which says better pages per photo matter more than more photos.

"Tens of thousands" of image visits has really happened, but never under conditions that resemble this site. Before Google's 2013 redesign, 87 publishers averaged roughly 70,000 image visits a month each, falling to roughly 20,000 after it ([Define Media](https://www.definemg.com/how-googles-image-search-update-killed-image-seo/)). A news publisher gained roughly 50,000 a month in 2019 by repairing a block that hid its images ([GSQi](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/fixing-a-google-images-indexing-problem-case-study/)), image search reached 30-45% of Google traffic at two online shops ([AJ Kohn](https://www.blindfiveyearold.com/the-problem-with-image-search-traffic)), and one niche publisher's image model runs on [80,000+ images and 600-900 bought stock photos a month](https://fatstacksblog.com/image-seo/), which the site's photo rules exclude. The only recent whole-site Search Console figure found was about 800 image clicks a month at an unnamed site ([Snippet Digital](https://suganthan.com/blog/gsc-mcp-image-seo-tools/)), so BuzzSkito is already above the one public benchmark. Peers set the outer bound: InsectIdentification.org gets an estimated 145,000 visits a month from every source combined and Better Termite about 107,000 ([Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/insectidentification.org/); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/bettertermite.com/)). Reaching 100,000 image clicks would take **about 2 million image impressions a day, or 4,000-14,000 more pages at measured yields, or about 25,000 distinct images** at today's four clicks per live image ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)). The site-wide goal of about 48,000 Google visitors in May 2027 and 63,000 in June is a different matter: it sits at roughly a third to a half of Better Termite's or Fox Pest's estimated traffic from all sources, inside a real peer range ([goal](../data/god-build/goal-2027.md); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/fox-pest.com/)).

The realistic plan for July 2027 is **about 4,500 image clicks a month, with a range of 2,000-10,000**, built up from measured yields: today's 1,530, about 840 from the late-October photo waves, 1,400-2,000 from planned pages, then a summer multiplier and a cut for school closures ([Oct 30 report](Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan.md)). The weak spot is that **no summer image data exists at all**, because the site had no images until Sep 2, so the season multiplier is borrowed from BugGuide, whose estimated traffic swings about threefold between spring and winter ([documented cases](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/documented_image_traffic_cases.md)). On the evidence, 10,000 a month is a stretch, 30,000 has no precedent at a site this size, and 100,000 has none since 2013.

### Pages built to hold photos are the risky kind

Real photos on real guides carry little risk; the danger sits in new, thin pages built around images. Google's spam policy lists, as an example of scraping, sites built to compile images or other media from other sites "without substantial added value to the user" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). John Mueller has said Google reads a gallery as one page holding many images, and that a photo earns its own page only when it comes with unique text ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-unique-image-landing-pages-can-help-boost-search-visibility/553282/)). A site whose every photo comes from elsewhere fits that policy whenever its pages exist mainly to hold those photos, and part of that look is already present: one CDC tick photo sits on 48 pages, and the state pages average 6.3 images, of which 5.5 appear on ten or more pages, while earning about 1.2 image clicks each a month ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)). The reward for building pages around pictures is small anyway: product pages have earned no image clicks, and a typical new identification page earns about six a month.

No named image gallery or one-page-per-photo site was found collapsing in 2025-2026 with data, so the warning rests on Google's own wording and on adjacent cases, not on a measured image-site crash. The adjacent case that matters most is Mueller's September 2026 remark that after a run of weak programmatic pages, Google's systems can lose faith in a whole site, and that repairing it takes significant time and effort ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-lose-faith-42032.html)). Weak image pages could therefore cost the pages that already earn. The practical rules follow: no galleries, no URL per photo, no page whose main difference from its siblings is swapped public photos, no single photo on more than about ten pages, and no new page whose only reason to exist is image search.

## Image clicks look safer from AI answers, not from Google

Every independent 2026 measurement finds large losses of web clicks where AI answers appear, so the first half of the owner's belief is well supported. In a randomized experiment with 1,065 US desktop users in January-February 2026, an AI Overview appeared on about 41% of queries, and when it did, **outbound organic clicks fell 39.8%**; the loss sat in informational searches, while navigational and transactional ones showed no measurable change ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/researchers-find-google-ai-overviews-cut-publisher-clicks-39-8/)). A second, preregistered experiment found that being assigned to AI Mode cut external click-through by 18.8 percentage points ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18352)), and Ahrefs, across 300,000 keywords, put position-one click-through on AI Overview keywords in December 2025 about 58% below its model of the same keywords without an overview ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/)). Publisher networks show the loss speeding up rather than levelling off: Chartbeat's Google Search referrals fell 21.9% in the year to July 2025 and **40.2% in the year to July 2026** ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/)), and search engines supplied 12% of North American publisher pageviews in the third quarter of 2026, against 21% a year earlier ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/search-drops-to-12-of-north-american-publisher-pageviews-chartbeat-finds/)).

The second half, that nobody can take image clicks, has no 2026 evidence on either side. Seer, Ahrefs, Chartbeat, SparkToro, Datos and the randomized experiment all leave Images-tab clicks out ([search notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/search_clicks_late_2026.md)). The only expert who looked, Glenn Gabe in June 2025, saw image clicks and impressions still moving together while web search came apart, and warned that this might not last ([GSQi](https://www.gsqi.com/marketing-blog/the-forgotten-coupling-image-search-video-mode-and-news-tab-in-search/)). "Safer for now" is a fair reading. "Safe" is not, because the largest recorded swings in this channel came from Google's own design choices: its 2013 redesign cut image clicks by an average of 63% across 87 publishers, with no recovery in eleven weeks ([Define Media](https://www.definemg.com/how-googles-image-search-update-killed-image-seo/)), and its 2018 removal of the View Image button raised them 37% ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-image-seo-updates/255573/)). Google controls this surface, and it is changing it now.

Google's 2025-2026 moves all pull pictures toward Google's own pages. On 16 pest searches checked on Oct 29-30, an AI Overview sat at the top of 14, and 13 of those carried 4-15 photos taken from other sites; **BuzzSkito was in none of about 155 image slots**, even on chigger bites, where it ranks fifth in the Images tab ([teardown](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/pest_image_search_teardown.md)). Google mixed ads among organic image results in June 2026 ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-image-search-mix-ads-41511.html)), added a browsable Images home and AI image generation inside AI Overviews in July ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-images-25th-anniversary/)), suggested collections in September and tested a Save button on image hover on Oct 28 ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-images-suggested-by-al-42096.html); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-images-save-button-overlay-42394.html)). As far as the research found, the Images tab is the one visual surface where Google is adding browsing, saving and ads rather than an AI answer, which makes it a place to stay rather than a doorway. Camera searches, the part that is growing, now run into AI answers: Lens shows AI Overviews for unfamiliar images without being asked a question ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-lens-ai-overviews-ios-app-38928.html)), Circle to Search was reported sending follow-up questions to AI Mode by default from November 2025 ([ChannelNews](https://www.channelnews.com.au/google-circle-to-search-now-uses-ai-mode-by-default/)), and on Oct 28, 2026 Google reported **over 50 billion visual searches a month, with one in five AI Mode searches using voice, images or documents**, and no click figure of any kind ([Google](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/)). The two sites that have published Lens numbers saw click rates of 0.5% and about 1% ([SEO-Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/search-console-multimodal-en/); [Linus Li](https://www.linusseo.com/en/gsc-web-multimodal/)).

The nearest threat has nothing to do with AI answers. On Nov 3, 2026, Google said Gemini and Google Search will be **off by default for K-12 student accounts until school administrators turn them on**, with no date and no word on whether Images or Canada is included ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/ai-learning-era-updates-2026/); [Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-off-k-12-schools-42447.html)). That matters because the October image surge looks like a classroom. Desktop took 84% of image clicks on Oct 26-29 against 38% in September, desktop weekends ran at about 0.27 times weekdays, Chromebook sessions on the 40 top image pages went from 0.2 to 17.8 a day, and those visits lasted about 22 seconds against about 93 on Windows ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)). Search Console gives no network or age data, so the school reading is an inference from several independent signals. If it holds, the K-12 change would cut the headline click count far harder than the income, because 22-second school visits may earn close to nothing, and a fall in school-day desktop clicks next year should be read as a policy change, not a Google penalty.

The site's own data can settle the belief. The owner is right if Images and Lens clicks hold or grow through the spring while web clicks per page fall, and if desktop holds up at Thanksgiving (Nov 26-27) and over the winter break (Dec 21-Jan 1). He is wrong if school-day desktop clicks collapse on those dates or after the K-12 rollout, or if image clicks fall after a layout change while impressions hold. One caution for the first read: some Search Console properties showed a sudden click spike with flat impressions over the Oct 31-Nov 1 weekend, which Google has not explained, so that weekend needs checking against Google Analytics before anyone relies on it ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-performance-reports-spike-clicks-42448.html)).

## Sources won the 2025-2026 updates while middlemen lost up to 46%

The market around BuzzSkito is shrinking, which makes the site's own growth both harder to read and easier to lose. Publishers in the Reuters Institute's 2026 survey expected search traffic to fall another 43% on average over three years, and about a fifth expected a loss above 75% ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/digital-journalism/google-traffic-down-2025-trends-report-2026/)). People Inc., owner of The Spruce and Better Homes & Gardens, saw Google's share of its traffic fall from 54% to 21% and its Google search traffic drop 40% in a year, while revenue not tied to sessions grew 16% ([Digiday via Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/geo/people-inc-google-search-traffic-down-40-percent/)). Reddit's shares fell 29% on Oct 30, 2026 after it blamed AI search for falling Google referrals ([Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/30/reddit-pinterest-shares-tumble-on-weak-user-and-revenue-growth)). Chartbeat's data shows where readers went instead: direct visits rose from 13.5% to 15.9% of publisher pageviews between July 2024 and July 2026, and privately shared links from 7.1% to 11.3% ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/)).

The 2025-2026 core updates sorted sites by whether they own their subject. In the March 2026 update, measured on SISTRIX visibility, winners included the National Park Service, the NIH, Nature (+24.1%), the American Cancer Society (+34.3%) and GoodRx (+54.5%), and Lily Ray summed up the split as sources winning while the platforms where people discuss a subject lost ([Amsive](https://amsive.com/insights/seo/google-march-2026-core-update-winners-losers-analysis/)). In the UK's December 2025 update the Mammal Society gained 49.6%, while general health publishers fell hard (**Verywell Health -46.3%, Medical News Today -37.4%, Healthline -24.7%**) and the UK bird-identification site BirdSpot lost 39.1% ([SISTRIX](https://www.sistrix.com/blog/google-december-2025-core-update-information-and-analysis/)). Middlemen lost in March too, from Tripadvisor and Yelp to Skyscanner, alongside the largest drop of any domain, YouTube's, and a fall for Reddit ([Amsive](https://amsive.com/insights/seo/google-march-2026-core-update-winners-losers-analysis/)). These are not permanent verdicts, since the same domain can move in opposite directions between updates (the American Cancer Society fell 45.8% in the UK in December and rose 34.3% in the US in March), but the direction of travel is consistent: the source beats the summary.

What the growing sites did is modest and human. Raptive found that sites stable or rising after the June 2025 update published at least one new post and **updated at least five existing posts a month**, while falling sites managed none or one, and that 84% of gaining sites showed a full author name against 67% of losing ones ([Raptive](https://raptive.com/?p=9843)). HouseFresh, an independent air-purifier review site that lost about 95% of its Google visibility in 2023, took about two years to pass its old peak, and credited product testing, design work and a YouTube audience rather than more pages, adding that luck played a part ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/housefresh-achieves-notable-traffic-recovery-after-google-algorithm-impacts-2/)). Google's own people point the same way. At Search Central Live Toronto in April 2026, Danny Sullivan asked for content that is unique, specific and first-hand, contrasting a running store's generic shoe-buying list with an analysis of why one customer's shoes collapsed after 400 miles ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-commodity-content-41200.html)). Gary Illyes said AI-made content should be curated by someone with editorial oversight who confirms it is correct before publication, and that a review label is not the point ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-ai-generated-content-should-be-human-reviewed/553486/)). Mapped onto BuzzSkito, a "ticks in Ohio" page rebuilt from CDC data is the commodity version, while what the technician finds in GTA yards in June is something only a working mosquito and tick company can publish; none of the 255 AI-drafted guides has yet had a human review ([named-site notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/named_sites_wins_and_losses.md)).

Nobody credible has published a modelled 2027 forecast for search clicks, and the numbers that exist come from news publishing. One large lifestyle publisher's model, reported by Digiday, has AI Mode reaching about 80% of Google searches by the end of 2027 in its slower case and Google referrals falling another 30-40% ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/how-publishers-are-modeling-and-mitigating-a-future-with-significantly-less-google-search-traffic/)). Google says AI Mode is not the default experience, and browser panels saw it handling well under 1% of US searches in early 2026 ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/what-google-ai-mode-push-means-for-publishers/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-still-under-2-but-growing-datos-q1-2026-state-of-search-report/)), yet it passed a billion monthly users by May ([Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/)) and some AI Overviews have been tested opening in full with a follow-up box that pushes links lower ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-ai-overviews-push-ai-mode-responses-41974.html)). On Oct 28 Google described one seamless experience across AI Overviews, AI Mode and classic search, said lower ad revenue per AI Mode query still weighed on growth, and said it is taking ads inside AI answers worldwide for the first time ([earnings call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/29/alphabet-googl-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). The likely 2027 path is therefore not a switch flipped to AI Mode but more AI-shaped results on more searches, with ads following; Lily Ray's test for a full switch is whether AI answers earn as much ad money as classic results ([Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/platforms/what-google-ai-mode-push-means-for-publishers/)).

Clicks survive where the reader must go somewhere to act: brand and site names, buying, booking and local service. Pest identification sits in the most exposed shapes, since Seer's 2026 client data shows AI Overviews on 85.9% of organic impressions for question searches and 95.4% for comparisons ([Seer Interactive](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update)). On BuzzSkito, established bite pages earned a median of 45 clicks a month and identification pages 33 in late September and October, against 4 for quick question-and-fact pages ([page-type note](C:/Users/buzzs/.claude/projects/C--Users-buzzs-buzzskito-website/memory/project_page_type_performance.md)). The click survives when Google's answer raises a next step it cannot settle: is it dangerous, is it really a bed bug, what do I do, who can treat it. That favours mosquito and tick pages twice over. They are the company's own trade, and Google's February 2026 Discover update says its systems judge expertise topic by topic ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)); they also click best in image search, at 7.1 clicks per 1,000 impressions for mosquitoes and 4.2 for ticks against 0.4 for bed bugs ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)). The rise of direct and shared visits argues for an audience Google does not control, and the site already has a browser-notification prompt built and switched off, waiting only on the owner's OneSignal ID ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)).

## Google's "low-value" verdict now outweighs every image channel

The biggest change to income per visitor happened on Nov 2, when Google disapproved buzzskito.ca for its ad exchange as "low-value content". Mediavine appealed, Google upheld the decision, and the site launched on Journey, Mediavine's program that runs without Google; approval can be requested again in six to twelve months ([Mediavine estimate note](C:/Users/buzzs/.claude/projects/C--Users-buzzs-buzzskito-website/memory/project_mediavine_rpm_estimate.md)). Search Console shows no manual action and no security issue, so Google Search has not penalized the site; the warning came from Google's ad review ([trust audit](google-trust-audit-2026-11-03.md)). Journey runs on separate exchange seats without Google's AdX demand and performs below full Mediavine ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/mediavine-ceo-addresses-ai-challenges-and-journey-expansion-in-reddit-ama/)), and the published figures bear that out: a travel blog with 27% US readers earned $7.50, $11.66 and $15.99 per 1,000 sessions in its first three Journey months ([Danny in the North](https://danny-cph.com/mediavine-journey-first-3-months/)), a wine blog with 66% US readers about $9, against $12-15 in its first weeks on Raptive, and a local blog with about 95% US readers averaged $33-35 on full Mediavine ([This Week in Blogging](https://thisweekinblogging.com/mediavine-vs-raptive/)). Country mix, niche and season confound every comparison, so the planning ratio of **0.6-0.75 times full-network pay** is low-confidence. Applied to the site, it gives about **CA$12-15 per 1,000 sessions for November-December 2026, CA$6-11 for January-March 2027 and CA$9-19 for summer 2027**, with the first two to four weeks reading low while the account ramps up ([income notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/income_per_visitor.md)). Journey pays 65 days after the end of each month, so November's earnings arrive around early February ([Productive Blogging](https://www.productiveblogging.com/everything-you-need-to-know-about-journey-by-mediavine/)), and since full Mediavine depends on the Google approval that Journey skips, earning more on Journey will not by itself get around the verdict.

At the traffic the goal expects for May and June 2027, roughly 76,000-100,000 sessions with 65,000-85,000 of them on pages that carry ads, Journey's summer range gives **about CA$0.6-1.6K a month from display ads, against the goal's Mediavine line of CA$1,905 in May and CA$2,599 in June** ([goal](../data/god-build/goal-2027.md)). With Google's demand back, the same traffic would earn about CA$1.0-2.1K ([income notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/income_per_visitor.md)). Display ads alone reach CA$5,000 a month only at about 333,000 ad-page sessions at CA$15 per 1,000, or about 108,000 at a US-heavy full-network rate. The goal was set when every Mediavine estimate assumed Google's demand, so on Journey the Mediavine line will likely land at a third to two-thirds of the goal even if traffic arrives on schedule.

The 2026 ad market will not close the gap. US programmatic prices rose 23.6-51% year on year in one network's data, but prices on open-web inventory rose only 2.2% in the second quarter ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/us-programmatic-cpms-gain-51-year-over-year-databeat-finds/)), and premium publishers' US ad requests fell roughly a third as AI search cut their traffic ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/publisher-ad-supply-fell-by-up-to-40-in-q2-as-ai-search-choked-the-open-web/)): the price per ad is rising while the number of ads falls. Season is a bigger swing than any setting, and the site's July traffic peak will arrive in Mediavine's July rate trough. Across 8.8 billion sessions, Playwire found revenue per session tracks ad impressions per pageview (correlation 0.59) far more than pages per session (0.27), and not at all session length (-0.03) ([Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/2026-state-of-publisher-ad-revenue-report)), so what pays is an ad in view on each page, not a long stay.

By that measure an image visit was valued on Oct 30 at about 0.7 times a same-country web visit, about 1.5 Canadian cents with Google's demand ([value notes](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/value_of_image_visitors.md)). On Journey, and with an unknown share of school sessions that may serve no ads, **about 1 cent is the planning figure until the first full month is measured**. School visitors arrive from real searches, so no rule found treats them as invalid traffic; the likely cost is simply that no ad serves, which Mediavine itself says dilutes RPM ([Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/blog/your-rpm-isnt-lying-to-you-but-it-might-not-be-telling-the-whole-truth/)). Amazon adds almost nothing to an image visit. About 85% of image clicks come from the US ([value notes](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/value_of_image_visitors.md)), information pages earn about 18 Amazon clicks per 1,000 Canadian views against about 275 on product pages ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)), and Amazon.ca's income comes to about 7 cents per Canadian session overall ([Amazon note](C:/Users/buzzs/.claude/projects/C--Users-buzzs-buzzskito-website/memory/project_amazon_unit_economics_oct30.md)). Image search is an ad channel, and a small one.

Mediavine's own rules point the same way as Google's. Its AI policy, unchanged since March 2024, says it does not monetize low-quality, mass-produced, unedited or undisclosed AI content scraped from other websites, and that it will act if previously approved sites slip ([Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/ai)); the page defines none of those words, and a fast cadence of templated pages is where a reviewer's judgement would bite. The phone video player turned out not to be the problem the audit feared. On one Mediavine site the video unit and the sticky phone ad each made about a quarter of revenue. A Nov 3 check on a 375 x 812 phone screen found the bottom ad at about 6% of the screen and the in-article sticky ad at about 25%, while Google's large-sticky-ad line is 30% per ad, and video ads inside a content player fall outside that standard ([Google](https://support.google.com/webtools/answer/7073774?hl=en)). The player stays on. The slideshow it plays is Mediavine's automatic video built from the site's own photos and titles ([Mediavine](https://help.mediavine.com/universal-player-plus)), and real short pest videos would make it worth watching. Taken together the arithmetic is lopsided: **Google's demand back is worth about CA$400-500 a month at goal traffic, roughly five to ten times the whole Images channel**, and it depends on exactly what fast templated publishing undermines. The unexplained 9.1/10 scores and the review markup built from Google reviews were removed on Nov 3. The self-ranking "best companies" city copies are the audit item still open before a re-application in May.

For Google Images, the work that pays is upgrading pages that already have web demand, never building pages for pictures. That means first photos on the roughly 45 identification, bite and how-to pages that have demand and none ([own-site test](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/own_site_image_test.md)), labelled photo pairs on "vs" pages (at least nine of the top fifteen results for tick vs bed bug show both bugs in one image), tall labelled cards like the ones holding three of sixteen first places, a coin or fingertip for scale, the best photo moved up the page as a controlled test, and no ninth photo before the Dec 30 test read ([teardown](../research_notes/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan/pest_image_search_teardown.md)). Lens is the visual channel already sending measurable traffic for no extra work. The one weekly reading showed 34 Lens clicks against 117 Images clicks ([Oct 30 report](Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan.md)), and on the one site that split it, 89% of Lens impressions were on phones ([SEO-Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/search-console-multimodal-en/)), so Lens traffic is largely school-free. A reader's own photo of a bug exists on no website, so Lens can only match it by look and by the page around it, which favours several life stages, angles and phone-style shots, each clearly named in the caption and alt text. Lens figures never reach Search Console's API, so the owner's monthly export of the Web: Multimodal report is the only way to see them. On the planning figures, with Lens at that one week's ratio, Images and Lens together reach about 6,000 visits a month by July 2027, under a tenth of the 71,000-visitor goal; the goal still rests on web search.

Bing is the cheapest real fix in this report. It carried 8.73% of US searches in June 2026 ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/united-states-of-america/)), and one practitioner reports that ChatGPT and Copilot draw on Bing's index for their answers, a claim made without published data ([Advanced Web Ranking](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/seo/visual-search-beyond-google)). Since at least Sep 10, Bing's IndexNow service has refused buzzskito.ca, most likely because the site's Bing ownership was imported from Search Console and lapsed ([Bing note](C:/Users/buzzs/.claude/projects/C--Users-buzzs-buzzskito-website/memory/reference_bing_indexnow_healthy.md)); a check of the site's code on Nov 3 found no native Bing verification file or tag. Fifteen minutes in Bing Webmaster Tools fixes it at no risk.

Discover is now a phone-only feed, since desktop Discover could no longer be found by mid-September 2026 ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-discover-on-desktop-not-found-42103.html)). Its first dedicated core update, in February 2026, set out to show more content from sites based in the reader's own country and to judge expertise topic by topic, starting with US English ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)), which puts a Canadian site writing for Americans on the wrong side in US Discover. Chartbeat measured Discover referrals down 34.3% in the year to July 2026 ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/)), and Google itself calls Discover traffic less predictable than search, to be treated as supplemental ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)). The groundwork is cheap and mostly done: large previews are already allowed site-wide, and what remains is a real 16:9 share image at least 1,200 px wide on the roughly 96 pages that used the generic one in October, cropped from the 310 public-domain or CC0 photos that are already that wide and landscape, with no graphic bite close-up as the lead image and no sensational headline, both of which Discover filters or penalises ([other-channels notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/other_visual_channels.md)). Canadian readers may see timely tick and mosquito stories once the update reaches Canada; chasing Discover beyond that is a lottery.

Pinterest is the only channel here that could plausibly add thousands of visits, and the only one that needs sustained work. The platform grew to 661 million monthly users in the third quarter of 2026, but only 107 million are in the US and Canada, up 4% ([Pinterest](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001506293/000150629326000151/q3-26xpressrelease.htm)); it says outbound clicks grew faster than users without giving a rate, and its new assistant is built to let people research, compare and decide without leaving Pinterest ([earnings call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/02/pinterest-pins-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). The best publisher evidence is one home-organising blog where Pinterest overtook search in year five, took over two years to pay off and pays more per visit ([Minimize My Mess](https://www.minimizemymess.com/blog/5-year-blog-income-report)), and Mediavine's chief executive reportedly rated Pinterest traffic as valuable as Google's or more ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/mediavine-ceo-addresses-ai-challenges-and-journey-expansion-in-reddit-ama/)). Against that, sellers have reported account-wide drops of about 90% since spring 2026 with no explanation from Pinterest ([Pinterest community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/sudden-permanent-traffic-collapse-since-april-2026/49953)), and no traffic data exists for pest pins anywhere. At one third-party average of 4.8 outbound clicks per 1,000 static-pin impressions ([GenSumo](https://gensumo.pages.dev/research/pinterest-content-mix-report/)), an account reaching 100,000-500,000 impressions a month would send about 480-2,400 visits, perhaps CA$5-50 a month on Journey. The licence fit is good: 573 of the 829 photos are public domain or CC0 and free to pin, 188 CC BY photos need a credit in every pin, and the 68 CC BY-SA photos should stay off pin graphics ([other-channels notes](../research_notes/Images%20and%20safe%20traffic%20growth%202026/other_visual_channels.md)). Because every pin is a public post needing the owner's approval, it is worth only a 12-week test started in February, so pins mature by May, on mosquito, tick and garden-pest topics, which suit a planning platform better than "what bit me".

AI assistants are a visibility surface, not a traffic channel. Chatbots sent under 1% of publisher pageviews in one Chartbeat reading and 0.01% in another ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/)); Perplexity licenses Getty's images with credit links ([MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/410356/perplexity-and-getty-images-sign-global-image-lice.html)), and ChatGPT users report image results shown without source links ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/feature-request-image-attribution-provenance-and-source-linking-in-chatgpt-image-results/1379995)). The site already lets the AI crawlers in, and nothing more is worth building.

## Twenty-five new pages a week is the safe ceiling, if each is distinctive

Google has never set a safe number, and its people have said pace alone is not the problem. Mueller said in 2024 that a publishing pattern by itself does not make content spam, and Google said in 2017 that releasing 100,000 pages at once is not an SEO issue ([Search Engine Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-publishing-content-in-bulk-spam-37077.html)), and Danny Sullivan's test is large amounts of unoriginal content however it is made, with content made mainly to capture traffic as the warning sign ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-on-scaled-content-its-going-to-be-an-issue/543308/)). That is the owner's best argument: if every page were distinctive and reviewed, a faster pace would be fine. The trouble is that pace multiplies whatever the pages are, and the two readings of these pages available so far, Google's ad verdict and a library of unreviewed AI drafts built from public sources, both point the wrong way.

The documented collapses all paired speed with sameness. Grokipedia, adding about 6,000 AI-written pages a day, peaked in January 2026 and had lost about half its search traffic by early March ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/wikipedia-vs-grokipedia)). In Lily Ray's study of more than 220 sites using AI content platforms, traffic peaked three to six months after page growth peaked and then fell: **54% lost at least 30% of peak traffic, 39% at least half and 22% at least three-quarters**, often ending below where they started ([ConfigDeck summary of Ray](https://configdeck.pages.dev/en/article/searchenginejournal/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai)). A 50,000-page programmatic site was deindexed overnight without a manual action ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/why-website-deindexed-by-google-for-programmatic-seo-bounced-back/552179/)), and among Glenn Gabe's August 2026 spam-update cases, a programmatic Amazon affiliate site padded with AI text lost more than 14,000 ranking searches ([GSQi](https://gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/)). The documented gainers moved slowly: Raptive's gainers published about one new post and updated five a month, and CreativeKidsColor added about 11 posts a month in its first seven months and is still growing ([Niche Site Growth](https://nichesitegrowth.beehiiv.com/p/these-niche-sites-are-flying)). BuzzSkito added **545 pages since July, 262 of them in October** ([trust audit](google-trust-audit-2026-11-03.md)), and its Oct 24 impressions plan called for about 60 a week through mid-December ([impressions plan](../data/god-build/impressions-3m-plan.md)). The release schedule on Nov 3 holds 36 pages for Dec 7 and 12-27 a week after that, with only 2 of them written. That is far above every documented gainer and far below the collapse cases, so the 50-a-day cap does not by itself make a release safe. If Ray's pattern applies here, the window to watch is **January to April 2027**, exactly when the season starts lifting totals and could hide a fall.

The trade-off is lopsided. Every 100 new pages the slower pace gives up would, at the site's measured average of about 40 Google clicks a month per new page in season by its third month (median 12) ([CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md)), add roughly 1,200-4,000 summer clicks a month, worth about CA$15-100 a month in Journey ad income plus a little Amazon. Losing a third of traffic, a loss most sites in Ray's study matched or exceeded, on a site heading for 70,000 Google visitors would cost several hundred dollars a month in ads alone, and every month of delay in Google's ad approval costs about CA$400-500 at goal traffic. The extra pages have a small upside; the pattern they create has a large, site-wide downside that would also reach the GTA service pages, which hold the very searches AI answers take least.

So the recommendation is a ceiling plus a gate. Publish **at most 20-25 new pages a week, never more than 10 in one day, spread through the week rather than in 40-page Monday batches**, and only pages that pass four checks: real web demand; something only BuzzSkito has, such as a technician's observation, local season data, an original diagram drawn from real data or the company's own photo; a read before release by someone who knows pests; and no near-copy of an existing page, enforced by the build check the audit proposed ([trust audit](google-trust-audit-2026-11-03.md)). The honest limit is how many pages a person can read and stand behind each week, and winter, the off-season, is when the owner has time to do it. Mosquito and tick pages come first, then bite and identification pages that answer the next step; the remaining 82 state and province pages wait for spring, and the top-100 build stays paused. The effort saved goes into existing pages, where edits are unlimited and Raptive's gainers made five updates for every new post: human review of the 20 most-visited pages and then 5-10 a week, first photos and better formats on pages with demand, and in May-June the technician's own photos from real jobs, the site's first original pest photos. Each weekly cohort of new pages should be checked at 8-12 weeks against earlier cohorts at the same age, alongside pages live before October; a fading cohort or a fall on the older pages is the early sign Ray's data describes, and either should pause new pages until the cause is clear, because season-lifted totals will not show it in time.

## Conclusion

The owner's two questions, how many images and how fast, turned out to be one question: does Google see BuzzSkito as a source, or as an aggregator of other people's pictures and facts? The first measured answer arrived on Nov 2 from Google's ad review, and it now prices every visitor the site has. That inverts the usual trade-off. For most publishers, quality is a slow ranking bet; here it is the fastest route to more money per visitor, worth several times the entire image channel, while page count has become the main thing standing between the site and Google's ad demand. Maximum pages and maximum income have come apart, and the image channel's own ceiling will be set by who is searching and how Google redraws its results, not by how many photos the site can find.

The decisive months are January to May 2027, when four clocks run out together: October's page surge enters the window where scaled sites have historically turned down, the K-12 change may strip the classroom share out of the image numbers, Journey's January trough tests the new ad income, and Google's ad review can reopen in May just as the mosquito and tick season starts. What the site does this winter decides whether the 2027 season is earned at Journey's rates or Google's, and whether this autumn's traffic gains survive the spring. The most useful thing a photo can do now is not to add a count but to make a page unmistakably the place the answer came from.
