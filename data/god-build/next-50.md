Written 2026-10-16 for the owner's goal of maximum Google traffic for display ads. **Analysis only: nothing was built, scheduled, committed or edited** apart from this file and `next-50.json` (same content as data).

## Summary

- **Build these 50 first, in this order.** The first ten: `pantry-pests`, `tiny-black-bugs`, `how-to-get-rid-of-carpet-beetles`, `bug-bites`, `venomous-spiders`, `brown-recluse-bite`, `asian-lady-beetles`, `wolf-spiders`, `how-to-get-rid-of-stink-bugs`, `spider-egg-sacs`. Together the 50 are expected to bring about **638 Google clicks a month in Nov-Mar** (range 255-1,907) once live, against a site baseline of roughly 985 clicks a week in November and 795 in December (forecast given Oct 16), so roughly 15-19% on top of the site's own winter level in the middle case. At the owner's Mediavine assumptions ($20-30 per 1,000 sessions, 1.3 sessions per click) that is about **$17-25 a month in the middle case** (range $7-74): a traffic asset that compounds into spring and summer, not a Q4 windfall. These are **model estimates, not measurements** (method below).
- **Format beats volume on this site.** Multi-candidate photo pages convert: look-alike roundups 1.0-1.5% (bugs-that-look-like-ticks 889 clicks in 90 days), bite pages up to 1.85-2.03%. Single-answer pages do not, even when they are photo pages: mouse droppings 0.28%, mosquito larvae 0.36%, deer tick vs dog tick 0.23%, deer fly vs horse fly 0.33%, male vs female mosquito 0.15%. The list therefore favours "which bug is this" sorters, look-alike pages and bite charts over single-fact and two-way comparison pages.
- **Head entity terms are out of reach for now.** All 10 single-species head terms checked on Google (brown recluse, black widow spider, wolf spider, house centipede, silverfish, stink bugs, boxelder bugs, bat bugs, brown dog tick, camel crickets) show a knowledge panel and Wikipedia, mostly followed by universities and state agencies. The 22 comparison, look-alike and "in my house" searches checked show no Wikipedia at all: page 1 is pest companies, forums and small blogs. Species pages are in the list, but below the sorters that win those searches.
- **The winter winners are indoors.** Rodent searches are flat all year (Trends Nov-Mar vs yearly average: rat vs mouse 0.97, roof rat 0.90, Norway rat 0.96, rat droppings 0.88, mouse nest 0.85, mice in walls 1.14) and the site has no US rat pages at all. Asian lady beetle searches peak in November (Nov-Mar at 1.27x their yearly average), carpet beetle searches peak in March (0.89 over Nov-Mar), pantry bugs hold steady (0.89-0.95). Spiders keep about half their demand (brown recluse 0.50, black widow spider 0.60); ticks and mosquitoes keep less (deer tick 0.49, lone star tick 0.30, skeeter syndrome 0.25).
- **Verdicts.** (a) Spiders in <state>: **pilot 3 states** (Texas, Florida, California), decide around Jan 31. (b) US pest identification hub: **no** as a stand-alone build; its value is internal links, which the site can get more cheaply. (c) **Defer all 83 remaining tick and mosquito series pages** (and the seasonal taxonomy pages) to a January-February build with February-March releases.
- **What is uncertain.** Search Console data stops at Oct 12 (API token expired); Ahrefs was not used (units exhausted until Oct 26); Google showed its unusual-traffic check after 37 results pages, so some pages were checked with another engine or not at all (marked per page); Trends gives relative, not absolute, volume. The model is built around this site's median new page and cannot predict outliers such as bugs-that-look-like-ticks (about 300 clicks a month), which is 10x what the model would give it.

### 3. How the estimate is built

`clicks/month (Nov-Mar) = 10 x sqrt(ratio / 0.0895) x Nov-Mar factor x format x SERP x Search Console`

- **10**: this site's median new page (12 clicks a month by its third month in season, measured Oct 14) x 0.85 for the ramp from a mid-November launch.
- **ratio**: the topic's US Google Trends average relative to "stink bug" (same request, Oct 2024 - Oct 2026). 0.0895 is the median ratio of eight keywords from the 86 US guides, so a typical guide-sized topic scores 1. For scale, 12 stored Ahrefs volumes put one ratio unit at roughly 175,000 Ahrefs searches a month (spread 52,000-310,000), while Search Console shows 4,000-10,000 US impressions per ratio unit where this site ranks 6-8: Ahrefs overstates what this site sees by 20-40x, which is why it was used only as a scale.
- **square root**: bigger topics have stronger results pages. In their first five days the small long-tail guides out-collected the head terms (bugs-that-look-like-cockroaches 665 impressions, flying-ants 23).
- **Nov-Mar factor**: the topic's Nov-Mar Trends average over its whole-window average (below a ratio of 0.06 Trends' weekly values are 0-3, so the parent topic's factor is used and named).
- **format**: roundup 2.5, bite chart 2.0, one thing / multi-trait comparison / ID + control 1.0, droppings 0.8, one-line comparison and myth 0.6, hub 0.5 (a roundup built as a hub x0.4), from the click-through table above.
- **SERP**: 1.3 forums and local pest companies, 1.15-1.2 pest brands without authority sites, 1.0 mixed or unchecked, 0.9 state pages, 0.8 authority with weak results, 0.7 for an unchecked single-species head term (the 10 checked ones averaged 0.72), 0.5-0.6 authority throughout.
- **Search Console**: 1.3 when the site already shows for the topic: a Canadian edition with 1,000+ impressions in 90 days at an average position of 10 or better (for the US editions), or 100+ impressions at position 10 or better on the page's own kind of query (product queries do not count); otherwise 1.
- **Range**: x0.4 to x3 (the site's mean new page earns 3.3x its median).
- **Check against real pages:** bugs-that-look-like-cockroaches ran at about 48 clicks a month in its first week, against a model value near 10; bugs-that-look-like-ticks earns about 300 a month at maturity. The model is deliberately median-shaped: most new pages here join the long tail (skill rule 2), and the outliers cannot be predicted.
