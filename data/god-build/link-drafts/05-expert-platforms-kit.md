## Six ready answers (Claude adapts them to the exact question)

**A1. Are ticks still active in the fall or winter?** (link: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/tick-season-ontario-when-are-ticks-active)

> Yes. Ticks don't stop when summer ends. Public Health Ontario says blacklegged (deer) ticks can be active any day the temperature is above 4°C, even in winter. Adult blacklegged ticks are most active in September and October, and some stay active into November. That's leaf-raking, hunting and fall-hiking season, so keep doing tick checks after time in leaf litter, tall grass or woods, and check dogs too: ears, collar, armpits, between the toes and under the tail. In Ontario we tell people to stay alert from early spring through late fall, and on any mild winter day.
> Alex Francisco, co-founder, BuzzSkito Mosquito & Tick Control (Mississauga, Ontario)

**A2. How do I check myself, my kids or my dog for ticks?** (link: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/how-to-do-a-tick-check)

> Do a full-body check within two hours of coming indoors, and feel as well as look: an unfed nymph is about the size of a poppy seed. On people, check the scalp and hairline, behind and inside the ears, the back of the neck, armpits, belly button, waistband, groin and behind the knees; on kids, start with the head, neck and ears. On dogs, check the ears, around the eyes and muzzle, under the collar, the armpits and groin, between the toes and under the tail. If a tick is attached, pull it straight up with fine-tipped tweezers, without twisting or crushing it, and skip petroleum jelly or heat. In Ontario a blacklegged tick generally needs to stay attached about 24 to 36 hours to pass on the Lyme bacteria, which is why a same-day check matters. See a doctor about any rash or flu-like symptoms after a bite.

**A3. Where do mosquitoes go in winter?** (link: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/where-do-mosquitoes-go-in-winter-ontario)

> They don't go anywhere; they wait. Depending on the species, mosquitoes get through a Canadian winter as frost-tolerant eggs (floodwater and container mosquitoes glue them to the dry walls of containers, tree holes and low spots), as mated females in a hibernation-like state called diapause, sheltering in storm sewers, culverts, sheds, garages and crawl spaces, or as larvae under unfrozen water. They become active again once temperatures stay above about 10°C, which in the Toronto area usually means April. Before spring, empty or cover buckets, planters, tarps, tires and toys, and clear clogged gutters: that is where overwintered eggs hatch when meltwater reaches them.

**A4. How many people get Lyme disease in Canada, and where?** (link: https://buzzskito.ca/lyme-disease-canada-statistics)

> Canada reported 5,809 Lyme disease cases in 2024, or 14.1 per 100,000 people, and the Public Health Agency of Canada's preliminary count for 2025 is 7,105. In 2009 the national count was 144. Ontario had the most cases in 2024 (2,369, about 41% of the total), Nova Scotia was a close second (2,350) and has by far the highest rate per person (about 217 per 100,000), and Quebec was third (834). These are reported cases only; many infections are never diagnosed or reported, so the real number is higher.

**A5. How can I tell a tick from other small bugs?** (link: https://buzzskito.ca/blog/bugs-that-look-like-ticks)

> Count the legs and look for antennae. Ticks are arachnids: adults and nymphs have 8 legs (newly hatched larvae have 6), no antennae and no wings, and a flat oval body that swells after feeding. The bugs most often mistaken for ticks, such as bed bugs, spider beetles, carpet beetles, weevils and stink bug nymphs, are insects with 6 legs and antennae. If it flies, it isn't a tick; if it jumps, it's more likely a flea or a springtail. A tick found loose indoors usually came in on a person or a pet.

**A6. What does professional mosquito or tick control cost in Canada?** (link: https://buzzskito.ca/mosquito-control-pricing)

> At BuzzSkito, which serves Mississauga and the Greater Toronto Area, a single mosquito treatment starts at $99 for a standard lot under 10,000 square feet, and season plans run May to September: $549 for 5 monthly sprays, $994 for 10 sprays every two weeks and $2,049 for 20 or more weekly sprays. A 5-spray tick season is $597 on its own, or $497 added to a mosquito plan. Prices are before HST and rise with lot size. Wherever you live, compare the number of visits per season, what happens if it rains after a visit, and whether the company holds a pesticide licence in your province.
