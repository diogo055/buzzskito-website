# What ranks for Canadian province tick and mosquito searches (studied Oct 14, 2026)

Method: WebSearch (US-located results, so Google.ca order may differ; government pages are
probably under-listed), ~40 pages opened. Ahrefs' Canadian SERP calls failed (API units
used up). Some figures came from search summaries, not opened pages, and must be checked at
source before use: BC 0.7% tick positivity, AB 15% (2020) and eTick rank, QC ~25%, NL ~20%.

## The field
- **No pest-control company has a province page** in any listing (Sawyer, a repellent brand,
  has 110 words on Alberta).
- None of the pages opened has an FAQ block or a trend chart; the longest is ~1,500 words.
- Nova Scotia: thin NS Health page (350-400 words, Oct 2024); novascotia.ca 1,500 words with a
  county risk-map image but no case counts; Global 2025: 2,350 cases in 2024.
- BC: gov.bc.ca updated May 22, 2026 (1,050 words, no data or map); BCCDC: 6-39 Lyme cases a
  year 2013-2022 (1-14 local), under 1% of ticks positive.
- Alberta: alberta.ca 1,350 words, 184 human Lyme cases 1991-2023, all acquired outside
  Alberta; no species list, map or date.
- Manitoba: surveillance page stops at 2018; the hub page has confirmed/probable Lyme 120/15
  (2025), 77/7 (2024), 47/11 (2023).
- Quebec: Quebec.ca (French) updated Mar 4, 2026, cases 709 / 586 / 652 for 2021-2023; English
  results are mostly dated news; French results led by INSPQ's table.
- New Brunswick: 23.5 cases per 100,000 in 2025 (preliminary) vs 12.3 five-year average
  (chco.tv, June 2026); gnb.ca "Be Tick Smart" risk areas by health zone, bite tracker
  launched June 2026.
- Newfoundland: gov.nl.ca ~130 unique words; MUN Grenfell (Mar 2025): no blacklegged ticks on
  10 transects in two years.
- Mosquitoes: Winnipeg's city trap-count page (closed for season, Sep 29, 2026); BCCDC season
  May-October, 50+ species, no regions; Alberta's WNV page is livestock-only; Global/CP:
  Edmonton's worst season since 1990 (2026).

## Ticks in a province: what the page must carry
1. A 50-word direct answer plus an at-a-glance table, every figure labelled with its data year.
2. The province's own case series as a dated chart and table (NS 2007-2024, MB 2019-2025,
   QC 2014-2023, NB per 100,000). Split local from travel-acquired for AB and BC.
3. Risk areas from the province's own surveillance, redrawn by us from public data. Where none
   are established (AB, BC interior, NL), say so and explain what that means.
4. Species table with real photos beside a millimetre ruler, province-specific (NS
   blacklegged, groundhog, American dog; BC western blacklegged, Rocky Mountain wood; AB wood,
   moose/winter, dog).
5. Month-by-month activity chart in °C (ticks active above about 4 °C; NS adults spring and
   fall; BC south coast year-round).
6. A province-specific "after a bite" table: who identifies and who tests, turnaround,
   pharmacist treatment, the 811 / Health Links line.
7. Infection rates by year and source, never averaged.
8. Other diseases (anaplasmosis, babesiosis, Powassan, BC tick paralysis); 6-8 FAQs.
9. Freshness: visible updated date, "data through", a "what changed since last year" box, a
   dated "this season" box. Refresh each spring and when provincial numbers land.
Length follows evidence: 2,500-3,500 words for NS, MB, QC, NB; 1,800-2,500 for BC, AB, NL.

## Mosquitoes in a province
Region-by-region season calendar; weather triggers (wet May, eggs that wait years); species and
when they bite (Winnipeg adult catch ~95% Aedes vexans; Culex tarsalis spreads West Nile in MB
and AB, mid-June to September, peak July-August); provincial West Nile counts by year; city
programs with live-data links (Winnipeg, Edmonton); yard steps; FAQ.

## Avoid
Prose walls without subheads; undated statistics; mixing confirmed with probable cases or
case-definition years; reading residence-based cases as local risk; reproducing provincial
maps (redraw from data); US data, units or US repellent wording; near-duplicate pages; quote
CTAs (these provinces are outside our service area); "Health Canada approved"; retailer links.

## Fastest wins / locked
Weakest: ticks in Manitoba, are there ticks in Alberta, Newfoundland, mosquito season Alberta,
mosquitoes in Manitoba, mosquito season BC; ticks in Nova Scotia is winnable (not Lyme-intent
queries). Locked or hard: "lyme disease nova scotia", "ticks in bc" (beatable on content, not
authority), French Quebec, NB (gnb.ca + tracker), "winnipeg mosquito" (the City's live counts).

## Canada-specific traps
- **eTick** is photo identification and monitoring, not pathogen testing. Testing differs by
  province (Alberta's Submit-a-Tick tests blacklegged ticks: photo result ~2 business days,
  lab 2-3 weeks; Newfoundland tests Ixodes through its form and vets). Never write one generic
  "send your tick" step.
- **Risk-area units differ:** NB health zones; MB areas south of the 53rd parallel; QC
  municipalities (annual map, merged with preventive-treatment municipalities in 2024); NS
  county map vs province-wide guidance (sources disagree: resolve it); none in BC, AB, NL.
- **PHAC gives national totals only** (Jul 27, 2026 page: 7,105 preliminary in 2025, 5,809 in
  2024) and warns case definitions changed in 2016 and 2024. Provinces publish their own.
- **Pharmacist treatment:** Nova Scotia since May 1, 2024; Quebec in at-risk municipalities.
- **Repellents (Health Canada):** icaridin, not picaridin; DEET up to 30% (12 and over), 10%
  for ages 2-12 (up to 3 times a day), 10% for 6 months to 2 years (once a day); lemon
  eucalyptus not under 3; permethrin-treated clothing not under 16. Check tick claims against
  labels.
- **Quebec in French:** "tique à pattes noires", "piqûre de tique", "traitement préventif";
  the top French result is a thin table, which leaves room for a French twin (hreflang) later.
- Style: Canadian spelling, °C first, mm / km / ha, per 100,000; "B.C." or "BC"; name the
  scope (island of Newfoundland vs Labrador).
