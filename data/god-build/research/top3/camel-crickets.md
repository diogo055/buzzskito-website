## What our page does that none of them do
1. **Credited photos where people find it:** a greenhouse camel cricket on a baseboard indoors (CC0, the lead) and on a
   basement floor in December, a side-view specimen, a native plains camel cricket, a house cricket for comparison.
2. **A quick-ID table under the answer plus look-alike cards:** camel cricket, house cricket, field cricket, cellar
   spider, wolf spider, house centipede, sizes in inches and the tell (Clemson, NC State, Maryland Extension, Penn State,
   UC IPM, MDC), and the spider test (insects six legs, spiders eight and no antennae: NC State, UC IPM).
3. **Which camel cricket is in the house:** Epps et al. 2014 (PeerJ): 88% of 163 homes that sent photos or specimens had
   the Asian genus Diestrammena, 12% native Ceuthophilus; 94% of species-level IDs were the greenhouse camel cricket;
   24.4% of surveyed households had seen camel crickets; up to 52 in one Raleigh yard in two days. None of the six says so.
4. **Sources at their strength, disagreements shown:** breeding indoors (MDC, Iowa State unlikely unless dark and damp;
   Maryland may; Clemson year-round in greenhouses); no source reports a bite; control in the extension order (moisture,
   sealing, vacuum, sticky traps, then labeled insecticides outdoors first); 10 FAQs from People also ask.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Moxie: jumps at people to intimidate. NC State, Clemson, MDC, Maryland, Iowa State, Kentucky do not say it (strong
  jumpers, Clemson; a surprisingly long jump, MDC). Left out; the FAQ says no source here describes it.
- Orkin: "can bite humans". MDC: completely harmless; Kentucky: harmless; NC State's researchers: they do not bite.
  Its snippet folds in camel spiders, a different animal (one line on our page).
- Wikipedia, Orkin: 5 cm bodies. House species: 1/2 to 1 1/2 in (Clemson), 1/2 to 1 1/4 in (Maryland), to about 1 in
  not counting appendages (MDC). PestWorld: region "All"; Epps: positive reports 28% east of Colorado, 7% west.
- Bug Out: diatomaceous earth kills them. None of the extension sources lists it.
