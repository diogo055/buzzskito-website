## What they get wrong
- Speed: NatGeo/Wikipedia/bettertermite give 10 mph as fact. Burke: 10 mph is the maximum cited, measured speeds < 1 mph;
  DCPH-A: about 1 mph.
- Diet: NatGeo lists rodents and small birds. DCPH-A: insects, scorpions, spiders, centipedes, sometimes small lizards;
  CSU and University of Arizona: insects and other arthropods.
- Aggression: DCPH-A's "very aggressive" vs CSU "do not attack" and UC IPM "bite only in self-defense". Page says they
  bite when handled, cornered or restrained.

## Settled: can the bite break the skin?
CSU 5.589 (Colorado species, up to ~1 1/2 in with legs): "no more than a mild pinch and cannot break the skin". DCPH-A:
"the larger species can easily puncture and tear human skin"; secondary infection may follow. Burke: a bite that breaks
the skin is likely to be infected; disinfectant prevents ill effects. Page states both, scoped by size (CSU writes about
Colorado's small species; DCPH-A says it is the larger species that tear skin), and treats a broken-skin bite as a wound.
No peer-reviewed human case series found (PubMed search for solifug*/camel spider + bite/venom: none).

Venom: UC IPM, DCPH-A no venom glands; Univ. of Arizona none known; CSU no poison glands. An unconfirmed 1978 Indian report
of venom glands in one species (Rhagodes nigrocinctus) is left off the page.
