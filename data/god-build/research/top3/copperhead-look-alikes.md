## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- N&O first aid: "keep the area still and raise it to chest level". CDC NIOSH (Sept 23, 2026) says to lie or sit with
  the bite in a neutral position of comfort; Poison Control's copperhead article says below the heart. Our box gives
  CDC's wording.
- N&O: newborns "6 to 8 inches" (quoting a biologist). NCWRC's own profile says 8 to 10 in., Poison Control 7 to 9,
  MDC (1999) 7 or 8: our page gives the published ranges with their sources.
- Google's AI answer leads with a "triangular head with vertical slit pupils". Poison Control says it is unsafe to get
  close enough to check pits and pupils; UF/IFAS says many nonvenomous snakes have triangular heads and water and
  garter snakes flatten theirs; MDC says a hognose may have a wider head than most nonvenomous snakes.
- VHS treats the yellow tail tip as shared only with the cottonmouth (true for Virginia). UF/IFAS adds young pygmy
  rattlesnakes in Florida: our page gives both.
