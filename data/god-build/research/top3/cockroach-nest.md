4. **Why roaches cluster, with the primary study**: 40 volatile acids in German cockroach feces, made with gut bacteria, that keep roaches faithful to the same sites (PNAS 2015); and the practical upshot (bait within 1 to 2 feet of each harborage, vacuum the droppings, never spray the nest because sprays scatter roaches).
5. **Look-alike cards for other "nests"** (mouse nest, bed bug hiding spot, ant nest, carpet beetle skins), each with the sourced tell.

## What they get wrong (and the source that says otherwise)
- Catseye: young roaches "mature in just a few weeks". UF/IFAS IN128: German nymphs need about 60 days at room temperature; Texas A&M: 50 to 60 days for German, about 600 days for American and smokybrown.
- Catseye: German cockroaches produce "six generations per year". UF/IFAS: the whole life cycle takes about 100 days; Texas A&M: a generation in about 100 days.
- Catseye and A-Z call the group a "colony". University of Kentucky ENTFACT-614: cockroaches do not live in centralized nests or colonies like ants, bees and termites.
- A-Z: "cockroach nests can happen anywhere". Illinois DPH: German cockroach hiding places are usually within 10 feet of their food source; Texas A&M: they congregate near heat and moisture.
- PF Harris: one pheromone that soap and water clears. PNAS 2015 (Wada-Katsumata et al.): 40 volatile carboxylic acids in the feces, many made with gut bacteria; UC IPM and Texas A&M advise vacuuming (HEPA) droppings, skins and egg cases.
- Orkin: "up to several feet" from food. Illinois DPH: adults usually roam less than 10 feet from harborage.
