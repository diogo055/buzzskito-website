## What they get wrong (and the source that says otherwise)
- Fantastic Pest Control calls silverfish a "subspecies" of bristletails. They are separate orders: Zygentoma
  vs Archaeognatha/Microcoryphia (NC State ENT 425; UWM field station says they were once grouped, now apart).
  The name confusion is real: Missouri Extension (G7376) says silverfish and firebrats are "also called bristletails".
- A-Z Animals says jumping bristletails jump "up to 12 inches using its tail". NC State ENT 425: some jump up to
  about 4 inches (10 cm) by snapping the abdomen against the ground (UWM: "six inches and more"). We give NC State's.
- A-Z Animals puts silverfish at 0.5-1 inch. UC IPM: about 1/2 inch, up to 3/4; UF/IFAS: adults 1/3 to 1/2 inch.
  (Virginia Tech does say 0.5-1 inch, so sources differ; we use UF and UC IPM.)
- A-Z Animals lists giant deep-sea isopods (20 inches): no help to someone with a bug in the bathtub.
- Dr. Killigan's gives firebrats "90-108°F"; UC IPM says they thrive above 90°F, optimum 98-102°F. Its table gives
  bristletails "three tail bristles" without saying the middle one is longer (only its FAQ does); NC State says the
  three are "dissimilar in length". It puts bristletails at up to 3/4 inch; UWM says generally under 1/2 inch.
- None says, with a source, that jumping bristletails live outdoors and are not pests (MDC: outdoors, "never in
  houses"; NC State: "None of the Archeognatha are considered pests").
