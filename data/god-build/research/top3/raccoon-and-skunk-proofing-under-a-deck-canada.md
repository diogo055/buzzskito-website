## Facts verified for the upgrade
**Ontario's rules**
- 1 km: "release captured live wildlife within 1 kilometre of where they were captured." 24 hours: "release captured
  live wildlife within 24 hours". Permission: "release wildlife on private property only with the landowner's
  permission". https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property (updated
  Dec 15, 2025).
- The regulation: within 24 hours ("not later than 24 hours after capture") release "into the area in close proximity
  to the capture site unless otherwise directed by the Ministry", or "deliver any wildlife that is sick, injured or
  immature to a wildlife custodian"; not "on private property without the permission of the owner". The 1 km figure
  is ontario.ca's; s. 133 gives no distance. O. Reg. 665/98 s. 133, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980665
  (consolidation from Jul 1, 2026; read via the e-Laws Word export).
- Who may trap: s. 31(1) of the Act, if a person "believes on reasonable grounds that wildlife is damaging or is about
  to damage the person's property", on "the person's land", they may harass it or "capture or kill the wildlife".
  Fish and Wildlife Conservation Act, 1997, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97f41 (consolidation from Mar 30, 2026).
- Offence and fine: s. 97(1) "A person who contravenes any provision of this Act or the regulations is guilty of an
  offence"; s. 102(1) "a fine of not more than $25,000", up to a year in jail, or both. Same Act.
- Why moving them is restricted: "Generally, provincial laws prohibit capturing and relocating live wildlife." "These
  laws help: prevent the spread of diseases; ensure wildlife are treated humanely; prevent wildlife from being
  relocated outside their home ranges". On the 1 km rule: "This is to ensure that they know where food sources and den
  locations are, and do not have to fight for new territory." A healthy-looking animal "may have the early stages
  (before signs show) of a disease such as rabies". And "Relocating an animal
  will not always solve the problem" because what attracted it "is likely still present". Same ontario.ca page.
- Sick, injured or orphaned: "take the animal to an authorized wildlife rehabilitator (wildlife custodian)". Same page.
- Dens: "not destroy the den of a furbearing mammal or a black bear without approval from the ministry"; "You do not
  need approval for fox or skunk dens". Same page. Raccoon, Striped Skunk and Red Squirrel are in Schedule 1
  "FURBEARING MAMMALS" (O. Reg. 669/98, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980669, consolidation from Sep 15, 2020).
  Whether closing off an empty under-deck space "destroys" a raccoon den is not defined on the page; ontario.ca's own
  tips say "eliminate access to sites that wildlife might use to build dens (homes), such as openings under decks".
- Agents: someone else may remove the animal if they meet one of ontario.ca's criteria, among them a valid trapping
  licence, municipal wildlife-control staff, an immediate family member, a removal business "only to capture and
  release (not kill)", or ministry authorization; "Wildlife agents can only act if the landowner of the property asks
  them to." Same ontario.ca page.

**Ontario prevention advice** (https://www.ontario.ca/page/preventing-and-managing-conflicts-small-animals, updated
Jan 19, 2026)
- "block potential den areas such as under decks or inside sheds"; "place gravel around the base of the building to
  discourage digging and nesting"; "seal ground-level holes or cracks ... but be careful not to seal animals inside".
- Timing: "If you need to remove a small animal from your home, you should do this in autumn when young have left the
  den or nest, to avoid separating a mother animal from her young."
- Raccoons in a chimney: "after eight to ten weeks, the female and young will leave and not return"; "adult raccoons
  can easily climb out of a chimney, but their young can't".
- The page gives no mesh size, gauge, burial depth, L-shape or one-way door. No separate ontario.ca raccoon or skunk
  page was found.

**Denning dates** (Hinterland Who's Who, Environment and Climate Change Canada with the Canadian Wildlife Federation)
- Raccoon (text updated 2008), https://www.hww.ca/wildlife/mammals/raccoon/ : "Mating tends to take place in March in
  most areas"; "Birth of offspring peaks during May"; "births have been recorded as early as March or as late as
  September"; gestation "averages 63 days"; "The young remain in the maternity den for about eight weeks".
- Striped skunk (1973, 1986, reprinted 1994), https://www.hww.ca/en/wildlife/mammals/striped-skunk.html : "Skunks begin
  to breed in late February or March"; "the young are usually born in early May"; at "approximately seven weeks old the
  female takes them out to search for food"; "at about two months they are weaned"; "They remain with their mother
  until autumn"; skunks may den "under a house or porch".

**Rabies**
- Who carries it: "In Ontario, the animals that spread rabies most often are bats, foxes, skunks and raccoons." Report
  line "1-888-574-6656 (monitored during business hours)". https://www.ontario.ca/page/rabies (updated May 5, 2026).
- Term: "Rabies reservoir species in Ontario are:" bats, raccoons, skunks, foxes.
  https://www.ontario.ca/page/rabies-information-veterinarians (updated Oct 22, 2026).
- The 2015 outbreak: "In 2015, it crossed into Southern Ontario in the Hamilton area and control operations kept it
  contained within 65 kilometres of the first case." "There have been no cases detected since 2023."
  https://www.ontario.ca/page/rabies-wildlife (updated Oct 15, 2026).
- "We successfully eliminated raccoon variant rabies from Southern Ontario in 2023." "The latest outbreak of raccoon
  variant rabies started in 2015 when an animal was inadvertently moved to Ontario from over 500 kilometres away."
  Baiting continues around recent cases and along the New York border.
  https://www.ontario.ca/page/wildlife-rabies-outbreaks-and-control-operations (updated Jul 15, 2026).
- December 2015: City of Hamilton's case table runs "from December 2015 to present"; "Total number of animals found
  positive for raccoon rabies in Hamilton from 2015 to date: 330." https://www.hamilton.ca/Rabies (no date shown).
  ontario.ca gives the year only.

**Raccoon latrines** (CDC, Removing raccoon latrines, Nov 5, 2024, https://www.cdc.gov/baylisascaris/prevention/index.html)
- "Wear disposable gloves to help prevent cross contamination."
- "Wear a N95-rated respirator if working in a confined space to prevent accidental ingestion of eggs or other harmful
  materials."
- "Avoid stirring up dust and debris—you can lightly mist the latrine area with water from a spray bottle".
- "Feces and material contaminated with raccoon feces should be removed and burned, buried, or sent to a landfill."
- "Treat feces-soiled decks, patios, and other surfaces with boiling water or a propane torch (please contact your
  local fire department for regulations and safety practices)." "Most chemicals do not kill roundworm eggs; however,
  heat kills the eggs instantly."
- "Newly deposited eggs take at least 2 – 4 weeks to become infective." No mention of vacuums.

**Mesh** (ICWDM, Decks and Porches, no date, https://icwdm.org/decks-and-porches/)
- "Use quality 1/4- or 1/2- inch hardware cloth. Galvanized is best." Extend it "3 to 4 inches below the ground and then
  out in a perpendicular fashion at least 12 inches". "Never secure an opening until you are certain that animals are no
  longer it" (sic). No gauge given.

## Claims on our page checked
1. **"released within one kilometre of the capture site, and generally within 24 hours"** (FAQ 5; the Ontario Law
   section says the same): **partly false.** 24 hours is a hard limit, not "generally" (s. 133: "not later than 24 hours
   after capture"). The 1 km figure is ontario.ca's (the regulation says "in close proximity to the capture site"). Say:
   "Ontario's rule: release it within 24 hours and within 1 km of where you caught it, on private land only with the
   owner's permission; a sick, injured or orphaned animal goes to an authorized wildlife rehabilitator." The same
   section's reason "spreads rabies and distemper": ontario.ca names disease (rabies), humane treatment and home ranges;
   distemper is not named there. "an offence that can carry provincial charges and fines": true (s. 97, s. 102).
2. **"raccoon litters arrive March through May and the kits cannot leave the den on their own for roughly eight to ten
   weeks"**: **mostly true.** HWW: births peak in May, recorded from March to as late as September; young stay in the
   maternity den about eight weeks. ontario.ca: the chimney family leaves "after eight to ten weeks". Better: "raccoon
   kits are born mainly in spring, peaking in May (Hinterland Who's Who records births from March to as late as
   September), and stay in the den about eight weeks; Ontario says a mother and young leave a chimney after eight to
   ten weeks".
3. **"skunks mate in late winter with kits arriving around May"**: **true.** HWW: breed "in late February or March",
   young "usually born in early May".
4. **"Ontario has been suppressing a raccoon-strain rabies outbreak in the Hamilton area since 2015"**: **false (out of
   date).** ontario.ca: it crossed into southern Ontario in the Hamilton area in 2015 (Hamilton counts from December
   2015), was contained, and was "successfully eliminated ... from Southern Ontario in 2023", no cases since 2023;
   baiting continues. Say: "Raccoon-strain rabies reached the Hamilton area in December 2015; Ontario contained it and
   eliminated it from southern Ontario in 2023, and still baits every year. Raccoons and skunks remain two of Ontario's
   main rabies carriers."
5. **30 cm down / 30 cm out L-footing**: **true.** City of Toronto: "at least 20 to 30 cm straight down and 20 to 30 cm
   angled 90 degrees outwards underground". ICWDM's minimum is shallower (3-4 in down, 12 in out).
6. **Half-inch 19-gauge hardware cloth**: **half true.** Half-inch galvanized: ICWDM ("1/4- or 1/2- inch hardware
   cloth. Galvanized is best"). 19-gauge (and "16-gauge if you can"): **not verifiable**; no source opened gives a gauge
   (Toronto: "galvanized heavy wire screening"; ontario.ca: "heavy gauge mesh" over groundhog holes). Keep it as our own
   spec, unattributed, or say "heavy-gauge".
- Also checked:
  - "Clean latrine sites with boiling water, gloves and a mask — never a dry sweep or a shop vac": boiling water and
    gloves true (CDC); "mask" should be CDC's "N95-rated respirator if working in a confined space"; "never a dry sweep"
    fits CDC's "Avoid stirring up dust and debris"; **"shop vac" not verifiable** (CDC does not mention vacuums). Add
    CDC's disposal line and the propane-torch option.
  - "Do not seal between March and late July" / "late August to late October is the clean window": raccoons match
    Toronto's "March and July". For skunks, Toronto says no one-way door "between April and September ... unless the
    babies are mobile", HWW keeps young with the mother "until autumn", and ontario.ca says "in autumn". Late August is
    earlier than all three; either start the window in September or keep late August only with a "young are out and
    moving" check.
  - "Untouched for three consecutive nights": Toronto says three to five days.
  - "both raccoons and skunks are rabies vector species": true (Ontario's word is "reservoir species").
  - "Ontario law caps relocation of captured wildlife at 1 km": true (ontario.ca; Mississauga).
  - "back within a night or two" and "A trapped animal in spring is very likely a nursing mother": not verifiable as
    worded. Sourced versions: ontario.ca "Relocating an animal will not always solve the problem"; Toronto "Trapping may
    also leave starving young behind to die".
