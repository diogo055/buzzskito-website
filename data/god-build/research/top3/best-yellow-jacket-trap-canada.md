## The three, taken apart
1. **Orkin, "Yellow Jacket Traps".** Short. Says commercial heptyl butyrate "attracts only western yellow jackets", bait
   with meat or sugar, hang far from people, freeze before cleaning, traps are "not effective in treating entire
   colonies". No products, no counts or spacing, no lure life, no season, no Canadian rules, no photos of the insect.
2. **carolinahoneybees.com (DIY trap).** Vinegar-sugar-banana recipe; hang 20-30 ft from living areas; vinegar keeps
   honey bees out; links two RESCUE! traps. Gets the bait season backwards in one place (sweet early, protein late,
   from reader comments). No East/West lure split, no spring queen advice, no regulation.
3. **RESCUE! (maker) pages** (reusable, disposable, "East or West" article): the strongest facts in the SERP. Western
   formula "Generally recommended for states west of the Rocky Mountains", Eastern formula for "states east of the
   Rocky Mountains"; the reusable "is sold nationwide" with a lure "similar to the lure used in the Western Formula
   Disposable Trap"; reusable lure lasts about 2-4 weeks (4-week liquid, 10-week cartridge refills); disposable "will
   last between 2 - 4 weeks"; hang at least 20 ft from patios; start at about 55°F for spring queens; the reusable
   will not catch hornets or paper wasps; designed to "prevent accidental capture of beneficial honeybees"; freeze
   overnight before emptying.

## What none of them give a Canadian, now on the page
- Answer-first hero line: the trap, where to hang it, the bait switch, and that a trap never removes the nest.
- A sourced comparison table (type, how it works, best for, limits) built from RESCUE!'s own pages.
- A situation list and a buyer's checklist (formula, PCP number, refills, 2-4 traps, 6 m rule, freezer bag).
- **The Canadian registration fact.** Lures are pest control products (Pest Control Products Act s.2: "attracting");
  Health Canada registered RESCUE!'s heptyl butyrate products on Dec 2025 (RD2025-10: Disposable Yellowjacket Trap W,
  Yellowjacket Trap Cartridge, Yellowjacket Trap Tube; PCP 35529-35531 per the database record read for the wasp trap
  page on Oct 23); devices sold to attract or destroy flying insects are in Schedule 1 of the Regulations, with
  exemptions (Schedule 2, items 5-6) only for CSA-certified high-voltage insect killers.
- Real photos: an eastern yellowjacket (CC0, lead and share image), a ground nest entrance (CC0), a nest in a storage
  box lid (CC BY 4.0).
- Two FAQs from the searches: the attractant (yellow jacket attractant) and how long a RESCUE! trap lasts.

## Corrected on the page (false against the primary source)
- "Traps are devices, not pesticides, no PMRA registration needed" (bullet, FAQ, comparison note, key facts, grey-market

- Comparison table called the reusable lure "species-matched" and "bee-safe": RESCUE! says the lure is similar to the
  Western formula, and "safe" is a banned word; now quotes RESCUE!'s honey bee wording.

## Flags for the owner (affiliate boxes, not edited)
- The "Best Disposable" pick is the East formula bag (YJTD-E). Health Canada's Dec 2025 registration names the West
  (W) bag, the cartridge and the tube, not an East bag; no Canadian registration was found for the East formula.
- The "Best Refillable / DIY Bait" bottle trap is a device sold to attract and destroy flying insects (Schedule 1);
  check whether the listings carry a PCP number.
