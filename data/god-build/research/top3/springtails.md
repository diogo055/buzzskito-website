- **Wikipedia "Springtail"** (#1): ~3,500-4,000 words of taxonomy and ecology; ~13 specimen photos, none indoors or at scale;
  one paragraph on houses. No drains, houseplants, fleas, mites or young; sizes in mm only.
- **UMN Extension "Springtails"** (#2; reviewed 2024): ~500 words, 3 Bugwood photos; 1/16-1/8 inch, slender vs round, the flea

1. **Real, credited photos**: a springtail beside a printed 1 mm scale (CC0 lead), white springtails found indoors under a potted

3. **"Baby springtails" from sources**: no larva or pupa (Clemson: ametabolous; juveniles molt to maturity and keep molting);

4. **Moisture fixes in order, then insecticides at each source's strength** (UMN: not effective, not indoors; Colorado State:
   avoid indoors; UC IPM: no better than vacuuming, never down drains; Texas A&M: short-lived indoor pesticides as the third of
   three options), no homemade sprays; **the drain question** (Kentucky: they reach drains after getting in another way).
5. 10 FAQs from People also ask; 13 university extension sources, each linked where it is used.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Bug Out "4 to 10 mm", Orkin "0.25 to 6 mm" (unsourced). Penn State: 0.2 to 10 mm across species, most in homes 1 to 2 mm.
- Terminix: "1/16th of an inch (6mm)" (1/16 inch is about 1.6 mm), "egg, larval, pupae, and adulthood stages" (Clemson HGIC 2445:
  ametabolous, no pupa; UC IPM: young differ only in size and color), springtails "sometimes referred to as snowfleas" (only
  some are: UMN and Penn State name Hypogastrura nivicola).
- DoItYourself.com (vinegar, bleach), STL (vinegar, neem), Better Termite's how-to (soapy water, vinegar, diatomaceous earth): no
  extension page read recommends them. UMN: pesticides are not effective and should not be used indoors for springtails.
- Better Termite: springtails need "at least 60% relative humidity", die off below 50%: unsourced, in no extension page read.
