## What they all lack (and what this page now adds)
- **Whether the traps reduce bites.** University of Wisconsin–Madison: studies confirm the traps catch mosquitoes, but
  homeowner traps "usually do not work to reduce human biting"; Mosquito Magnets catch thousands without changing yard
  biting rates; Kline (2006) island exception at one trap per acre; all 3 of their Mosquito Magnets failed within a
  year; no CO2 measured from a Dynatrap (unpublished). New FAQ "Do CO2 mosquito traps actually work?".
- **Canadian registration.** Pest Control Products Regulations, Schedule 1 item 2: devices sold to attract or destroy
  flying insects stay under the Act; the Schedule 2 exemption covers only CSA-certified high-voltage insect killers.
  Registry: Mosquito Magnet Executive 29944 and Patriot Plus 32375 registered; octenol lures registered (e.g. 28440);
  **no Biogents product or registrant found**; no Dynatrap found. The old FAQ said traps need no registration: replaced.
- **New table** "CO2 Traps Compared: Lures, Registration and What the Research Found" (propane, BG-Mosquitaire,
  BG-Home, BG-GAT, UV "CO2" traps), **buyer's checklist**, **two FAQs** (do they work; is Biogents registered).
- **Answer-first opening** (how a CO2 trap works, propane vs Biogents, registration check, honest expectation).
- **Three photos**: mosquito bites on a forearm (CDC, public domain, lead), Culex pipiens in Ontario (CC BY 4.0),
  Asian tiger mosquito eggs (CDC, public domain). No share image: the ledger has no public-domain photo of a trap or
  of a Canadian biting female mosquito.
- Product facts corrected in text: consumer Biogents traps use BG-Sweetscent (Biogents: same formulation as the
