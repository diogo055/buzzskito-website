## What was wrong on our own page (corrected Oct 23, each to a primary source)
- "Exactly two families" / "only bromethalin and FGARs" are legal: cholecalciferol baits (BASF TC 411, 412, 413) were
  registered DOMESTIC on 2026-06-25 (PMRA registry), and corn-cellulose baits are domestic too.
- Table row "Cholecalciferol: not registered for Canadian consumers": false since June 25, 2026. Selontra (BASF) is registered
  commercial the same day. No current d-CON product is registered (only a historical "D-CON DESTROY").
- "Only sold as ready-to-use, tamper-resistant bait stations": Health Canada's wording is "Domestic class products must always
  be used in bait stations" and "must be sold packaged with a bait station"; refillable domestic kits exist, and TC 413's label
  says "THIS BAIT STATION IS NOT TAMPER RESISTANT".
- "Loose pellets = not legal": the 2010 rule prohibits loose bait for the eight actives it covered; corn-cellulose pellets
  (RatRid, MouseRid, Wilson Rat Out pellets) are registered domestic products.
- "Took full effect in 2013": Health Canada says manufacturers had until December 31, 2012 for new labels (REV2010-17, 2010).
- Bromethalin "kills in 24-48 hours": the Canadian label says "it may take two or more days from time of consumption for them
  to die". Fixed everywhere, including the TopPick blurb and one pro (affiliate attributes untouched).
- Anticoagulants "4-10 days": Health Canada says "death is usually delayed 5 to 10 days" (SGARs).
- "The main PMRA-registered consumer option": unsourced (diphacinone has twice as many domestic products); now "one".
- Owls "brodifacoum the most frequent": B.C. review table says brodifacoum and bromadiolone were most commonly detected; added
  the review's own caveat (6 of 164 owls, 4%, died of rodenticide toxicity).
- Removed unsourced claims: border seizures, municipal motions in Ontario and Quebec, "provincial offence" for commercial use,
  vets' ranking of rodenticide calls.
