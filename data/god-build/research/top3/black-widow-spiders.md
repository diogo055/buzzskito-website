## What our page does that none of the three do
1. **Real, credited photos.**
   - The three US black widows, plus Florida's red widow and the brown widow's hourglass.
   - Two show widows where people meet them: on a stone or concrete block beside her egg sac, and in a corner with two egg sacs.
2. **A quick-ID table right under the answer.** It has sourced sizes and marks for the three black widows, males, young, the red and brown widows, and four look-alikes. Below it come look-alike photo cards.
3. **A range table by species and region.** Sources: UF/IFAS Extension, UC IPM, MDC, Clemson and Penn State. It includes where the northern and western widows reach Canada.
4. **The places people actually meet widows.** Meter, utility and irrigation boxes, garage doors and vents, porch furniture, toys, grills and sandboxes, from UC IPM, Utah State, Clemson, MedlinePlus and Florida's Department of Agriculture (FDACS). The page also says how bites happen (FDACS).
5. **Males and young, and current health facts.**
   - Males and young are described with their colors (all three skip them or give one line).
   - Health facts are at CDC and MedlinePlus strength, with the Poison Help number and no venom-potency comparisons.

## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- **animalcorner, venom potency.** It says the venom is "15 times more potent" than a rattlesnake's, with no source. CDC calls the venom a neurotoxin, and MedlinePlus says death in a healthy person is very rare. We make no potency comparison.
- **animalcorner, sizes and lifespan.**
  - Its 12-16 mm body runs past the agency figures: MDC about 3/8 in, UC IPM about 1/2 in (13 mm), FDACS 8-15 mm for Florida widows.
  - "Up to 5 years" for females has no source. Our sources give no lifespan, so the page gives none.
- **Vedantu, size and eggs.** Its body length is "1.5 cm" in the table and "1.5 inches including legs" in an FAQ. It gives 200-900 eggs a sac; Penn State gives 200-800 for the southern widow and UC IPM about 300 for the western.
- **Old figures elsewhere on the page.**
  - The EPFL Wikipedia copy converts sizes wrongly ("5.0 inches (51 mm)") and repeats a pre-antivenom "5% fatal" figure.
  - Texas A&M's urban entomology page (#20) still says adults "average 1½ inches long" and that "less than 5%" of bites are fatal.
  - Current sources disagree: MedlinePlus says death is very rare, and Poison Control's review of 15,000+ exposures found no deaths.
- **Wikipedia.** It gives no habitat near homes and has no male or juvenile photos. Its range covers only *L. mactans*.
