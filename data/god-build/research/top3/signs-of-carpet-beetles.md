## The three, taken apart
- **Bug Out (Rentokil), "6 Signs You Have a Carpet Beetle Infestation"**: about 1,000 words; one stock photo of rugs
  and no photo of any sign; no table, FAQ, sources, author or date. Three overlapping h3s on droppings ("droppings and
  fecal pellets", "poop", "droppings and casings"). Droppings "the size of a pinhead". Never mentions bed bugs or clothes
  moths. Quote and inspection buttons throughout.
- **Colonial Pest, "What Are the Signs of Carpet Beetles?"**: about 450 words, five numbered signs (rug damage, worn
  woolens, clipped fur, shed skins, adults at windows). No images, sources, FAQ, author or date; nothing on droppings,
  bites or bed bugs. Adults "less than 1/8-inch long".
- **bettertermite, "Signs of Carpet Beetles: How to Spot an Infestation"** (technician byline, "updated Sep 24, 2026"):
  2,500-2,800 words; about 5 editorial photos, all of adult beetles (none of skins, droppings or damage; no scale; no
  credits); 8 FAQs; cites UMD Extension, Virginia Cooperative Extension and UC IPM. Mattress section, "carpet mites vs
  carpet beetles", DIY treatment with diatomaceous earth, a $49-a-month plan and $200 setup fee.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- bettertermite: carpet beetles "don't live in mattresses". CDC's EID report (2021): larvae are commonly found in
  wardrobes, on stuffed animals, mattresses or under carpets; UC IPM: some mattresses and pillows are stuffed with hair
  or feathers.
- Colonial: adults "less than 1/8-inch long". UC IPM: black carpet beetle adults are 1/8 to 3/16 inch.
- Colonial: larvae clip fur hairs at the base, leaving bald spots. Not in any carpet beetle source opened (UC IPM says
  it of clothes moths on fur); left out.
- Bug Out: droppings "the size of a pinhead" (unsourced). UC IPM: about the size of a grain of salt.
- Puffy and Sleepmax (mattress sellers): holes in the mattress fabric as a sign. Kentucky: cotton and synthetics are
  rarely attacked unless blended with wool or heavily soiled; UC IPM: larvae don't feed on synthetic fibers.
