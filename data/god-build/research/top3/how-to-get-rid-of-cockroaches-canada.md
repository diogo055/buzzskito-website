## Claims corrected (primary sources)
- Bait-plan intro: "Combat-style discs ... domestic-class versions are PMRA-registered ... child-resistant" ->
  Combat Roach Killing System 26519 cancelled (expiry 2006-05-31); Raid Max Roach Bait 26645 and Raid Double
  Control Roach Baits 27731 cancelled (expiry 2025-09-25); current stations Raid 25735 ("CONTAINS 12 CHILD
  RESISTANT BAITS") and PIC 20471; Health Canada's 48-96 hours.
- Advion "typically US-market product" (FAQ, Quick Answer bullet, key facts) -> no Advion product and no product
  containing indoxacarb, current or historical; Health Canada: "Online purchases of unregistered pesticides and
  their shipment to Canada are not permitted"; "It's a violation of the Pest Control Products Act to buy and use
  unregistered pesticides."
- "Insect growth regulators" as a professional tool (FAQ, "When to stop DIYing") -> no current product with
  hydroprene or pyriproxyfen lists cockroaches (also none with novaluron, noviflumuron, fenoxycarb, kinoprene,
  diflubenzuron, hexaflumuron; the two methoprene hits are permethrin flea sprays); commercial gels VANECTO
  35686 ("Only to be used by individuals holding an appropriate pesticide applicator certificate or license") and
  Seclira 33424.
- "A typical kitchen wants 8-12 placements" -> Raid 25735 label: at least 10 in the kitchen and 2 per bathroom,
  12-15 and 2-4 for large kitchens or heavy infestations.
- "replace ... typically every 2-3 months" -> Raid: every 4 weeks; PIC: when necessary, e.g. every 2-3 months.
- Advion section "same carry-home-and-cascade mechanism ... child-resistant, no-questions path" -> a PCP-numbered
  station or gel at the label's count (the Ortho 32560 label says cockroaches "will not share the bait").
- Verified and kept: boric acid dust not sold for home use since 2018 (Health Canada: sale prohibited July 22,
  2018; use ended July 22, 2019; registry: the only current domestic boric acid products are Raid 25735, Nisus
  30293 and a wood preservative).
- Not changed, unsure: the page's secondary-kill premise (carcasses and droppings poisoning nestmates) is not on
  any Canadian label read; the Ortho label says the opposite for its gel.
