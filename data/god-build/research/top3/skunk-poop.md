## What they get wrong (ours states it from the source)
- Mothballs and ammonia as skunk deterrents (Animal Corner): UNL Extension G1769 says using mothballs or ammonia in the landscape is dangerous and violates federal regulations; NPIC says mothballs are not meant to be used as animal repellents.
- Baylisascariasis listed as a skunk-poop disease with no word about raccoons (Animal Corner): CDC says *B. columnaris* is the skunk's roundworm and the raccoon's *B. procyonis* is thought to pose the greatest risk to people.
- Two different skunk sizes on one page, including a 1/2 to 3/4 inch thickness (Critter Control): ICWDM says 1/4 to 1/2 inch thick and 1 to 2 inches long; WDFW says 1/2 inch and 2 to 4 inches. No agency source gives 3/4 inch.
- "Tapered tubes" (Critter Stop): WDFW and Wildlife Illinois say skunk droppings usually have blunt ends; tapered (pointed) ends are the fox and coyote tell (UF/IFAS).
- "Skunks cannot climb" (Critter Control): Montana State says striped skunks normally do not climb, but WDFW says spotted skunks are excellent climbers that may den in an attic.
- Skunk feces "more likely to transmit leptospirosis" (Critter Control): CDC says leptospirosis spreads through the urine of infected animals; UC IPM lists skunks as carriers without tying it to droppings.
- "Mushes when prodded = skunk" (Animal Corner): no agency or extension source gives that test; MDC and ICWDM say to use a tool or gloves and wash afterward.
