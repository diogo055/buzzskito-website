  no wild rabbits, nothing on dogs or tularemia.
- Bunny Bellies: pet only; up to 300 pellets a day; cecotropes "like small grape clusters"; tiny, chain-link, dark, misshapen,

- Bunny Lady: pet only; pellets 7-12 mm ("pea to chickpea"); 1/4 cup a day; no poop for 12 hours = emergency; GI stasis, litter

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Bunny Lady says rabbit droppings are safe on food crops; Chewy says pellets can go on a garden without composting. SDSU Extension:
  wildlife droppings are raw manure that can transmit pathogens to people (the same for pets); use no manure unless it is at least six
  months old or carefully heat composted, and grow nothing eaten raw where droppings piles turned up that season.
- Chewy's 8-hour no-stool rule rests on one quoted vet; the Merck Veterinary Manual lists diarrhea or no droppings for more than
  12 hours among signs to contact a vet right away, and fewer droppings as a reason for prompt care (the page gives Merck's wording).
- Bunny Lady's 7-12 mm pellet size has no source; for wild cottontails UC IPM gives closer to 1/4 inch, Wildlife Illinois under 1/2 inch.
