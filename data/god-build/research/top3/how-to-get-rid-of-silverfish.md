## The three, taken apart
- **Scotts:** about 500 words; one stock-style photo on white; no table, FAQ, sources, author or date. Description
  (1/2 to 1 inch), four steps (food, humidity, sealing, then its own barrier spray along baseboards, windows and
  entryways), product cards. No firebrats, no baby silverfish, no humidity numbers, no damage photos.
- **pest-ex.com.au:** about 900 words; no silverfish photos; no sources; written for Australia. Seven "natural"
  methods: diatomaceous earth ("non-toxic, safe"), essential oils, cedarwood, a baking soda and sugar trap, cucumber
  slices, lower humidity, sealing. None is backed by anything.
- **WebMD:** about 1,200 words; one stock photo; cites UC IPM, NPIC and Orkin. Description, diet, "do they bite"
  (no), a list of dusts and sprays, two FAQs. No identification table, no baby silverfish, says baits are not very
  useful and that females lay eggs daily.

   gray silverfish beside painted trim, common silverfish, a booklouse; rows for common and gray silverfish,

   search) with UC IPM's hatchling size and when scales appear; look-alike cards.
2. **A plan built on measured evidence:** relative humidity above 75 percent at higher temperatures (UC IPM) and the
   EPA's 30 to 50 percent target, with MU's caveat that gray silverfish tolerate a wide humidity range; sticky traps
   to find them, not to end them (Norwegian field study: about 40 traps per apartment cut gray silverfish by 9.5
   percent, and numbers rebounded); bait vs spray in the same study (71 vs 53 percent in 10 weeks; more than 90
   percent building-wide with bait); dusts, baits and sprays by type and active ingredient; boric acid dust fails on
   firebrats (UC IPM); foggers not recommended; the label is the law (EPA).
3. **Damage identification and searchers' questions:** scraped surfaces, irregular holes, scales nearby, fungal

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- pest-ex: diatomaceous earth is "non-toxic" and "safe" around children and pets. NPIC: breathing large amounts may
  cause coughing and shortness of breath; follow the label and limit exposure.
- pest-ex: essential oils "unbearable for silverfish", a baking soda and sugar trap, cucumber slices. None of the
  government or university sources we opened supports any of these; the page says so.
- WebMD: females lay eggs daily. UC IPM: clutches average 50 eggs (1 to 200); Virginia Tech: fewer than 100 eggs in a
  lifespan of 2 to 8 years, so populations build slowly and a large one has been there a while.
- WebMD and Scotts name one or two species. MU Extension: four house-dwelling bristletails (common, gray, four-lined, firebrat).
- WebMD (following UC IPM's 2009 note): baits are not very useful. NC State: boric acid baits can work well in homes;
  UF/IFAS lists boric acid granular baits; the 2020 Norwegian study found indoxacarb bait outdid traps and a
  permethrin spray. Our page presents the disagreement and the label rule.
- Scotts: a barrier spray along baseboards and windows. UC IPM: small amounts into cracks and voids, large infestations only.
