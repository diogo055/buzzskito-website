## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- pest-ex: diatomaceous earth is "non-toxic" and "safe" around children and pets. NPIC: breathing large amounts can
  cause coughing and shortness of breath, and long-term inhalation of crystalline forms is linked to silicosis;
  follow the label and limit exposure.
- pest-ex: essential oils "unbearable for silverfish", a baking soda and sugar trap that dehydrates them, cucumber
  slices as a repellent. No government or university source we opened supports any of these; we say so.
- WebMD: females lay eggs daily. UC IPM: clutches average 50 eggs (1 to 200); Virginia Tech: fewer than 100 eggs in
  a lifespan of 2 to 8 years, which is why populations build slowly and a large one has been there a while.
- WebMD and Scotts list one or two species. MU Extension: four house-dwelling bristletails in North America (common,
  gray and four-lined silverfish, and the firebrat), and they differ in the conditions they like.
- WebMD (following UC IPM, 2009): baits are not very useful. NC State says boric acid baits can work well in homes,
  UF/IFAS lists boric acid granular baits, and the 2020 Norwegian study found indoxacarb bait outdid traps and a
  permethrin spray; we present the disagreement and the label rule.
- Scotts: a barrier spray along baseboards and around windows. UC IPM: direct small amounts into cracks, crevices
  and voids, and reserve chemicals for large infestations.
