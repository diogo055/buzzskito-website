## The three, taken apart

- **Wikipedia, Shrew.** About 1,100 words of prose; headings Characteristics, Echolocation, Classification, then long species lists. 3 photos (a group of four shrews, a water shrew skeleton, a northern short-tailed shrew) and a world map; nothing in a yard or house, nothing at a scale. No table, no FAQ. Says shrews are not rodents and that some are venomous; nothing on bites to people, disease, rabies, houses, droppings or what to do. Weak spots: the "venom could kill 200 mice" line rests on a general book; its medical-use claims carry a "better source needed" tag.
- **EBSCO Research Starters, Shrews (2022).** About 1,300 words; Overview, Shrew Facts, Classification, Physical Characteristics, Life Cycles, The American Short-Tailed Shrew, glossary. No photos seen, no table, no FAQ, nothing on houses. Gets wrong: the overview says shrews possess venomous saliva (only some do: MDC names the three short-tailed shrews); puts the musk glands at the knee and elbow (Animal Diversity Web: belly and sides of the short-tailed shrew; the 1994 handbook chapter: the hindquarters); bite risk only as "painful", unsourced.
- **Critter Control, Shrews.** About 550 words under a lead form and reviews; Appearance, Habitat, Entry, Signs (links only), Damage, Control and Safety, Trapping. One stock photo. Two bolded questions, no FAQ, no table, no sources. Gets wrong or overstates: says shrews forage for seeds and other vegetation (MDC: shrews typically do not eat much plant material); bites "may cause minor allergic reactions" (no source); names no venomous species; no word on rabies; says only a trained specialist can remove them (the 1994 handbook chapter gives homeowners exclusion and mouse-trap sets).

## Facts to get right (sources that say otherwise than the three)

- Venom: only the short-tailed shrews (MDC; handbook chapter: Blarina brevicauda, and presumably B. carolinensis and B. hylophaga).
- Diet: mostly insects and other small animals; little plant material (MDC).
- Not a rodent: no chisel-like front teeth (MDC); mice have four toes on the front feet, shrews five on every foot (PGC).
- Disease: "potential exists ... but this is poorly documented" (handbook chapter): no stronger claim. CDC's hantavirus pages are about rodents; used only for cleaning up droppings that cannot be identified.
- The handbook chapter's 1994 note that one southeastern shrew subspecies is federally protected is out of date and is not used.
