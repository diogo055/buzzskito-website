## Teardown
**Merck Manual consumer (Jan 2025, Barish and Arnold; about 400 words).** No photos, tables or FAQs. Covers where biting mites come from (birds, rodents, pets, pigs, straw, hay, seeds), burrowing vs not, grocer's itch, dust mite wheezing, anti-itch care, scabies creams. The professional edition adds: chiggers "probably the most common", diagnosis from the history, mites rarely found, bites resemble other rashes, symptomatic treatment, bedding bites may be bed bugs. No sizes, body sites, timing, prevention or how to get a mite identified.
- Note: merckmanuals.com is not a host the validator accepts, so the page cannot cite Merck. Its points are carried by CDC, MedlinePlus, StatPearls and extension sources. Its consumer line "antibiotics taken by mouth are given if mites have burrowed under the skin" is not repeated: CDC says scabies takes a prescription scabicide.

**Medical News Today (May 2020, Eske; about 1,300 words).** H2s: pictures (empty in the capture), chiggers, Demodex, oak mites, scabies (each with symptoms and treatment), other symptoms, dust mites, prevention, when to see a doctor. No table, no FAQ, no sizes beyond "0.5-2.0 mm". Wrong or weak:
- It says dust mites do not bite, then that they cause "patches of small, red bumps". Kentucky (ef649) says dust mites do not cause itching or bite-like reactions; IDPH says they rarely cause skin irritation.
- Oak mite outbreak states unsourced; Demodex "bites"; not updated since 2020.

**Healthline (Nov 2024, Raypole; about 1,600 words).** H2s: can dust mites bite, symptoms, what bites look like, how do I know what bit me (chiggers, scabies, Demodex, bird and rodent mites, oak mites, straw itch mites), treatment, see a doctor. No sizes, no table, no FAQ; a promised photo guide does not show in the capture; a paid telehealth ad mid-article. Wrong or weak:
- It suggests permethrin for mite bites generally. CDC: scabies needs a prescription, and nonprescription products have not been tested and approved for it.
- No body sites or season for bird and rodent mites, and no advice on them (Texas A&M: rooms near the nest; spring-summer for birds, fall-winter for rodents).

## What our page does that none of the three do
1. **A sorter by where you picked them up:** grass, oaks, hay, an emptied nest, trapped rodents, skin contact. Each leads to the mite, its body sites and its own page.
2. **Real, credited bite photos for four mites** (chigger bites beside a ruler, bird mite, straw itch mite, scabies), plus a card row of the mites themselves and their look-alikes.
3. **A quick-ID table with sourced sizes in inches:** where you pick it up, where the bites show up, can you see it. Also a timeline table (when the itch starts, how long it lasts).
4. **How to get the mite identified** (UC IPM, Texas A&M, Kentucky: traps, brush and alcohol vial, no tape test), and the non-biters stated plainly (dust, clover, spider mites).
5. **Health at source strength:** CDC on scabies, the AAD's emergency signs, and CDC's Yellow Book on rickettsialpox spread by house mouse mites (a reason fever after rodent mite bites needs a doctor).
