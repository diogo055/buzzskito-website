## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- EcoShield: not harmful to pets. A 2011 veterinary case report (J Vet Emerg Crit Care, PubMed 21827597) describes a
  dog's corneal ulcer from a two-striped walkingstick's spray; UF/IFAS EENY-314 and MDC describe eye injuries in people.
- WebMD: pesticides are the easiest answer. Iowa State Extension: walkingsticks seldom do enough damage to warrant
  management; Texas A&M Forest Service: no control is needed.
- WebMD: giant walkingstick up to 6 inches. MDC and Texas A&M Forest Service: females reach 7 inches, the longest US insect.
- WebMD: no first aid for spray in the eye. Poison Control: rinse at once with lots of room-temperature water for at
  least 15 to 20 minutes, then call 1-800-222-1222; UF/IFAS: immediate irrigation, care for decreased vision or light
  sensitivity.
- San Diego Zoo: never says which US species spray. Wilderness Environ Med 2016 (PubMed 26961847) and the 2011 vet
  report: the two Anisomorpha (two-striped) species of the Southeast.
