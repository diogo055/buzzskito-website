## What each answers
- **Orkin** (orkin.com/other/stink-bugs/eggs): ~200 words. BMSB eggs barrel-shaped, light green, 20-30 per mass
  under leaves, May-August, "nearly 500" per female, five molts. No egg photo at all, no size, no hatch time,
  no sources, no look-alikes. Quote and phone CTAs.
- **UConn Extension EXT197** (Mar 2026): ~600 words. Life cycle, host plants, damage, control, a gallery of
  ~9 small (300 px) photos by the author incl. spined soldier bug eggs and a large egg raft; advice to crush
  eggs only when correctly identified and to spare predator eggs. No scale, no nymph photos, no FAQ.
- **PestWorld** ("Sting Bug Eggs", NPMA): ~330 words. Do stink bugs lay eggs, what they look like (barrel,
  light green to white or yellow, 20-30), where (leaf undersides). One uncredited photo of empty eggs, no size,
  no hatch time, no species, no look-alikes named; "contact a professional" if you see eggs.

## What they get wrong or leave out (and the source that says otherwise)
- PestWorld tells readers to call a professional on finding eggs; UC IPM says to destroy BMSB egg masses under
  leaves yourself, and Utah State prefers leaving dark (parasitized) masses on the plant. Its H1 says "Sting Bug".
- Orkin says BMSB was "likely introduced" in 1998; Penn State says it was first collected in Allentown in 1998
  but probably arrived several years earlier, and EPA says the mid-1990s (not used on our page; out of scope).
- Orkin and PestWorld give no size; Penn State measures the egg at 1.6 x 1.3 mm (about 1/16 inch).
- UConn gives conflicting counts ("sometimes 30 or more" and "up to thirty") and says adults overwinter under
  leaf litter, which does not fit BMSB: Kentucky says BMSB overwinters in buildings, unmated.
- None of the three says that overwintering adults do not lay eggs indoors (Kentucky, UC IPM, Penn State, EPA).
- None mentions parasitized (dark) egg masses, egg bursters, or the predator eggs beyond UConn's one line.

## Sources that disagree (stated on the page, not averaged)
- BMSB egg color: light green (StopBMSB, UMD), white to pale green (UC IPM, Virginia Tech, Illinois), light
  green or yellow (EPA), light yellow to yellow-red (Penn State).
- Hatch time: roughly 4-5 days (Illinois) vs 5-7 days (Utah State).
- Laying season: May-August (EPA, Penn State) vs June-August (UMD).
- Green stink bug eggs: pure white until ready to hatch (Virginia Tech) vs yellow to green, later pink to gray (UMD).
- Wasp exit holes: rough-edged (UC IPM) vs round (Penn State); the page uses UC IPM's description.
