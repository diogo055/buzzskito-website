## What ours does that none of them do
1. **Two sourced tables.** A quick-ID table right under the answer, with traits as rows: size in inches, shape, color, nest, colony at its peak, where you meet it, when it is active, behavior near people, sting. Then a second table covering 8 look-alikes (yellowjacket, bald-faced hornet, paper wasp, mud dauber, cicada killer, honey bee, bumble bee, carpenter bee), with size, the tell, nest and sting for each. None of the three gives a single size; Almanac's chart has 3 rows and no numbers.
2. **The night test.** The European hornet is the wasp that flies after dark and comes to porch lights (Penn State calls it the only nocturnal wasp in Pennsylvania; MDC agrees on the lights). Yellowjacket nests are largely inactive at night (UF/IFAS). None of the three mentions night activity.
3. **Current, dated status of the invasive hornets.** Yellow-legged hornet: confirmed near Savannah in Aug 2023 (Georgia Department of Agriculture); found in Beaufort County, SC, in spring 2024 and in York County in Dec 2025 (Clemson DPI); USDA APHIS asks GA, SC and NC to report it. Northern giant hornet: declared eradicated Dec 18, 2024 (WSDA and USDA). Bob Vila still calls the Asian giant hornet "currently rare" in the US. M&M and Almanac omit the eradication.
4. **Real, credited photos of each insect, plus 6 look-alike cards and a checklist.** Photos of the European hornet, yellowjacket, bald-faced hornet, paper wasp, honey bee, bumble bee, yellow-legged hornet and northern giant hornet. Six cards link the sibling pages, and a "which one do I have?" checklist closes the identification.
5. **Sting facts at the source's strength.** Smooth vs barbed stingers, and only females sting (Missouri Extension). A European hornet sting is about as painful as a honey bee's (Penn State). CDC NIOSH covers anaphylaxis as an emergency and how to scrape out a bee's stinger.

## What they get wrong (our page states it from the source)
- **Bob Vila:**
  - It names the cicada killer ("sand hornet") as one of three North American hornets. MDC calls cicada killers solitary wasps that dig soil tunnels and build no paper nests, and MDC says true hornets are the genus *Vespa*.
  - It says hornets are "not attracted to sugary foods". Penn State says European hornets take sugars, especially in fall, come to fallen apples and pears, and strip lilac bark for sap; MDC adds fruit juice, sap, garbage and picnic areas.
  - It says the European hornet is "twice" a paper wasp. Paper wasps are about 3/4 to 1 inch (Missouri Extension) and about 1 inch (UC IPM); European hornet workers are up to about 1 inch (Penn State).
- **M&M Pest Control:**
  - Its wasp sizes disagree with themselves (1/3 to 1 inch next to 1 to 2.5 cm).
  - It says hornets are "notably larger than most wasps", but paper wasps reach about 1 inch, and cicada killers are 1 1/2 to 2 inches (Missouri Extension).
  - It gives a "5% acetylcholine" venom figure that we did not check and do not repeat.
- **Almanac:** no factual error found, but no sizes, and a drawn header image.
