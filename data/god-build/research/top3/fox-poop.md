## What they get wrong, and the source that says otherwise
- Critter Control: droppings "just as dangerous" as the fox. CDC's 2008 rabies recommendations: contact with feces is indirect contact and
  not a rabies exposure; CDC's AE page: human tapeworm cases are rare (but can be fatal if untreated). Overstated, unquantified.
- Critter Control / Field & Stream: length 2-3 inches as the main size. Length depends on how the dropping breaks; thickness is the useful
  number (Alabama Extension 3/8-5/8 inch vs coyote about 1 inch; Reid 2015 DNA study: gray fox mean 11.5 mm vs coyote 16.45 mm, and size
  and shape are "not generally diagnostic").
- Woodland Trust: 8-12 cm (3.1-4.7 inches) is longer than every US source we opened (UF/IFAS 2 inches, Arizona gray fox 2 x 0.5 inch,
  Alabama about 2 inches).
- Field & Stream on raccoons: roundworm infection by inhaling. CDC's raccoon roundworm pages: people are infected by swallowing eggs;
  the eggs need at least 2-4 weeks to become infective.
- All three: a distinctive smell for fox droppings. No US agency source we opened describes one; NPS says it is male fox URINE that turns
  skunk-like in the February mating season.
