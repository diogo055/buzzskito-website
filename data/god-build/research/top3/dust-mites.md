## The three, taken apart (Google's own top 3)
- **American Lung Association** (updated Oct 28, 2026): ~650 words; no photo of a mite (one embedded video); what they are,
  sources, health effects, protection steps. Humidity below 50% with a hygrometer; wash bedding at "at least 120 degrees F";
  "4 out of 5" US homes with allergen in a bed and "up to 85%" of asthma patients, both unattributed; not parasites that bite,
  sting or burrow; most exposure during sleep. No size, no look-alikes, no table, no FAQ, no sources.
- **Mayo Clinic, Dust mite allergy: symptoms and causes** (Sept 12, 2025): ~600 words; no photos; related to ticks and spiders,
  too small to see without a microscope; full symptom lists (nose and eyes, asthma); worse while sleeping or cleaning; when to see
  a doctor and emergency signs; risk factors; complications. Control steps sit on a second page. Nothing on bites or look-alikes.
- **NIEHS, Dust Mites and Cockroaches** (reviewed Mar 6, 2026): ~400 words on mites; the prevention list (humidity at or below
  50%, encasements, weekly wash at 130-140 F, freeze non-washables overnight, synthetic bedding, bare bedroom floors, HEPA or
  double bag, mask and 20 minutes out of the room). No size, no photos of a mite, nothing on bites, no evidence caveats.
- Others read: Wikipedia (~700 words, SEM lead photo, 0.2-0.3 mm, biology and allergens, no control section); rememberthefilter
  (~1,050 words, unsourced, sells filters, 300-400 microns, 3 FAQs); Healthwise (~115 words, no numbers).

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- ALA washes at "at least 120 F"; NIEHS and MedlinePlus give 130-140 F. The AAAAI/ACAAI practice parameter (2013) says high
  temperature is not required for weekly washing and advises home hot water below 120 F to avoid scalding. We give NIEHS's
  figure, the scald caution, and the one point all five sources share: wash every week.
- Healthwise calls them "insects" in one sentence; they are eight-legged arachnids (practice parameter; Mayo, Kentucky: related
  to ticks and spiders).
- Orkin, quoted in Google's AI Overview, gives ".002 inches"; adults are 0.2-0.4 mm (practice parameter) or about 0.5 mm
  (Kentucky), roughly 0.008-0.02 inch. rememberthefilter's "a hundredth to a sixtieth of an inch" for 300-400 microns is loose
  (about 1/85 to 1/64 inch).
- The AI Overview (citing a retailer) says itching without puncture marks "often points to dust mite waste proteins". No source
  opened says so; UC IPM says some itching has no arthropod cause and to see a family physician first; Mayo says an ongoing
  dust mite allergy can flare eczema. We say exactly that.
- rememberthefilter recommends sunlight and sells filters; no health source opened lists sunlight. Our page makes no filter,
  air purifier or spray claim.
