## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Wikipedia (uncited) and bettertermite: 40-100 eggs a sac. Penn State: each sac can contain 200 or more cream-colored eggs.
- bettertermite: "about half an inch" and "one of the most common house spiders across North America". UC IPM: 3/8 inch
  (10 mm); Penn State: the coastal states of the Atlantic, Gulf and Pacific, with *S. borealis* more common in the North.
- insectio.com (page one): "False widows are never jet black." NC State Parks: the black individuals have no red
  markings (some are black); Penn State: purplish-brown to black.
- Google's AI Overview: false widows "matte", black widows "glossy, jet black". UC IPM: a well-fed black widow can look
  brownish or plum-colored and its hourglass is on rare occasions absent; our test is the underside plus where it was
  found, never color alone.
- The UK pages' bite advice (NHS) and "relatively harmless" framing do not carry to US readers; ours gives UC IPM, Penn
  State and published case data at their strength, with MedlinePlus, CDC and AAD care advice.
