## The three, taken apart
- **Orkin:** about 1,400 words; one illustration, no photos; no table, no sources. Prevention list (12-inch clear
  zone, firewood on a rack, dehumidifier, yellow bulbs), then a branch inspection and quote. FAQ: ear myth (myth),
  do they pinch (rarely break skin), not poisonous, 30-50 eggs. Gives two different sizes (5-16 mm and 5-25 mm),
  says they mainly eat sprouts and decaying plants, calls its treatments "scientifically proven" with nothing cited,
  and links a page on earwig "larvae".
- **UC IPM:** about 1,600 words; 5 photos (earwigs, leaf and apricot damage, an oil can trap). Best of the three:
  identification, life cycle, damage, trapping (rolled newspaper, cardboard, bamboo, hose; low-sided can with 1/2
  inch of oil), cultural control, spinosad baits as the preferred insecticide, indoor sprays not recommended. No
  table, no FAQ, no look-alikes, California species only, little on earwigs in the house.
- **NCAP:** about 1,400 words; 4 photos (two earwigs, two traps); 7 trap designs; habitat removal; "managing earwigs
  in the home" (caulk, weatherstrip, fix leaks, stone border, yellow lights, vacuum). No pesticides at all, no
  table, no FAQ; written for the Northwest.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Orkin's two sizes (5-16 mm and 5-25 mm). UC IPM: about 3/4 inch; Penn State and Colorado State: about 5/8 inch;
  UF/IFAS: ringlegged earwig 1/2 to 2/3 inch.
- Orkin: earwigs mainly eat live sprouts and decaying plants. Colorado State: their main food is insects, including
  aphids; UC IPM: important predators of aphids.
- Orkin: "earwig larvae". UC IPM: young earwigs are nymphs that look like small adults without wings.
- Orkin: pinches "rarely break skin" (unsourced). UC IPM: they can bite if trapped in clothing or sat upon;
  Colorado State: a mildly painful bite when sat upon or handled.
- NCAP: 20-50 eggs. Sources differ (UC IPM: 30 or more; Penn State: 30 to 55 in the first batch; Wisconsin: 50 to
  90), so our page gives the range by source rather than one number.
