# Beat the top 3: insect-eggs ("insect eggs identification", "bug eggs")

Read Nov 19, 2026 (WebSearch, US). Page 1 is pest-company blogs (Mosquito Joe, J.C. Ehrlich), a garden
blog (Growing in the Garden), content farms (brainbound, brainvoyage, scicentric, kidadl, eHow), forensic
research abstracts (MDPI, OJP), Scribd, BugGuide single photos and one university card set (NMSU "Whose
Eggs Are These", six garden eggs, no sizes). Skipped: farms, abstracts, Scribd, BugGuide. Taken apart below.

## 1. Mosquito Joe, "A Guide to Insect Egg Identification" (2022)
- ~600 words. H2s: why care; common characteristics (color, shape, size, arrangement, texture); white
  eggs (whiteflies, cutworms); yellow eggs (aphids, spider mites); "the verdict". One image, empty alt.
  No table, no FAQ, no sources, nothing organized by where the eggs were found.
- Wrong or loose: "Malaysian stick insect egg ... half an inch long and bigger than ... a peanut" contradicts
  itself; aphid eggs called bright yellow and whitefly egg counts given with no source (not checked by us,
  so our page says nothing about either); "wasp eggs about the size of a sand grain", no measurement anywhere.
- No indoor eggs at all (bed bug, flea, lice, moth, cockroach).

## 2. Growing in the Garden, "How to Identify Beneficial Insect Eggs in Your Garden" (June 2026)
- ~2,000 words, about 21 real photos by the author (finger and rice-grain scale in two). H2s: four traits
  (color, shape, pattern, location); ladybug, lacewing, assassin bug, hoverfly and parasitic wasp eggs; six
  FAQs. Pest look-alikes: squash, stink and leaf-footed bugs, potato and bean beetles.
- No sizes for ladybug or lacewing eggs; no sources; no table. Garden only: nothing indoors, on screens,
  siding or cars. "There are no pest eggs that look similar" (lacewings) is unsourced.
- Hoverfly eggs "the size of a grain of rice" looks too big; no source opened, so our page leaves them out.

## 3. J.C. Ehrlich (Rentokil), "Types of insect eggs: an identification guide"
- ~1,000 words of article (2,200 with menus); 7 stock photos (Getty/Deposit), no scale, no where-found
  shots. H2s: why care; characteristics; fly, bed bug, mosquito, termite, cockroach, flea and spider eggs;
  white and yellow eggs; tips; every section ends in a call to book an inspection. No sources, no FAQ.
- Wrong: mosquito eggs "laid on the surface of water in batches ... up to 300", for all mosquitoes. CDC
  (About Culex; Life Cycle of Aedes) says only Culex makes floating rafts (100 to 300 eggs); Aedes glue
  single eggs to container walls above the waterline. Fly eggs "in large batches ranging from 120 - 1000";
  Virginia Cooperative Extension says a house fly lays up to 100 in a cluster. Bed bugs lay "batches of
  10-50 eggs"; EPA says 1 to 3 eggs a day. "Termite eggs are nearly microscopic" and "visible to the naked
  eye" on the same page. "Mosquitoes can bite and sting."

## What searchers ask
- Plan and picker: bug eggs; what bug eggs look like; look like seeds / sesame seeds / rice; on window
  screen; tiny white eggs in house; on clothes; on car; insect eggs identification guide pictures.
- "People also ask" could not be read with the search tool. FAQs come from those variants, Ask Extension
  threads (eggs on screens, glass, wood) and what the three answer: can you see them, how long to hatch,
  should you destroy them, which are beneficial, can they survive winter.

## Things our page does that none of the three do
1. Sorts by WHERE the eggs were found (bed, pet bed, hair, closet, kitchen, screens and glass, siding,
   leaves and twigs, car and patio gear, standing water) with a table, then hands off to our seven
   single-species egg pages. None of the three is organized by place; none covers screens, siding or cars.
2. A quick-ID table with sourced sizes in inches for every row (EPA, CDC DPDx, UC IPM, Clemson, Virginia
   Tech, Penn State, K-State, USDA APHIS); the three give almost no measurements.
3. Real, credited photos where people find the eggs: mystery eggs on a porch screen, lacewing eggs on window
   glass beside a penny, katydid eggs on a twig, a mantis egg case in brick mortar, bed bug eggs in a seam,
   spotted lanternfly masses on bark. Ehrlich's are stock; Mosquito Joe has one unlabeled image.
4. The "looks like rice / seeds / sesame" answers nobody gives: house fly egg clusters, dried pet tapeworm
   segments (CDC: like grains of rice), katydid eggs (UC IPM: disklike, gray, overlapping rows), old
   spotted lanternfly masses (Virginia Tech: seed-like eggs), and that a moving sesame-seed-sized thing in
   hair is an adult louse (CDC).
5. Things that are not eggs at all: slime mold spore cases (WSU: often mistaken for insect eggs), drywood
   termite pellets (UC IPM: six-sided), droppings, with links.
6. What to do per egg, at the source's strength: leave predator eggs (UC IPM natural enemies), scrape
   spotted lanternfly masses into alcohol and check vehicles (APHIS), empty and scrub water holders weekly
   (CDC), and a mantis case brought indoors hatches in 4 to 6 weeks (K-State).
