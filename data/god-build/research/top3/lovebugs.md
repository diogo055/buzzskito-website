## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Freeport (from UF Gardening Solutions): first identified in southern Louisiana in the 1920s, reaching Florida in 1947.
  UF/IFAS Featured Creatures EENY-47/IN204: described in 1940 from Galveston, Texas; first collected in Florida in 1949
  (Escambia County). Living with Lovebugs (IN694) also gives 1949.
- Wikipedia: copulation lasts 2 to 3 days. IN694: sperm transfer averages 12.5 hours, though pairs may stay coupled for
  several days while they feed and disperse.
- Wikipedia: insect eaters avoid lovebugs "due to their acidic taste" (no citation). IN694: their warning coloration
  suggests a defense, but they have not been chemically analyzed or tested as food for predators.
- Wikipedia: remains get extremely hard to remove after an hour or two (no citation). IN694: no permanent damage if
  cleaned before the coating bakes in the sun for a day or two; paint coated for 21 days in humid indoor air was not
  damaged. IN204: soak about five minutes, scrub within 15 to 20 minutes.
- Also read: Clemson HGIC (#5 on Oct 26) says over 200 kinds of March flies live worldwide and only two in the US. IN204:
  over 200 species in the genus Plecia, two of them in the US; the US also has other March flies (Dilophus sayi).
