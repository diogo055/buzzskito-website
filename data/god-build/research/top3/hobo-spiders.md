## The three (plus two), taken apart
- **Wikipedia (#1)**: about 650 words of prose. One photo (an animated GIF of a female, no scale, no setting) and a
  North America range map with no state list; no table, no FAQ. ID covered in the abstract (no leg bands, chevrons,
  sternum stripe vs spots, no distinct stripes, microscope for certainty). Says "as of 2017, the CDC no longer lists" the
  hobo spider, citing a CDC page dated after its own retrieval date. Nothing on giant house spider tells, season, where
  people meet it or what to do.
- **UC IPM Pest Note 7488 (#2)**: about 1,500 words, R. S. Vetter, updated Nov 2015. Six figures (a hobo spider, its web
  under driftwood, a typical funnel web labeled NOT a hobo web, two California look-alikes, an eye diagram). Written for
  California: it does not live there, "no need for control". Strong on the bite evidence (2001 venom study, 2004 review,
  2014 Oregon bite) and the eye-row check; no leg or chevron tells, no season, no where-found photo, no table, no FAQ.
- **Utah State University Extension (#3)**: about 2,000 words, April 2023. Twelve figures, mostly microscope shots
  (palps, setae, teeth) and look-alikes. Strong on ID (chevrons, no dark leg bands, sizes), life cycle (males Aug-Oct),
  habitat, management. Range given for Utah only; the giant house spider gets one sentence; no table, no FAQ.
- **Animal Corner** (WebSearch #2): about 3,000 words, a quiz widget, one photo and drawn diagrams (anatomy, a
  four-spider comparison), 10 FAQs. Cites an unlinked "2017 CDC reassessment"; says the CDC removed the hobo spider.
- **PMP, mypmp.net** (WebSearch #3): about 480 words, dated Nov 2001. Calls the hobo "medically important", with
  "serious tissue damage and systemic illness", welts up to six inches and scars lasting months. No sources.

## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Wikipedia, Animal Corner and the social posts: CDC "no longer lists" or "removed" it, with no checkable CDC page. Ours
  says only what CDC's current page shows: it names the black widow and the brown recluse (reviewed Sep 28, 2026).
  (University of Idaho CIS 1221, 2016, also says CDC removed it in 2015; no archived CDC page was opened, so per the
  brief that stays off the page.)
- PMP: medically important, serious tissue damage. UC IPM: not considered of medical importance, necrotic reports
  erroneous; WSU: not shown harmful; McKeown et al. 2014 (PubMed 24726469): 33 verified Oregon bites, none serious; the
  one hobo bite gave pain, redness and calf twitching that resolved in 12 h.
- Animal Corner: hobo and brown recluse ranges overlap in the West. Penn State lists 16 established recluse states, none
  in the hobo range UC IPM gives; CDC puts the recluse in the Midwest and South.
- Wikipedia: body 7-14 mm. USU: females 9.5-16.5 mm (3/8-5/8 in.), males 7-13.5 mm; UC IPM about 1/2-5/8 inch.
