## What they get wrong (and the source that says otherwise)

- Kidadl calls chiggers insects and says they feed on blood. They are mite larvae with six legs (UF/IFAS ENY-212; MedlinePlus 001333) and do not suck blood: they feed on dissolved skin tissue through a stylostome (Missouri G7398, UMD, Ohio State ANR-0182). Kidadl also says chiggers stay on the body about four days; UMD says up to 3 days and Ohio State says most are knocked off long before they finish.
- PestWorld says chiggers "only feed for a short period": UMD says up to 3 days, Missouri three or four days if undisturbed.
- Connor's gives flea bites a 1 to 2 week duration; Cleveland Clinic says flea bites typically last a few days, UC IPM says itching can last up to 5 days (allergic reactions a week or more, Texas A&M ENTO-PU-046). It also puts chigger itch at 1 to 3 hours after attachment; UMD says 3 to 6 hours, UF 4 to 8 hours.
- Native Pest gives a flea jump of 8 to 10 inches (Cleveland Clinic: over 12 inches) and says chigger bites carry no disease, full stop; UMD says not known to transmit infectious diseases in the US, and CDC PHIL notes chiggers can carry scrub typhus in parts of Asia.
- All of them present one pattern per bite (chiggers clustered, fleas in lines of three) as decisive. Cleveland Clinic says flea bites come in a straight line or a cluster, and MedlinePlus lists the waist among flea-bite sites, so neither pattern nor a waistband settles it alone (UC IPM 7443: a firm answer needs the biter).
