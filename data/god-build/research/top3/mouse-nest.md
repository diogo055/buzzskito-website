# Beat the top 3: mouse-nest ("what does a mouse nest look like", "mouse nest")

Read Nov 19, 2026 (WebSearch, US). Page 1 is all pest-company and content pages: A-Z Animals (two URLs,
one article), Colonial Pest Control (several URLs, one Q&A), Mosquito Squad FAQ, Orkin (two URLs), Terminix
(403 to WebFetch), Fantastic Pest Control (UK), thedonutwhole (content farm) and a spam mirror. No .gov or
.edu page ranks. Skipped: the UK page, the content farm, the mirrors. Taken apart: the three below.

## 1. A-Z Animals, "Mouse Nest: What Do They Look Like And What To Do If You Find One?" (Aug 2024)
- ~1,450 words. H2s: why mice build nests; where in your home; what it looks like; what to do; mouse vs rat
  scat. Six stock images (Shutterstock, iStock); the only nest is a grasshopper mouse litter. No house mouse
  nest, no scale object. Says nests are round, 3 to 6 inches. One citation (ScienceDirect). No table, no FAQ.
- Wrong: "Mouse pellets ... 1/4 to 1/2 inches" (Nebraska Extension G1105 and Cornell IPM: 1/8 to 1/4 inch;
  Cornell puts roof rat droppings at about 1/2 inch). "Mice poop 1-2 times a day" beside its own "80-160
  droppings in a single day" (Nebraska: about 50 to 75 pellets a day).
- Unsafe: "pick up the nest, and put it in a bucket" and "Burn the nest", no disinfecting. CDC: spray the
  nest with disinfectant, soak 5 minutes, double-bag it; never sweep or vacuum nesting material.
- Nothing on rat nests, nothing on telling an active nest from an old one, nothing on cars.

## 2. Colonial Pest Control, "Could You Recognize a Mouse Nest?" (Q&A)
- ~600 words, no photos, no sources, no health or cleanup advice; ends in a sales call.
- Nest "about the size of a grapefruit"; mice "rarely travel more than 30 feet" (UC IPM agrees: 30 feet);
  droppings about 1/4 inch; fresh droppings shiny and soft, old ones dull and brittle (CDC's survey manual
  says the same in its own terms).

## 3. Orkin, "Mouse Nest" (what-is-a-mouse-nest)
- ~450 words; one drawing of a mouse, no nest photo, no sources. Dome-shaped "with one exit hole"; lists
  drawers, wall voids, subfloor cabinets, false ceilings, refrigerator motor compartments, garage clutter.
- No size, no rat comparison, no active vs old test, no cars or attics, no health advice.

## Things our page does that none of the three do
1. Real, credited photos of real mouse nests where people find them: desk drawer (NPS), shed corner,
   shredded paper on a shelf, insulation pulled from a building, car engine, a grill nest beside an inch
   ruler, a research-grade house mouse nest in a driveway, CDC's deer mouse nest. The three have no real
   house mouse nest photo between them.
2. A quick-ID table with sourced sizes: mouse nest ball 4 to 6 inches (Arkansas Extension MP546) against
   rat, squirrel (12 to 20 inches, Missouri Department of Conservation), vole, yellowjacket and bumble bee
   nests; droppings, holes and gnaw marks from Nebraska, Cornell, Arkansas and UC IPM.
3. Active vs old and rat vs mouse tests from CDC's rodent survey manual (fresh droppings dark and soft, old
   hard, gray and brittle; fresh gnawing light colored), Arkansas (fresh shavings or chewed insulation) and
   UC IPM (rat tooth marks 1/6 inch vs 1/12 inch).
4. Car nests from CDC and Public Health - Seattle & King County (battery and frame, wiper motors, air filter,
   ducting to the cabin), CDC-correct cleanup in brief with the steps linked, and deer mouse hantavirus and
   house mouse LCM at CDC's own strength.
5. Outdoor look-alikes nobody mentions: vole grass nests (UC IPM), bumble bees in old rodent dens
   (University of Minnesota) and yellowjackets in rodent burrows (UC IPM).
