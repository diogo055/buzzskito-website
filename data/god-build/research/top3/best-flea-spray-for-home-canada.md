## Sources opened (Oct 23, 2026)
- NPIC methoprene fact sheet http://npic.orst.edu/factsheets/methogen.html (reviewed July 2012): "By acting like an
  insect hormone, it interferes with insect growth and development"; "Sunlight and micro-organisms break down
  methoprene rapidly in soil, water, and on plants"; "commonly found in flea treatments for dogs and cats".
- NPIC pyriproxyfen fact sheet https://npic.orst.edu/factsheets/pyriprogen.html (reviewed Dec 2015): mimics a
  natural insect hormone; "rarely toxic to adult insects"; affects fleas; "rapidly broken down by sunlight and soil
  microbes".
- NPIC permethrin fact sheet http://npic.orst.edu/factsheets/PermGen.html (reviewed July 2009): "affects the nervous
  system in insects, causing muscle spasms, paralysis and death"; "Cats are more sensitive to permethrin than dogs or
  people".
- NPIC pyrethrins fact sheet http://npic.orst.edu/factsheets/pyrethrins.html (reviewed Nov 2014): from chrysanthemum
  flowers; "excite the nervous system of insects"; Pyrethrin I half-lives of 11.8 hours in water and 12.9 hours on
  soil in sunlight.
- **PMRA label search** (https://pr-rp.hc-sc.gc.ca/ls-re/index-eng.php) returned HTTP 503 three times, so **no PCP
  number or product registration is stated in the new copy.** Retailer listings give Zodiac Premise Plus as
  (S)-methoprene 0.01% + permethrin 0.25% and a Greenhawk listing gives Zodiac Premise 1000 as PCP 25738: not
  primary, not used. Worth a PMRA check later: a short "IGR home sprays registered in Canada" table with PCP numbers
  would be the strongest possible addition for "flea spray for house canada".
