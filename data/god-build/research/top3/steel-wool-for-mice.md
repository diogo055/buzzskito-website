## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- HowStuffWorks: alone, mice "pull it out or chew through it". UC IPM: coarse steel wool mice can't chew through. Pulling out
  is the risk the CDC designs for (caulk "to keep it in place").
- HowStuffWorks: copper wool means skipping the caulk. No source reviewed says so; Nebraska says wedge copper or stainless
  wool in tightly, and the CDC puts caulk around the wool.
- HowStuffWorks calls #0 to #2 "coarse". Not checked: the only extension grade chart found (Utah State Extension, wood
  finishing fact sheet 6) returned 403 to WebFetch. Our page gives no grade number, only "coarse", as UC IPM and Nebraska do.
- EKKSOL: "cannot be gnawed through", "permanent". Nebraska: proofing is no guarantee; UC IPM, Missouri, Nebraska: temporary.
- EKKSOL, Western: no caulk step. CDC: caulk around the steel wool, or spray foam.
- HowStuffWorks: peppermint oil as a deterrent. Our peppermint page covers the evidence; a scent does not close a hole.
