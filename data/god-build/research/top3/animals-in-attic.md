## What our page does that none of the three do
1. A sourced quick-ID table (when you hear it, how it gets in, the sign that confirms it) for 7 animals, every cell from
   UGA Extension, UC IPM, Penn State, Nebraska and UNL Extension, Cornell IPM, CDC or the USFWS bat manual.
2. Real, credited photos of the animals where people meet them: a raccoon on a house chimney at night, a flying squirrel on
   a chimney inside an attic (trail camera), a roof rat in rafters, bat droppings stuck to siding.
3. Health facts at CDC's strength, linked: bats and rabies, histoplasmosis from droppings, rodent cleanup (no sweeping or
   vacuuming), raccoon roundworm in attic latrines, hantavirus and the deer mouse. Breda links only CDC cleanup.
4. Bats and the law: CDC (do not trap young bats inside, May to August), New Jersey (no exclusion May 1-July 31) and
   Wisconsin (June 1-August 15) show the rules vary by state; Canada lists three bats as endangered. None of the three
   mention it.
5. Do not seal an animal in: wait until it leaves (Minnesota DNR, UNL, Penn State). The two wildlife firms sell removal.
6. Ten FAQs in the searchers' words (People also ask below).

## What they get wrong or leave unsourced
- Critter Control: "At 20 pounds or more ... highly likely you are hearing a raccoon." UNL Extension (G1688): adult raccoons
  usually weigh 7 to 35 pounds, so a raccoon can be well under 20.
- Critter Control: an entry hole "the size of a dime" is enough for bats. CDC: bats fit through openings as small as 1/4 inch.
- Wildlife Company: a residential bat colony averages "about 20" (no source); the USFWS manual gives big brown bat
  colonies of 12 to 200, little brown bat colonies up to 2,000.
- Vertex: home-remedy claim with no source (our page gives no repellent advice).
