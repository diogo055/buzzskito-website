## The three taken apart
1. pestsguides.com (Google #1): ~1,300 words; H2s: quick answer, beetles, true bugs, is it dangerous, why in
   house/garden, what to do, "Final thoughts", 6 FAQs. 3 uncredited images of unknown origin. One safety
   table (insect type / bites or stings / main risk). No sources at all, and a stray link to an unrelated site.
   No sizes beyond "under half an inch". No flies, no fly test, nothing on where each one turns up.
2. leafyplace.com (WebSearch #1): ~2,700 words, 14 species sections with one uncredited photo each, no
   table, no FAQ, no sources. Half the list is not North American (yellow man-faced stink bug, an Australian
   rose chafer, the crucifer shield bug). Hover fly gets one paragraph; no sting column; no first aid.
3. friendsofthebluehills.org (Google #5): ~950 words, 13 bolded entries (bees, wasps, hover fly, robber fly,
   a swallowtail), 13 photos, 7 credited. No sources, no sizes, no table, no headings a searcher types.

## What our page does that none of them do
- Sorts by where the bug was seen (food and trash, house and deck, lawn, flowers, vegetable garden, a web,
  indoors), with the one check for each place, plus a season calendar of when each one turns up.
- The fly-or-bee test from NDSU E1914 (two wings vs four, eyes that fill the head, short antennae, hovering),
  shown in a real photo of a drone fly beside a bumble bee on the same flower; "flies that look like bees"
  gets its own section (hover, drone, bee and robber flies; clearwing moths).
- A 12-row quick-ID table with sourced sizes in inches and a "stings or bites?" column; male carpenter bees,
  male cicada killers and all the flies marked as unable to sting, robber flies as possible biters.
- Sting first aid and the 911 list from MedlinePlus; 20 government, university and museum sources.
- Real, credited photos where people meet the bugs: a yellowjacket in a cup of soda, a mud dauber on a house,
  a cicada killer in a lawn, a locust borer and a hover fly on goldenrod.

## What they get wrong (and the source that says otherwise)
- pestsguides lists mud daubers with a "painful sting" risk; MDC (mud daubers) says they do not aggressively
  sting people and females sting only if mishandled.
- pestsguides calls the four-lined plant bug black with a yellow stripe; UConn Extension describes four black
  stripes on yellow to yellow-green wing covers. It calls the spotted cucumber beetle yellow-spotted; the
  Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station describes it as pale green with 12 black dots.
- leafyplace puts the European hornet at 38-50 mm (1.5-2 inches); Penn State gives workers up to an inch and
  queens about 1.3 inches.
- friendsofthebluehills says the eastern carpenter bee does not sting; MU Extension (G7424) says males cannot
  but females can if handled.
- None of the three says that flies cannot sting or explains how to tell one (NDSU E1914; Purdue says hover
  flies cannot bite either).
