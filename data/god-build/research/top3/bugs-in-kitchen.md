## The three taken apart

- **PestWorld** (~1,300 words): h3 per pest (ants, cockroaches, rodents, flies, pantry pests), then why bugs come to kitchens, prevention, threats, how to get rid of them, "find a pest pro". 4-5 iStock photos, no captions or credits, no table, no FAQ, no sources cited. Gets sizes wrong: ants "usually between 1/3 and 1/2 inch" (UC IPM: odorous house and Argentine ants 1/8 inch, pavement 3/16, pharaoh 1/16, thief 1/32; only carpenter ants reach 1/4 to 1/2), cockroaches "usually between 2/3 and 3 inches" (UC IPM: the German cockroach, the most common indoor species, is about 1/2 inch). Fly pathogen count uncited.
- **Orkin pest library** (~150 words of body): 59 drawn pest cards with names only; no kitchen content, sizes, places or sources; quote buttons; card errors (whitefly card carries the cigarette beetle's scientific name).
- **Dr. Killigan's** (~1,800 words): numbered sections for ants, cockroaches, house flies, fruit flies, drain flies, pantry moths; sizes in mm only; filenames suggest drawn graphics, not photos; no table, no FAQ, no sources; product pitches in every section; "non-toxic" and "100% safe" claims; essential oils as repellents with no evidence; a salt, baking soda and vinegar drain-fly recipe said to "kill all flies". UC IPM says boiling water and bleach do not remove the drain film; scrubbing with a stiff brush does.
- Also seen: Google's AI answer for "Why am I getting little bugs in my kitchen?" tells readers to flush drains with boiling water or vinegar (same correction). Bob Vila gives booklice as "about 1/2 to 3/4 inch" (Missouri Extension: about pinhead size; Penn State: 1 to 2 mm) and suggests bay leaves and peppermint as deterrents (UMN: no evidence bay leaves keep pantry pests away). All stock photos, no sources.
