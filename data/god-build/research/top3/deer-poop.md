## Top 3 taken apart
- **Field & Stream:** seven "varieties" read for hunting (moist = 6-12 hours old, "large pellets = mature buck"); no sizes
  sourced, no rabbit comparison, nothing on health, dogs, gardens or cleanup.
- **Grand View Outdoors:** sizes by finger ("end of your pinkie"); deer vs rabbit, elk, moose in one paragraph; no sources.
- **Outdoor Life:** the strongest: sizes (deer .2-.5 inch, citing Maryland DNR), look-alikes (rabbit, bear, pronghorn, elk,
  moose), buck vs doe myth, FAQ (harmful? where? fertilizer?), USDA manure interval via NC State. No table, no scale photo of
  deer pellets, no rodent look-alikes.

## Gaps (what our page adds)
1. **A quick-ID table with sourced sizes** for deer, rabbit, squirrel, Norway rat, raccoon and coyote (Wildlife Illinois
   from the Illinois DNR, UF/IFAS, MDC, UC IPM, New York State Parks, ICWDM), and an h2 owning "deer poop vs rabbit poop".
2. **Real, credited photos where people find droppings**: NPS photo of white-tailed deer pellets on snow (public domain lead),
   cottontail pellets in pine litter, gray squirrel pellets on a porch ledge, plus cards with a ruler and a thumb for scale.
   None of the three shows deer pellets beside a scale object; Outdoor Life's only scale is a boot toe.
3. **The homeowner's questions the hunting pages skip**, each from an agency: E. coli O157:H7 (the 2011 Oregon strawberry
   outbreak traced to deer feces, Clinical Infectious Diseases), chronic wasting disease (CDC: prions in feces; no human
   infections reported), dogs (Cornell's vet college on coccidia from other animals' feces), garden produce (FDA produce
   rule names deer; FDA washing tips; Penn State's USDA manure intervals), handwashing (CDC), deer fences (NC State, CDC).

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Outdoor Life quotes NDA's Matt Ross that deer droppings are "not a concern for pets or gardens". The Oregon outbreak (15 ill,
  2 deaths; all positive farm samples contained deer feces) and FDA's produce rule, which names deer, say otherwise; CDC says
  animals can carry E. coli O157:H7 while looking healthy.
- Field & Stream sizes pellets at 1/2 to 3/4 inch and up with no source, and Outdoor Life gives .2-.5 inch from Maryland DNR;
  the Illinois DNR's Wildlife Illinois gives 1/2 to 1 inch long. The page gives Illinois' figure with its source (the
  Maryland PDF could not be read with WebFetch).
