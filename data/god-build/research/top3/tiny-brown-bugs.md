# Beat the top 3: tiny-brown-bugs ("tiny brown bugs in house")

Read 2026-10-20. Order from the spec's `serp` (Google, Oct 16): Orkin, UMD Extension, Reddit, Bob Vila, Facebook, YouTube (Clean My Space), Quora.
Skipped: Reddit, Facebook, YouTube, Quora (forum threads and videos; people posting "what is this tiny brown bug?" photos is itself the finding: they want a photo match by place).
"People also ask" was on the page on Oct 16 but its questions were not recorded, and I could not open Google today (built-in browser at its tab cap). FAQs come from the spec's variants, the competitors' questions and the bite question.

## The three, taken apart
1. **Orkin, "What are these small, round, brown bugs in my house?"** About 300 words of answer, no subheadings. One bug only (furniture carpet beetle), "a little larger than the head of a pin". One thumbnail illustration, no photo, no scale. No table, no FAQ, no sources. The same answer is reused for five other people's different bugs. Ends in an inspection pitch.
2. **UMD Extension, "Identifying Common Household Insect Pests"** (the UMD page that matches; the spec did not record the URL). About 2,500 words: a key sorted by flying, jumping, crawling, with sizes for most entries. One photo (Indian meal moth). No FAQ, no photos of the brown crawlers, no "where in the house" sorting beyond a few words per entry.
3. **Bob Vila, "14 Common Tiny Brown Bugs in the House"** (updated Jun 30, 2025). About 2,300 words, a flat numbered list of 14, each with a how-to-get-rid-of list. About 16 images, mostly stock; no scale objects. No table, no FAQ. Sources: one pest-company entomologist, Mayo Clinic, NPIC. Includes stink bugs, spiders and silverfish, which are not tiny or not brown.

## What our page does that none of them do
1. **A quick-ID table right under the answer**: one row per suspect with a sourced size in inches, where it turns up and the one tell. None has a table; Bob Vila gives sizes for 9 of 14, Orkin for one.
2. **Real, credited photos of the actual species, two of them where people find them** (a bed bug nymph on fabric, a rice weevil on skin) plus scale bars on the drugstore beetle and flea photos. Orkin has one drawing, Bob Vila mostly stock, UMD no photo of a brown crawler.
3. **Sorted by where you found it** (kitchen and pantry, bed, closets and rugs, bathroom and damp rooms, pets) with a jar checklist: Bob Vila is an unsorted list and UMD sorts only by movement.
4. **The bite question answered by source**: bed bugs, fleas and brown dog ticks bite; carpet beetle larvae cause a rash without biting (Penn State); booklice do not bite (Penn State).
5. "Commonly confused with" photo cards and 8-10 FAQs; none of the three has an FAQ.

## What they get wrong (our page states it from the source)
- Bob Vila: clover mites "3/4 cm". Penn State Extension: adult clover mites are 0.75 mm (about 1/32 inch).
- Bob Vila: cockroaches "1/4 to 1/2 inch" as the adult size. UC IPM: German and brownbanded adults are 0.5 inch; small ones are nymphs, which have no wings.
- Bob Vila lists stink bugs as tiny brown bugs. UMD Extension: brown marmorated stink bug adults are 14 to 17 mm (over 1/2 inch). Left off this page.
- UMD's key gives bed bugs as 1/4 to 3/8 inch before feeding; EPA gives 3/16 to 1/4 inch (5 to 7 mm, apple-seed size). Our page uses EPA.
- Orkin: one carpet beetle answer for every "brown bug" question. Our page sorts by place first.

## Sources used (all opened Oct 20)
On the page (15): UC IPM pantry pests, carpet beetles, fleas, cockroaches, ants; Univ. of Kentucky ENTFACT-612; UF/IFAS EDIS IN128 (German cockroach); Penn State booklice and carpet beetle dermatitis; UMD bed bugs; EPA bed bug appearance; CDC ticks on pets and tick lifecycles; Texas A&M indoor flies; Colorado State spider beetles. Also read, not cited: UMD booklice and household key (the key is the top-3 page), Penn State clover mites (for the Bob Vila error).
Notes: Penn State's booklice page prints "0.4-0.8 in" for 1-2 mm (should be about 0.04-0.08 in); Colorado State prints 1/16-1/8 inch for 2-4 mm (closer to 1/12-1/6 inch). Our page gives the mm and the correct inch figure.
