# Beat the top 3: "bear poop" (Nov 9, 2026)

Target: "bear poop" (US 10K-100K, CA 1K-10K a month); same page for "bear scat", "bear droppings", "black bear poop",
"bear poop vs dog poop", "bear scat with berries". Audience "na". Writer research only; facts below come from the
government and university sources linked on the page, never from these three.

## The three
1. **Field & Stream, "What Does Bear Poop Look Like?"** (~1,150 words). H2s: shape, size, color and content, scent,
   black vs brown, "fun fact" (fecal plug). Two Adobe Stock photos plus a hero; no scale object. No table, no FAQ.
   Sources: news stories and bear.org only. Sizes (5-12 inches long, 1-2 inches across) unsourced.
2. **NPS Yosemite blog** (~340 words, Oct 2019). Spring scat green and tubular with grass; late summer and fall looser
   plops with berries and apple pieces; coyote twisted rope, raccoon latrines, cat scat segmented; all smaller than
   a bear's. Four photos, no scale, no credits. No sizes, no safety advice, nothing on garbage or homes.
3. **Outdoor Life** (~2,400 words, May 2024). Sections by diet (vegetation, berry, insect, meat, fish, garbage),
   grizzly vs black, other animals, 4 FAQs. 11 photos: 4 from a Montana FWP biologist, 5 stock, 2 uncredited; two
   "scat" sections show bears, not scat; no scale object anywhere. Expert quotes, no numbers or citations.

## What our page does that none of them do
1. Real, credited, licence-checked photos with a scale object (pen, lens cap, fingers, 16-inch paver) and photos
   where homeowners find it (backyard paver, beside eaten hummingbird feeders, plastic-filled scat in an alley).
2. A 12-row quick-ID table with sourced sizes: NY DEC (over an inch thick), MDC (a plop up to about 1 lb), IGBC
   (grizzly about 2 inches), Iowa DNR (wolf over 1 inch, coyote under 1 inch, dog full of cereal matter, no hair),
   ICWDM (raccoon 2-3 x 1/2 inch; deer pellets clump in summer).
3. A season table from Minnesota DNR and USDA APHIS (grass, ants in June, berries, acorns and hazelnuts), not
   anecdotes.
4. "Why is there bear poop in my yard?": APHIS attractant list (69% of agencies name garbage), BearWise basics and
   feeder calories, and MDC's case where a big plastic-filled pile by tipped cans turned out to be coyotes.
5. Health and handling from APHIS (disease spread unlikely) and CDC (handwashing, stream water, bites), plus
   hibernation (APHIS, NPS Katmai) and 10 FAQs.

## What they get wrong (and the source that says otherwise)
- Field & Stream says black and grizzly ranges overlap only in Wyoming, Idaho and Washington; APHIS (2020) says parts of
  Wyoming, Montana, Idaho and Washington.
- Field & Stream's fecal-plug explanation is muddled; APHIS and NPS Katmai say simply that bears do not eat, drink or
  defecate in the den (Katmai: they recycle waste).
- Field & Stream and Outdoor Life give sizes or freshness timings with no source; NY DEC, MDC and IGBC give sourced
  ones, and IGBC and the Forest Service say size cannot settle black vs grizzly (Forest Service: only DNA).
- Yosemite and Field & Stream give no safety or handling advice at all.
