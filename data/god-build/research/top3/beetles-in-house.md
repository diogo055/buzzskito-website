## The three, taken apart
1. **UMN Extension, "Ground beetles"** (reviewed 2024). About 740 words. One beetle group only: size 1/8 to 1/2 inch, black or brown, enter homes mid to late summer, harmless, do not reproduce indoors, vacuum or sticky traps. Three photos of ground beetles with "actual size" captions. No table, no FAQ. Accurate, but useless if the beetle is not a ground beetle.
2. **bettertermite.com, "Little Black Beetles in House"** (Jul 3, 2025). About 1,200 words, five beetles in tabs (black carpet, flour, spider, ground, powderpost), DIY methods, 8 FAQs, a named technician. About 8 images, mostly staff and stock; a "size reference" photo with no scale object. Cites Penn State and UMN. Washington DC-area framing.
3. **Orkin, "Black Carpet Beetle Facts & Information"** (title targets "black beetles"). About 330 words, one species, one illustration, no sources, no table, no FAQ; the "how do I get rid of" section is a sales pitch.

## What our page does that none of them do
1. **Covers every beetle a homeowner meets indoors, of every size**, in one quick-ID table with a sourced size, where and when it shows up, and the one tell: carpet beetles, pantry beetles, weevils, spider and larder beetles, lady beetles, ground beetles, May beetles, wood borers. The three cover 1 to 5.
2. **Real, credited photos, three of them where people find them** (a black carpet beetle found indoors, lady beetles massed on a door frame, a May beetle on a fingertip) plus a scale-bar photo of a carpet beetle. Orkin has one drawing; bettertermite mostly staff and stock photos.
3. **"Is it a beetle at all?"** A sourced test (hard wing covers that meet in a straight line, MDC) and cards for the non-beetles people call beetles: cockroach nymphs, the Oriental cockroach UC IPM says is sometimes called a black beetle, bed bugs, stink bugs.
4. **Wood-boring "furniture beetles" with the two quick tests from UC IPM** (ballpoint pen in the exit hole, rub the dust between your fingers) and the hole sizes from Penn State.
5. Sorted by where and when (windowsills, pantry, walls in fall, basement floors in summer, wood), with a jar checklist and 8-10 FAQs.

## What they get wrong (our page states it from the source)
- bettertermite attributes "second only to termites" for powderpost damage to Penn State; Penn State's powderpost beetle page (read today) says nothing of the kind. Our page makes no ranking claim.
- bettertermite's intro calls the small ones "under 1/8 inch" but lists black carpet beetles at 3-5 mm and powderpost beetles at 2-6 mm. UC IPM: black carpet beetle 1/8 to 3/16 inch; Penn State: powderpost beetles usually under 1/4 inch.
- bettertermite gives powderpost exit holes as 1/32 to 1/8 inch; Penn State gives about 1/16 to 1/8 inch.
- Orkin gives the black carpet beetle as 2.7 mm in one place and 2.8 mm in another; no source. UC IPM: 1/8 to 3/16 inch.
- UMN is accurate but describes only ground beetles; nothing for the carpet or pantry beetles most readers have.
