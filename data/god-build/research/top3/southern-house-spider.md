## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Spam pages call it the southern black widow, *Parasteatoda tepidariorum* or family Caponiidae. UF: *Kukulcania
  hibernalis*, family Filistatidae; Penn State: *P. tepidariorum* is the common house spider.
- Bug Out: female body "0.5 to 1 inch". UF: 1/2 to 3/4 inch (13 to 19 mm). Bug Out: "venom is not toxic". UF IN1366:
  most spiders are venomous, few of concern to people; UF IN301: not known to have a dangerous bite.
- Wikipedia and the AI box: males "lack the violin". UF: a narrow brown stripe behind the eyes that narrows to a line;
  Vetter (UC Riverside): a slight darkening near the eyes that people mistake for a violin. Check the eyes instead.
- bettertermite: the recluse is "smooth" and "relatively hairless". Vetter 2022: recluse legs carry fine, flat hairs;
  the test is spines (copious on the crevice weaver, none on the recluse). Its "males live 1 to 2 years" is in no
  source we opened (left off our page), and its "University of Kentucky EF631 (Southern House Spider)" citation looks
  like UK's brown recluse sheet (ENT-631); UK has no southern house spider sheet that we could find.
- Wikipedia and the AI box: a 2-inch leg span with no source. Ours gives body lengths from UF only.
