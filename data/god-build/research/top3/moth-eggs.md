## What each answers
- **iNaturalist, "Commonly Observed Moth Eggs"** (project journal post, Aug 2023): a gallery of ~400 egg photos
  on host plants, grouped by moth family, worldwide (spongy moth, tent caterpillars, tussock moths and a bagworm
  among them). Says many moth eggs look alike and that the host plant, place and date matter. No sizes, no
  household moths (no clothes or pantry moths), no FAQ, no sources, mostly NC-licensed photos.
- **Better Termite, "What Do Pantry Moth Eggs Look Like?"** (Jul 1, 2025, ~1,200 words): pantry moths only.
  0.3-0.5 mm, pearly to gray-white, singly or in loose clusters on food and package seams; hatch about 7 days at
  70-80°F; freeze 0°F for 72 hours; heat 120-140°F for 20 minutes; 10 FAQs; ~9 images, mostly adult moths and
  company photos, one egg shot with no scale. Nothing on clothes moths or outdoor egg masses. Service pitch.
- **WhatsThatBug, "Moth Eggs: All You Need to Know"** (~1,400 words, undated): generic. Eggs "round, oval or
  teardrop", "small to medium"; gypsy moth masses 1-2 inches with 600-1,000 eggs; scrape into soapy water;
  clothes vs pantry moth table; mothballs pros and cons. No indoor egg sizes, no FAQ, almost no usable photos.

## What they get wrong (and the source that says otherwise)
- WhatsThatBug says moth traps focus on female moths; UC IPM and the University of Kentucky say pheromone traps
  attract and catch males.
- WhatsThatBug recommends freezing for 48 hours; UC IPM says several days below 18°F, and Kentucky says at least
  a week at 0°F (72 hours only in a freezer that reaches minus 20°F).
- Better Termite's 72 hours at 0°F is shorter than Kentucky's week at 0°F; Kentucky's 72 hours needs minus 20°F.
- WhatsThatBug presents mothballs as a general repellent; UC IPM says the vapors build up enough only in an
  airtight container, and Kentucky says never to scatter them in open closets.
- iNaturalist's post and WhatsThatBug use the old name "gypsy moth"; Penn State gives spongy moth (formerly gypsy moth).
- Better Termite's "width of a table-salt grain" is unsourced; UF gives 0.3-0.5 mm (UF's own inch conversion,
  0.12-0.15 in, is off by ten: 0.3-0.5 mm is about 0.012-0.02 inch, under 1/50 inch).
- None of the three says that spent spongy moth egg masses stay on trees for years without hatching (Penn State).

## Sources that disagree (stated on the page, not averaged)
- Clothes moth eggs per female: 40-50 (UC IPM, Kentucky) vs 100-150 (Clemson). Hatch: 4-10 days in warm
  weather (UC IPM, Penn State), about 5 days (Clemson), 3 weeks or more in winter (Penn State).
- Freezing clothes: several days below 18°F (UC IPM) vs at least a week at 0°F (Kentucky) vs one week (Clemson).
- Spongy moth eggs per mass: up to 1,000 (APHIS) vs often 600+ (Wisconsin).
- Bagworm hatch: late May through early June and about mid-June, both in UConn's own sheet.
