## The three, taken apart
- **Wikipedia, Cotinis nitida (#1)**: about 1,150 words on the green June beetle only. Five photos (a specimen, a dead
  beetle on a wall, a mating pair, a larva, fruit damage), none with scale; no table, no FAQ; about 12 references,
  three of them extension. Gives two sizes that disagree (15-22 mm, then 3/4 to 1.5 inches), carries "needs more
  citations" banners, never says whether it bites, and ignores the brown May/June beetles most people mean.
- **Britannica, June beetle (#2)**: about 300 words, one photo (a rugose June beetle on a leaf), no sources, no FAQ,
  no table. 0.5 to 1 inch, nearly 300 species, grubs feed "three years" before pupating; sends the green and ten-lined
  June beetles to another article. Nothing on bites, porch lights, home lawns or what to do.
- **Terminix (#3)**: about 450 words, one image, no FAQ, no table, two citations (Penn State, Live Science). About 100
  species; the common June bug 1/2 to 5/8 inch; grubs feed months to three years; never says whether they bite;
  names no species beyond Phyllophaga; ends in a service pitch.

## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Wikipedia: two sizes for the green June beetle. Texas A&M (3/4 to 1 inch), MDC (about 1 inch), NC State (nearly
  1 inch), UC IPM (3/5 to 9/10 inch in California). The page gives 3/4 to 1 inch with its sources.
- Britannica: grubs feed three years. UF/IFAS and MDC: 1 to 4 years by species (three common in the destructive
  ones); Purdue: 2 to 3 by latitude; Texas A&M: the main Texas lawn species take one year.
- Terminix: the common June bug is 1/2 to 5/8 inch. UF/IFAS, MDC and UW-Madison: 1/2 to 1 inch.
- Britannica and Terminix are silent on bites. MDC: May beetles cannot hurt people; Ohio State: green June beetles
  cannot bite, though their leg spurs can prick skin; NC State: harmless to humans.
- None says the beetles at lights are mostly males or that they do not predict lawn damage (Texas A&M ENTO-PU-016,
  Purdue E-271).
