## The three
- **Cleveland Clinic, Flea Bites** (about 1,700 words, reviewed 08/2021): 17 question H3s, one bite photo (an ankle),
  no table, no look-alike photos. Good on where (feet, calves, ankles, rarely above the knee), a ring or halo, a few
  days' duration, ice pack 10 minutes, see a provider for allergy. Lists aloe, chamomile tea, honey and oatmeal as
  home remedies with no source. No signs of infection listed. Cites CDC and Harrison's.
- **Healthline, Fleabites** (about 1,600 words): no bite photo in the text despite "Images" in the title; prose-only
  comparisons; "breakfast, lunch, and dinner" pattern stated as fact with no source; a long complication list
  (gangrene, pneumonia, sepsis) with no sense of how rare; "almost impossible to get rid of without pesticide
  treatment". No duration given. Sources: StatPearls, AKC, ASPCA, a UK pest trade body.
- **WebMD, How to Protect Yourself From Fleabites** (about 2,300 words): reviewed by veterinarians only, one stock
  photo, no bite photos; 4 FAQs. Says "most people are allergic to fleas' saliva" and later "many people"; home
  remedies (honey, aloe, chamomile) with no clinical source; "fleas don't bite through clothing" as an absolute;
  "mosquito bites swell more than fleabites" next to "large, swollen" allergic flea bites.

3. The confirm-it-is-fleas steps none of them give properly: UC IPM's white-sock test, Texas A&M's flea dirt and

   the one-week rash rule, and CDC's flea-borne disease facts with where they occur (typhus mostly southern
   California, Hawaii and southern Texas; plague about five cases a year in the West).

## What they get wrong, and what our page says instead
- WebMD says both "most people" and "many people" are allergic to flea saliva (Cleveland Clinic's flea page also
  says "most people have a flea bite allergy"). Our page avoids a share: UC IPM says bites usually cause minor
  itching, and Cleveland Clinic's papular urticaria page says one child in a family may react while others do not.
- Healthline's complication list without rates: CDC averages five US plague cases a year and says most typhus cases
  come from southern California, Hawaii and southern Texas; our page gives those numbers and places.
- Healthline "almost impossible to get rid of without pesticide": Texas A&M calls a flea comb and a good bath a pet's
  first line of defense, with vacuuming and washing bedding; control is left to how-to-get-rid-of-fleas.
- Cleveland Clinic and WebMD home remedies (honey, aloe, chamomile): not repeated. Our care is cooling,
  hydrocortisone or an oral antihistamine (MedlinePlus, AAD), clean broken skin, no scratching; no doses.
- "Breakfast, lunch and dinner": none of the medical sources opened use it; MedlinePlus says "often in groups of three".
