## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- PetMD gives the human flea as "around 4 milliliters long": wrong unit, and UF/IFAS gives adults an average of 2-3 mm
  (DPDx 1-4 mm). Size does not separate it from the cat flea (UF: 1-3 mm); the missing combs do (UF, DPDx, UC IPM).
- PetMD calls tapeworms the most common flea disease spread to people; CDC says only that people can get them by swallowing an
  infected flea and most have no symptoms. Not repeated.
- Orkin: fleas jump "nearly 150 times their own height" (about 19 inches for a 1/8-inch flea); Kentucky gives up to about
  6 inches vertically. Orkin names no human flea-borne disease; CDC names typhus, plague, cat scratch disease and tapeworm.
- Cleveland Clinic's home remedies carry no source; our page names only what MedlinePlus and AAD name.
- No conflict on the main answer: MedlinePlus says fleas prefer to live on dogs and cats and "may also be found on humans"
  (checked word for word; a fetch summary first misread it as "can also live on humans"); Texas A&M says cat fleas "do not
  normally live on humans". Real conflict, flagged for the checker: Texas A&M says cat fleas "do not carry plague"; UF/IFAS
  says they are capable of it, rarely. The page follows CDC (ground squirrel fleas in the US) and makes no cat flea plague claim.
