3. **An "is it still in use?" test from CDC's rodent survey manual** (fresh droppings dark and soft, fresh rub marks black, soft and greasy, fresh gnawing light in color, active burrow entrances free of cobwebs), ICWDM's flour tracking patch and CDC's one-week rule.
4. **CDC's own nest cleanup steps**, word-for-word strength: 1.5 cups of bleach per gallon, 5-minute soak, double-bagged, plus CDC's vehicle air-intake step. Theirs give no ratio or no steps.
5. **Pack rats (woodrats)**, which none of them cover: stick houses 3 to 5 feet across for dusky-footed woodrats (CDFA), middens at the base of cactus (NPS), and CDC's note that kissing bugs live in rodent nests in the southern US.

## What they get wrong or leave out (ours states it from the source)
- **Nest size.** 4-6 inches (Trutech), 6-8 inches (Mosquito Joe), "the size of a football" (Rid A Pest): none sourced, and they disagree. No extension or health source we opened gives a standard rat nest size; ours says so and gives what is sourced (burrow openings, pack rat houses).
- **Burrows "two to four inches wide and 18 inches deep"** (Trutech, Critter Control, same text). ICWDM: active burrow holes 2 to 3 inches wide; Clemson: rat tunnel entrances as large as 3 inches. Depth: no source we could open gives it (the Timm handbook chapter PDF returned 403), so ours gives none.
- **Rats per nest.** Google's AI answer to "How many rats can live in one nest?" says 5 to 15, citing UK pest firms and wikiHow. No US government or university source we opened gives a count; ours says that and gives UC IPM's litter sizes and NPS's "packrats live alone unless the female has young".
- **Hantavirus.** Trutech and Critter Control list it for rats without saying CDC names the deer mouse as the carrier of the most common US hantavirus; Norway rats carry Seoul virus (CDC).
- **Removal.** None gives CDC's order or its steps; Mosquito Joe's "bleach solution" has no ratio.
