## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- PestWorld: house crickets were introduced from Europe. UF/IFAS EENY-063: probably native to southwestern Asia;
  K-State Extension Entomology says the same of their natural range.
- PestWorld, Scotts, The Bug Master: house crickets survive indefinitely indoors, anywhere. Missouri: all stages can
  live in buildings year-round, but K-State says escaped house crickets survive Kansas winters only minimally, around
  heated areas, and UF/IFAS says wild populations persist only in the eastern US (outside peninsular Florida) and
  southern California.
- Scotts and Tom's Guide: crickets come in when it is hot ("warmest months"). Missouri G7366, UF/IFAS ENY-230 and
  Minnesota: mostly late summer and fall, as nights cool.
- Tom's Guide: house crickets are larger than field crickets, field crickets usually half an inch. UF/IFAS ENY-230:
  field crickets are more robust and slightly larger (1/2 to 1 1/4 inches against 3/4 to 1).
- Scotts: branded insecticides first. Every extension source here puts sealing, lights and sanitation first, and
  Minnesota says indoor pesticides are generally not effective or necessary.
