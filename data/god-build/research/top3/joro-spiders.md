## The three, taken apart
- **Wikipedia**: ~1,000 words, ~13 photos (mostly Asian specimens); female 17-30 mm, male 4-8 mm; states GA, SC, NC, TN plus
  undated "reports" from AL, MD, OK, WV, PA; lead says it "spans the Eastern US"; cold tolerance in one line, no source date;
  no look-alike comparison, no what-to-do, no reporting, no bite advice beyond "weak venom".
- **NAISMA (Coyle, Clemson)**: ~1,100 words, 3 credited photos (spider on a hand, deck, window); range as of mid-2024 (GA,
  Upstate SC, parts of TN and NC, MD); good on ballooning ("they don't fly") and calm on bites; no table, look-alikes, FAQ,
  map, reporting or what-to-do; cites no papers by name.
- **Rutgers FS1386 (Master Gardeners, Feb 2026)**: ~1,700 words, 6 credited Bugwood photos; text on golden silk orb weaver,
  yellow and banded garden spiders; state list (AL, TN, NC, SC, WV, MD); control section advises crushing and pyrethroid
  sprays (cypermethrin, lambda-cyhalothrin, bifenthrin); no table, FAQ or map.
- Also read: Terminix (~1,100 words, 4 uncredited images, 12-state list sourced to Farmers' Almanac, no science sources);

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- NAISMA: female body "upwards of 1.5 inches (about 4 cm)". UGA C1273: 0.67-1.2 in (17-30 mm); Penn State: up to 1 in.
- Rutgers: introduced 2013, first detected in Hoschton; species spelled "clavate". Hoebeke et al. 2015 (PeerJ): first
  confirmed records from Barrow, Jackson and Madison counties, late Oct-early Nov 2014; UGA C1289: first population centered
  on Braselton and Hoschton. (UGA Today 2022 also says "around 2013"; the page states the 2014 records only.)
- Terminix: 12 states incl. FL, KY, MS, OH, WV, from a Farmers' Almanac map. UGA June 2026: 5 established + 3 isolated;
  Penn State: single individuals do not necessarily mean establishment.
- Wikipedia lead "spans the Eastern US": not supported by its own body or by UGA's list.
- "Flying" headlines and fear videos: Clemson HGIC: ballooning spiderlings are the only life stage that travels through the
  air; Joro Watch: unlikely for adults.
- Rutgers control (crush, pyrethroids): UGA C1273 advises against insecticides over large areas; Penn State: pesticide
  controls not recommended; NC State: chemical control not advised. Our page gives no spray advice.

## Source notes
- Sizes disagree a little: female body up to 1.2 in (UGA C1273), up to 1 in (Penn State), over 1 in (Clemson, UGA C1289), up
  to 1.25 in (Joro Watch species page); leg span 3+ in (Clemson), up to 4 in (Penn State). The page uses UGA C1273 and gives
  both spans. Eggs per sac: 400-500 (UGA C1273, Clemson, Penn State) vs several hundred to over 1,000 (UGA C1289): page uses
  400-500 from C1273.
- Ballooning distance: Penn State "tens to hundreds of miles" vs Joro Watch "on their own ... a few miles": both given.
- Penn State's page header says "Updated September 8, 2025" but its range map is labeled a 2026 update: cited as such.
