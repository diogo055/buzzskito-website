## The three (plus one), taken apart
- **Wikipedia, Halictidae (#1)**: about 1,200 words, a world view. Seven photos, none of a sweat bee on skin and none
  with scale; no table, no FAQ. Says only females sting and the sting is minor; no help telling sweat bees from hover
  flies or yellowjackets, no allergy signs, no advice. Contradicts itself on species (nearly 4,500 in the lead,
  more than 2,000 further down).
- **Best Bees (#2)**: about 2,000 words, a beekeeping company, May 2024. Three photos (one credited to MDC), no scale,
  no table; 6 FAQs; typos ("basil vein"). Sizes 3 to 10 mm. Hover flies "hairless". Never says males cannot sting.
- **MDC, Halictid Bees (#3)**: about 700 words; a state agency page and a source for ours (1/4 to 3/4 inch, nearly all
  nest in the ground, a gentle scrape moves one on, a pressed female may give a mild sting). Missouri only; no table;
  a "similar species" list with no notes; nothing on hover flies, males or allergy signs.
- **WebMD** (WebSearch #5): about 1,050 words, medically reviewed Jan 2025. No photo of a sweat bee. 3 to 15 mm.
  Home remedies (a baking soda paste) and "hire a pest removal service".

## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Best Bees: 3 to 10 mm (under 0.4 inch). MDC and Texas A&M TAIS: 1/4 to 3/4 inch; MDC's Wild Guide (May 2018): the
  bicolored striped sweat bee is 0.3 to 0.6 inch.
- Best Bees: hover flies are hairless. NDSU E1914: some hover flies are quite hairy, so hair is not a reliable clue;
  count the wings (Illinois Extension, Purdue).
- Best Bees and MDC: silent on males. UF/IFAS EENY-715: males cannot sting; MU Extension G7391: only females have
  stingers.
- WebMD: they lick sweat for salt and proteins their young need. MDC, Texas A&M and UF/IFAS: moisture and salts.
- WebMD: hire pest removal. MU G7391: controlling them is not desirable; MDC: avoid pesticides.
- Wikipedia: two species counts. MDC: more than 500 species in North America north of Mexico.
