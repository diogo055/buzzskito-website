## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Orkin: longhorn crazy ant colonies are "small in number, containing 1,500 to 2,500 individuals", while the same
  page calls them larger than the average ant nest. UF/IFAS EENY-142 says colonies are moderate to very populous.
- Texas A&M urban page: 36 counties and the 2015 exemption. Texas A&M AgriLife's Dec 12, 2022 overview gives 43
  Texas counties and confirmed records in LA, MS, AL, GA and FL; Mississippi State added three Mississippi counties
  on Oct 6, 2026.
- Texas Invasive Species Institute (a planner source, not top 3): Mississippi first confirmed "2014", Georgia "2017".
  Mississippi State says 2009 (Hancock County); UGA reported Georgia's first find on Aug 15, 2013 (Albany). TISI was
  not used as a source for that reason.
- Bites: Alabama Extension (ANR-2547) says tawny crazy ants do not bite or sting; Texas A&M, UF/IFAS (ENY-2006) and
  Mississippi State say they can bite or nip, with minor pain. Texas A&M's 2005 chart lists the (longhorn) crazy ant
  as not biting; UF/IFAS EENY-142 says it may bite. Our page gives each source's view instead of picking one.
