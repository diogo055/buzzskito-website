## What our page now does that none of the three do
1. **The answer first, in the agencies' own words**: ticks don't die off; Public Health Agency of Canada (Jul 3, 2026): ticks can be
   active whenever the temperature is consistently above freezing and the ground isn't covered by snow; CDC Where Ticks Live (Jul
   30, 2025): adults may be out searching for a host any time winter temperatures are above freezing; Ontario's Tick-borne diseases
   page (Feb 4, 2026): ticks can be found at any time of year when the temperature is above freezing (0°C).
2. **A sourced fall-and-winter table**, one row per tick and life stage (blacklegged adult, nymph, larva; American dog tick): CDC How
   Lyme Disease Spreads (Sep 24, 2024: nymphs quest April through July, adults most active in early spring and fall), CDC Tick
   Lifecycles (Oct 11, 2024: larvae hatch in summer; nymphs feed the next spring and moult into adults in the fall), CDC Where Ticks
   Live (nymphs and adult females most likely to bite; dog tick bite risk highest in spring and summer).
3. **Real photos** (CDC, public domain): an adult female and a nymph on a fingertip (lead and share image), a nymph beside a
   fingernail.
4. **FAQs in the searchers' words**: "Are ticks still active in September and October?", "Is it still tick season after the first frost?"
5. Already there: the month-by-month table for both species, the two peaks, the five-treatment season, local calendars.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Tree Doctors: tick season "officially ends in late fall", October "Low". PHAC and Ontario: active or found whenever it is above
  freezing; CDC: adults most active in early spring and fall.
- Global News (a vet): ticks hibernate once it "hits zero", re-emerging in spring. CDC: adults may be out any time winter
  temperatures are above freezing, so winter thaws count.
- Weather Network: "officially active down to four degrees" (a health-unit staffer). The federal, Ontario and CDC wording is
  "above freezing". Our page's older text also uses about 4°C (quick answer, two FAQs, the calendar note): left as is, flagged.
