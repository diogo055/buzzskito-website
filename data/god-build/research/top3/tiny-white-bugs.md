## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Hawx: dust mites "resemble fine dust when gathered in large numbers". Kentucky ENTFACT-646: visible only with a microscope; the
  moving dust on food is grain or mold mites (Purdue E-222, UC Davis Bohart).
- Hawx: psocids "about 1/32 to 1/4 of an inch". UF/IFAS ENY-225: indoor booklice usually under 1/16 inch; 1/4 inch is outdoor barklice.
- Terminix: a "clothes moths" image under the Termites heading. Bob Vila and Terminix: clothes moth adults "cream", 3/8 inch. UC IPM
  7435: the webbing clothes moth is gold, about 1/4 inch at rest; the larvae are the shiny white stage.
- Bob Vila: a garlic, onion and cayenne spray for mealybugs; no extension page read recommends it (UC IPM: inspect plants and discard
  infested ones; Clemson: isolate new plants six weeks).
- Google's AI answer ("in bathroom"): humidity "below 60%". Penn State and DCPH give below 50% for booklice.
