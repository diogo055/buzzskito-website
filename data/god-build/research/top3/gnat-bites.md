## Teardown
**Orkin (no date or author; ~700 words; same text on both URLs).** H-headings: can gnats bite humans; description; biting midges, sand flies, black flies; prevention. No photos (icons only), no table, no FAQ, no sources. Wrong or weak:
- Biting midges "1/25 to 1/8 inch": UF/IFAS IN626 gives 0.04 to 0.08 inch (1 to 2 mm) and Montana State 1 to 2.5 mm; 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) is too large.
- Puts phlebotomine sand flies and leishmaniasis on a US gnat-bite page but never names the gnats that do not bite (fungus gnats, fruit flies, drain flies, eye gnats).
- Medical advice is one line ("see a doctor if symptoms persist or worsen"); no emergency signs; "symptoms appear within about 30 minutes" is unsourced.

**Wyndly (Feb 26, 2025; "Wyndly Care Team", no medical reviewer; ~2,400 words; 9 FAQs).** No photos of gnats or bites (product bottles only), no table, no sources. Wrong or weak:
- Sells sublingual immunotherapy for gnat-bite reactions with no evidence cited; a product pitch every few screens.
- Healing time is "three to six days" in its FAQ, "a few days" in one section and "more than a week" in another.
- An unsourced UK Blandford fly claim; never says when or where each biter bites.

**Healthline (Jun 17, 2025; reviewed by an APRN; ~1,150 words).** Sections: what are gnats, what a bite looks like, five treatments, when to get care, prevention. Cites CDC, Clemson, Iowa State, NPS, Mississippi State and the UK NHS. No bite photos in the capture, no table, no FAQ, no sorter. Wrong or weak:
- "Gnats are about 1/4 inch long, though some types are smaller": the midges behind most "gnat bites" are 0.04 to 0.08 inch (UF/IFAS); only black flies come near 1/4 inch (Iowa State).
- Its when-to-see-a-doctor list rests on the UK NHS; ours uses the AAD, MedlinePlus and CDC.

**KidsHealth (reviewed Jul 2021, ~350 words):** says gnats live "wherever there is a river or stream", which fits black flies but not midges (UF/IFAS IG081: salt marshes, mud and wet debris). **bettertermite (Aug 2025, ~2,800 words):** a gnat-vs-mosquito table; its "midge bites itch longer" and "mosquito bites itch 3-7 days" are unsourced.

## What our page does that none of the three do
1. **The sorter:** which gnats bite (biting midges, black flies), which only pester (eye gnats), which do not bite at all (fungus gnats, fruit flies, drain flies), with a quick-ID table of sourced sizes in inches, and a where-and-when table that names the likely biter.
2. **Real, credited photos:** CDC's photo of midge bites on a man's ankles, a midge on skin, a black fly, a fungus gnat, a mosquito bite beside a quarter, and a card row of bite look-alikes (mosquito, flea, bed bug, chigger) each linking its page.
3. **Duration and comparison at source strength:** midge bites minutes to hours, 2 to 3 days in hypersensitive people (UF/IFAS); black fly swelling up to 1 to 2 weeks (Iowa State); a mosquito, flea, bed bug and chigger comparison from CDC and Cleveland Clinic, never a diagnosis.
4. **Health and prevention matched to each biter:** the AAD's emergency signs in a warning box, MedlinePlus and CDC first aid, black fly fever (Purdue), and the right defense per gnat: 20 x 20 screens and fans for midges (CDC, Clemson), light clothing and head nets for black flies (Minnesota, Purdue), repellents labeled for biting flies (CDC) with honest notes on how well they work (UF/IFAS, Minnesota).
