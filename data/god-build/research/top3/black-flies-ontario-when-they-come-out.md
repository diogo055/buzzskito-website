## The three, taken apart
- **Wikipedia, "Black fly"** (~1,300 words, 4 images): biology, regional effects, public health; populations "swell from late
  April to July"; no Ontario calendar, no first aid for an ordinary bite, no comparison with mosquitoes, no-see-ums or deer flies.
- **The Canadian Encyclopedia, "Black fly"** (Monckton and Brust; edited Jul 13, 2026; ~1,000 words, 2 images): at least 164
  species in Canada; 1-5 mm; adults in late spring and early summer; daytime; light clothing and repellent. No month-by-month,
  no other biting flies, no first aid, no Ontario detail.
- **Spokane Regional Health District, "Black Flies"** (~350 words): says black flies are "5-15 mm" (ECCC: 1-5 mm), that some are
  called "punkies" or "no-see-ums" (those are biting midges), and to avoid them "especially at dawn and dusk". US only.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Spokane: 5-15 mm. ECCC: 1-5 mm. Spokane: "no-see-ums" as black flies. UF/IFAS: no-see-ums are biting midges (Culicoides), 1-2 mm.
- Iowa State Extension says black flies feed most at dawn and dusk; Penn State Extension gives peaks of 9-11 a.m. and 4-7 p.m. The
  page states Penn State's, which matches its own "morning and late afternoon".

## Notes for the owner
- The page says "more than 60 black fly species in Canada"; ECCC counts 162 (The Canadian Encyclopedia: at least 164). True but
  understated; left as is.
- The page says "Unlike mosquitoes, only the female black flies bite." Male mosquitoes do not bite either; worth one word ("As with
  mosquitoes"). Left as is (no rewording of existing text in a retrofit).
- ECCC (federal, not on the brief's list of source types) is the only source found for the Canada-wide range.
