## The three, taken apart
- **Terminix eggs (#1):** about 750 words. H2s: can you see them, what they look like, where they are found, how to
  find them, need help. One photo of eggs close up, no ruler or coin, no table, no FAQ, no sources. Says about 1 mm,
  creamy white to pale yellow, rice-shaped with a hinged cap, an eye spot after about five days, sticky, in clusters,
  hatch in 7 to 10 days, 1 to 7 a day, "up to about 100" in a lifetime (its own body says "more than 100"). Nothing
  on killing eggs and no look-alikes. Free-inspection and phone calls to action.
- **Terminix life cycle (#2):** about 1,300 words, one drawn life-cycle diagram, no photos, no sources. Eggs
  "spherical", about 1 mm ("mustard seed"), laid in groups of 1 to 50, hatch in 6 to 17 days but elsewhere "within 10
  days"; egg to adult in about 21 days, elsewhere 37 days.
- **M&M Pest Control (#3):** about 600 words (July 2021) around a video with a board-certified entomologist; about 5
  images (colony on a bed frame, egg beside a grain of rice, an egg casing close up, a cluster under furniture), no
  ruler, no sources. Says about 1 mm, pearly white-gray, rice-shaped, hinged cap, visible without a microscope, hatch
  in 7 to 10 days, 1 to 7 a day, hundreds a lifetime, hatched eggs show a hole where the cap was, eggs resist
  chemicals so a follow-up comes about two weeks later. No kill temperatures, no look-alikes named.
- Also read: pestnet (about 300 words, one unlabeled image, no sources, "85% in a bed" unsourced, "freezing and

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Terminix life cycle: eggs "spherical". Tennessee: elongate, white, 1 mm; EPA: pinhead-sized, pearl-white.
- Terminix eggs: "up to about 100" eggs in a lifetime. UC IPM: 200 to 500; Kentucky: potentially hundreds; Tennessee:
  as many as 100 or more.
- Terminix life cycle: egg to adult in about 21 days. Kentucky: as little as a month at 70 to 80°F with blood; UC IPM:
  five weeks to four months.
- Terminix eggs and M&M: one hatch window (7 to 10 days) with no temperature. Kentucky (about a week), Tennessee (6 to
  10 days) and UC IPM (10 to 15 days) disagree, and UC IPM and Kentucky say temperature drives it.
- pestnet: freezing works, with no temperature or time. EPA: only at 0°F, sealed in a bag, for three days, and many
  home freezers are not cold enough.
