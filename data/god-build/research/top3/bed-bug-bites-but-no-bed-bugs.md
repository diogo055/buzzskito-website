## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- PestWorld: 3 bites in a row with no other signs means "another biting pest may be responsible". EPA: low-level
  infestations are much more challenging to find; Kentucky (ENTFACT-649): people are often bitten repeatedly yet never
  see an insect. A clean first look does not clear bed bugs.
- Moxie: "Mites are small insects." Cleveland Clinic: mites (chiggers) are close relatives of spiders and ticks.
- Moxie: most spider bites look like bed bug bites; Hawx: spider bites are "more painful and swollen". Kentucky: spider
  bites are a rare event, extremely difficult to diagnose from the lesion alone.
- Hawx never mentions a doctor; UC IPM and Kentucky: with no pest found, see a family physician or dermatologist.
- Moxie and Hawx never say plainly that bites cannot identify the biter (PestWorld says it once, then offers "3 bites
  in a row"); EPA: bites are a poor indicator of bed bugs.
