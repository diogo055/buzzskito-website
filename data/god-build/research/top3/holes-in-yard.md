## What our page does that none of the three do
1. A quick-ID table with hole sizes in inches, each from an extension source (Clemson HGIC 2364 and 2366, Ohio State,

2. Real, credited photos of the holes themselves: a chipmunk burrow in a yard, mole hills, a mole ridge, a vole at its

3. The timing test (overnight = night animals, daytime = squirrels, chipmunks, woodchucks: UGA), the snake-hole answer

## What they get wrong
- The Spruce: "Treating grub issues with milky spore is the best way to halt skunk and raccoon damage." Purdue E-271:
  commercial milky spore is active only against Japanese beetle grubs and has not proven effective as a stand-alone,
  single application.
- The Spruce: rat holes "about 2.5 to 3.5 inches wide". UF/IFAS: 2 to 3 inches; Clemson: up to 3 inches.
- Western Exterminator: an antlion-pit stock photo captioned as insect nesting by a foundation; no sizes at all.
- Iowa State's chart pins "sod neatly rolled up" on striped skunks; UNL Extension (G1688) and Minnesota DNR say raccoons
  roll up or shred sod and skunks dig precise cone-shaped holes. Our page tells readers to go by the holes.
