## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Orkin: black carpet beetle larvae "as large as 8 mm". Ohio State, Penn State (12.7 mm) and the Connecticut station say about 1/2 inch; UC IPM says 5/16 inch. The page gives both.
- Orkin: larvae survive "long periods without food", no figure. Colorado State: several weeks if the food disappears.
- Bug Out: the bristles irritate skin "if you handle the larvae". CDC EID: hairs spread through a home on air currents, lesions mostly under clothing; Penn State: sensitization follows years of exposure.
- Terminix: larvae eat "stored food". UC IPM: occasionally spices and grains; the black carpet beetle is the stored-product pest in arid areas.
- Speed is no test: Kentucky calls the larvae slow moving, Ohio State says common and furniture larvae often crawl rapidly. Both are stated.
- (signs research) bettertermite: carpet beetles "don't live in mattresses"; CDC EID: commonly found on mattresses.
