## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Tom's Guide: American egg case "about 8 mm", brown-banded "8-10 mm". UC IPM: brown-banded about 1/4 inch, American
  about 3/8 inch. Also "more than 33 types of bacteria" and lavender or lemongrass repelling roaches: no source.
- Scotts: big-roach droppings "as big as a grain of rice". CDC's key: about 1/8 inch (American), 1/16 (Oriental,
  smokybrown); NPS likens mouse droppings, 1/8-1/4 inch, to rice. Also "flush egg cases" and "20-50 eggs" for all.
- Western Exterminator: daytime roaches mean the "nest" is full. Kentucky: no centralized nests; they gather where
  their feces smell. Scotts, Terminix: one roach means many. UF: one to a few adults most likely a new introduction.
- Terminix: the odor "comes from cuticular hydrocarbons". UF ties it to feces and pheromones; Ebeling ties the musty
  odor to heavy infestations.
- bettertermite: "children exposed to high allergen levels are 3.7 times more likely to be hospitalized". The EHP
  paper it cites says this of children sensitized to the allergen with high bedroom levels; its "63% of homes" is
  detectable allergen somewhere in the home in a 1998-1999 survey. Per-species smells and "5 diseases": no source.
- ClearDefense, Terminix: cholera, leprosy, plague. UC IPM only says German cockroaches are "believed to be capable"
  of carrying organisms; no source gives a rate of illness from a household infestation.
