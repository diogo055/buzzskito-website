## The three, taken apart
- **Orkin** ("Do home remedies for roaches work?"): about 1,000 words, one chart image (Professional vs DIY), no
  species, no photos of roaches, no table, no FAQ block, no sources. Rates nine remedies (foggers, boric acid,
  baking soda + sugar, diatomaceous earth, bleach, fabric softener 3:2 with water, bay leaves, vinegar, essential
  oils) and pushes a phone number. Gel bait, the method every extension source puts first, is only a link.
- **UK ENTFACT-614** (Potter, revised 3/26): about 2,600 words, 14 figures (species, bait and dust application),
  strong on bait placement, boric acid dust, aiming sprays, foggers and hiring a pro. No sticky-trap monitoring,
  no finish line, no insect growth regulators, no sizes table, no FAQ, no fogger study or injury data, little on
  apartments beyond sealing, no rotation by chemical class. Nothing found that contradicts our sources.
- **Food & Wine** ("The Gross Places Roaches Hide in Your Kitchen", Oct 13, 2026): about 600 words, an affiliate
  shopping post for one boric acid powder, one Getty photo, buyer quotes, prices, no sources, no species.
- **Scotts** (now #3): about 1,000 words, stock and product photos, no baits at all; sells a contact spray and a
  perimeter spray applied "as a wide band" along baseboards; "3 ways" lists two; no sources.

## What our page does that none of them do
1. **Identification first, with real credited photos and a quick-ID table that maps each species to its plan**:
   German, brown-banded, American, Oriental, smokybrown and wood cockroaches (sizes from Texas A&M, UC IPM,
   Rutgers, Penn State). Three photos show the roach as people meet it (Oriental on pavement, smokybrown on a
   porch rail at night, a nymph on fingertips). The plan differs: bait the indoor breeders, seal and fix drains
   for the big outdoor roaches, and leave wood cockroaches alone (Penn State: pesticides not needed).
2. **A measured plan with a finish line**: sticky traps first and how to read the catch (UF/IFAS IN1190), bait
   amounts and placements from Rutgers (10-30 g for a one-bedroom apartment), Kentucky (a dozen-plus placements
   under one sink) and a 188-unit New Jersey study (about 18 g per apartment, 0.1 g dots, 48 of 49 apartments
   clear at 12 months), a timeline table, rotation by chemical class with active ingredients named (UF/IFAS
   table), and the stop rule (no roaches trapped for 4 weeks, Rutgers).
3. **Sprays and bug bombs at the evidence's full strength**: the NC State field study (no decline in 20 homes,
   residues about 600 times baseline), CDC MMWR (3,222 fogger illnesses and injuries in 10 states, 2007-2015) and
   EPA's fogger precautions, plus where sprays still fit (outdoor harborages, Texas A&M).
4. Apartment plan (Purdue E-241, Rutgers, Kentucky), children and pets, a 2025 University of Kentucky in-home
   study showing some store baits that work in the lab fail in apartments, and 10 FAQs from People also ask.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Food & Wine says the boric acid powder "attracts the roaches, pulling them out". UC IPM: even baits do not
  attract cockroaches over long distances; Kentucky: roaches avoid heavy deposits of dust.
- Food & Wine quotes a buyer laying "a line on edges of counters". Kentucky: never apply boric acid to countertops
  or other food-preparation surfaces; use a barely visible film in hidden voids.
- Food & Wine calls the powder "safe around pets". NPIC: signs of boric acid poisoning in animals can start
  within 2 hours of eating it; exposures happen when products are within reach of pets.
- Food & Wine: results "in just 72 hours". UC IPM: boric acid may take 7 days or more to affect a population.
- Food & Wine lists an ultrasonic repeller and a peppermint-oil spray. Rutgers: no scientific evidence supports
  electronic repellers; essential-oil sprays work only on direct contact and leave little residual effect.
- Scotts: a wide spray band along baseboards. Kentucky: do not spray walls, floors, baseboards and countertops,
  where roaches spend little time; UC IPM and Rutgers: sprays repel and scatter roaches and meet resistance.
- Orkin talks of colonies and nests and offers a baking soda + sugar bait and a fabric-softener spray with no
  evidence. NPIC: home remedies may not be tested and come with no label, dose or first-aid directions.
