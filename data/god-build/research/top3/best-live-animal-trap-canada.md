**Ontario's rules** (https://www.ontario.ca/page/harass-capture-or-kill-wild-animal-damaging-private-property, updated
Dec 15, 2025, unless noted)
- "release captured live wildlife within 1 kilometre of where they were captured." "This is to ensure that they know
  where food sources and den locations are, and do not have to fight for new territory."
- "release captured live wildlife within 24 hours"; "release wildlife on private property only with the landowner's
  permission"; "follow all municipal by-laws and other applicable laws".
- "If you capture sick, injured or orphaned wildlife, take the animal to an authorized wildlife rehabilitator (wildlife
  custodian) who can provide proper care and rehabilitation."
- Why: "Generally, provincial laws prohibit capturing and relocating live wildlife." "These laws help: prevent the
  spread of diseases; ensure wildlife are treated humanely; prevent wildlife from being relocated outside their home
  ranges". A healthy-looking animal "may have the early stages (before signs show) of a disease such as rabies".
  "Relocating an animal will not always solve the problem" because what attracted it "is likely still present".
- The regulation: within "not later than 24 hours after capture", release "into the area in close proximity to the
  capture site unless otherwise directed by the Ministry", or "deliver any wildlife that is sick, injured or immature
  to a wildlife custodian described in section 44 of the Act"; never "on private property without the permission of
  the owner". O. Reg. 665/98 s. 133, https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980665 (consolidation from Jul 1, 2026,
  read via the e-Laws Word export). The 1 km figure is ontario.ca's; s. 133 gives no distance.
- The Act: s. 31(1) a person who "believes on reasonable grounds that wildlife is damaging or is about to damage the
  person's property" may, "on the person's land", harass it or "capture or kill the wildlife"; s. 44(1) a wildlife
  custodian keeps "injured, sick or immature game wildlife or specially protected wildlife" to rehabilitate them;
  s. 1 "wildlife" means "an animal that belongs to a species that is wild by nature"; s. 97 makes breaking the Act or
  its regulations an offence; s. 102(1) "a fine of not more than $25,000", up to a year in jail, or both.
  https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/97f41 (consolidation from Mar 30, 2026).
- Squirrels in Ontario law: Red Squirrel is in Schedule 1 "FURBEARING MAMMALS"; "Gray (Black) Squirrel" is a game
  mammal (Schedule 2); Northern and Southern Flying Squirrel are specially protected mammals (Schedule 6). Raccoon and
  Striped Skunk are furbearers; woodchuck is in none of these lists. O. Reg. 669/98,
  https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/980669 (consolidation from Sep 15, 2020).
- Den rule (same ontario.ca page): "not destroy the den of a furbearing mammal or a black bear without approval from the
  ministry"; "You do not need approval for fox or skunk dens".

**When the young are dependent** (Hinterland Who's Who, Environment and Climate Change Canada with the Canadian
Wildlife Federation; municipal pages as noted)
- Raccoon (text updated 2008), https://www.hww.ca/wildlife/mammals/raccoon/ : "Birth of offspring peaks during May";
  "births have been recorded as early as March or as late as September"; "The young remain in the maternity den for
  about eight weeks". Toronto: check they have moved out "especially between March and July when there may be babies".
- Striped skunk (1973, 1986, reprinted 1994), https://www.hww.ca/en/wildlife/mammals/striped-skunk.html : "the young are
  usually born in early May"; "at about two months they are weaned"; "They remain with their mother until autumn".
  Toronto: no one-way door "between April and September when skunks are rearing their young, unless the babies are
  mobile" (https://www.toronto.ca/community-people/animals-pets/wildlife-in-the-city/skunks/, Oct 27, 2017).
- Woodchuck (1973, 1991), https://www.hww.ca/wildlife/mammals/woodchuck/ : "Young woodchucks are born in April and May"
  "(in Canada, mainly in May)"; weaned "when they start to emerge from the burrow at five to six weeks of age"; "all
  woodchucks are underground in October". ontario.ca: "if necessary, evict groundhogs in late summer after the young
  have been weaned and are living independently"; fill the hole "only after you are sure the burrow is unoccupied" and
  "install heavy gauge mesh over the hole to prevent return"
  (https://www.ontario.ca/page/preventing-and-managing-conflicts-small-animals, Jan 19, 2026). SUNY ESF: "Woodchucks are
  solitary except for the family group (female and young)"; young disperse "In July and early August"
  (https://www.esf.edu/aec/adks/mammals/woodchuck.php; text from Saunders 1988).
- Eastern grey squirrel (1983, 1990), https://www.hww.ca/en/wildlife/mammals/eastern-grey-squirrel.html : "two breeding
  seasons each year, the first in January and February and the second in June and July"; "Gestation, or pregnancy,
  takes 40 to 44 days"; "at about 12 weeks the youngsters will be almost adult size and quite independent of their
  mother". So a summer litter is born from about mid-July into September and depends on the mother into autumn.
- General: "If you need to remove a small animal from your home, you should do this in autumn when young have left the
  den or nest" (ontario.ca small animals page).

## Claims on our page checked
1. **"release it within 24 hours and within 1 km ... or turn it over to someone authorized to receive it"** (FAQ 1;
   also the body "turned over to someone authorized to receive it" and the FAQ on moving a raccoon, "or handed to an
   authorized person"): **partly false.** 24 hours and 1 km match ontario.ca. The hand-over is only for "sick, injured
   or orphaned wildlife" (ontario.ca) / "sick, injured or immature" (s. 133), to "an authorized wildlife rehabilitator
   (wildlife custodian)", not for any animal. Also missing: private property only with the owner's permission.
   Say: "release it within 24 hours and within 1 km of where you caught it, on private land only with the owner's
   permission; if it is sick, injured or orphaned, take it to an authorized wildlife rehabilitator instead."
2. **"groundhogs are solitary and not a rabies-vector species"** (FAQ and the groundhog section, "no colony to
   orphan"): **partly true, reason misleading.** Adults live alone, but a female raises a litter (born April-May,
   mainly May in Canada; in the burrow until five to six weeks; dispersing July-August), so trapping her in May-July can
   orphan young; ontario.ca says evict in late summer after weaning. Rabies: not one of Ontario's reservoir species
   (bats, raccoons, skunks, foxes), but Ontario lists groundhogs among animals that "have occasionally tested positive".
   Say: "groundhogs are not one of Ontario's main rabies carriers, though they have occasionally tested positive" and
   drop "no colony to orphan".
3. **Trap lengths "small roughly 45-60 cm, large around 80-100 cm"**: **mostly true.** Havahart: small 43-45 cm, medium
   61 cm, large 81 cm (one-door) and 91 cm (two-door), extra-large 107 cm. The small range runs into the medium. Say:
   "small about 45 cm (17-18 in), medium about 61 cm (24 in), large 81-91 cm (32-36 in), extra-large about 107 cm".
4. **"Medium traps suit groundhogs, larger squirrels, and skunks"**: **false for groundhogs** (true for skunks and
   squirrels). Havahart lists the Medium 1078 for "rabbits, skunks, and squirrels", sells groundhog traps at 32-42
   inches, and leaves the Medium out of its groundhog category; ICWDM gives a 10 x 12 x 32 in trap for woodchucks. The
   same error appears in: the Quick Answer bullet "medium (groundhogs)"; FAQ "What is the best live animal trap in
   Canada?" ("A medium single-door steel trap suits a groundhog"); FAQ "What size live trap do I need?" ("a
   medium-to-large single-door is the safer choice"); the "Best for Groundhogs" award and its pro "Right size for
   groundhogs and skunks"; the StickyBuyBar and TopBox pick ("Havahart medium 1-door live cage trap", "For legal on-site
   release"); "the trap we point to is the Havahart medium single-door cage"; the BuyLink "Medium 1-door (groundhogs)";
   the size table row "Medium: Groundhogs, large squirrels, skunks"; the "Groundhog under a shed ... Buy a medium cage
   trap" row; and the whichToBuy line. Havahart's matching groundhog trap is the Large 1-Door 1079 (32 x 10 x 12 in).
   Changing the affiliate pick is the owner's call; the facts point to the Large.
5. **"From roughly March through August, most female raccoons, squirrels, and skunks in Ontario are raising dependent
   young"**: **mostly true, but the window is too short.** Raccoons: peak births in May (some to September), about
   eight weeks in the den. Skunks: born early May, with the mother "until autumn"; Toronto's caution runs April to
   September. Grey squirrels: a second breeding season in June and July, 40-44 day gestation, independent at about 12
   weeks, so summer litters depend on the mother into autumn. Say: "roughly March through September, and into autumn
   for a squirrel's summer litter; ontario.ca says do it in autumn, when the young have left".
