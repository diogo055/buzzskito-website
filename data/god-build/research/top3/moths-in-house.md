## The three, taken apart
1. **Scotts Miracle-Gro, "How to Get Rid of Moths in Your House."** About 900 words. Names Indian meal moth, brown house moth, webbing and casemaking clothes moths, then drifts into garden moths. Two generic moth images, no scale, no table, no FAQ, no sources. Sells four Ortho products with prices. Eggs never mentioned.
2. **Pest Solutions (UK), "Brown House Moth."** About 1,200 words on one British species. Gives its size three different ways (16-26 mm, an impossible "18-12 mm", 8-15 mm) and two different colors. Three FAQs, no sources, no scale. Branch phone numbers across the UK.
3. **moth-prevention.com, "House Moth Identification."** About 1,900 words, six species (two UK house moths, Mediterranean flour moth, Indian meal moth, two clothes moths), eight FAQs, about 11 images (several stock), product links in nearly every section. Sizes inconsistent; eggs mentioned but not described.

## Questions at least one answers that our page must answer
- Which kind of small moth is this: pantry or clothes moth? (all three)
- Where do they come from, and why so many suddenly? (Scotts, moth-prevention)
- What do the larvae look like, and the cases and webbing? (moth-prevention, Pest Solutions)
- What do they eat; do they eat cotton or synthetics? (Scotts, Pest Solutions)
- How do I get rid of them, in the kitchen and in the closet? (all three)
- Are the adults harmful? (Pest Solutions, moth-prevention)

## What our page does that none of them do
1. **The test that settles it in seconds, from extension sources**: where it is and how it acts. UC IPM and Kentucky: clothes moths avoid light and stay near the infested item; a small moth flying in a lit kitchen is more likely a food moth (UF: Indian meal moths fly to light). At rest a clothes moth is about 1/4 inch, a food moth about double (UC IPM); only clothes moths have the head tuft.
2. **Moth eggs, with sizes from sources**: Indian meal moth eggs grayish white and 0.3 to 0.5 mm (UF), clothes moth eggs pinhead-sized and glued to the fibers (Kentucky, UC IPM). None of the three describes an egg.
3. **A quick-ID table for every small moth a US reader meets indoors** (Indian meal moth, two clothes moths, Angoumois grain moth, drain fly, miller moth), with sourced sizes and the tell; plus real credited photos of both moths and both larvae, several where people find them (a larva in cereal, webbing in a bag of seed, a clothes moth on a wall).
4. **Clothes moth control with the numbers that matter** from Kentucky, UC IPM and Penn State (wash at 120°F or hotter for 20-30 minutes; freeze at 0°F for at least a week; pheromone traps are species-specific; cedar and open-closet mothballs do not work). Scotts says "dry on low heat"; no source given.
5. No product sales, no prices, no brand names; US species and US sources.

## What they get wrong (our page states it from the source)
- Scotts: dry clothes "on low heat to kill the caterpillars". UC IPM: launder 20 to 30 minutes in water at least 120°F, or dry clean; Kentucky: hold frozen items at 0°F for at least a week.
- Pest Solutions: brown house moth size given three ways, one impossible ("18-12 mm").
- moth-prevention.com: Mediterranean flour moth "about 23 mm long" while the same page says pantry and clothes moths are under 14 mm. UC IPM: Indian meal moth 1/3 to 2/5 inch long, wingspan about 3/5 inch.
- moth-prevention.com and Pest Solutions lead with British house moths; UC IPM says the webbing clothes moth is probably the most common clothes moth in the US and the Indian meal moth the most common moth in food in the home. Our page leads with those two and leaves out the brown house moth (no US extension source found).

## Sources used (all opened Oct 20)
On the page (9): UC IPM clothes moths, pantry pests and carpet beetles; Univ. of Kentucky ENTFACT-609 (clothes moths) and ENTFACT-612; UF/IFAS EDIS IN153 (Indian meal moth); Penn State clothes moth; Texas A&M indoor flies; Colorado State miller moths. Also read, not cited: UF/IFAS IG090 (clothes moths; it gives 100-150 eggs per female and says the eggs are not glued strongly, against UC IPM's and Kentucky's 40-50 glued eggs, so the page uses UC IPM and Kentucky) and the UMD household key.
Note: UF IN153 prints 0.3-0.5 mm as "0.12 to 0.15 in"; it is about 0.012-0.02 inch. Our page says "under 1/50 inch".
