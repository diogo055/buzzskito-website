## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- AgriLife files American grass spiders under "sheet webs" (waiting underneath for insects to drop in) and only barn
  funnel weavers under "funnel webs". Grass spiders are funnel weavers whose sheet narrows into a funnel or tunnel and
  who wait in or at the funnel (Penn State; MDC grass spider guide; MDC common spiders guide); hanging under a sheet
  with no funnel is the sheetweb weavers' habit (bowl and doily, filmy dome, scarlet sheetweaver: MDC guide).
- AZ Pest: you cannot tell who spun a web unless you see the spider. MDC: a web's shape and structure give important
  clues to the type of spider and are a helpful first step in identification.
- Western Exterminator and Terminix: a cobweb is an abandoned web. Only half right: OSU Extension uses "cobwebs" and
  "dustwebs" for inactive, dusty silk, but MDC and UC IPM use "cobweb" for the active tangle webs of the cobweb
  spider family (common house spider, widows). NHM and the Australian Museum name only British or Australian species.
