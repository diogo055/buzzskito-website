## What our page now does that none of the three do
1. **The plain answer first** (title and quick answer): numbers drop after Labour Day, mild October evenings still bring bites, the
   first hard frost ends the season; they survive winter as eggs or as sheltered adults.
2. **A sourced September-to-spring table**: Ontario Parks ("Camping comfortably with bugs", May 14, 2026: mosquitoes from June all
   the way through September), Public Health Ontario (2017: traps collect mosquitoes from June through October), Penn State
   Extension ("Fall Mosquitoes and Mosquito-Borne Diseases", Oct 1, 2025: a threat well into autumn if it stays warm), University
   of Georgia Extension (Feb 2023: most mosquitoes overwinter as eggs; some as adults in catch basins, storm drains, culverts,
   barns and sheds); the 10°C and hard-frost lines restate the page's temperature section.
3. **Ontario's most-trapped mosquitoes with real photos**: Public Health Ontario's five most identified species (Aedes vexans 30%,
   Culex pipiens/restuans 14%); an Aedes vexans (CC BY 2.0) and a Culex pipiens photographed in Ontario (CC BY 4.0).
4. **FAQs in the searchers' words**: "Are mosquitoes still out in October in Ontario?", "Where do mosquitoes go in the winter?"

## What they get wrong or leave vague
- CP24 2025 says peak abundance "lasts through October"; CP24 2026 says the season runs "May through September". Same outlet,
  opposite answers. Public Health Ontario's trap season (June through October) is the official reference point.
- "As soon as we have a frost, they'll be gone": true for biting adults in the open, but University of Georgia Extension notes
  that some mosquitoes overwinter as sheltered adults and most as eggs, which is why the season always comes back.
