## The three, taken apart
1. **UC IPM, "Pest Notes: Moth or Drain Flies"** (Publication 74167, updated 03/2014, Theuret and Gerry). About 1/8
   inch; life cycle "as little as 8 days but can take as long as 24 days depending on temperature"; eggs hatch in
   about 48 hours; the Vaseline-cup test; a hard-bristle brush with a drain cleaner; enzymatic cleaners "may help to
   prevent the organic buildup"; boiling water or bleach "a common misconception ... at best short-term control";
   chemical control "generally considered unnecessary", discouraged in homes; moth flies "do not bite". Photos, no
   table, two references. Lacks: what to buy, a decision guide, time to expect, anything Canadian.
2. **Terminix Canada, "Drain Flies Identification & Facts"** (no date, about 600 words). 1.5-5 mm; 30-100 eggs; cycle
   about 21-27 days; the jar test; scrub with a stiff wire brush and "industrial drain cleaners"; "Residentially
   speaking, pesticides are an unnecessary form of drain fly control." No sources, no product types, no PCP or Health
   Canada rule; the page sells a service visit.
3. **PestHQ (Canadian retailer), "How to get rid of small flies"** (no date, about 900-1,000 words). Recommends an
   enzyme- or bacteria-based drain cleaner; "Chemical drain cleaners will not clean drains well enough to eradicate
   the fly larvae." Names products it sells (Invade Bio Foam, Invade Hot Spot, sticky strips, and a BTK caterpillar
   insecticide among the small-fly products). No PCP numbers, no sources, no date.
- Also read: **University of Arkansas Extension** (top US result): 2-4 mm; "No insecticide products are registered
  for use in drains or sewer systems" (a US statement); "thorough cleaning and maintenance for at least 3 weeks or
  longer to break the life cycle"; recurring flies "can indicate a broken pipe or leak under a slab or in a
  crawlspace". One photo, no sources.

## Corrected (false or unverifiable against the primary source)
- "Foaming US drain insecticides ... frequently not registered for sale in Canada" (FAQ) and "several of the foaming
  drain insecticides ... are not registered" (body) -> registry: no current insecticide names drain flies.
- "importing an unregistered pesticide is not legal here" -> PCPA s. 6(1), with the narrow personal-use exemption in
  the Pest Control Products Regulations, s. 3(1)(f).
- "a bacterial or enzyme drain product sold as a cleaner is not a pesticide at all, because it works by digesting the
  organic film" -> the test is the claim: PCPA s. 2 covers anything "manufactured, represented, distributed or used"
  to control, destroy, attract or repel a pest.
- "carries no re-entry concerns around a kitchen sink" (FAQ) and "no re-entry or residue concerns beside a kitchen
  sink" (body): safety-type claims, removed.
- "Ultrasonic plug-in repellers have no credible evidence for any household pest" -> the ultrasonic repellers in the
  registry are all for mice and rats (32151, 31743, 29050, 30674, 21465, 33070, 29080, 32060); none for flies.
- Identification table, fungus gnats: "Dry the soil and use BTI" -> no home-use BTI in Canada lists fungus gnats
  (Mosquito Dunks 28888: mosquito only; the domestic products that name fungus gnats are light traps 35296, 35297,
  35801).
