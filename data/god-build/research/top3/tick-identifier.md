## The three, taken apart
- **TickEncounter Field Guide (URI)**: ~150 words plus a widget: pick one of 9 US census regions and a month, get a list of ticks by
  activity with photos (larva, nymph, adult, part-fed and fed female). No Canada, no size, color, place-found or legs question, no
  "is it a tick?" step, no disease or what-to-do text on the page (links out to TickSpotters and other pages), no sources.
- **ALDF "Tick Identification and Information"**: ~2,900 words, 23 images, species profiles (8 ticks) plus long disease and
  prevention sections. No sizes, no interactive narrowing, Canada never named, disease sections carry bracketed reference numbers
  with no reference list, typos ("The make has shorter mouthparts").
- **PA Tick Research Lab "Tick Identification Guide"**: ~800 words, 9 species cards (one photo each, empty alt text), life stages
  in three lines, tick anatomy. No Canada, no what-to-do, no sources; sells tick testing ("99.9% accurate", "TEST MY TICK"); its
  free photo ID was "temporarily unavailable" on Oct 22.

## What our page does that none of the three do
1. **Narrows by the reader's own clues**: 6 questions (region incl. 3 Canadian ones, where found, size against seeds, shield
   markings, legs and body, season) scored against 11 ticks plus "probably not a tick", with the sourced reason for every point.
2. **US and Canada**: PHAC, Public Health Ontario, INSPQ, BCCDC and B.C. ranges; lone star "found but not established" in Canada.
3. **Swollen and young ticks handled honestly**: a fed-tick flag with CDC's 72-hour preventive-antibiotic window (CDC PEP poster:
   engorged raises risk, flat unlikely, PEP possible even if the tick is unidentified), seed-tick swarms, nymph sizes.
4. **What to do now on every result**: removal, keep the tick, free ID (TickSpotters in the US, eTick.ca in Canada), testing not
   recommended (CDC, PHAC), when to see a doctor, 911 for a severe alpha-gal reaction; plus a real CDC photo per result.
5. **"Is it even a tick?"**: legs/antennae/wings question; look-alikes (spider beetle, weevil, bed bug, clover mite, deer ked)
   go to our bugs-that-look-like-ticks, red-ticks-and-clover-mites and tick-vs-bed-bug pages. 45 linked sources, no sales.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- ALDF: "Transmission of the Lyme disease spirochete occurs after 36 hours of feeding... removing it before that time will prevent
  Lyme disease." CDC How Lyme Disease Spreads (Sep 24, 2024): in most cases more than 24 hours; no "will prevent".
- ALDF: see a doctor if redness "expands beyond 4 inches". CDC: see a doctor for any rash or fever within several weeks.
- PA Tick Lab: the deer tick "has been identified in every U.S. state except Hawaii". CDC Where Ticks Live: widely distributed
  across the eastern US; naturally occurring populations do not occur in Alaska.
- PA Tick Lab: the brown dog tick "lacks a distinct dorsal shield". All hard ticks have one (CAES handbook); UF/IFAS: it has no
  markings on its back, not no shield. Its Rocky Mountain wood tick range (Nebraska, South Dakota, Arizona, New Mexico, California)
  is not CDC's (Rocky Mountain states and SW Canada, 4,000-10,500 ft).
- PA Tick Lab sells testing; CDC: tick testing is in general not recommended; PHAC: not meant to guide diagnosis or treatment.
