## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
1. Mosquito Squad: crane flies "grow up to four inches long". AMCA (Mistaken IDs) and Lake County VCD: up to about 1 1/2 inches.
2. Western Exterminator: biting midges "around one-eighth of an inch". UF/IFAS EENY-349: usually 0.04 to 0.08 inch (1 to 2 mm).
3. A-Z Animals files the swarming look-alike "midges" under Ceratopogonidae (the biting family). MDC: the swarming look-alikes are chironomid midges, which do not bite; biting midges are a separate family.
4. Feathery antennae treated as a midge trait (Santa Barbara MVMD bulletin, and implied on pest-company pages). NM Department of Health: male mosquitoes have bushy antennae too; MDC: male midges often do. It marks a male, not a midge.
5. Santa Barbara MVMD bulletin (2001; we cite it only for other facts): "Mosquitoes DO NOT swarm". Lake County VCD: male mosquitoes may swarm when mating, in a defined spot that is hard to see. We say that.
6. Orkin's list is padded with wingless or aquatic things (fleas, chiggers, tadpoles) and misses phantom midges, dixid midges, winter crane flies and mayflies.
