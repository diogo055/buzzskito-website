## Why each wins
- Moxie (~3,000 words, no date shown): six look-alikes (ticks, bed bugs, carpet beetles, lice, flea beetles, fungus
  gnats), each with the same five subheads; an infographic; 15+ cited sources. No FAQ, no table.
- A-Z Animals (~1,000 words, updated Sep 15, 2026): eight look-alikes (aphids, bed bugs, bat bugs, black carpet
  beetles, springtails, confused flour beetles, flea beetles, ticks), a stock photo of each, sizes in inches.
- DoctorSniffs (~2,600 words): thirteen look-alikes incl. roach nymphs, minute pirate bug, fruit flies, biting midges,
  lice, ticks, booklice, springtails; a comparison image per bug; size and colour per bug. No sources to speak of.

## What they give that we did not
1. Ticks (all three). 2. A flying look-alike: fungus gnats (Moxie), fruit flies and midges (DoctorSniffs).
3. Lice (Moxie, DoctorSniffs). 4. Cockroach nymphs, aphids, flour beetles (one page each). 5. A photo of every bug:
our springtail and flea beetle sections had only placeholders.

## What we give that none of them do
- The one-line flea test, a sourced comparison table and quick-ID box, the jar checklist, which ones bite, what to do
  for each, real credited photos (CDC, museum, iNaturalist), bird mites and bat bugs with extension sources, the AAD
  "when to get help" box. None of the three has a table, an FAQ or a checklist.

## What the searcher needs first
The test that rules a flea in or out (wingless, jumps, flat side to side, on pets), then the list of suspects with
the single tell for each, most common first.
