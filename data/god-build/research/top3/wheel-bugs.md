## The three, taken apart
1. **Wikipedia, "Reduviidae"** (~1,000 words of body). Taxonomy, morphology, feeding, phylogeny. 7 images, none of a
   wheel bug, masked hunter or kissing bug, none in a house, no scale. Size 12-36 mm sourced to a kissing bug chapter
   (one subfamily). Wheel bug only as a name; masked hunter one unsourced line. No table, FAQ, bite care or what to do.
2. **UC IPM Natural Enemies Gallery** (~750 words). Good identification (narrow head, beady eyes, hinged needlelike
   beak, 1/5 to 1 1/4 inches) and egg/nymph photos, but California garden species only (Zelus, Sinea): no wheel bug,
   no masked hunter, nothing about bites to people, no what-to-do. Says assassin bugs are not important biological
   control agents (a fact the others skip; our page uses it).
3. **PestWorld, NPMA** (~650 words). Fact box: 1/2 to 3/4 inch, up to 1 1/2; region "mainly the southern US, especially
   Arizona, California, New Mexico and Texas" (that is kissing bug range; wheel bugs live from Rhode Island to
   California, UF). Bite care names aspirin/ibuprofen with no medical source; "signs of an infestation" and a
   pro-finder for an outdoor predator; nearly 200 North American species. No masked hunter, no photos of note.

## What they get wrong or leave out (our page states it from the source)
- PestWorld's southern-US "region" for assassin bugs: UF lists wheel bugs from Rhode Island west to California and
  south to Texas and Florida; MDC lists them statewide in Missouri; Kentucky sees them around homes in late summer.
- PestWorld's bite medicines and Wikipedia's "handled with caution, if at all" with no first-aid: our page gives
  Texas A&M's and Mayo's steps without doses, and when to get care.
- Wikipedia's 12-36 mm for the family: UC IPM gives 1/5 to 1 1/4 inches (5-30 mm); MDC up to 1 1/2 inches.
- Species count: PestWorld "nearly 200" in North America vs Texas A&M "roughly 150"; the page uses Texas A&M.
