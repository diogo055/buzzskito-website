# Beat the top 3: flies-in-house ("why are there so many flies in my house")

Read 2026-10-20. Spec `serp` (Google, Oct 16): Reddit, JC Ehrlich, Quora, Facebook group, Orkin, HuffPost UK, EarthKind,
Good Housekeeping. Checked again on Google (en, US) today: Reddit, Ehrlich, videos, Quora, Facebook, "What people are
saying" (Reddit, Facebook), HuffPost UK, EarthKind, Orkin. Skipped: Reddit, Quora, Facebook, the video box (people posting
"why are there suddenly flies everywhere?" threads is itself the finding; the top Reddit post describes slow, half-alive
flies at the windows, which is cluster flies). Top 3 editorial results read: Ehrlich, Orkin, HuffPost UK.

## The three, taken apart
1. **JC Ehrlich, "Signs of a Fly Infestation"** (built-in browser; WebFetch is refused). About 700 words, one stock photo
   (a fly on cake), no table, no FAQ block, no sources. Names house, fruit, drain and cluster flies in one line each; never
   names blow flies or flesh flies, though it says an animal dying in the attic can cause an infestation. Fly specks
   "the size of a pinhead", maggots as the sign of breeding. Ends in a free-inspection pitch.
2. **Orkin, "House Flies"** (the page Google shows for this search). About 1,100 words with its FAQ. One illustration,
   no photo, no scale, no table, no sources. Sizes in mm only (4 to 7.5 mm), "over 100 pathogens", eggs hatch in 12 to 24
   hours, rest "5 to 15 feet off the ground" (unsourced). Mixes blow flies in as "blue house flies". Quote buttons.
3. **HuffPost UK, "Why Does My House Suddenly Have So Many Flies?"** (May 27, 2026). About 430 words, no fly photo, one
   UK entomologist quoted: the sudden rise is open windows in hot weather, because breeding takes "weeks or months".
   No species, no hidden-source check, advice is mesh screens and no insecticides.

## Questions at least one answers that our page must answer
- Why so many flies all of a sudden; where they come from; why in a clean house (all three)
- Signs of an infestation: specks, maggots (Ehrlich, Orkin)
- Which fly is it: house, fruit, drain, cluster (Ehrlich), house fly look and life cycle (Orkin)
- Are they dangerous / do they carry disease (Orkin)
- How to get rid of them and keep them out: sanitation, screens, traps, sprays (all three)

## What our page does that none of them do
1. **A sorter by fly and by source**: house fly, blow fly (green bottle fly), flesh fly, cluster fly, plus the biting
   stable fly and the hovering little house fly, each with a sourced size in inches and the one tell, in a quick-ID
   table right under the answer and a "where and when you see them" table. None of the three has a table.
2. **Real, credited photos**: a CDC house fly, a green bottle fly on a wall beside a 1 mm scale bar, a flesh fly, a
   cluster fly, a stable fly on painted wood and a fly maggot. Ehrlich has one stock photo, Orkin a drawing, HuffPost none.
3. **The hidden-source answer, sourced**: a dead animal in a chimney, wall void or ceiling gives a sudden crowd of blow
   flies days later and ends when the carcass is consumed (UMN Extension); flesh flies follow the odor (Texas A&M); the
   CDC steps for removing a dead rodent; garbage left over a week breeds blow flies (UC IPM). Plus cluster flies, which
   explain flies in a clean house (Penn State: they do not breed indoors).
4. Look-alike cards (stable fly, cluster fly, the small flies) and 10 FAQs from People also ask.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- HuffPost: a sudden rise cannot be breeding because that takes weeks or months. UMN: blow flies from a hidden carcass
  appear several days after the animal dies; Texas A&M: a house fly's life cycle shortens to 7 days at 95 °F.
- Ehrlich lists water pooling in gutters, rain barrels and tires as a fly sign. Those are mosquito sites; UMN and UC IPM
  give garbage, manure, decaying vegetation, pet waste and dead animals as where house flies and blow flies breed.
- Orkin files metallic "blue house flies" under house flies. UMN and UC IPM: those are blow flies, a different family
  with a different source (meat scraps, carcasses).
- Orkin gives size in mm only; UMN gives 1/6 to 1/4 inch. Orkin's "5 to 15 feet" resting height has no source; left out.
