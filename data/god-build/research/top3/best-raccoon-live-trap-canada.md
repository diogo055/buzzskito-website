## Our own Ontario statements, checked against the sources (the user's rule: they must match)
- Quick Answer "Trapping is legal, but..." -> "You may live-trap a raccoon that is damaging your property, on your own
  land, but..." (Act s. 31(1); ontario.ca "restrict activity to your property").
- FAQ "Is it legal to trap and relocate a raccoon in Ontario?" and the Ontario section said a captured animal "must
  either be released within 1 kilometre ... within 24 hours, or killed humanely". Corrected: release within 1 km and
  24 hours, never on someone else's private property without permission, a sick, injured or immature animal to a wildlife
  custodian, killing only as a last resort without unnecessary suffering.
- A sick-looking raccoon: "contact local animal control or a licensed wildlife agent" (FAQ and section) -> Ontario's
  Wildlife Health Information Line, 1-888-574-6656 (ontario.ca/page/rabies, updated May 5, 2026; the veterinarians page
  gives the same number; the dead-animal page lists 1-888-574-5565, a conflict on ontario.ca itself). "Rabies is present
  in raccoon populations in parts of Canada" -> Ontario eliminated raccoon-variant rabies from southern Ontario in 2023
  and calls its eastern border high-risk (ontario.ca, updated Jul 15, 2026).
- Left as they are (they match): the 1 km / 24-hour lines in FAQ 1, the Quick Answer bullet and the warning box.
  "Licensed wildlife (control) agent" is loose: Ontario's agents are licensed trappers, municipal wildlife staff,
  nuisance-wildlife removal businesses (capture and release only) and the landowner's immediate family; nothing on the
  page suggests an unlawful route, so the wording was kept.
