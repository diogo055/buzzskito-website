## Claims checked, and what is wrong on the page (owner decisions)
- **CimeXa is not in PMRA's registry.** API searches on Oct 23: product name contains CIMEXA (0), registrant Rockwell
  Labs (2 products: Aprehend, Boractin, both commercial), active ingredient AMORPHOUS (0), SILICA (Drione family,
  Protect-It, technical grades only). The only current silica-aerogel bed bug dust is Drione (15255, commercial).
  The site's own report found the same on Oct 7 (`reports/us_pages_product_findings.md`). The page's Best Overall
  (AwardRow, TopBox, StickyBuyBar: search "cimexa insecticide dust") is therefore an unregistered product for Canada.
  Left in place (affiliate elements); the new text states the registry result plainly.
- **"Food-Grade Diatomaceous Earth (Bulk)" award** (search "food grade diatomaceous earth bulk"): DE sold without a
  PCP number is not a registered pest control product; Health Canada: "If the product label does not have a PCP
  registration number, do not buy or use it." EPA: "Pool or food grade diatomaceous earth should not be used." Left in
  place; flagged. (Some bulk bags are registered: the Doktor Doom Be Green label covers 100 g to 7 kg.)
- **Electrical boxes**: the page said to "puff a light dust into the box". CimeXa's label: "dust shouldn't be applied
  directly in electrical boxes". Fixed in the body bullet and in the "How do I apply" FAQ (wall void, never the box).
- **"Far lighter application rate" for silica** (Quick Answer, AwardRow): the labels give similar rates (CimeXa 2 oz
  per 100 sq ft, about 6.1 g per square metre; Doktor Doom DE 5.0 g per square metre). Not repeated in new text; the
  AwardRow copy is an affiliate element, left as is.
- Existing FAQ "Is diatomaceous earth safe to use indoors": says "only food-grade DE ... is appropriate". EPA says use
  only desiccants registered as pesticides. Not edited (existing FAQ); the new table and FAQ give the PCP rule.
