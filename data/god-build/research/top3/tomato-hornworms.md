## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- UMN: no tobacco hornworm. UF/IFAS EENY-700: in Florida hornworm damage on tomato is typically the tobacco hornworm's;
  Illinois Extension: the tobacco hornworm is the more commonly seen; UMaine: Maine homeowners meet the tomato hornworm
  far less often; Virginia Tech: the tobacco hornworm is more abundant in Virginia.
- UMN and the Almanac: the wasp's eggs are laid "on" the hornworm. NC State (Cotesia factsheet) and Clemson: inside it.
- Almanac: photo of "parasitic wasp eggs" on a hornworm. NC State: cocoons, which gardeners mistake for eggs and destroy;
  Illinois: commonly misidentified as eggs.
- Almanac: Bt is "safe"; dill or basil deter pests. UMN: Bt is specific to caterpillars and spares other insects; no
  extension source read recommends companion plants against hornworms (our garden hub: Minnesota's research list says
  nothing about hornworms).
- Almanac: two generations only "in warmer regions". UMN: two generations in Minnesota; Wisconsin: up to two in the
  upper Midwest.
- Wikipedia: found "across North America and Australia". UF/IFAS EENY-700: northern Mexico to southern Canada; Clemson:
  southern Canada through the southern US.
