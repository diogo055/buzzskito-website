## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Wikipedia: alpha-gal reactions "usually occur 3–6 hours" after red meat. CDC AGS symptoms (Jan 5, 2026): usually 2 to 6 hours.
- Wikipedia contradicts itself on feeding: lead "as long as seven days", development section "about 10–15 days" (lab). URI: females
  need a week to 10 days or more to engorge. Our page gives URI's figure only.
- PMC: Bourbon and Heartland viruses "are vectored by the lone star tick". CDC: Heartland spreads "primarily" through lone star
  bites; Bourbon is believed to spread through bites of infected ticks, likely lone star ticks; both rare.
- PMC: lone star ticks are the most common human-biting ticks in the US (a 2006 paper). No CDC page we opened ranks them; left out.
- PMC suggests a homemade dry-ice trap for control; no CDC or extension source we opened advises it; left out.
- Cleveland Clinic: symptoms take "a few days to two weeks". CDC: STARI rash within 7 days, ehrlichiosis 1 to 2 weeks, Heartland a
  few days to several weeks, alpha-gal weeks to months after the bite.
- Cleveland Clinic: clean the skin with rubbing alcohol first and grasp the tick "by its head". CDC (After a Tick Bite, Jun 9, 2026):
  grasp as close to the skin as possible, pull straight up, then clean the bite and your hands.
