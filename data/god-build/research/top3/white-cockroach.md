## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Orkin: "seven to eight molts". Rutgers: German nymphs 5 to 6; UF: American 6 to 14; Nebraska: German 5-7,
  brown-banded 6-8, Oriental 7-10, American 10-13 nymphal instars. It varies by species.
- Orkin and Waynes: a white roach is a juvenile. Adults are white too after the last molt (Ebeling: completely white
  nymphs or adults; UF's photos of newly molted German and American adults).
- Terminix: an albino cockroach "has never been documented". Schal: albino mutants turn up occasionally in lab colonies.
- Terminix: color returns "over the next few minutes" (though some species take several hours); Better Termite:
  "minutes in small nymphs". UC IPM: usually within hours; Gould and Deay: full color 4 or 5 hours. No source we
  opened gives minutes for color.
- Terminix: a white roach in view means something, often a technician, disturbed the harborage. Only partly sourced:
  Ebeling says that when roaches are abundant, moving things in pantries, cabinets and closets brings a few into view;
  Texas A&M and NC State say daytime sightings mean many roaches or crowding.
- Better Termite: soft shells "absorb products faster". No source; our page makes no pesticide claim.
- pestnet.com (#4 for "albino roach"): an albino cockroach is a hatched larva. Texas A&M: egg, nymph, adult; no larva.
