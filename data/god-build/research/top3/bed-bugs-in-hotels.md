## The three, taken apart
- **Orkin** (pest company, no author or date): ~550 words of article; no photo of a bed bug or a sign, no table, FAQ or
  sources. Checks mattress, box spring, headboard; luggage "on a luggage rack or in the bathroom"; says switching rooms "is
  not always a safe decision"; vacuum the suitcase at home; sends readers to a registry; phone and quote buttons throughout.
- **AHLA** (hotel industry association, no author or date; facts credited to NPMA): ~800 words; no photos, table or FAQ. A
  facts list (1/4 inch, feed once a week, up to 10 months unfed, up to 500 eggs, a "sickly, sweet smell"); traveler tips
  (sheets back, seams at the corners, behind the headboard, not an adjacent room or one above or below, suitcase in a
  plastic bag, vacuum and steam it at home, wash all clothes in hot water); housekeeping tips.
- **Outside** (Jen Murphy, Jan 16, 2023): ~1,500 words, first-person story; 3 iStock/Getty photos; quotes NPMA and Ehrlich
  entomologists and a Rutgers professor; a 2017 pest-company survey figure; a registry plug; luggage in the bathroom on tile
  or an inspected rack, no hollow-legged racks, dryer on high, freezing.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Outside's caption: linear or zigzag bites "indicate bedbugs". CDC: marks look like mosquito or flea bites, random or in a
  line; EPA: bites are a poor indicator; Virginia Tech: not a good way to diagnose an infestation.
- Outside: bites appear in one to several days; most people react. CDC: as long as 14 days in some people. Kentucky:
  about 30 percent do not react even when bitten repeatedly.
- AHLA: wash all clothes in hot water (no dryer). EPA: just washing generally will not kill bed bugs; a hot dryer does.
- AHLA and Orkin: vacuum the suitcase. Kentucky: inspecting or vacuuming suitcases is less useful (bugs are hard to spot in
  luggage); heat-treat it (a hot car in summer) or discard it.
- AHLA: a "sickly, sweet smell". Kentucky: the odor is seldom apparent except in extreme infestations; do not rely on it.
- Fox: a new room "preferably on a different floor". NC Agriculture and AHLA: not a room directly above or below;
  Kentucky: a different area of the hotel.
- Orkin and Outside send readers to registries. Texas A&M calls them consumer hotel registries where guests report
  infestations: reports, not inspections. Our page names none.
