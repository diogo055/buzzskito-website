## What they get wrong or leave out (our page states the sourced version)
- STL and Google's AI answer: hairs "about as long as" the eye. Colorado State: longer than the eye on a bat bug, shorter
  on a bed bug; Iowa State: as long or longer; UC IPM: longer than the diameter of the eye.
- STL and Wikipedia name only the eastern bat bug; Colorado State names the western bat bug, *Cimex pilosellus*, too.
- WebMD: a bug bomb. UC IPM: over-the-counter foggers have been shown ineffective against bed bugs; Nebraska: beds
  usually need no insecticide once the bats are gone. STL: vacuum where bats roosted, no warning. UC IPM: never breathe
  dust from bat droppings (histoplasmosis); CDC: large amounts of droppings go to hazardous-waste professionals.
- No exclusion timing in any. UC IPM: not while flightless young may be present (May-September in California); CDC:
  fall or winter, do not trap young May-August; Iowa State: late summer to fall. None says not to handle bats (CDC).
- Sizes (WebMD 1/4-3/8 inch, STL 3-5 mm) could not be checked in a source we could open: Colorado State says "similar
  in size" to bed bugs but gives 3/8 inch for the whole group, against EPA's 3/16-1/4 inch for bed bugs. Ours gives EPA's
  bed bug size and "similar in size", no bat bug number.
