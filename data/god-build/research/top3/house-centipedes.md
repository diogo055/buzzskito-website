## What they get wrong (and the source our page uses)
- "Jaws" / "strong mouthparts with large jaws" (WSU, UArk): centipedes bite with forcipules, hollow modified front
  legs (Poison Control; Kentucky ENTFACT-645), and Kentucky says house centipedes find it hard to pierce human
  skin (ENTFACT-645) because their small mouthparts "cannot penetrate the skin very well" (ENTFACT-647).
- UArk "severe swelling and pain": MDC says a bite is generally no worse than a bee sting; MedlinePlus says
  centipede-bite symptoms most often last under 48 hours.
- Wikipedia's uncited 3-7 years for the house centipede: Penn State says females live "several years"; the 3-7
  figure in extension sources (Ohio State ENT-50) is for centipedes in general; UC IPM: known to live 6 years;
  Texas A&M: 1 to 6 years. The page says so.
- None answers the drain question (Iowa State: they do not come up through drain pipes; the one in the tub was
  trapped there) and none gives first aid or when to see a doctor.
