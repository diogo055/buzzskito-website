## What they get wrong (the page states it correctly)
- **Critter Control, young venomous snakes "typically still deadly":**
  - CDC estimates 7,000 to 8,000 venomous bites a year in the US and about 5 deaths;
  - Hayes and Morris (Loma Linda University, Toxins 2026) report that baby rattlesnakes carry less venom and cause less severe bites than adults;
  - but babies are venomous from birth (UC IPM; MDC for copperheads).
- **Critter Control, a brood means an infestation, so call pest control:**
  - VCE says snakes give no care after laying eggs or giving birth;
  - MDC says the young move about on their own in late summer and fall;
  - UF/IFAS, Virginia DWR and UGA say to leave nonvenomous snakes alone.
- **cuteness.com, pupil shape as the test:**
  - Poison Control says getting close enough to check pits and pupils is unsafe;
  - ICWDM and UF/IFAS give every feature with its exceptions;
  - MDC says triangular heads mislead.
- **cuteness.com, copperheads "7 to 10 inches":** Poison Control gives 7 to 9 inches, MDC 7 or 8.
- **cuteness.com, garter snakes "all harmless", with no bite warning:** Connecticut DEEP says garter snakes bite if captured, and the bite can be painful.
