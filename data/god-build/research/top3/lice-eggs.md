- **Cleveland Clinic (#1)**: about 1,100 words, a general head lice page (updated Apr 29, 2025). Nits: "white,
  yellowish, tan or brown", "tiny", hatch in 5 to 10 days; "might look like dandruff" but do not come off easily. No
  size, no distance rule, nothing on live vs hatched or hair casts. One drawing (a louse, a nit and a comb), no photo

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- LCA: nits are ".03 to .08 mm". CDC DPDx and the AAP: 0.8 mm by 0.3 mm; UC IPM: 1/32 inch. Off by a factor of ten.
- LCA: eggs "can be found on a person's hair, skin, clothes, and bedding". CDC DPDx: head lice cement eggs to hair
  near the scalp; it is body lice that lay eggs on clothing.
- LCA: "Dead eggs are much darker in color, or even black" (Healthline, #1 for "dead nits vs live nits", says the
  same). UC IPM: developing eggs are dark, white after hatching; AAD: a hatched egg looks clear; CDC: the empty shell
  turns a dull yellow. CDC's working test is distance from the scalp, not color.
- LCA: seal unwashable items "for a few days"; adults live "3 weeks"; adults "2-4mm". CDC: two weeks in a sealed bag;
  about 30 days on a head; adults 2 to 3 mm.
- Cleveland Clinic: hatch in "five to 10 days". CDC: about a week, 6 to 9 days (AAP: 7 to 12, by temperature).
