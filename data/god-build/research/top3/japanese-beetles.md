## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- UConn: traps "can be used to eliminate the pest". University of Kentucky research (ENTFACT-451): traps attract many
  more beetles than they catch and raise damage near the trap; Purdue E-75 does not recommend them; Colorado State: never
  shown to reduce damage to nearby plants. USDA's handbook: a trap catches only about 75% of the beetles that approach it.
- UConn: about 3/8 inch and no word of the white tufts (the one feature that settles it); genus misspelled "Popollia".
  UK: 7/16 inch with white tufts on each side; Purdue and USU: about 1/2 inch; USU: six tufts along each side.
- dimensions.com: "lifespan typically 1 year". UK: each adult lives about 30 to 45 days; the year is the whole cycle.
- Wikipedia: 15 mm (0.6 inch) long, unsourced. That is the top of the range (Colorado State also says 15 mm); UK,
  Purdue, USU, USDA and UMN give 1/3 to 1/2 inch. Our page gives the extension range.
- Not a top-3 page, but noted: MDC's field guide says grubs "descend 4-8 feet" to overwinter; USDA's handbook and USU
  say 4 to 8 inches. Our page uses inches.
