## What they get wrong or leave out (with the source that says otherwise)
- Backpacker ranks dog and wolf prints as the largest canid tracks. Size overlaps: the NH Fish and Game pocket guide
  gives domestic dogs 2 1/4 to 4 in against a coyote's 2 1/2 in front print, and NMSU CR561 says some dog tracks are
  easily confused with coyote tracks even when well defined.
- Backpacker names rabbits as hoppers but never describes a rabbit track; MDC gives cottontail front 1 in, hind 3 1/2 in,
  NY DEC the Y-shaped group, ICWDM the I-formation vs a squirrel's two-by-two.
- Maryland DNR leaves out coyotes, one of the three biggest searches here (KP: "coyote tracks" 1K-10K US).
- Common claim elsewhere (not in the top 3): an "X" between the pads marks a coyote. The NPS Mount Rainier tracking
  guide gives an X for red fox and an H shape for coyote. Our page treats it as one clue, not a test.
- Salt Lake City (rank 5-6) says a five-toed track with claws belongs to a skunk; raccoon and opossum prints also show
  claws (MDC Animal Detective: the skunk's tell is the long front nails well ahead of the toes, Maryland DNR key).
