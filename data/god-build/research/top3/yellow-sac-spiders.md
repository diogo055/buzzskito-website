## What our page does that none of them do
1. **Real, credited photos of North American yellow sac spiders where people find them**: on a textured white wall at a
   corner, inside its silk sac where a wall meets a ledge, the silk retreat under a flowerpot kept on an indoor porch, and a
   close-up of the darker jaws and foot tips. Western's are stock photos of a European species; the others are uncredited.
2. **A sourced pale-spider sorter for "white house spider"**: yellow sac, cellar, common house, woodlouse and crab spiders and
   the brown recluse, with body length, color, silk and one tell (Penn State, UC IPM, UMN, Kentucky, Mayo), a jar checklist
   and 5 look-alike cards. insectio's table has no sources and puts white crab spiders "near windows, houseplants" (UMN:
   crab spiders are rarely found indoors).
3. **The bite at the evidence's strength**: Vetter et al. 2006 (20 verified US and Australian bites, none necrotic; 39
   worldwide, one mild case, in *C. punctorium*), Penn State, Bohart Museum; care from Poison Control, Mayo and CDC.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- "Ulcerated sores" (Bug Out, Western), "necrotic... cytotoxic" (USU 2008): Vetter et al. 2006; Penn State (verified-bite
  studies failed to confirm necrotic lesions); Bohart (no necrotic sores that keep growing).
- "Builds webs" (Bug Out): Penn State, UMN: no web; the silk is a day retreat. The fiddle as the test (Bug Out): UC IPM says
  the violin is not reliable; count the eyes (8 in two rows vs 6 in pairs).
- "Only indoors by accident" (Western): Penn State: *C. mildei* lives mostly in buildings and lays eggs almost only indoors.
- insectio: the yellow sac spider as "uniform pale yellow" (Penn State: dark jaws, palps and foot tips, darker stripe).
