## The three (plus two), taken apart
- **Western Exterminator (#1)**: about 1,000 words; quick facts, life cycle, "signs of an infestation", a free-
  inspection pitch, 3 FAQs. One photo, a mining bee (the checker corrected an earlier note that called it a honey bee); no hole photo, no table, one
  source. Never mentions yellowjackets, sting first aid or allergy.
- **Gainesville Times (#2)**: retired UGA agent's column, about 650 words, no headings, no photo, no FAQ. The "many
  holes, one bee each / one hole, many bees" rule; 1/2 to 3/4 inch; March-April; 4 to 6 weeks; water, thicken turf,
  mulch; then, if "necessary", a carbaryl dust at night, naming a brand.
- **UGA Field Report / Newswire (#3)**: about 550 words, one bumble bee photo; lists bumble bees, sweat bees, digger
  bees, leafcutters, digger wasps, cicada killers; "do not present a stinging hazard"; watering, tilling, dense turf,
  mulch. No sizes, months or hole photos.
- **Bulwark (#4)**: about 1,200 words; the only table (ground bees vs yellow jackets, no sizes, no sources); no bee or
  hole photo; quote pitch.
- **bettertermite.com**: about 1,000 words, 7-18 mm size box, five uncredited gallery photos, 8 FAQs, honey bee and
  yellowjacket look-alikes; treats nests with a residual insecticide.

## What they get wrong (our page states it correctly)
- Western: its only photo is a honey bee, a hive or hollow-tree nester (Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station),
  and it never warns that MDC says some people call yellowjackets "ground bees".
- Gainesville Times: an insecticide dust "if necessary"; Clemson does not recommend insecticides, the Connecticut
  station says pesticides should not be used on ground-nesting bees, NC State: almost never necessary.
- Gainesville Times: bumble bees can "cover you with stings very quickly"; MDC: bumble bees are not aggressive and
  sting if molested or the nest is endangered; USDA agroforestry note: not aggressive unless the nest is exposed.
- Gainesville Times: 1/2 to 3/4 inch; MDC: miner bees 3/8 to 3/4, sweat bees 1/4 to 3/4; Maryland: mining bees
  range from about honey bee size down to much smaller species.
- UGA: "do not present a stinging hazard"; Clemson and Connecticut: females can sting if provoked, stepped on or
  cornered, though rarely.
- Bulwark: the bees leave "in a few months"; Maryland: flying lasts 2 to 4 weeks; Connecticut, NC Extension: 3 to 4.
- bettertermite: residual insecticide (see above); "spend the winter as pupae" for all ground bees, where Maryland
  says mining bee adults winter in the burrow.

## Three things our page does that none of them do
1. Credited real photos where people meet these insects: a mining bee burrow and mound in a lawn, a USGS mining bee,
   yellowjackets at their ground nest, a cicada killer on its mound, plus five look-alike cards. None shows a hole.
2. A sourced quick-ID table (mining bee, sweat bee, bumble bee, yellowjacket, cicada killer: size, nest, season,
   stings) under the Connecticut station's one-line traffic test, sizes in inches from MDC, Clemson and Maryland.
3. The sting at the sources' strength (only females have stingers; where Clemson and Bee Basics disagree), with

   (water, turf, mulch, organic matter) with no insecticide.
