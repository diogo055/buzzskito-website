## The top 3 (live editorial pages), taken apart
- **PURCOR Pest** (#2, no author or date; images from Aug 2025): ~1,200 words; one uncredited hero image of a rodent in a
  tunnel; no table; 5 FAQs (the last is a sales pitch); one source (PetMD). Wild rats "generally less than a year" and, a
  line later, "up to two years"; pet rats 2-4 years; "females outlive males by about 0.3 years" yet females average 2.7 and
  males 3 years; "longest recorded lifespan is nine years" with no source. Quote and coupon buttons throughout.
- **PetMD** (#3, Lauren Jones VMD, reviewed by a DVM, Apr 2024): ~1,000 words; three iStock photos; no table, no FAQ;
  4 references (VIN, LafeberVet, an exotics clinic, Guinness). Pet rats 2-4 years; wild rats "typically live less than a
  year"; maturity 37-75 days; an unsourced "each month = 2.5 human years" conversion. Nothing on roof vs Norway rats,
  breeding rate, or what a lifespan means for a rat problem.
- **A-Z Animals** (#6, Jan 2022, updated Jun 2025): ~1,000 words; three Shutterstock/iStock photos and an uncredited
  infographic; no table, no FAQ, no sources. Says wild rats average "two years" and "one to two years"; pet rats "up to six
  or seven years in extreme cases" and later "likely won't live more than five years". Oldest rat "Rodney, 7 years 4 months"
  with no source.

## What our page does that none of the three do
1. **One sourced table** (Norway rat, roof rat, pet rat, house mouse): life span wild and in captivity, maturity, pregnancy,
   litter size, litters a year, every cell from NC State, the Smithsonian's National Zoo, Texas A&M AgriLife, ICWDM, UC IPM

2. **Why a rat problem outlasts any one rat**, at the sources' strength: maturity at about 3 months, 21-23 day pregnancies,

3. **Real, credited photos where people meet rats** (a Norway rat in yard debris, a roof rat in a building's rafters, burrows

   stock or uncredited images only.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- PURCOR and A-Z contradict themselves on wild and pet life spans; sourced figures: Norway rat average one year (NC State),
  believed closer to two years in the wild (National Zoo), roof rat adults 5-18 months (Texas A&M), pet rats 18-36 months
  on average (Merck, reviewed May 2026), up to four years in human care (National Zoo).
- Mosquito Joe (#10) says rats can reproduce at 5-7 weeks. ICWDM and NC State give about 3 months for wild Norway rats; the
  Merck manual gives 6-10 weeks (males) and 8-12 weeks (females) for pet rats.
- "Oldest rat" records (7 years 4 months; nine years) appear with no primary source; the page gives none.
