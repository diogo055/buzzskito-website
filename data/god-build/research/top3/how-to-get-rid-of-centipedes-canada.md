## Claims corrected (primary sources)
- "A narrow list of minimum-risk products - mostly certain essential-oil formulations - is exempt from registration
  and sells here legally without a PCP number" (FAQ and the "Canadian Product Reality" section) and the
  "conventional insecticides" qualifier (FAQ, body, Quick Answer bullet, key facts) -> the Pest Control Products
  Regulations (s. 3, s. 4, Schedule 2) have no such exemption; the EPA's minimum-risk exemption is from US federal
  registration only; Health Canada: "It's a violation of the Pest Control Products Act to buy and use unregistered
  pesticides."
- "a modern 50-pint machine corresponds roughly to what used to be sold as a 70-pint" -> ENERGY STAR's conversion:
  old 70-pint portable about 40-45 today, old 90 about 55; test moved from 80F to 65F (June 2019).
- "'Food-grade' DE ... carries no pesticide registration, no PCP number" and "the registered version costs only a
  few dollars more" -> Doktor Doom Be Green Premium Food Grade Diatomaceous Earth is registered (32561); the PCP
  number is the test; Safer's 32278 lists centipedes; price claim removed.
- "the 30-50% summer indoor range Health Canada publishes" -> Health Canada's mould page gives 30-50% with no
  season (2023-02-13).
- "the safe default" -> "the sensible default".
