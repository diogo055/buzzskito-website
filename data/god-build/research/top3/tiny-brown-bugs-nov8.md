## Who ranks (WebSearch, US, Nov 8; it is not Google, so read it as "who is strong", not exact positions)
Skipped: keyword-spam pages on hijacked subdomains (several per search), Reddit, Quora, ThriftyFun and Ask Extension threads.
1. **Bob Vila, "14 Common Tiny Brown Bugs in the House"** (bobvila.com/articles/small-tiny-brown-bugs-in-house, updated Jun 30, 2025).
   Wins on brand and breadth: 14 bugs (adds silverfish, firebrats, pantry moths, fruit flies, spiders, stink bugs), a
   "how to get rid of" list under each, ~16 credited stock photos, ~2,000 words. No table, no FAQ, no scale, no "sudden" section.
2. **Orkin, "What are these small, round, brown bugs in my house?"** (Ask Orkin). Wins on domain strength and an exact
   question title. ~300 words about one bug (furniture carpet beetle), no photo of the real thing, no sources (unchanged since Oct 20).
3. **Leafy Place, "Small Brown Bugs (With Pictures and Names)"** (leafyplace.com/small-brown-bugs, Jun 2023). Wins the
   singular/plural head terms ("small brown bug"): ~19 bugs each with a photo and a size line, and it names earwigs and
   centipedes as the "long skinny brown bug". No credits, no scale, one source (CDC), several wrong unit conversions.
Also strong: Alabama Extension (ACES) "What are the small brown beetles in my home? Drugstore beetles" (~750 words, one
photo, no sources) and insectsexplained.com; UMD Extension's household pest key was in Google's top 3 on Oct 16.

## What they give the searcher that we did not
- An answer to "long, skinny brown bug": Leafy Place (earwigs, centipedes) and Bob Vila (silverfish, firebrats). We had none.
- Silverfish, firebrats, earwigs, centipedes, millipedes and stink bugs as named suspects. We had none of the six.
- Nobody has a real "all of a sudden" section either (Bob Vila only says pantry moths appear "unexpectedly"), but it is our
  biggest query and our only answer was one FAQ.

## What we give that they do not
- Sorted by where you found it, with a quick-ID table of sourced sizes in inches; real, credited photos with scale bars.
- Which ones bite, stated from extension and EPA/CDC sources; look-alike cards; 10 FAQs; 15 (now 19) sources.
- Their errors stay out: clover mites "3/4 cm" (Penn State: about 1/32 inch), stink bugs as "tiny" (Kentucky: about 5/8 inch).

## What the searcher needs first
1. What is it (the six usual suspects): answered in the first two sentences already, so the quick answer is unchanged.
2. Why now ("all of a sudden"): new section, second on the page.
3. If it is long and skinny: which of earwig, millipede, house centipede, silverfish, carpet beetle larva.
