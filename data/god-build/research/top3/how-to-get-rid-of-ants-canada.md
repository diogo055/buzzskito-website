## The top 3, taken apart
1. **Health Canada, Ants** (page details 2017-06-19): short and authoritative. Baits: "Place bait stations directly in
   the path of foraging ants, but out of the reach of children and pets."; "Use plenty of bait stations. Two different
   baits at the same time will give better results."; "Keep baits available for at least two weeks."; "Do not use
   chemical sprays to kill ants while using a bait system, or the bait system will not work." Spraying indoors may
   "encourage the colony to split into multiple new colonies". Species: carpenter, little black, odorous house, thief and
   pharaoh ants. "Always look for a Pest Control Products (PCP) number on the label". Lacks: products, species-by-species
   choices, outdoor and provincial rules, photos.
2. **Orkin Canada, ant control** (about 800-1,000 words of article in 2,500 with menus): species damage, a four-question
   FAQ, free-estimate forms. "Most DIY treatments only target the ants you can see." No product, no regulation, no
   sources; one overstated claim ("the stings are fatal").
3. **Terminix Canada, ants pest library** (about 1,500 words): five species profiles, prevention tips, a seven-question
   FAQ; "Homemade ant killers and DIY ant traps aren't nearly as effective as our solutions." No regulation, no products,

## Claims on our page checked
- "Indoor baiting is not affected by the cosmetic ban": true (Ontario, "inside buildings").
- Granular bait "runs straight into provincial cosmetic-pesticide rules on lawns and gardens": true for ants in grass
  ("a cosmetic use and would be prohibited"); a product labelled only for around the outside of a home falls under the
  structural exception. Added, not changed.
- Spraying "causes budding" in multi-queen ants: consistent with Health Canada ("encourage the colony to split").
- Box claim (not edited): AwardRow "Best for Grease Feeders", Combat-style solid stations "domestic-class" with a Canadian
  PCP number; the only Combat ant registration was cancelled in 2006 (same finding as /blog/best-ant-killer).
- "The practical rule that keeps you safe" (granular paragraph): wording, not a product claim; changed to "keeps you
  within the rules" to meet the no-"safe" rule.
