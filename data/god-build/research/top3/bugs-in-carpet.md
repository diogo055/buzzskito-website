## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Home Depot: larvae "take over a year to grow into adults". UC IPM's table gives 70-94 days for the furniture carpet beetle's
  larval stage and 166-330 for the black carpet beetle (220-630 only for the varied).
- Home Depot recommends boric acid on carpets, a fogger, perimeter granules, vinegar, essential oils and rubbing alcohol. UC IPM:
  insecticides may not be necessary if food sources are removed and areas kept clean; Kentucky: sprays are often of limited use
  and clothing or bedding should not be treated. Our page names no product.
- Home Depot: adults "less than an inch long". Kentucky: 1/16 to 1/8 inch; UC IPM: black carpet beetle 1/8 to 3/16 inch.
- Hunker treats the beetles in general as the fabric eaters and says females lay eggs near light. UC IPM and Kentucky: adults eat
  pollen and do not damage fabric; the larvae feed in dark, undisturbed places.
- Hawx: carpet bed bugs mean "likely" a severe problem. Kentucky: early infestations stay near beds, and finding the bugs or their
  signs confirms it (often needing a professional); no source opened grades severity by carpet finds, so our page does not.
