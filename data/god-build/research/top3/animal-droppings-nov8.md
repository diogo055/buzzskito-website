## Who ranks (WebSearch, US, Nov 8; Google differs: our page came up first for "...chart by photo" in WebSearch)
Skipped: UK pages (Wildlife Trusts, Mammal Society), Australian state pages, spam "chart" pages, an App Store listing.
1. **Poo Squad, "Backyard Animal Poop Identification Guide (With Photos)"** (Jun 2025; #2 on Google Oct 20). ~3,500 words,
   a chart by diet group, "what's inside" clues (seeds, fur, white uric acid), safety checklist, 5 light FAQs. No sources,
   no sizes, no credits or scale; no raccoon, opossum, skunk or squirrel photos; a dog-waste service pitch.
2. **ICWDM droppings key** (icwdm.org; #3 on Google Oct 20). The old scat-ID URL now 404s; its "Brown or black droppings"
   page sorts by shape and adds what nobody else has: raccoon 2-3 inches by 1/2 inch, shrews, toads under floodlights,
   caterpillar droppings, flying squirrels, scale photos with coins.
3. **Orvik, "Animal Poop Identification Chart: Scat Key by Shape & Size"** (updated Sep 3, 2026). A free filter tool over
   30 North American animals with sizes (in cm), 7 FAQs (rat vs squirrel in the attic, deer vs rabbit pellets, white part,
   freshness), 3 CDC sources. No photos. A "finish the task" page.
Also: A-Z Animals' North American scat field guide, ngpest.com, Critter Control, Earthkind's pest droppings chart (rodent
and insect searches; most results for those two were spam).

## What they give the searcher that we did not
- A side-by-side of small droppings (rodent vs bat vs insect): Orvik's filter and Earthkind's chart do this; our facts were
  spread over three sections, and the chart lacked squirrel droppings.
- More animals: squirrel (with a size), shrew, toad, caterpillar (ICWDM); skunk, fox, goose, dog and cat (Orvik, Poo Squad).
- ICWDM's raccoon length and width.

## What we give that they do not
- Real, credited photos at scale (coyote, raccoon, deer, rabbit, bat, lizard, rodent droppings); a sourced 12-row chart;
  place-by-place sections; CDC cleanup and health steps at CDC's strength; links to every animal's own page; 10 FAQs.

## What the searcher needs first
The chart (it sits right under the answer), then "is it a rodent, bat or insect?" for small droppings, then safety.
