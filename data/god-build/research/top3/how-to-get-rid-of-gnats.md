## The three, taken apart
1. **Scotts, "How to Kill and Get Rid of Fungus Gnats Indoors".** About 800 words, a stock hero photo, no gnat photo, no
   table, no FAQ, no sources. Fungus gnats only: 1/8 inch adults, 1/4 inch larvae, "100-300 eggs". Sells an indoor
   insecticide in a product box. Recommends an apple cider vinegar trap for fungus gnats.
2. **diypestcontrol.com, "Gnat Traps: 7 Ways to Catch and Kill Gnats Fast"** (Jun 2, 2026; a pest-product retailer).
   About 3,000 words, no images at all, one comparison table (fruit fly, fungus gnat, drain fly), 5 FAQs, links to Penn
   State, Colorado State, Wisconsin, Arizona and Cornell. No phorid flies, no biting gnats.
3. **Orkin, "How To Get Rid of Gnats in My House?"** About 350 words, three small thumbnails (fungus gnat, fruit fly,
   phorid fly), no scale, no table, no sources. Skips drain flies. Vinegar pan and aerosol spray for fruit flies.
- Southern Living (Getty photo, about 700 words, no table): drain, fungus gnat, fruit and phorid flies in a paragraph each.

## What our page does that none of them do
1. **Identification first, with real credited photos and a quick-ID table**: fungus gnat, fruit fly, drain fly (on a
   wall), drain fly larva, phorid fly (on fruit, 1 mm scale bar) and a biting midge on skin, sizes in inches from Texas
   A&M, UC IPM, Kentucky, Montana State and Iowa State, plus a gnat-vs-fruit-fly-vs-drain-fly trait table. Orkin has three
   thumbnails, the other two no gnat photo at all.
2. **The fix for each gnat at its source, with the test that confirms it**: potato chunks for fungus gnat larvae (UC
   IPM), a bag or tape over the drain overnight (Kentucky, Texas A&M, UMN), and the sewer-line check for phorid flies
   (UMD, Texas A&M). Wisconsin's point that vinegar traps do not work for fungus gnats answers "why won't the gnats go
   into the vinegar?".
3. **Pesticides by class and active ingredient, at the sources' strength**: Bti (NPIC, UC IPM, Wisconsin), the
   *Steinernema feltiae* nematode, the growth regulator S-hydroprene for drains, pyrethrins and pyrethroids for
   temporary knockdown, no fogging, and EPA's "the label is the law". Plus the outdoor biters (biting midges, black
   flies) that no top-3 page separates from house gnats, and 10 FAQs from People also ask.

## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- Scotts recommends a vinegar trap for fungus gnats. Wisconsin: vinegar traps used for fruit flies do not work for
  monitoring fungus gnats; Colorado State: the adults are attracted to yellow sticky cards.
- diypestcontrol says Colorado State notes vinegar is less effective on fungus gnats. Colorado State's page does not
  mention vinegar (Wisconsin says it).
- diypestcontrol: a half-inch sand layer "blocks egg laying". Wisconsin: the layer keeps the surface drier and less
  attractive for egg laying.
- diypestcontrol: Bti is "non-toxic to people, pets, and plants". NPIC: little evidence of illness in people, but some
  products have irritated eyes and skin, and other ingredients can pose more risk than the Bt itself.
- Orkin: fungus gnat larvae "generally don't harm roots". UC IPM: in large numbers they damage roots and stunt seedlings
  and young plants.
- Southern Living: let the plant dry out completely, and boiling water kills drain gnats. Colorado State and UC IPM:
  let the top 1 to 2 inches (the surface) dry; UMN: hot or boiling water, bleach and drain cleaners are not effective.
- Scotts gives 100 to 300 eggs per fungus gnat; Colorado State gives up to 200. Our page uses Colorado State.
