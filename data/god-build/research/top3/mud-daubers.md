## The three, taken apart
1. **Wikipedia, "Sceliphrini"** (~850 words of prose). Nesting, food, airplane pitot-tube accidents. 4 photos (one
   wasp, three nests in Italy, Czech Republic, India), none on a US house, no scale. One size (25 mm). No table, no
   FAQ, no removal, no sting care. Two "citation needed" tags; prey claims lean on a personal web page. It is about
   the Sceliphron/Chalybion tribe only, so the organ-pipe mud dauber (Trypoxylon) is missing.
2. **MDC, "Mud Daubers"** (~900 words; also one of our sources). Best of the three: three kinds, nests, prey (blue
   mud daubers use black widows), winter in the nest, "do not aggressively sting". One wasp photo and one nest with
   spiders. No sting care or when-to-get-help, no "is this nest active?", removal is only "consider allowing" nests.
3. **CSU Extension Pueblo County** (~475 words, a Master Gardener's essay). Black-and-yellow mud dauber only, no size,
   3 photos. Removal: destroy nests winter to mid-spring. Says they "can sting if nests are approached too closely"
   and that yellowjacket venom is about ten times as potent, with no source.

## Things our page does that none of the three do
1. **The three nest types side by side, each with a real photo**: black-and-yellow mud daubers on nests on a garage
   wall and under a house light fixture, organ-pipe tubes on concrete, a blue mud dauber on a reused nest.
2. **A sourced quick-ID table** (three mud daubers, paper wasp, yellowjacket, cicada killer): waist and color, nest,
   sting risk, from MDC, Purdue, Clemson, Mississippi State and MU Extension; plus look-alike cards.
3. **Reading a nest**: a hole chewed through a cell means the adult left (Clemson, Mississippi State); sealed cells
   hold young wasps through winter (MDC, MU, Purdue); up to 25 spiders in a cell (Texas A&M).
4. **The sting at the sources' strength**: rarely, only when handled or pinned (MU, MDC, UF, Purdue), painful
   (Mississippi State; Clemson's 1-of-4 pain rating), plus Mayo/CDC care and Mayo/AAD emergency signs.
5. **Removal as extension pages give it**: leave tolerable nests (MDC, Purdue); scrape with a putty knife and dispose
   of the nest so no wasps emerge next year (Purdue); wash off the soil, no insecticide needed (MU); spray only for
   allergies or heavy traffic, per Clemson. FAQs from People also ask.

## What they get wrong or leave out (our page states it from the source)
- CSU Pueblo's "can sting if nests are approached too closely": MU, Purdue and MDC say mud daubers do not defend
  their nests and sting only if handled or pinned against the skin; organ-pipe males that buzz have no stinger (MDC).
- CSU Pueblo's "ten times as potent" yellowjacket venom has no source on the page and none of ours says it: left out.
- Wikipedia's single 25 mm size: MDC gives 1/2 to 1 inch, MU 1/2 to 1 1/4 inches, Texas A&M 3/4 to 1 inch.
