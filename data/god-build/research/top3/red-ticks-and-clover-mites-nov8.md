## The three, taken apart
- **Terminix** (~900 words, updated Apr 15, 2026): "key takeaways" box; "no naturally occurring species of bright red tick";
  names spider mite, red velvet mite, clover mite and chigger larvae; velvet mite "a fifth of an inch"; tick nymph = poppy
  seed, adult = apple seed; tick bite vs mite bite; says a little red tick is "likely a soft tick" that turns red after a
  meal (unsourced); prevention lists. One "tick vs mite" image. No sources, no table, no FAQ.
- **USDA APHIS red sheep tick** (3 pages): the one tick named red. Identified in the US in summer 2020, from ticks
  collected on vegetation on Block Island, Rhode Island, 2013-2020; native to Eurasia and Africa; hosts livestock, dogs,
  humans; diseases mostly of livestock. No size or color. One photo.
- **A-Z Animals** (~950 words, 2022): "no naturally occurring red tick"; red = feeding, "most likely a soft tick";
  **adult female deer tick has a red and black pattern**; American dog tick; "some brown dog ticks look more red than
  brown"; lone star tick. 4 stock photos (incl. a deer tick). No sizes, table, FAQ or sources.

## What they give that we did not (before Nov 8)
1. **The adult female blacklegged (deer) tick as the "small red tick"**: A-Z says it; our page said unfed deer ticks are
   "dark" (true of males and nymphs only). Ohio State ENT-0096: red-orange body around a dark brown shield. Biggest gap.
2. **Red velvet mites** (Terminix names them; our page never did). The likeliest "bright red tick" on soil or rocks.
3. **Brown dog tick** (A-Z, garbled): the reddish-brown tick that lives indoors with dogs (UF/IFAS IN378).
4. **The red sheep tick** (USDA): the only tick in the results actually named red.
5. A direct answer to "are red ticks dangerous": none of our headings asked it.
Not taken: Terminix's "red tick = soft tick" claim (no source found that says soft ticks are the usual red tick).

## What we give that they do not

## What the searcher needs first
Is my red bug a tick, which one, and is it dangerous. Sort by where it was: swarming a sunny wall (clover mite), velvety on
soil (velvet mite), small red-and-dark tick crawling on you (female deer tick: remove it), swollen on skin (fed tick).
