## The three, taken apart

- **Mayo Clinic, Bedbugs: Symptoms and causes (Jan 5, 2024).** About 600 words plus a diagnosis page; one photo of the bug and one of bites; no table, no FAQ. A four-line bite description (inflamed spots with a darker center, itchy, rough line or cluster, face, neck, arms and hands), a one-line "see a doctor" for allergic or severe skin reactions, signs on the second page. Good but thin: no timing, no look-alike comparison, no photos of the signs.
- **Cleveland Clinic, Bed Bugs (last updated 10/14/2022).** About 1,800 words in Q&A headings; one photo of bugs on furniture, no bite photo; no table. Lists bite looks (pimple-like bump, blister, hives; line, zigzag or random). Gets wrong or overstates: says marks take "one to a couple of days" to appear (CDC: as long as 14 days in some people); says bed bugs "don't carry or spread disease" (CDC: "not known to spread diseases"; Kentucky: they can harbor pathogens, transmission unproven and considered unlikely); names a product strength (hydrocortisone 1%).
- **Healthline, Bedbug bites (updated Mar 3, 2026).** About 2,000 words; comparison photos of flea bites, mosquito bites and hives but no clearly identified bed bug bite photo; no table, no FAQ. Says bed bugs "tend to bite the upper parts of your body" after saying they can bite any part (Kentucky: any skin exposed while sleeping); presents lines and clusters as a bed bug signature (EPA: bites are a poor indicator of an infestation); gives a 113°F laundry figure that Mayo's 120°F does not match.

## Facts to state correctly

- Delay: one to several days for most people, as long as 14 days in some (CDC); not "one to a couple of days".
- Disease: "not known to spread diseases to people" (CDC); not "cannot carry disease".
- Pattern: AAD's clusters of 3 to 5 in a zigzag; CDC's random or straight line; Mayo's rough line or cluster; none of it is proof (EPA).
- Where: any skin exposed while sleeping (Kentucky; CDC: face, neck, arms, hands or any other body parts).
