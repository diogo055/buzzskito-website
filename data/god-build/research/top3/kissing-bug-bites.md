## What they get wrong (our page states the sourced version)
- WebMD: Chagas "infects about 300,000 people" in the US. CDC's current page: an estimated 280,000.
- WebMD: "about a third" develop serious heart or intestinal disease. CDC: about 20-30% of those infected.
- WebMD and Healthline: bites are mostly on the face, near the mouth. Texas A&M: that was an old belief, and the bugs bite
  anywhere they can reach. The University of Arizona: often the face, also the neck, arms and legs. Our page gives both.
- Google's AI Overview lists the eyelids "(Romaña's sign)" as a bite location. CDC: Romaña's sign is eyelid swelling, a sign
  of acute Chagas disease, usually from droppings rubbed into the eye or into a bite near it.
- News stories and old pages repeat CDC's retired advice to drop a found bug in rubbing alcohol (that CDC FAQ now returns 404).
  Texas DSHS, which tests bugs for Texans, says to use no alcohol or other preservatives. Our page gives Texas A&M's freezing
  step and the DSHS rule.
- MedlinePlus (not in the top 3) says Chagas spreads "by the bite". CDC and Texas A&M: through the droppings. Not cited.
