## The top 3 editorial results, taken apart
1. **Screen Mobile**, "What are No See Ums & How To Get Rid Of Them" (screen franchise blog). About 1,000 words;
   one uncredited header image, no alt text; h2s: what are they, where found, identifying a bite, how to get rid
   of them (climate control, CO2 traps), no-see-um screens (sales pitch). No table, no FAQ, no sources.
   Wrong or unsupported: life stages listed "eggs, pupae, larvae, adults" (UF/IFAS: egg, larva, pupa, adult);
   "less than 1/8 inch" (UF/IFAS: usually 0.04 to 0.08 inch); bites "much more severe" than mosquito bites (no
   source); says no-see-ums and sand flies are different species, though UF/IFAS lists "sand flies" as a name
   for biting midges; no mesh size anywhere, on a screen company's page.
2. **The Bug Squad**, "No See Ums - Appearance, Bites And Getting Rid Of Them" (Sep 2022). About 2,400 words;
   two body images of unknown origin, no scale; no table; 4 FAQs (bite through clothing, live in bed or hair,
   citronella, lifespan). Cites Purdue for CO2 attraction, then WHO and PMC pages on leishmaniasis and
   Phlebotomus: a different insect family (UF/IFAS: biting midges are Ceratopogonidae). Size inconsistent
   (0.04 to 0.12 inch, then 0.04 inch). DIY Listerine spray and vinegar or yeast traps with no evidence
   (UF/IFAS IN1451: plant-oil midge repellents have not been rigorously tested). No mesh size, no US regions.
3. **A-Z Animals**, "No See Ums" (encyclopedia template, updated May 2024). About 2,500 to 3,000 core words;
   4+ Shutterstock stock photos, none with scale; stat boxes with errors: lifespan "2 years" beside adults
   "2-30 days", "found in 31 countries" beside "everywhere", an unexplained "62 mi", placeholder "0 in"
   sizes; 13 FAQs. Sources: Wikipedia, Mosquito Nix, SWAT Mosquito (2021). Says they slip through standard
   screens but gives no mesh. Lists Oropouche and other diseases with no US context.
Also read: Mosquitonix (company page, no sources; misting "highly effective": NC State says sprays are only
temporarily effective because adults rapidly reinvade) and Today's Homeowner (no photos; "up to 0.03 inches"
against its own 1-3 mm; FAQ calls DEET and picaridin pesticides that kill instantly, which CDC lists as
repellents). Unreadable: Gardening Know How (body did not load), J.C. Ehrlich (403).

## What our page does that none of the three do
1. Real, credited photos: a research-grade Culicoides furens under a microscope (CC0 lead), a biting midge on
   skin, a fungus gnat beside a US penny for scale, a salt-marsh mosquito on a vest, and CDC's bite photo.
2. Screen numbers from the sources: standard 16x16 and 18x16 mesh lets them through (U. of Arizona); CDC says
   look for 20x20; Arizona says 30x30 where possible; UF/IFAS on no-see-um screen.
3. A sourced quick-ID table (no-see-um, mosquito, black fly, fungus gnat, fruit fly) plus look-alike cards.
4. Where they live in the US, region by region (coasts, Florida, eastern tree holes, the Southwest, Montana,
   California's valley black gnat), and when, by region.
5. Disease at CDC strength: no locally acquired Oropouche in the US, travel-associated counts, the eastern
   tree-hole vector; livestock bluetongue and EHD at UF/IFAS and Montana State strength.
6. An honest answer on yard spraying (NC State, U. of Arizona, UF/IFAS) and on repellents (help, not complete).
