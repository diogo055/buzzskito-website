# What ranks for US state tick, mosquito and chigger searches (studied Oct 14, 2026)

Method: WebSearch (US results, not Google itself) for ten queries, then the top non-government
and government pages opened and read. No People Also Ask data; questions are inferred from
titles, headings and FAQs. 33 of 44 fetches returned a page.

## The field
- **pestworld.org ranks in all five tick queries with one ~350-450-word template**: question
  headings, the state name swapped in, no date, an exterminator box. On the New York page it
  names Michigan.
- 9 of 33 pages are undated; dated ones mostly run 2011 to 2024.
- **No pest, news or publisher page embeds a state county map or a county table.**
- Original identification photos are rare (Penn State, UGA Circular 937, probably Hawx); the
  rest use wire or stock photos. No chigger page has a real bite photo.
- Only three pages have FAQ blocks (HomeGrail, Native Pest, Mosquito Joe's sales questions).
- Government and extension pages: Penn State (~3,200 words, data 2012-2019), OSU Ohioline
  (2013), UGA Circular 937 (2022, real photos), CDC EID Texas study (Aug 2026: lone star 48.9%,
  dog 21.2%, Gulf Coast 11.9%, blacklegged 10.0% of 1,625 ticks, 2014-2021). State data that
  ranks nowhere: Ohio ODH county maps and counts, NYS DOH 2025 report and regional risk map,
  Texas DSHS West Nile counts (455 cases, 56 deaths in 2024, provisional).

## Ticks in a state: what the page must carry (priority order)
1. A 60-word answer plus an at-a-glance box: species ranked on a state measure, counties
   established, latest case count with its year, the season.
2. **Our own county map and a plain-text county table** (CDC county status table and the
   state's map). None of the 33 pages has one.
3. Disease counts by year and top counties, with the residence-versus-exposure caveat;
   pathogen-in-tick data where CDC or the state publishes it.
4. A species table with real macro photos beside a ruler, by life stage; a real bite photo.
5. A month-by-species season chart from state sources, with the temperature threshold
   (40 to 45 °F in the pages read).
6. State services: free identification lab (Penn State, Michigan), where to report Asian
   longhorned ticks, whether the state tests ticks.
7. Freshness: "data through <month year>" on each table, a visible updated date, 5 to 8
   primary sources, 6 to 8 FAQs.
Avoid: boilerplate with the state name swapped, stock photos, statistics frozen in 2018,
another state's name left in, quote CTAs, retailer links, "a bump means infection", PDF-only
sources.

## Mosquitoes in a state
1. A regional season calendar (month-by-zone strip) from NOAA normals, rainfall and
   vector-control dates (Arizona: low desert, high desert, high country; Texas: Gulf, Hill
   Country, Panhandle; Florida: north, central, south).
2. Species with real photos, when each bites, what it carries.
3. Disease counts by year from the state health department; the date of this year's first case.
4. Weather: how monsoon, wet springs or warm winters moved the season in the last three years.
5. Links to county vector-control programs and spray calendars (Maricopa's trap map).
6. A dated season label and FAQ.
Avoid: leading with a pest-company city ranking, single-city framing, agency attributions not
verified (Arizona: one pest site credits ADHS with April-October; Maricopa's FAQ says May-October).

## Chiggers in a state (possible later series)
Straight yes/rare/no first, species and range (Missouri: Eutrombicula alfreddugesi and
E. splendens), month calendar (MU Extension: 2-3 generations, May to first killing frost), bite
timeline and real bite photos (no page has either), prevention and care, look-alike table.
Avoid folk remedies (nail polish), "burrows under skin", pesticide advice beyond the label.

## Quick wins
chiggers in utah (junk results; little modern data), when is mosquito season in arizona,
mosquitoes in texas (nothing explanatory ranks), ticks in ohio, types of ticks in georgia,
chiggers in missouri. Harder: Pennsylvania (Penn State), New York (state agencies), Texas ticks
(AgriLife, CDC), Florida season (many local pages).
