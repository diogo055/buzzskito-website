# bettertermite.com teardown: what Google rewards there, and what we should copy

Written 2026-10-16. **Analysis only.** Nothing on our site was changed; this file is the only repo edit. Sources: the Ahrefs API (estimates; US unless stated; **6,893 units used of the 12,000 allowed**), their live site read in the built-in browser (15 pages measured, 14 images inspected), their sitemap and llms.txt, the Internet Archive (their old WordPress sitemaps and page snapshots back to 2023), Google's Search Status Dashboard and Google's own documentation. Ahrefs traffic is an estimate: on our own site it lands within x0.8-1.4 of Search Console (path-to-50k.md, section 2).

## Summary for the owner

**Who they are.** Better Termite & Pest Control is a family pest company in Alexandria, Virginia, in business since 1968, with about 1,800 reviews. Their website went from about **1,100 Google visits a month in June 2025 to about 56,000 in October 2025**, and has run at 65,000-105,000 a month through 2026 (Ahrefs). About 94% of it is from the US. They run **no ads**: the pages exist to make the phone ring.

**What they do right, in 7 points**

1. **They write the exact words a worried homeowner types.** "Roach poop", "baby roaches", "tiny black bugs in bathroom", "fleas in hair", "wasp vs hornet". One search, one page, and the page title starts with those words.
2. **Almost everything is identification.** About 80% of their Google traffic comes from pages that help someone name a bug: photos of droppings, eggs, babies and bites; "tiny black bugs in the bathroom" pages; look-alikes; and "X vs Y" comparisons. Questions, how-to, cost and local pages are small.
3. **Some page types almost always work for them.** More than half of their pages about droppings, eggs, babies, larvae and bites earn 100+ visits a month (25 of 43). So do "tiny bugs in a room" pages (18 of 31) and look-alikes (5 of 9). "X vs Y" pages: 36 of 87. Their 757-page bug encyclopedia: 17 of 757 (2%). Their 392 city pages: none.
4. **They published fast, in one burst.** In March 2025 their blog had 12 posts. By the end of August it had about 290: 97 in July and about 120 in August alone, three to four a day. Traffic followed one to four months later. **27 of their 35 biggest posts today were published in July and August 2025.**
5. **They cover whole families, not single pages.** Cockroaches alone: poop, eggs, babies, albino, flying, smell, big roaches, plus "vs" pages against water bugs, wood roaches, palmetto bugs, Asian roaches and ground beetles. About 20 cockroach pages are in their top 140.
6. **Every page looks like a real company wrote it.** Real photos of bugs where people find them (on tile, in a corner, next to a ruler, on skin), a named technician with his photo and credentials, a short summary at the top, 8-12 questions answered at the bottom, links to university extension sites. They refreshed most of their winners in September and October 2026.
7. **Links were not the reason.** Their best posts have 0-3 other websites linking to them (one has about 40, all but one of them not followed). The number of websites linking to the domain stayed flat (about 410-450) the whole time traffic grew more than 50-fold; the jump in links came a year later, and much of it is automated spam.

**What that means for us.** Our guides are already as long, as well sourced and as photo-rich as theirs, and our sourcing and photo licensing are better. What differs is **what we choose to write, and how fast**: they put up hundreds of identification pages in a few months, in the page types that work, family by family. That is the part to copy. Their shortcuts (one technician named as the author of about 370 posts, near-duplicate pages, bought links, a chart drawn in an image-generator style, photos with no credit) are the part not to copy.

**What we cannot copy.** They are a .com and a 58-year-old US company that already had about 450 linking websites before the growth. We are a .ca, and Google says a country domain is a strong signal of which country a site is for; our Domain Rating is 1.9 with 55 followed links. Their numbers are a ceiling for us, not a forecast.

## What we should copy, ranked by expected impact

### 1. Pick page types by their hit rate, and build whole families (highest impact)

- **Change:** make at least 70% of the new US pages (path-to-50k step 4) one of four types:
  1. **signs and life stages, with photos:** droppings, eggs, babies or nymphs, larvae, bites, shed skins, nests;
  2. **"tiny/little [colour] bugs in [room]"** (bathroom, kitchen, bed, basement, car);
  3. **look-alikes** ("bugs that look like X");
  4. **"X vs Y"**, one page per pair.
- Build them **family by family** (cockroaches, ants, beetles and pantry pests, spiders, flies and gnats, wasps and bees, fleas, bed bugs and carpet beetles, rodents): each family complete within 2-4 weeks, every page linking to its siblings.
- **Evidence:** section 3c. Hit rates for these four types: 58%, 58%, 56% and 41%. Questions: 26%. How-to and product pages: 24%. Encyclopedia species pages: 2%. City pages: 0%. In their top 140 pages (83% of their traffic), the four types carry about 60% of the visits, and identification of every kind about 80%.
- **The biggest single gap is "X vs Y".** They have 87 such pages and 36 earn 100+ a month (appendix A lists them). We have about 9 identification comparisons live (6 pest pairs such as rat vs mouse, 3 bite pairs) and about 8 more in our plans.
- **Caveat:** topic and page type are mixed together in these numbers (ops skill rule 10), and their domain is far stronger than ours. Our own US guides are read on Dec 15 (path-to-50k caveat 1). Scale this only if those pages average 20+ clicks a month.

### 2. Publish each family before the season, in a concentrated burst

- **Change:** keep the plan's 25-35 extra pages a week from November to mid-March, but **order the queue by family** so that each family goes live complete and cross-linked. Finish the summer families (wasps, ants, fleas, ticks, mosquitoes, flies) by March 15.
- **Evidence:** section 1. Posts went from 12 (March 2025) to about 290 (October 2025): 18 in April, 18 in May, 34 in June, 97 in July and about 120 in August. Traffic: 1,081 (June) → 7,460 → 21,690 → 41,277 → 56,156 → 73,622 (November). 27 of their 35 biggest posts are from July and August 2025. Their pages took one to four months to pay, the same curve as ours (10/35/65/85% of mature traffic in months 1-4).
- **Caveat:** they did it with persona authorship and no visible fact-checking (see "not to copy"). Our pace must keep the fact-check and real-photo pipeline.

### 3. Five changes to the guide template (moderate impact, cheap)

- **(a) A quick-ID table right after the Quick Answer**, one row per suspect: size, colour, where it is found, crawls/jumps/flies, bites or not. Their biggest page (house spiders) opens with exactly this table. Our tiny-black-bugs page has its "where you found it" table halfway down.
- **(b) At least two "where you find it" photos per page**: the bug on tile, carpet, a wall or skin, or next to a ruler or a coin, as a homeowner sees it. Nine of the twelve bug photos we inspected on their pages are like this, most taken with a phone. Ours lean to specimen shots: 5 of the 8 photos on tiny-black-bugs have a scale bar or a lab background. Keep our licence and credit rules; iNaturalist CC0 and CC BY observations often show bugs indoors.
- **(c) 8-10 questions in the FAQ instead of 6**, taken from Google's "People also ask". Theirs carry 8-12 (median 9).
- **(d) A "Commonly confused with" row of 3-6 photo cards** linking sibling pages. Their species pages and newer posts do this. It doubles as internal links (point 4).
- **(e) A named human reviewer, only when it is true.** Their author box (face, years in the trade, membership of the state pest association) is a trust signal we lack. When Alex has actually read and approved a page, add `reviewedBy` and `reviewedOn` (the template already supports both) and a short, true credential line (Ontario pesticide licence, 150 five-star reviews). Never write invented first-person experience.

### 4. Give every new identification page 10+ internal links pointing in (moderate)

- **Evidence:** their top posts have 7-21 internal links pointing in (Ahrefs). On our own site, pages with 10-19 inbound internal links get 3.4x the impressions of pages with 0-4 (ops skill rule 11). Their posts each carry 33-51 internal links (median 39), most of them in fixed blocks; ours carry 22-25. In-text links are about the same (their median 11, ours 11-14).
- **Change:** each family gets a hub with a photo grid (their spider hub has 59 photo cards and 285 internal links), plus the sibling cards from 3(d), plus in-text links. Check the count before each release.

### 5. Build the domain's trust floor (necessary, slow)

- **Evidence:** their winning pages needed no links of their own, but the domain started the growth with about 450 linking websites and a Domain Rating of 14-26, mostly the ordinary footprint of an old local business: the BBB, Yellow Pages and YP, a chamber of commerce site, Expertise.com, ThreeBestRated, BirdEye, ProvenExpert, Crunchbase. We have 55 followed links.
- **Change:** do what path-to-50k 5d already lists, in this order: business citations a real five-star Ontario company qualifies for, association membership listings, supplier and dealer pages, local press follow-ups, our own pest photos offered under CC BY. Their editorial links (wikiHow, Wikipedia, the University of Cambridge, extension.org, Grokipedia) arrived after the traffic: they are a result, not a starting point.

### 6. Refresh the winners once a year, before the season (low to moderate)

- **Evidence:** 23 of their top 35 posts show an update in September-October 2026, and every page shows both the published and the updated date. Since 2025 their winners have gained summary boxes, first-person intros and far more extension citations (bed bugs: 3 external links in July 2025, 16 now; baby roaches: 4, now 16).
- **Change:** one refresh pass in March-April on pages that already rank 4-20 (path-to-50k step 2). Change the date only when the content really changes (our sitemap rule).

### 7. Test one regional identification page (speculative)

- **Evidence:** their newest big page, "Virginia House Spiders: 7 Species You Find Indoors", went up on Sep 15, 2026 and was **#1 in the US for "house spider"** (34,000 searches a month in Ahrefs) by Oct 9, with no links. Ahrefs puts it at about 8,500 visits a month: 10% of the whole site. The results page is weak: Facebook groups, small pest companies, no Wikipedia, no .gov or .edu in the top 8.
- **But** 32 other state-named posts of theirs ("spiders in Maryland", "ticks in Virginia", "types of wasps in Virginia") mostly earn under 100: 1 hit in 33. That matches our own finding that a place name in the slug costs reach (ops skill rule 6).
- **Change:** one test page, not a series: a house-spider identification page built like theirs (one row per spider, where it turns up in the house, which ones matter). Check Search Console demand first.

### Already at parity, or not worth copying

- **Titles.** Theirs are "exact search words: specific promise", 45-56 characters, with no brand. Ours already put the search words first. No global change needed.
- **Root-level addresses** (/roach-poop/). Not a reason to move ours (ops skill rule 11).
- **Image sitemap, table of contents, per-page share image.** They have no table of contents, use one generic share image on every post, and dropped their image sitemap when they moved to Astro; traffic kept rising.
- **Platform.** Their growth happened on WordPress; the move to Astro (late December 2025) did not change the trend.
- **Speed.** Their pages are light because they carry no ads (about 21 requests on first load, against 54 on ours with Mediavine). Our own tests found third-party scripts do not move our LCP. Not a lever for us.

## What we should not copy

1. **Persona authorship.** Every post we checked (13 of 13) names one technician as author, including posts written at three to four a day in 2025. In 2025 the same posts named the company as author; the person was added after the move to Astro. One post says he has been licensed since 2015; the author page and the newest post say five years. Google asks for accurate authorship, and Mediavine does not pay on undisclosed AI content. Keep our honest "drafted with AI assistance and fact-checked" byline.
2. **Near-duplicate pages for word-order and plural variants.** Four separate pages for flying ants vs termites (flying-ants-vs-termites, termites-vs-flying-ants, winged-ant-vs-termite, winged-ants-vs-termites), three for springtails vs fleas, three for carpenter bees vs bumblebees, each a full-length page with its own canonical. At most one page in each set earns 100+ visits a month (none in the carpenter bee set). This is the pattern Google's scaled content abuse policy describes (many pages made mainly to rank).
3. **City-by-service pages and tag pages.** 392 city pages (about 80 towns times pest, ant, mosquito, rodent and termite control) earn 98 visits a month between them. 577 blog index, tag, category and page-2 addresses sit in their sitemap and earn almost nothing.
4. **A templated encyclopedia.** 757 species pages built mostly in January-April 2026 on one fixed outline earn about 12 visits a page; 2% reach 100. Query-shaped posts earn about 187 a page on average.
5. **Drawn charts and uncredited photos.** Their roach droppings chart is an illustration in the style of an AI image generator, with no disclosure. None of their blog photos carries a credit, including a lab-style jumping spider photo with a 1 mm scale bar that looks like museum or agency work. Our rules (real photos only, licence and credit on every one) are safer.
6. **Bought links and keyword anchors.** In April-May 2025 a burst of guest posts on general "magazine" sites (artdaily, devx, homebusinessmag, kulfiy, siit.co, metapress) and spam links briefly lifted their Domain Rating to 26; more than half of those links have since been lost. Their city pages get hundreds of sitewide links with exact anchors such as "pest control manassas va" (372 followed links from 18 domains) and "termite exterminator" (358 from 14). Google's link spam policy names both practices.
7. **Quote buttons on reading pages.** Every post carries 7 phone links, 5-7 quote blocks, a sticky call bar and a "before you go" quote box. That fits a company whose readers live in its service area. Ours mostly do not (owner's decision of Oct 14, 2026).
8. **Leaning on one page and on AI Overviews.** A one-month-old page is 10% of their traffic, and for 25 of their 35 biggest searches their best position is a citation inside Google's AI Overview, which brings far fewer clicks than a normal result (on our own site such citations get 0-0.6% click-through).

## How fragile is their success?

Partly fragile.
- **Traffic has come down from its peak:** 104,567 (July 2026) to 64,966 (September) and 81,311 (October). Much of that is the normal summer-to-fall drop (comparable pest blogs run 1.3-2.2x higher in July than in October, path-to-50k section 3; theirs is 1.3x), and October is propped up by the new house spider page.
- **Their count of top-3 rankings has also fallen 42% from its March 2026 peak** (11,490 to 6,711), and by 37% since July, through Google's August and September 2026 spam updates. Seasonality does not explain that well. Treat it as a warning sign, not proof of a penalty.
- **The structural risks** are the ones above: persona authorship, keyword-variant duplicates, a link profile with paid-looking guest posts and exact-match anchors, and roughly 1,800 low-value pages (encyclopedia, city, tag) next to about 370 good ones.
- **What looks durable:** the July-August 2025 identification posts. They have kept ranking for over a year, through three core updates and five spam updates, though with the 2026 decline above.

### 10. Method and limits

- **Traffic:** Ahrefs estimates, not their Search Console. On our site Ahrefs lands within x0.8-1.4 of real clicks.
- **History:** Ahrefs would not return their top pages for 2025 dates on our plan, so the growth-period page list comes from their own published dates, their archived WordPress sitemaps and Internet Archive snapshots. Archive first-capture dates and sitemap "last modified" dates are upper bounds on publish dates.
- **Hit rates:** page types were assigned by rules on the address. Topic and page type are mixed together; the domain is held constant.
- **Images:** where a photo came from was judged by eye. We did not trace the sources.
- **Google updates:** dates are from Google's Search Status Dashboard. That an update coincided with a traffic change does not show it caused the change.
- **Country domains:** Google's documentation says country-code domains are tied to a specific country and give a strong signal of the country a site targets, while .com is not associated with a location.
