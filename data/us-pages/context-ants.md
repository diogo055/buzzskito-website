# Cluster: ants

## New pages in this cluster (link targets: use these exact addresses)

- `/learn/ants` — **ants**. The complete ant reference for US homes: how colonies work, the common household species and how to tell them apart, why ants come indoors, what they eat, bites and stings, structural risk (carpenter ants) versus nuisance species, prevention, baiting versus spraying, home remedies and what the evidence says, outdoor and lawn ants including fire ants, when to call a professional, plus the remarkable species readers ask about.

- `/blog/sugar-ants` — **sugar ants** (also: how to get rid of sugar ants). What Americans mean by 'sugar ants' (not one species: usually odorous house ants, pavement ants, pharaoh ants, Argentine ants or little black ants; the true banded sugar ant is Australian), how to tell which one you have, why they show up, and a step-by-step plan to get rid of them (the page also answers 'how to get rid of sugar ants'): bait, do not spray, cut off food and water, seal.

- `/blog/tiny-ants` — **tiny ants**. An identification key for very small ants in a house: size, color, smell when crushed, where the trail runs, then which species that points to (odorous house, pharaoh, thief or grease ant, ghost, little black, pavement, Argentine). A table. Why the species changes which bait works. Keep control advice brief and link to the how-to pages.

- `/blog/black-ants` — **black ants**. Black ants in and around US homes: little black ants versus carpenter ants versus pavement ants versus odorous house ants. Big black ants versus small black ants, which ones matter for the structure, and what each one needs. Not a carpenter ant treatment guide: link out.

- `/blog/ghost-ants` — **ghost ants**. Ghost ants (Tapinoma melanocephalum): identification, where they live in the US (Florida, Hawaii, Texas, and heated buildings farther north), nesting and budding behavior, why sprays split colonies, and how they are controlled.

- `/blog/red-ants` — **red ants**. Which ant is the 'red ant' you are seeing: red imported fire ants, native fire ants, and red or reddish look-alikes that do not sting badly. Range in the US, mounds, stings and when a sting is a medical emergency, and how fire ants are controlled (the two-step method from university extension).

- `/blog/flying-ants` — **flying ants** (also: flying ants in house). What flying ants are (reproductive swarmers on a mating flight), when and why swarms happen, how long they last, and a clearly headed section on flying ants in the house (the page also answers 'flying ants in house'): what an indoor swarm means, winter swarms and carpenter ants, what to do. Link to the termite comparison rather than repeating it.

- `/blog/flying-ants-vs-termites` — **flying ants vs termites** (also: termites vs ants). The comparison page (also answers 'termites vs ants'): swarmers side by side (antennae, waist, wing length), then workers, then the damage each does and the signs they leave (frass versus mud tubes). A table. What to do if it is termites, and why that is a professional job.

- `/blog/do-carpenter-ants-bite` — **do carpenter ants bite**. Carpenter ant bites: yes they can bite and may spray formic acid, what it feels like, how to treat it, and why the bite is the minor problem compared with a nest in the structure. Short identification section. Link to the carpenter ant removal guide.

- `/blog/do-ants-bite` — **do ants bite**. Bite versus sting: which US ants bite, which sting, which do both (fire ants, harvester ants, carpenter ants, pavement ants, and the harmless majority). What reactions look like, first aid, and when to seek care. A species table.

- `/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-ants-in-the-house` — **how to get rid of ants in the house** (also: ants in house). Ants in the house (the page also answers 'ants in house'): why they came in, how to follow the trail to the entry point, why bait beats spray, which bait for which ant, sealing and moisture fixes, how long it takes, and when the nest is inside the walls. Whole-house scope; the kitchen gets its own page, so link to it.

- `/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-ants-in-the-kitchen` — **how to get rid of ants in kitchen**. Kitchen-specific: where kitchen ants nest and enter (under the sink, behind the dishwasher, window frames, wall voids near moisture), bait placement that keeps products away from food-prep surfaces, cleaning the scent trail, food storage, and what not to spray where food is prepared.

- `/blog/borax-for-ants` — **borax for ants**. Borax and boric acid as ant bait: how borates kill ants and why a low concentration matters (workers must live long enough to share it), sugar bait versus protein bait, the common DIY recipe and its limits, ready-made borate bait stations named editorially (no links), safety around children and pets citing the National Pesticide Information Center, how long it takes, and when it fails.

- `/blog/does-vinegar-kill-ants` — **does vinegar kill ants**. Vinegar on ants: it can kill some on direct contact and wipes out the scent trail for a while, but it does not reach the colony. How to use it for cleanup, what it will not do, and what actually ends the trail.

- `/blog/what-do-ants-eat` — **what do ants eat**. Ant diets: sugars, proteins, fats, honeydew from aphids, seeds, fungus (leafcutters), other insects. Diet by common household species, how colony needs shift by season, how liquid food is shared (trophallaxis), and why this decides which bait works.

- `/blog/how-long-do-ants-live` — **how long do ants live**. Lifespan of workers, queens and males by common species, the egg to adult timeline, how long a colony lasts, and how long ants survive without food or water. A table by species.

- `/blog/how-many-ants-are-in-the-world` — **how many ants are in the world**. The 2022 PNAS estimate of about 20 quadrillion ants: how it was calculated, what it works out to per person, total ant biomass compared with wild birds and mammals, how many species are known, where ants are most abundant, and the limits of the estimate.

- `/blog/army-ants` — **army ants**. Army ants: what makes an ant an army ant (nomadic, no permanent nest, mass raids), bivouacs, the best-known species, where they live including the lesser-known US species in the South and Southwest, what they eat, and whether they are dangerous to people (the myths).

- `/blog/bullet-ants` — **bullet ants**. The bullet ant (Paraponera clavata): size, range in Central and South America (not established in the US), the sting and its place at the top of the Schmidt sting pain index, how long the pain lasts, the Satere-Mawe initiation ritual, and behavior.

- `/blog/honeypot-ants` — **honeypot ants**. Honeypot ants: the replete workers that store liquid food in swollen abdomens, the species in the US Southwest (Myrmecocystus) and in Australia, how the colony uses them, where they nest, and their role as food in some cultures.

- `/blog/zombie-ants` — **zombie ants**. The 'zombie ant' fungus (Ophiocordyceps): how it infects carpenter ants and manipulates their behavior, the death grip, where it occurs including the US Southeast, what the research says about how it controls the ant, and why it cannot infect people.

- `/learn/ant-killer-recipes` — **homemade ant killer**. Three or four homemade ant killer recipes, each taken from a university extension or other authoritative source and given with EXACT measurements in US kitchen units: a borax and sugar liquid bait, a borax protein or grease bait for ants that ignore sugar, a vinegar and water trail-breaker for cleanup, and soapy water as a contact spray if a source supports it. Use one recipe block per recipe. For every recipe state what it does and does not do, how long it takes, and a pet and child safety tier with the reason. Explain how to make a simple bait station that keeps bait away from pets and children, why a weak borax bait beats a strong one, and when homemade bait fails. End with a printable recipe card. Never say the recipes were tested by BuzzSkito: say which source publishes each one.
