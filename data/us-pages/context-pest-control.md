# Cluster: pest-control

## New pages in this cluster (link targets: use these exact addresses)

- `/blog/how-much-is-pest-control` — **how much is pest control**. What pest control costs in the US: one-time visit versus quarterly or monthly plans, typical ranges by pest and by treatment type with the source and date stated for every number, what drives the price (home size, severity, method, region), what contracts usually include, how to compare quotes, and when paying is and is not worth it. Use reputable published cost surveys and say clearly they are estimates.

- `/blog/diy-pest-control` — **diy pest control**. Do-it-yourself pest control done properly: the integrated pest management sequence from EPA (identify, prevent, monitor, then treat), what a homeowner can realistically handle and what should go to a professional (termites, bed bugs at scale, stinging insects in structures, fumigation), the product types sold to consumers and how to read a label, protective equipment, storage and disposal, and cost versus hiring.

- `/blog/natural-pest-control` — **natural pest control**. Natural pest control with the evidence attached: exclusion, sanitation and habitat change first; biological controls (beneficial insects, Bti, nematodes); mechanical controls (traps, heat, vacuuming); low-risk materials (diatomaceous earth, boric acid, insecticidal soap, horticultural oil); EPA minimum-risk 25(b) products and what that exemption does and does not mean. Be exact about risk language: natural does not mean harmless.

- `/blog/lawn-pest-control` — **lawn pest control**. Lawn pests in the US and how they are managed: white grubs, chinch bugs, sod webworms, armyworms, billbugs, mole crickets, fire ants, plus ticks and mosquitoes at the lawn edge. How to confirm the pest and the damage threshold before treating, timing by season and region, cultural controls, product classes and biologicals, protecting pollinators, and doing it yourself versus a lawn service.

- `/blog/commercial-pest-control` — **commercial pest control**. Pest control for businesses: how it differs from residential service, which industries need it and why (restaurants and food plants, healthcare, hospitality, warehouses, multifamily housing, offices, schools), integrated pest management programs and documentation, the rules that drive it (FDA Food Code, health inspections, third-party audits, school IPM laws), service frequency, what a contract should contain, pricing factors, and how to choose and check a provider.

- `/learn/pet-safe-pest-control` — **pet safe pest control**. Pest control around cats and dogs, remedy by remedy, cited to veterinary sources (ASPCA Animal Poison Control, Pet Poison Helpline, the Merck Veterinary Manual, FDA, AVMA, veterinary schools). Cover at least: essential oils (tea tree, peppermint and others, with the specific risk to cats), permethrin and other dog-only spot-on products around cats, rodenticides and secondary poisoning, borax and boric acid baits, diatomaceous earth, slug and snail bait, mothballs, DEET and human repellents on pets, glue traps, and flea and tick products. A table with one row per remedy: risk to dogs, risk to cats, and the lower-risk swap. What to do after an exposure, with the poison-control numbers copied from the organizations' own pages. Never call anything simply 'safe': say lower-risk, and 'when used as the label directs'. Tell readers to ask their veterinarian about products used on the animal.
