# Cluster: mice

## New pages in this cluster (link targets: use these exact addresses)

- `/learn/mice` — **mice control**. The complete mouse reference and the page that answers 'mice control': species in North America, biology and breeding, signs of an infestation, health risks (hantavirus, salmonellosis, LCMV per CDC), how mice get in, the control sequence (seal up, trap up, clean up), traps, rodenticides and the US EPA consumer rules, repellents and what the evidence says, safe cleanup, cost and when to hire an exterminator.

- `/blog/types-of-mice` — **types of mice**. The mice a US homeowner actually meets: house mouse, deer mouse, white-footed mouse, plus the animals often called mice (voles, shrews, young rats). How to tell them apart, which come indoors, which carry hantavirus. A comparison table. Link to the species pages for depth.

- `/blog/deer-mice` — **deer mice**. Deer mice (Peromyscus maniculatus): identification versus the house mouse, range, habitat and habits, why they matter (the main carrier of the hantavirus that causes HPS in the US, per CDC), how to keep them out of homes, cabins and sheds, and the CDC cleanup method.

- `/blog/field-mice` — **field mice**. What 'field mice' means in the US (a loose name for deer mice, white-footed mice and voles), how to tell which one you have, when they move indoors, the damage they do in gardens and outbuildings, and how control differs from house mice. Do not repeat the deer mouse page: summarize and link.

- `/blog/baby-mice` — **baby mice**. Baby mice (pups): what they look like day by day and week by week, litter size, how fast mice breed, what finding a nest means for the size of the problem, baby mouse versus baby rat, and what to do with a nest safely.

- `/blog/how-long-do-mice-live` — **how long do mice live**. Mouse lifespan in the wild, indoors and as pets, how long they survive without food and without water, the breeding math that makes a short life irrelevant, and what that means for waiting a problem out.

- `/blog/what-do-mice-eat` — **what do mice eat**. What mice eat in the wild and in a house, how much and how often, what they gnaw that is not food, their water needs, and what this means for trap bait (why peanut butter works) and for food storage.

- `/blog/are-mice-nocturnal` — **are mice nocturnal**. When mice are active: mostly at night with peaks at dusk and before dawn, why, what seeing a mouse in the daytime suggests, the noises you hear at night and where, and how to use this for trap timing and inspection.

- `/blog/can-mice-climb-walls` — **can mice climb walls**. Climbing and squeezing ability: which wall surfaces mice can and cannot climb, how high they jump, how small a gap they fit through (give inches), pipes and wires as highways, how they reach attics and upper floors, and what this means for sealing a house.

- `/blog/steel-wool-for-mice` — **steel wool for mice**. Steel wool as a mouse barrier: does it work, which grade, how to pack it and lock it in with caulk (the CDC seal-up method), where it fails (rust, large gaps, pulled out), copper mesh and hardware cloth as alternatives, which gaps need which material, and a room-by-room list of holes to check. Products named editorially, no links.

- `/blog/mice-repellent` — **mice repellent**. The overview of mouse repellents by type: scent products (peppermint and other essential oils, pouches and granules), ultrasonic devices (and the FTC's history with them), predator scents, mothballs and ammonia. What the evidence says for each in a table, why repellents alone do not end an infestation, and what works instead. This is the hub for the peppermint oil, smells, mothballs and Vamoose pages: summarize and link, do not repeat them.

- `/blog/peppermint-oil-for-mice` — **peppermint oil for mice**. Peppermint oil specifically: where the idea comes from, what the limited research shows, how people apply it and how fast it fades, why mice get used to it, risks to cats and other pets, and where it can and cannot fit in a real plan.

- `/blog/what-smells-do-mice-hate` — **what smells do mice hate**. The list people search for (peppermint, ammonia, vinegar, cayenne, cloves, dryer sheets, cinnamon, predator scents) with an honest evidence rating for each in a table, why a strong smell rarely moves an established mouse, and what to do instead. Brief on peppermint and mothballs: link to those pages.

- `/blog/do-mothballs-keep-mice-away` — **do moth balls keep mice away**. Mothballs for mice: they do not work at any amount that is tolerable to people, they are registered pesticides labeled only for clothes moths in sealed containers, using them otherwise is illegal off-label use, and the vapors are a health risk (cite the National Pesticide Information Center and EPA). What to do instead.

- `/blog/vamoose-for-mice` — **vamoose for mice**. An honest explainer on the Vamoose mouse repellent product searchers are asking about. RESEARCH FIRST: find the manufacturer's own page and label, state exactly what the product is and what its listed ingredients are, and what the maker claims. Then what independent evidence exists for those ingredients as rodent repellents, what a repellent can and cannot do, and what to pair it with. Do not review it as if tested. If you cannot establish what the product is from a primary source, say so plainly in the page rather than guessing. No links to any seller.

- `/blog/mice-traps` — **mice traps**. Mouse traps by type: snap, electronic, multi-catch, live-catch, glue boards (and why CDC advises against glue and live traps). How each works and when to choose it, how many to set, where and how to place them, bait, how often to check, safe disposal. A comparison table. Brands named editorially, no links, no prices.

- `/blog/mice-poison` — **mice poison**. Rodenticides in the US: the active ingredient classes (first and second generation anticoagulants, bromethalin, cholecalciferol, zinc phosphide), the EPA rules for consumer products (bait stations required, loose pellets and second-generation anticoagulants removed from the consumer market), risks to children, pets and wildlife, what to do in an exposure (Poison Control and ASPCA numbers from their own sites), and why trapping usually comes first.

- `/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-mice-humanely` — **how to get rid of mice humanely**. Humane mouse control: exclusion as the most humane method, what 'humane' means for a kill trap (fast), the problems with live-catch and release (survival, state rules, CDC's advice), why glue traps and poison are the least humane, and how to clean up and prevent a repeat.

- `/blog/mice-removal` — **mice removal**. Getting mice out and cleaning up after them: removal from walls, attics, crawl spaces, garages and vehicles, finding and removing a dead mouse and the smell, the CDC method for cleaning droppings and nests safely, what a professional removal service includes (trapping, exclusion, sanitation), and how to decide between doing it yourself and hiring it out.

- `/blog/mice-exterminator` — **mice exterminator** (also: exterminator for mice; mice pest control). Hiring a professional for mice (the page also answers 'exterminator for mice' and 'mice pest control'): signs you need one, exactly what an exterminator does on a mouse job, typical US price ranges with the source and date stated, one-time versus ongoing service, questions to ask, licensing and how to check it with your state, how long it takes, and what you still have to do yourself.

- `/blog/fall-mouse-proofing-checklist` — **how to mouse proof your house**. A fall exclusion guide. The 7 entry points mice use most (write them as a numbered list that matches a house diagram: utility and pipe penetrations, gaps under exterior doors, the garage door corners, dryer and other vents, the foundation and sill plate, weep holes and siding gaps, the roofline and soffits), with the gap size that lets a mouse through in inches. A step-by-step steel wool and caulk how-to for small holes and what to use for larger ones (hardware cloth, metal flashing, cement), following CDC's seal-up guidance. A trap placement guide for the mice already inside. Why fall is the season. A printable exclusion checklist as a card block. Pet and child safety for traps and for any bait.
