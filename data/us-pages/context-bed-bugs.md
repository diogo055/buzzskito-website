## New pages in this cluster (link targets: use these exact addresses)

- `/learn/bed-bugs` — **bed bugs**. The complete bed bug reference: what they are and how to identify every life stage, biology and life cycle, how infestations start and spread, signs, bites and health effects, inspection, prevention when traveling and at home, every treatment option (heat, steam, encasements, desiccant dusts, EPA-registered sprays, fumigation), what does not work, cost and when to call a professional, tenant and landlord basics in the US.

- `/blog/what-do-bed-bugs-look-like` — **what do bed bugs look like**. Identification of the bug itself: adult size (compare to an apple seed, give inches), shape, color before and after feeding, the five nymph stages, eggs, and what they look like on a mattress or skin. Include a stage-by-stage size table. Not a look-alike guide and not a treatment guide: link out for those.

- `/blog/black-bed-bugs` — **black bed bugs**. Answers 'are bed bugs black?': real bed bug colors by life stage and feeding status, why a bed bug can look nearly black, what the black spots on a mattress are (fecal stains), and the small black bugs commonly mistaken for bed bugs (carpet beetles, spider beetles, bat bugs, fleas, cockroach nymphs). Keep the look-alike section short and link to the full look-alike guide.

- `/blog/bugs-that-look-like-bed-bugs` — **bugs that look like bed bugs**. The look-alike guide: bat bugs, swallow bugs, carpet beetles and their larvae, spider beetles, booklice, cockroach nymphs, fleas, ticks. For each: how to tell it from a bed bug, where it is found, whether it bites. Comparison table. How to get a specimen identified through a university extension office.

- `/blog/where-do-bed-bugs-hide` — **where do bed bugs hide**. Hiding places ranked by distance from where people sleep: mattress seams and tags, box spring, bed frame and headboard, nightstands, baseboards, outlets, furniture, luggage. How far they travel, daytime versus night, how to inspect a bedroom and a hotel room step by step, and the tools that help.

- `/blog/bed-bugs-on-mattress` — **bed bugs on mattress**. What bed bug signs on a mattress look like (live bugs, fecal spots, shed skins, eggs, blood smears) and exactly what to do about a mattress that has them: vacuum, steam, encase, hot-dry the bedding, isolate the bed. Whether you need to throw the mattress out (usually not) and which pesticides are and are not labeled for mattresses.

- `/blog/how-to-get-rid-of-bed-bugs` — **how to get rid of bed bugs** (also: how to get rid of bed bugs fast). The full elimination plan following the EPA's integrated approach: confirm the ID, contain, prepare, kill with heat and steam, use desiccant dust and EPA-registered products correctly, monitor with interceptors, repeat. Must include a clearly headed section on the fastest way to get rid of bed bugs (what speeds it up, realistic timelines, why 'overnight' is not real) because the page also answers 'how to get rid of bed bugs fast'. DIY versus professional and when DIY is not enough.

- `/blog/does-alcohol-kill-bed-bugs` — **does alcohol kill bed bugs**. Rubbing alcohol on bed bugs: it kills only on direct contact and inconsistently (cite the university research on kill rates), does nothing to hidden bugs or most eggs, and is a documented fire hazard. What to use instead.

- `/blog/does-lysol-kill-bed-bugs` — **does lysol kill bed bugs**. Lysol and other household disinfectants on bed bugs: a disinfectant is not an insecticide and is not labeled for bed bugs, a direct soaking may kill some on contact, it leaves no residual and misses eggs and hidden bugs. Explain how to check an EPA registration and what is actually labeled for bed bugs. Cover bleach and other cleaners briefly.
