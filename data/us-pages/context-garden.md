# Cluster: garden

## New pages in this cluster (link targets: use these exact addresses)

- `/learn/natural-garden-pest-control` — **natural garden pest control**. The hub for the garden guides. The integrated pest management order for a home garden (identify, tolerate, prevent, physical controls, biological controls, then low-risk sprays). A companion planting chart: marigolds, basil, mint, nasturtium, chives, dill and others, with what each is claimed to repel or attract AND an honest evidence column from university research (much companion-planting lore is weakly supported: say so). Pollinator-protection rules (when not to spray, what not to spray on flowers), citing EPA, extension services or the Xerces Society. A seasonal calendar by spring, summer and fall. One section each that summarizes and links to the whitefly, fungus gnat, aphid and weed guides. Pet and child notes for the sprays and dusts mentioned.

- `/learn/how-to-get-rid-of-fungus-gnats` — **how to get rid of fungus gnats**. The full sequence for fungus gnats in houseplants: confirm the pest (and tell it from a fruit fly or drain fly), let the top of the soil dry and by how much, yellow sticky traps for adults, a Bti drench (the 'mosquito bit tea' method: give the steeping method and amount only as an extension source or the product label gives it), a hydrogen peroxide drench with the EXACT ratio a source gives and an honest statement of how well it is supported, and when to repot. The life cycle timing that explains why treatment must run for weeks. Plant-safety notes for each step. A printable watering-schedule card. Pet and child notes for Bti, peroxide and sticky traps. This guide covers the method for any reader; the existing page on the site covers products sold in Canada, so link to it and do not repeat it.

- `/learn/aphid-control-guide` — **how to get rid of aphids naturally**. Aphid identification (colors, cornicles, where they cluster, honeydew and sooty mold, curled leaves). The controls in order: a strong spray of water and how often, pruning, natural enemies and how to keep them, releasing purchased lady beetles (when sources say it works and why they often fly away), insecticidal soap and neem or horticultural oil with the dilution a university source or the product label gives, and what to avoid (broad-spectrum sprays, excess nitrogen). The ant and aphid relationship: why ants farm aphids for honeydew and why controlling the ants helps. Plant-safety and pollinator notes. Pet and child notes for soap and oil sprays.

- `/learn/whitefly-identification-control` — **how to get rid of whiteflies**. Whitefly identification and how to tell whiteflies from aphids, mealybugs and fungus gnats (a comparison table). The 'shake the plant' test. The life cycle and why the undersides of leaves matter. Five to seven natural controls in order: yellow sticky traps, a strong water spray, vacuuming adults in the cool of the morning, reflective mulch, insecticidal soap and horticultural or neem oil with the dilution a university source or the label gives, removing heavily infested leaves, and natural enemies. Why broad-spectrum insecticides make whiteflies worse. A printable cheat-sheet card. Plant-safety, pollinator, pet and child notes.

- `/learn/natural-weed-control` — **natural weed killer**. Natural weed control with the evidence attached. Vinegar: household 5 percent versus horticultural 20 to 30 percent acetic acid, the EXACT recipe or concentration a university extension source gives, which weeds and growth stages it works on, and an honest limits section (it burns top growth, perennial roots regrow, it is non-selective, strong vinegar is corrosive to skin and eyes and is a registered pesticide in the US, salt in the popular recipe harms soil). The boiling water method and where it makes sense. A mulch-depth guide in inches by mulch type. Lower-risk lawn options (mowing height, overseeding, hand weeding, corn gluten meal and what the research found). Pet and child notes for every method. A printable cheat-sheet card.

- `/blog/spotted-lanternfly` — **spotted lanternfly** (also: spotted lanternfly nymph; spotted lanternfly eggs; spotted lanternfly map). Lycorma delicatula: every life stage in photos, the tree of heaven host, egg masses on outdoor items, the state quarantines and reporting, and what homeowners can do.

- `/blog/leaf-footed-bugs` — **leaf footed bug** (also: leaf footed bug nymph; leaf footed bug tomato damage; leaf footed bug vs assassin bug). Leptoglossus in southern and western gardens: nymphs and adults, fruit damage on tomatoes, pomegranates and citrus, hand removal, and how they differ from kissing and assassin bugs.

- `/blog/japanese-beetles` — **japanese beetle** (also: japanese beetle traps; japanese beetle grub; japanese beetle vs june bug). Popillia japonica in the US and Canada (including the Vancouver and Ontario situations): identification, skeletonized leaves, why traps can attract more beetles, hand-picking, and the grub stage.

- `/blog/spider-mites` — **spider mites** (also: spider mites on plants; spider mites on houseplants; spider mites treatment). Two-spotted spider mites on houseplants, tomatoes and roses: the paper tap test, hot dry weather outbreaks, water sprays, insecticidal soap and oils, and predatory mites.

- `/blog/mole-crickets` — **mole crickets** (also: mole cricket damage; mole cricket holes; mole cricket sound). Tawny, southern and short-winged mole crickets in the Southeast: the soap flush test, tunnelling damage, the June-July nymph treatment window, and biological control.

- `/blog/houseplant-bugs` — **bugs on houseplants** (also: houseplant pests; white bugs on plants; tiny bugs in houseplant soil; spider mites on houseplants; mealybugs; thrips on houseplants). A "which bug is on my plant" photo sorter for indoor plants, by the sign you see: spider mites (webbing), mealybugs (white fluff), scale, thrips, aphids, whiteflies, fungus gnats and springtails. Each gets a photo, its size and the one feature that settles it, then a link to the page that treats it: /learn/how-to-get-rid-of-fungus-gnats, /learn/aphid-control-guide and /learn/whitefly-identification-control keep those pests. The species searches (spider mites, thrips, mealybugs) go to knowledge panels and to spider-mites (planned): show each as one candidate and link. Photos on record: spider mite webbing, twospotted spider mite, aphid and whitefly records; mealybug, scale and thrips photos are still needed.
