## 5. What a US reader needs that a Canadian page naturally omits

### 5.1 US species and their ranges
| Species | US relevance | On our page? |
|---|---|---|
| **Cat flea** (*Ctenocephalides felis*) | Dominant US household flea | ✅ Named |
| **Dog flea** (*Ctenocephalides canis*) | Named by CDC as a common US flea | ❌ Absent |
| **Ground squirrel / rock squirrel flea** (*Oropsylla montana*) | CDC: the **primary plague vector to humans in the western US**; readily bites people; NM, AZ, CO, CA | ❌ Absent |
| **Oriental rat flea** (*Xenopsylla cheopis*) | CDC: primary murine typhus vector alongside cat flea | ❌ Absent |
| **Human flea** (*Pulex irritans*) | Rare | ✅ Named |
| **Common bed bug** (*Cimex lectularius*) | The US bed bug | ❌ **Never named anywhere on the page** |
| **Tropical bed bug** (*Cimex hemipterus*) | **Re-established in Florida since ~2015**; established in **Honolulu, Hawaii** (detections 2009 & 2019). Carries **kdr insecticide-resistance mutations** — pyrethroid/pyrethrin resistance | ❌ Absent |

The *Cimex hemipterus* + kdr point is unusually valuable: it is the *mechanistic explanation* for why a Florida or Hawaii reader's drugstore pyrethroid spray fails. No top-10 competitor covers it.

### 5.2 US disease geography (CDC-sourced)
Our FAQ says "the CDC lists flea-borne (murine) typhus, plague… and cat scratch disease" — accurate, but **geographically weightless for a US reader**. What's missing:

- **Flea-borne (murine) typhus** — CDC: most US cases from **California, Texas and Hawaii**. **Endemic in Los Angeles and Orange counties** (California Dept. of Public Health). Texas DSHS and CDPH both report **rising case counts in recent years**; CDC issued provider guidance in 2025. Historically ~300 cases/yr nationally, with recent years running far higher.
- **Plague** — CDC: **rural western United States**, roughly seven to ten human cases a year; vectored chiefly by *Oropsylla montana* off ground and rock squirrels. NM, AZ, CO, CA, NV, OR.
- **Cat scratch disease** — CDC: "occurs wherever cats and fleas are found" (i.e. nationwide).
- **Tungiasis** (burrowing sand flea) — a travel-associated risk Healthline covers and we do not.

A Toronto-based page has no reason to write any of this. A US reader in San Antonio or LA County has every reason to want it.

### 5.3 US seasonality and regions
Our whole site runs on a Canadian **May–September** season. For fleas in the US that is simply wrong in half the country:
- **Year-round flea activity:** Florida, Texas, Louisiana, Georgia, Alabama, Mississippi, South Carolina, coastal California, Hawaii, and much of the Gulf Coast corridor.
- **Seasonal (April–November):** northern-tier states — Minnesota, Ohio, Colorado, the Northeast — which behave much like the GTA.

This changes the reader's mental model materially: our "expect 2 to 8 weeks" plan assumes a season that ends. In Houston it does not. **No top-10 competitor handles US regional flea seasonality at all** — this is a differentiation opportunity, not merely a gap.

### 5.4 US agencies and regulatory framing
Our page says *"choose a product registered for indoor domestic use in your jurisdiction"* — that is PMRA/Health Canada phrasing. A US reader needs:
- **EPA** — the **Bed Bug Product Search Tool** (300+ EPA-registered products, the majority consumer-available; searchable by company, name, EPA reg. number, active ingredient and place of application).
- **EPA IPM guidance** — pesticides as one component of an Integrated Pest Management plan.
- **EPA's explicit warning against relying on foggers/bug bombs**, plus its safety warning against rubbing alcohol, kerosene and gasoline. This **corroborates our existing anti-flea-bomb section with a US federal authority** — a free credibility win on content we already have.
- **Land-grant extension services** — the real US authorities on mystery bites, and exactly the kind of citation that earns AI Overview inclusion: **Texas A&M AgriLife "Insects in the City"**, **University of Kentucky Entomology (ENTFACT-649, "Mystery Bites")**, **NC State Extension**, **UF/IFAS**, **UC ANR**.
- **AAD** and **ACAAI** for the allergy/derm angle (both cited by AAFA at #4).

### 5.5 US retail and consumer context
Our only retail signals are Amazon **search** links for flea traps and flea sprays, plus internal links to **"best flea spray for home *canada*"** and **"the honest verdict on flea bombs in *canada*"**. A US reader who clicks either lands on a page written for a market they are not in. We need US-framed product context — **EPA-registered**, **IGR active ingredients named**, the EPA product search tool — and US-workable BuyLink search terms. (Constraints hold: `search=` only, never `asin=`; never remove the existing Canadian links, which are load-bearing for our actual service market.)

## 6. Where `app/blog/flea-bites-on-humans/page.tsx` falls short — precisely

**What the page already does well** (and must not be touched): the Quick Answer capsule is genuinely excellent and is the page's best AIO asset; the 4-column flea/bed bug/mosquito table is competitive with Healthline's 5-axis table; the `bite-pattern-comparison.svg` Figure is a real differentiator no competitor has; the CDC + Health Canada sourcing already beats Mosquito Squad, Terminix and Smithereen; the medical-advice disclaimer is present and correctly worded; the 95%-immature-stages and 96%-vacuuming statistics are strong, citable facts; and the honest "that is not us — BuzzSkito treats mosquitoes and ticks only" referral is a rare trust signal.

**The shortfalls, in priority order:**

1. **Bed-bug asymmetry — the primary ranking defect.** The query is bed-bug-led; the page is flea-led. Bed bugs appear as one table column (line ~156), two sentences in a FAQ, and one internal link to `/blog/bed-bug-bites`. There is no bed bug identification section, no "where they hide", no mattress-seam inspection walkthrough, no bed bug life cycle, no bed bug treatment reality. Every top-10 page gives bed bugs equal or greater weight. **Fix additively:** a dedicated bed-bug half — H2s under the existing flea H2s, never replacing them.
2. **Five of eight PAA questions unanswered.** Nothing addresses "What is biting me at night, not bedbugs?" (on **both** SERPs), "What are the first signs that you have bed bugs?", "What are three signs you might have bed bugs?", or "Which is easier to get rid of bed bugs or fleas?" — the last being the highest-commercial-intent question on the SERP and the one a pest-control brand is uniquely credible answering.
3. **No mystery-bite differential diagnosis.** The page assumes the reader already knows it is fleas. The r/pestcontrol and Quora discussion block reveals the real emotional query: *"I have bites and I can't find a bug."* We never address the no-visible-insect case — bird and rodent mites (~1 mm, dark slow-moving specks, migrate indoors when a nest is abandoned, do **not** hitchhike between locations), carpet beetle larval-hair dermatitis (does not bite; hairs raise bite-like lesions), scabies (*Sarcoptes scabiei* — burrows, night itch, finger webs/armpits/waist, **contagious**, unlike either fleas or bed bugs), chiggers (we link out but never list here), no-see-ums, and non-insect dermatologic causes. Extension services (Texas A&M, UK ENTFACT-649, NC State, UF/IFAS, UC ANR) are the authoritative sources.
4. **No allergy/immunology section.** AAFA ranks #4 on this angle alone. We have no anaphylaxis section, no papular urticaria, no named epinephrine/corticosteroid/antihistamine guidance, no "how a doctor diagnoses biting-insect allergy". Our emergency line exists but is one bullet inside a treatment list.
5. **Zero US species specificity.** *Cimex lectularius* is never named. *C. hemipterus*, *O. montana*, *X. cheopis*, *C. canis* all absent. The page says "North America" where CDC says specific states.
6. **Zero US disease geography.** The CDC diseases are listed without the US map that makes them matter.
7. **Zero US seasonality.** May–September framing does not survive contact with Florida or Texas.
8. **EPA absent entirely.** No EPA, no Bed Bug Product Search Tool, no EPA IPM framing, no EPA fogger warning — despite EPA's fogger position directly reinforcing our existing flea-bomb argument.
9. **Retail context is Canada-only.** Both internal product links are `-canada` slugs; the reader in Ohio is sent to a Canadian buying guide.
10. **One image, no anchor IDs.** All AIO-cited competitors carry image thumbnails; there is an image pack on the sibling SERP; GoodRx earns 4 sitelinks off section anchors. We have one SVG and no `id` attributes on any H2.
11. **No named clinical reviewer.** Healthline fronts a PA-C; Pinnacle and Cumberland are dermatology practices; AAFA is a medical non-profit. We have `AuthorByline` + `SpecialistDisclosure` (honest, and the disclosure is a genuine asset) but no clinical review signal on a health-adjacent page.
12. **Locale friction (observation only, not an edit).** "favour", "behaviour", "centre", "colour" throughout. The prime directive forbids rewriting existing prose — this is noted only so that **newly added** sections read naturally to a US audience. The page's existing dual-unit convention (1–3 mm / roughly 1/8 in) is already correct and should be continued.

---

## 7. The one-line strategic read

This SERP is won by **completeness of the two-pest comparison plus PAA coverage**, not authority — proven by a DR 46 dermatology blog outranking CDC, Harvard and Cleveland Clinic, and by a DR 55 mosquito-control franchise earning an AI Overview citation off 1,300 uncited words. BuzzSkito's page is already better-sourced and better-illustrated than the pest-control competitor that is winning. **Everything needed is additive: a bed-bug half, five PAA-matched H2s, a mystery-bite differential, an allergy section, and a US layer (species, disease geography, seasonality, EPA and extension services) sitting alongside — never instead of — the existing Canadian content.**
