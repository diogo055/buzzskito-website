## 5. What a US reader needs that a Canadian page naturally omits

**Regulatory (the biggest single gap — our page is 100% PMRA-framed):**
- **EPA registration** replaces PMRA/PCP. Every US product carries an **EPA Reg. No.** (e.g. Harris Egg Kill = 1021-1767-3). "PCP number" is meaningless to a US buyer.
- **FIFRA Section 25(b) minimum-risk exemption** — the US concept with no Canadian equivalent. Botanical sprays like EcoSmart and Grandpa Gus's are *exempt* from EPA registration, meaning **no efficacy review at all**. This maps directly onto our existing "Best Natural Option" pick and our existing "are natural sprays effective" FAQ without disturbing either.
- 25(b) products can be **state-restricted** — EcoSmart's mattress aerosol is legal in only 10 states. A Canadian page would never think to say "check your state."
- US mattress labels are **narrower than our page implies**. The standard US label language restricts application to *edges, tufts, folds, sides and seams* and says do **not** apply to bed linens or surfaces in direct, prolonged contact with occupants. Our page's "treat the surface per the label" is under-specified for a US reader and should be tightened additively.
- EPA's **Bed Bug Product Search Tool** (cfpub.epa.gov/oppref/bedbug) is the US analogue of looking up a PCP number — searchable by product name, company, EPA reg. no., use site, or pesticide type.
- EPA on foggers: *"Foggers and bug bombs should not be used as the only method to attempt to control bed bugs."*

**Species and range:**
- **Cimex lectularius** (common bed bug) — nationwide.
- **Cimex hemipterus** (tropical bed bug) — absent from the US since the late 1930s, **re-documented in Florida from ~2015**; historically recorded in Alachua, Pinellas, Polk and Sarasota counties; confined to roughly 30° latitude of the equator, so effectively a Gulf-state/Florida issue. This species does not occur in Canada and is a genuinely US-only section.

**Resistance — the dominant US fact our page never mentions:**
- University of Kentucky: **88% of 110 US bed bug populations** carried one or two kdr mutations linked to pyrethroid resistance.
- 7 of 8 field populations from US pest-management firms were **>100-fold resistant to deltamethrin**.
- Virginia Tech: the Richmond, VA strain showed resistance ratios around **5,200-fold** to deltamethrin; 6 of 7 Virginia strains carried the L925I genotype.
- This matters commercially: the US mass-market sprays dominating the shopping carousel (Ortho, Hot Shot, Raid, most Harris SKUs) are pyrethroid-based. A US reader deserves the class-rotation advice EPA gives.

**Seasonality and geography (a Canadian mosquito/tick page has no reason to carry either):**
- US bed bug activity peaks **June–October**, sharpest in **August–September** — driven by return-from-travel and college move-in, not weather.
- **Orkin 2026 Bed Bug Cities list:** Chicago #1 again, then Los Angeles, Detroit, Cleveland, Indianapolis. **Ohio is the most-represented state with six metros in the top 50.** Vacation destinations Tampa and Myrtle Beach are climbing. (Ranking window: 12 May 2025 – 12 May 2026.)

**US agencies and institutions to cite by name:**
- **EPA** — bed bug pesticide guidance, product search tool, fogger guidance
- **CDC + EPA joint statement (2010)** — *"Although bed bugs are not known to transmit disease, they are a pest of significant public health importance"*; effects include allergic reactions and secondary skin infections (impetigo, ecthyma, lymphangitis). This is the correct source for any bite/health sentence we add.
- **NPIC** (National Pesticide Information Center, npic.orst.edu) — the US public helpline for pesticide questions
- **State extension services** — Texas A&M AgriLife (citybugs), Alaska DEC, University of Kentucky, Virginia Tech, Rutgers, Michigan

**US retail context:** Home Depot, Walmart, Lowe's, Ace Hardware and Tractor Supply all rank or appear on this SERP. Our page mentions none of them.

## 6. Where our page falls short — specifics

File: `C:\Users\buzzs\buzzskito-website\app\blog\best-bed-bug-spray-for-mattress-canada\page.tsx`

The page is genuinely good *for a Canadian reader*. Its structure, the "read the label for the word mattress" thesis, and the honest egg-gap argument are all stronger than anything ranking. The problem is that for a US reader it is not merely incomplete — in two places it is **actively hostile**.

**(a) The page tells the US reader not to buy the correct US products.**
The H2 **"A Word on Grey-Market US Sprays"** (line 291–292) says of Harris, Ortho Home Defense and Crossfire: *"Resist it."* The FAQ **"Can I just buy a strong American bed bug spray online instead?"** (line 62–63) calls them *"risky and often not legal."* Both are correct advice for a Canadian. For a reader in Ohio, those are the legal, EPA-registered, top-ranking products — Harris is at **#10 organic and cited in the AI Overview**, Ortho and Crossfire are in the shopping carousel. A US visitor who lands mid-page hits a paragraph telling them their own market's mainstream products are contraband. This is the highest-value fix on the page and it is purely additive: the existing paragraphs stay, but they need a US-reader carve-out immediately adjacent, and a real EPA section so the page has a US answer at all.

**(b) Zero EPA framing.** "PMRA" or "PCP" appears roughly a dozen times across the Quick Answer, the comparison table footnote, a dedicated H2, and four FAQ answers. "EPA" appears only as the thing that *isn't* good enough. There is no EPA registration section, no EPA reg-number explanation, no EPA product search tool, no seven-chemical-class framing, no FIFRA 25(b). A US reader has no regulatory handhold anywhere on the page.

**(c) No pyrethroid resistance content whatsoever.** The page's "contact-kill vs residual" H2 (line 258) and the Konk "Best Residual" pick recommend residual sprays without ever noting that the pyrethroid class most US residuals rely on is widely failing (88% of US populations carry kdr mutations). This is a substantive accuracy gap for a US audience, not just an SEO one, and it is the single thing both #7 (Texas A&M) and #11 (EPA) lead with. Adding it also strengthens the page's existing honest-limits voice rather than contradicting it.

**(d) No chemical-class rotation guidance.** EPA's core recommendation — if pyrethroids fail, switch class (pyrrole/chlorfenapyr, neonicotinoid, desiccant, IGR) or use a combination product — has no equivalent anywhere on the page.

**(e) The highest-intent US question is unanswered.** *"Can we spray bed bug spray on mattress?"* is PAA, and three discussion results (Reddit r/Bedbugs "Safe Spray to Apply Directly to Mattress?", Facebook, Quora) hold position 7 on the mattress query. Our FAQ #2 (line 26–27) *does* answer this well — but it is an accordion `<details>` element eight items down, not an H2, and it is framed around PMRA rather than the "is it safe to sleep on tonight" anxiety that actually drives the query. **"What to do if you slept in a bed with bed bugs?"** (PAA #6) has no answer on the page at all.

**(f) The mattress-surface instruction is looser than US labels allow.** Line 268 says *"treat the surface as the mattress label directs"*, and the Quick Answer bullet (line 116) correctly emphasises seams/piping/tufts/tags. But nowhere does the page state the standard US restriction — apply to **edges, tufts, folds, sides and seams only**, do **not** apply to bed linens or surfaces in direct prolonged contact with occupants. A US reader following our page could over-apply.

**(g) Every commercial link says Amazon.ca.** All four `BuyLink`s in the comparison table, the four inline buttons (lines 248–251), the `StickyBuyBar` and the `AwardRow` all read *"Check price on Amazon.ca"*. For 15.2% of clicks this is the wrong store, and the label itself is visible before the click. (Per the affiliate-ceiling memo, US OneLink is a separate workstream — but the visible copy is a same-day CTR problem independent of tag routing.) No US retailer is named anywhere despite Home Depot, Walmart, Lowe's, Ace and Tractor Supply all appearing on this SERP.

**(h) The product shortlist is Canada-only.** EcoRaider is the one pick with real US presence (it appears in the US shopping carousel, as does its successor branding EcoVenger). **Doktor Doom Sleep Tight** and **Konk** are Canadian brands a US reader cannot buy. The US SERP's actual product set — Harris (Egg Kill / 5-Minute / Black Label), Ortho Home Defense, Hot Shot, Raid, Bedlam, JT Eaton, CrossFire, EcoSmart, Grandpa Gus's, Hygea Natural, Nature's Mace, Bonide — appears on our page only in the paragraph telling readers to avoid it. Additive fix: a US-market section alongside the existing `AwardRow`, using `search=` BuyLinks only, with no ASINs, no prices, no ratings.

**(i) No US species, seasonality or geography.** No *Cimex hemipterus* / Florida content, no Jun–Oct / Aug–Sep peak, no Orkin cities list. All three are pure additions that cannot disturb Canadian content.

**(j) No US agency citations.** No CDC, no CDC/EPA joint statement, no NPIC, no state extension services. Health Canada/PMRA is the only authority cited. On a SERP where a state extension factsheet (#7) and EPA (#11) are the two strongest content pages, this is a visible E-E-A-T deficit.

**(k) Medical-notice status.** The page currently makes almost no symptom or health claims — it has `SpecialistDisclosure` but **no "not medical advice" notice**. If we add any bite, allergic-reaction or secondary-infection content (and the CDC/EPA joint statement is the natural US authority anchor), a not-medical-advice notice must be added at the same time, and every claim sourced inline to CDC or Mayo Clinic by name.

**(l) Format gaps.** No video despite three YouTube results in the top 20 and a YouTube citation in the AIO. Schema is `BlogPosting` + `FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList` + `Speakable`; the page contains a genuine step sequence under "How to Actually Spray a Mattress" that carries no `HowTo` markup (low priority — HowTo rich results are deprecated — but it is a structured-data gap on a page whose competitors are all machine-readable product pages).

**(m) Structural ceiling to be honest about.** Title, H1 and slug are locked by the prime directive and all three say "Canada". The page will not win the head term *bed bug spray* on those signals. The realistic and still-large win is: the **AI Overview citation slot** (currently held only by thin retail pages that answer no questions), the **PAA-shaped long-tails** (§3), and *bed bug spray for mattress* at **KD 0 / 1,300 traffic potential**.
