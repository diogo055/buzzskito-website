## 5. What a US reader needs that our Canadian page omits

### A. Regulatory — the biggest additive win, and it *strengthens* the skeptical verdict

Our page's only compliance test is *"no recognized Health Canada / CSA certification."* That is meaningless to an American. The US has three named, checkable gates:

- **FDA / CDRH — 21 CFR 1040.10 & 1040.11.** Every laser product manufactured, assembled or **imported into the US** since Aug 1, 1976 must comply with the federal laser product performance standard. The manufacturer must file a **product report with CDRH** describing design, hazard class and test data, and FDA issues an **accession number** — which is needed to clear customs. *A US buyer can literally ask the seller for the CDRH accession number.* That is a concrete, actionable test our page cannot currently give.
- **EPA / FIFRA — "device" status.** A laser mosquito killer is a **pest control device**, not a pesticide. Devices are **not subject to EPA registration** under FIFRA §3 — so "not EPA registered" is *normal* and is not a red flag (an important nuance; a US reader will otherwise misread it). But a regulated device **must** be produced in an EPA-registered establishment, **bear an EPA establishment number**, and hold **scientific data substantiating its efficacy claims**. EPA also prohibits statements or diagrams likely to mislead. Source: EPA Pesticide Registration Manual, Chapter 13 — Devices.
- **FCC.** The unit uses **millimetre-wave radar** as its human/pet interlock — an intentional RF radiator, which needs FCC equipment authorization to be marketed in the US.

### B. Laser class and the 976 nm indoor model — our strongest unused safety argument

The **indoor** model is **976 nm invisible infrared**. 976 nm sits inside the **700–1400 nm near-IR band**, which is within the **400–1400 nm retinal hazard region**: it is focused onto the retina by the lens, but it is **invisible, so there is no blink reflex / aversion response**. A stray beam or reflection may be detected only as the result of an injury (ANSI Z136.1; FDA/CDRH 21 CFR 1040). This is the model marketed for **bedrooms and hotels**. Our safety section is entirely generic and never makes this point.

Pair with the arithmetic: **40 W × 10 ms ≈ 0.4 J per pulse.** Ask plainly what class an emitter like that falls into and what the claimed class actually is — the vendor publishes no laser class anywhere.

### C. US species and their ranges (page currently names zero species)

- **Culex pipiens** (northern house mosquito), **Culex quinquefasciatus** (southern house mosquito), **Culex tarsalis** (western), **Culex nigripalpus** (Florida) — the primary **West Nile** vectors, and crepuscular/night-active, which is exactly when a 3–6 m device is pointed at an empty yard.
- **Aedes aegypti** and **Aedes albopictus** — aggressive daytime biters expanding across the South, Southwest and Mid-Atlantic; the dengue/chikungunya/Zika vectors. Both have also tested positive for West Nile (CDC, 1999–2016 surveillance).
- **Anopheles** spp. — relevant because the entire photonic-fence programme was built for *malaria*, an angle the page raises but never lands for a US audience.

### D. US disease burden (CDC — page cites no US health authority at all)

- **West Nile virus: 771 human cases across 39 states as of Sep 9, 2025, of which 490 neuroinvasive** (CDC ArboNET). WNV is the leading domestically acquired arboviral disease in the US.
- Also worth naming: **Eastern equine encephalitis** (rare, high fatality, Northeast/Gulf) and **locally acquired dengue** in Florida, Texas and California.
- The Canadian page's health framing is implicitly "nuisance biting." US readers in Culex country are searching with a disease motive.

### E. US seasonality and regions (page assumes a single May–Sep season)

- **Gulf Coast, Florida, Hawaii:** activity Feb–Nov, effectively year-round in south Florida; worst Jun–Sep.
- **Texas runs three seasons at once:** Gulf Coast (Harris/Galveston/Brazoria/Nueces) Mar–Nov; North Texas (DFW) May–Oct; Trans-Pecos/El Paso Jun–Sep and much drier.
- **South / Southwest:** from March into September.
- **Mid-Atlantic, Midwest, West:** April to September.
- **Northern tier:** May–September (the only season our page describes).
- Directly relevant to the product: a year-round Gulf Coast buyer amortises a $988 device over ~9 months; a Minnesota buyer over ~4.

### F. US institutions the page should point to alongside the Canadian ones

- **400–500 mosquito abatement / vector control districts** of meaningful size, plus **1,000–2,000** smaller health-department and municipal programmes (AMCA / Kline). Many US readers already have free public treatment — a fact with no Canadian equivalent and a genuinely useful answer to "what is the absolute best mosquito killer?"
- **American Mosquito Control Association (mosquito.org)** — the US authority equivalent in role to our Canadian references.
- **State land-grant extension services** (e.g. university extension entomology) — the standard, trusted US answer path for "does this device work?"
- **CDC** for disease and repellent guidance; **EPA** for device claims; **NPIC** (National Pesticide Information Center) for non-chemical device guidance.
