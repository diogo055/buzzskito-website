### 5b. THE GALLINIPPER PROBLEM — accuracy, not just coverage
Our page currently lists **"gallinipper"** as a regional nickname for the crane fly. In the US South — Florida and the Gulf Coast especially — **"gallinipper" means *Psorophora ciliata*, a real, quarter-sized, aggressively biting mosquito** that UF/IFAS entomologists (Eva Buckner, Phil Kaufman) discuss every wet season; it bites through clothing and its bite is described as feeling like a stab. It carries no venom and is not a major disease vector in Florida.

**"gallinipper" is a 2,500/mo US keyword in its own right.** A Floridian who lands on our page, reads that a gallinipper is a harmless crane fly that cannot bite, and then gets bitten by one, has been given wrong information by us. This must be disambiguated — additively, as a new subsection, without removing the existing nickname list.

### 5c. US seasonality — ours is Ontario-only, and Ontario is wrong for most of the US
Our page gives exactly one emergence model: Ontario, spring April–May (small) and fall September–October (large). A US reader in Houston or Sacramento will read that and conclude the page is not about them.
- **South Texas / Houston: adults emerge February–March.**
- **California: adults emerge February–March** (Compton Creek MAD, UC IPM).
- **Pacific Northwest (WA/OR): September–October** for *T. paludosa*; *T. oleracea* spring and fall.
- **Michigan (Grand Rapids/Detroit): October** turf damage window (MSU Extension).
- **Upper Midwest / Northeast: late spring through early fall.**
The Ontario block stays exactly as it is. A US regional table goes **alongside** it.

### 5d. US agencies and institutions to cite by name
We currently cite **no agency, no university, and no public-health body** anywhere on the page — despite making disease claims (West Nile, EEE), a venom claim, and a "harmless to humans" claim.
- **CDC** — for West Nile virus and EEE (the diseases we already name), and for the fact that crane flies are not vectors.
- **EPA** — pesticide registration framing for any US treatment guidance.
- **US extension services**, all already ranking on this SERP: **UC ANR / UC IPM**, **Texas A&M (texasinsects.tamu.edu)**, **UF/IFAS**, **Cornell CALS IPM**, **Oregon State Extension**, **Michigan State Extension**, **Northeastern IPM Center**, and the **PNW Pest Management Handbooks**.
- Keep **Health Canada / Ontario public health** for the Canadian half — do not swap, add.

### 5e. US retail and service context
- Body copy currently says **"Check indoor flying-insect spray on Amazon.ca →"** and **"Beneficial nematodes on Amazon.ca →"**. A US reader is told, in the anchor text, that this page is not for them. The `search=` props stay untouched; the visible **label** is what reads Canadian.
- **"Contact a lawn care specialist (LawnSavers, TruGreen)"** — LawnSavers is GTA-only. TruGreen is US-national and fine. A US reader gets one usable referral out of two.
- **Nematode timing** is given as a single "early fall" instruction. In the US, the correct nematode window follows the *local* emergence — early fall in the PNW/Northeast, but different in the South.
- **BuzzSkito does not serve the US.** The service CTA and CTASection stay (prime directive), but the page should make it obvious the US reader is being helped, not sold to.

### 5f. Units
Every measurement on the page is metric-first (15–25 mm, 3–6 mm, 25–40 mm). The ranking US pages use **inches** ("roughly an inch", "two inches", "ten-inch leg span"). Add imperial in parentheses; do not remove the metric.

## 6. Where `app/blog/mosquito-hawk-explained/page.tsx` falls short — precisely

The page is genuinely good: 13 FAQs, two comparison tables, correct on the core myth, well interlinked, `faqSchema` + `speakableSchema` + `blogPostingSchema` all present. The shortfalls are additive gaps, plus two accuracy items.

**A. Zero images.** `page.tsx` renders no `<Image>`, no `<img>`, no figure. The AIO-cited page's differentiator is a single labelled crane-fly-vs-mosquito comparison photo, and the SERP carries `image_th` on results and a full image pack on the sibling terms. This is the biggest single format gap.

**B. Zero named species.** "family Tipulidae" appears once. *Tipula paludosa*, *T. oleracea*, *Holorusia hespera* — all absent. Wikipedia's whole advantage is entity density.

**C. Zero citations, on a health-adjacent page.** The page asserts a disease list (West Nile, EEE), "no venom", "completely harmless to humans, pets, and livestock", and explicitly rebuts a "most venomous insect in the world" claim — with **no source named anywhere**, and **no "not medical advice" notice**. Google's own AIO on the bite query cites WebMD. Under the medical-accuracy rule this needs CDC/Mayo/Health Canada attribution inline, plus the notice.

**D. Four PAA questions have no answer at all.** "What is the point of a mosquito hawk?" (ecological role), "What scent do mosquito hawks hate?", "Are giant crane flies harmful?", "Is a crane fly and a mosquito eater the same thing?" — none are answerable from the current page.

**E. "Mosquito hawk" is never defined as its own entity.** Mosquito Joe wins position 4 partly by giving "What is a Mosquito Hawk?" its own H2 next to "What is a Crane Fly?". Our page treats the phrase only as a myth to be corrected. (H1 and Quick Answer are locked — this is a new H2 lower down, not a rewrite.)

**F. No damselflies.** The competitor at #4 names them; we name only dragonflies, bats, fish and frogs.

**G. Seasonality is Ontario-only** (§5c). Two headings — "When Are They Active in Ontario?" and the Ontario FAQ — are the page's only timing information. Both stay; a US regional block is added beside them.

**H. The gallinipper mis-assignment** (§5b) — the one outright accuracy defect for a US reader.

**I. Adult lifespan divergence.** We say **5–10 days** in six separate places (Quick Answer, table, FAQs, body). Wikipedia and cvmosquito.org, both ranking above us, say **10–15 days**. Entomology Today says "days". The Quick Answer is locked and 5–10 is defensible for many species, but the page should acknowledge the published range (e.g. "commonly 5–10 days, with sources citing up to 10–15 days depending on species") rather than contradict the two pages Google is currently citing.

**J. No leatherjacket US turf-damage depth.** "Leatherjackets" is named once. US extension services treat leatherjacket turf damage as the *practical* reason a homeowner searches this — MSU flags an October damage window for Michigan; OSU Extension publishes a lawn-management guide. There is a whole US-lawn section available here that Orkin only half-covers.

**K. Metric-only units** (§5f).

**L. Canadian-labelled affiliate anchors and one GTA-only referral** (§5e) — visible label text only; the `search=` props and the links themselves must not change.
