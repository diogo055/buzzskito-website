# The path to $5,000/month

Researched 2026-09-09, against Amazon's published rate card, our own Associates dashboard, GSC,
and the Supabase leads table.

**The short version: $5,000/month is reachable. It is not reachable from the Amazon line, and the
traffic that gets you there is already ranking at position 1.9.**

---

## 1. What $5,000 from Amazon Canada actually requires

First, a correction to a number we have been using. Amazon's "conversion %" is *items per click*,
not orders per click. Verified against the live dashboard (Aug 10–Sep 8: 3,015 clicks / 303
ordered / 10.05% — which is 303÷3,015 exactly). So:

- items per click **10.73%**
- **orders per click 7.08%** ← the real figure
- $3.32 per order

$5,000 ÷ $3.32 = **1,506 orders/month** (we do 198) → at 7.08% that is **21,270 Amazon clicks**
(we do 2,795) → **7.6× everything.**

Our blog-to-Amazon pass-through is already ~90–100% — roughly one Amazon click per reader. That
stage is maxed; there is no slack there. So 7.6× has to come from traffic × commission rate.

- **7.6× traffic** is not available in Canada. The entire sub-position-12 Canadian pool converts
  about 42 clicks/month across 45,784 queries.
- **7.6× rate** would need a ~37% blended commission. The best rate in the Levanta catalogue is
  25%, on a brand with **zero** Canadian impressions.

**Honest probability of a sustained $5,000/month from Amazon Canada: under 5%, and not before
2028.** A single peak month in Jun–Jul 2027 is maybe 10%. The realistic 2027 peak is $1,100–1,900.

I am not going to dress that up. But it is only the Amazon line, and it is not the answer to your
question.

## 2. What IS real on Amazon — a verified 3x arbitrage

From Amazon.ca's own rate card (primary source, read 2026-09-09):

| Band | Rate | What sits there |
|---|---|---|
| Pet Food & Supplies | **9.00%** | flea & tick for dogs and cats |
| Home, Home Improvement, **Patio Lawn & Garden**, Outdoor Recreation, Clothing | **6.00%** | yard sprays, traps, zappers, encasements, treated apparel |
| Health & Household | **3.00%** | **personal repellent — DEET, picaridin** |
| Grocery & Gourmet | **0.00%** | food-grade diatomaceous earth |

**The same household's problem sits in three different bands depending only on which shelf Amazon
files it on.** Repellent for your skin 3%. Spray for your yard 6%. Tick prevention for your dog
9%. Verified from actual product breadcrumbs — Natrapel (our #4 seller) is Health & Personal Care
at 3%; The Executioner swatter (#2 seller) is Patio Lawn & Garden at 6%.

Two things follow:

**Canada is the better marketplace for us.** Amazon.ca pays **6%** on Patio/Lawn/Garden where
amazon.com pays 3%. For this product mix, staying Canadian is not a limitation — it is an
advantage. That independently confirms the US call.

**There are traps as well as wins.** Recommending food-grade DE earns literally zero. Recommending
skin repellent — the most on-brand thing a mosquito company can suggest — pays half what a yard
spray pays.

Realistic value of re-pointing existing links out of the 3% band and into 6%, plus the pet angle:
**~$200–300/month, with no new content.** That is a real gain. It is not $4,344.

## 3. Why "more content for bees and wasps" is the wrong aim

Your instinct — more products, more content — is right about effort and wrong about direction.

| Cluster | $/blog click | season |
|---|---|---|
| bedbug | $0.784 | year-round |
| wildlife | $0.443 | autumn peak |
| general | $0.320 | mixed |
| roach | $0.171 | year-round |
| mosquito | $0.164 | May–Sep |
| **wasp** | **$0.100** | **6-week window** |
| flies | $0.067 | May–Sep |
| tick | $0.000 | May–Sep |

**Wasp is the second-worst non-zero cluster.** And it is a six-week August–September aggression
window that ends at first frost.

Three more things make the "build more" route worse than it looks:

- **Measured: new pages do not pay.** The July cohort was 215 pages, **median $0.16/page/month**,
  40% earning exactly zero. Any content plan has to beat that number explicitly.
- **We are already 61% calendar-bound.** Mosquito + tick + wasp are 61% of Canadian clicks. Adding
  more seasonal content deepens the winter hole rather than filling it.
- **The decline has already started.** Inside August, mosquito clicks were already **down 37%**
  from the June peak and tick **down 54%**. The cluster table everything rests on was taken on the
  back slope.

If you want to build, build **indoor and year-round** — bedbug, roach, rodent. Those earn 3x per
click *and* they are the winter hedge. The bedbug cluster went from zero to ~1,100 impressions/day
in eight weeks and its position is still improving (12.1 → 8.3). It is young and unfinished.

**One correction to something I told you earlier:** I said bedbug earns 4.8x mosquito per click.
The correct figure is **3.3x**, and part of even that is an artifact — Stage G deliberately routed
traffic *toward* bedbug pages, so some of "bedbug converts better" is us reading our own routing
back as a discovery. The 3x is real; the mechanism is partly link density, not purely buyer intent.

## 4. Where the $5,000 actually is

Our best-converting query in Canada:

| Query | Clicks | Position | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|
| **worst areas for ticks in ontario** | **174** | **1.9** | **9.90%** |
| tick map ontario 2026 | 44 | 6.6 | 2.92% |
| ticks in ontario 2026 | 11 | 6.7 | 2.19% |
| ticks in toronto 2026 | 10 | 6.0 | 3.83% |
| ontario tick map 2026 | 8 | 6.6 | 3.21% |

That is **anxious, in-territory, geographic risk intent** — people worried about ticks where they
live — inside the 19-city service area of a licensed operator with a van and 150 five-star reviews.

The tick cluster earning **$0.00 in affiliate on 55 pages** was never an affiliate failure. Its
11% pass-through to Amazon is the loudest signal in the whole dataset that **these readers are not
shoppers.** They are service prospects, and we have been measuring them with the wrong ruler.

The arithmetic that matters:

- One service customer = **$1,222**. That is 368 Amazon orders, or 1.9 months of the entire
  affiliate programme.
- **Four service customers a month = $4,888.** That is the $5,000, from a business you already run.
- We get ~6,800 organic clicks/month, roughly 2,000 of them in-territory. At a 0.2–0.5%
  blog-to-lead rate and a 25% close, that is **$1,200–3,000/month** — already happening, or
  already not happening, and never once measured.

## 5. The blocker, and it is small

I checked the leads table. **Attribution is broken, and not in the way anyone assumed.**

`landing_page` is populated on 406 of 414 leads — but it records the **form page, not the entry
page**:

| landing_page | leads |
|---|---|
| `/free-yard-assessment` | 327 |
| `/exit-intent-popup` | 71 |
| `/contact` | 6 |
| **any `/blog/` post** | **0** |

Zero blog attribution — not because the blog produces no customers, but because the field captures
where the form was, not where the visitor came from. **We cannot currently tell whether 6,800 blog
clicks a month produce service leads.** That is the largest unmeasured number in the business, and
it is a small Hub change: capture first-touch entry path and referrer at session start, persist it
through to the quote submission.

## 6. What I would do, in order

1. **Fix lead attribution** (Hub, small). Until this exists, every other decision is guesswork.
   It is the only thing on this list that could reveal $5,000 already in flight.
2. **Re-point the 3% band to 6%** on existing pages where the higher-band product is genuinely the
   better recommendation. ~$200–300/month, no new content. Do not force it where it is not honest.
3. **Pin the Combat Max ASIN** (one link). 97% of our earnings aggregate to "Unknown" because our
   links are searches, not ASINs. Without per-page visibility nothing else can be measured.
4. **Stop building seasonal outdoor content.** Build indoor/year-round if building at all, and
   hold it to beating $0.16/page/month.
5. **Do not put pet affiliate links on the tick cluster.** It measures ~12 clicks/month of actual
   pet intent, and it would monetise a $1,222 service prospect at $0.40.

## 7. What has to be true for $5,000

Stated plainly, so it can be checked rather than argued:

- From **Amazon Canada alone**: a ~37% blended commission or 7.6x traffic. Neither exists. Under
  5%, not before 2028.
- From **the business as a whole**: four service customers a month. That is not a stretch — it is
  one customer a week, in a 19-city territory, from traffic that already ranks at position 1.9 for
  the exact anxiety that precedes buying tick control.

You were right that $5,000 is possible and right that the category is big enough. The category
being big is not the constraint. **The constraint is that we have been pointing a $1,222 business
at a $3.32 product.**
