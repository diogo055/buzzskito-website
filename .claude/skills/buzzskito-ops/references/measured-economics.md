# Measured economics — the numbers behind the rules

Every figure here was measured on this business in September 2026, most of it from the Amazon
Associates reports rather than inferred. Read this when sizing an opportunity, building a
forecast, or deciding whether a piece of work is worth doing.

## The funnel, confirmed against the Associates report

| | Inferred beforehand | Report says |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon clicks/month | 2,833 | **2,795** |
| items ordered | 295 | **300** |
| conversion rate | 10.4% | **10.73%** |
| commission rate | 4.75% | **4.80%** |
| earnings | $651 | **$656.48** |

The funnel model was right to ~2%. Two things it had missed:

- **Orders are not items.** 300 items came from 198 orders — 1.52 items per order. Earnings per
  **item** are $2.19; per **order** they are **$3.32**. Levanta pays per *conversion*, i.e. per
  order, so per-order is the correct basis for any Levanta comparison. Getting this wrong
  inflates the multiple by ~1.5×.
- **Returns cost 7.3% of gross earnings** — 17 items of 311 shipped, $51.61 clawed back from
  $708.09. Carry a ~7% haircut in any forecast.

## Which term can supply growth

| Term | Now | Can it supply 6×? |
|---|---|---|
| blog clicks | 8,983/mo | +52% available from CTR. Not 6×. |
| click-out to Amazon | **32.7%** | No — already ~16× the industry benchmark |
| purchase conversion | **10.4%** | No — would need 64% |
| **commission per order** | **$3.32** | **Yes — and only this one** |

$4,000/month at today's traffic needs $13.53 per order. Levanta's measured Canadian rate on
Harris bed bug killer is **CA$16.81 per conversion** — 5.1× what Amazon pays. That single row
is the whole ceiling, and it is blocked on brand approval, not on content or authority.

**A dehumidifier click is worth 15× a mosquito click, and the site sends 41× more traffic to
mosquito.** The traffic is aimed almost opposite to where the money is.

**Four clusters have earned exactly zero.** Note `tick` in particular: 55 pages, 219 blog
clicks, no affiliate revenue ever. That is not dead weight — tick is the company's core service
and all 62 tick pages route to the quote form. Its value is leads, not commissions, and it
should not be counted as affiliate inventory or "recirculated" to affiliate pages.

**Sample-size caveats:** dehumidifier is n=6 orders and steamer n=8 — directional only. Bedbug
at n=42 is the trustworthy high-value row.

Not one high-basket item — no propane trap, no bed-bug heater, no $400 zapper. **What sells
here is $8–40 consumables and gadgets.** Ranking Levanta brands by basket size has now been
wrong twice; rank by what the reports say actually sold.

Four of the five are ASIN-pinned while pins are only 1.5% of links (35 `asin=` against 2,357
`search=`). That is suggestive, not causal — the report marks four of five as "Indirect", and
pins were placed on the pages judged most commercial anyway. **Combat Max is the #3 seller with
no pin at all, which makes it a clean one-page test.**

## The CTR picture

Canadian traffic, by position band, 84 days:

| Band | Impressions | Share | CTR |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | 29,971 | 6.1% | 3.22% |
| 3–5 | 45,888 | 9.4% | 2.34% |
| 5–8 | 178,907 | 36.7% | 1.47% |
| **8–12** | **195,292** | **40.1%** | **0.52%** |
| 12–20 | 25,985 | 5.3% | 0.28% |

**Forty percent of everything the site earns in Google sits at position 8–12**, converting at a
third of the band above it.

Two corrections worth carrying:

1. **Do not use published CTR curves to size anything here.** This site earns roughly a fifth of
   them at every position. An estimate built on a standard curve will be ~5× too high.
2. **The distribution is bimodal, not a flat penalty.** Among top-5 Canadian pairs with ≥100
   impressions, 20 earn 5–13.8% (13.8% at position 2.0 on "summit mosquito bits canada") while
   52 earn under 2%. There is no uniform brand wall — some pages clear it daily. The winners are
   specific and intent-matched; the losers are bare head terms whose title answers a narrower
   question than the one typed.

Sizing the gap against the site's **own** 75th-percentile CTR per position — asking a page only
to do what its neighbours already do — gives **+2,091 Canadian clicks per 84 days, +52%**, with
no new pages and no ranking change. At $0.0758 per blog click that is ~$56/month at full
capture. That is the entire prize behind the site's biggest measured traffic problem, which is
why the service side matters more than any of it.
