# Named websites that grew or collapsed in Google traffic, 2025-2026

Research date: Nov 3, 2026. This builds on the Oct 30 notes (`research_notes/Google Images traffic growth plan/`) and the Nov 3 policy timeline. Figures reused from the Oct 30 notes are marked "(Oct 30 notes)" and were not fetched again.

**Grades.**
- **[A]**: the site's own Search Console or analytics, shown.
- **[B]**: a statement by the site, the company or its consultant without the underlying data, or anonymised consultant cases.
- **[C]**: third-party estimate (Similarweb, Semrush, SISTRIX visibility index, Ahrefs blog estimates).
- **[D]**: unsourced, untraceable, or from a weak secondary site.
- **[Google]**: a Google staff statement or Google documentation.

**How to read the Similarweb figures.**
- All come from Similarweb's free pages for September 2026, unless stated.
- The page puts the visit figure under a "Total Visits Last 3 Months" heading. For a-z-animals.com, though, Similarweb's 3.2M is close to Semrush's single-month September figure of 2.98M, so I treat these figures as monthly.
- "Organic" means organic search's share of desktop visits only.
- September is when interest in insects, birds and gardens falls, so month-on-month drops in September are mostly seasonal. The 3-month global rank change is a better signal, but season affects it too.
- The Oct 30 notes found a 10x spread between tools for one small site. Treat anything under about 200K as rough.

**Limits.**
- Search Engine Land returned 403, so the HubSpot figures come from search summaries.
- Lily Ray's original Search Engine Journal article would not open. Only commentary on it could be read.
- Side Hustle Nation showed a bot check and was skipped. Reddit and X were not tried.
- No Ahrefs tools were used. One Ahrefs blog article was read as an ordinary web page.
- Free Similarweb and Semrush pages show only three months, so peers' year-long trends are not available.
- Nothing in the website repository was changed.

## 1. Which named sites gained Google traffic in 2025-2026, and what did they do?

### Takeaway
The named winners of the 2025-2026 updates are brands writing about what they own or sell, government, nonprofit and academic sources, and long-running hobby sites. Communities answering questions were also winners until late 2025. I found no named nature, insect, garden or pest content site with a documented large gain.

The best-documented small-publisher comeback, HouseFresh, followed real product testing, design work and an audience built on YouTube, not more pages. No winner in this research grew by publishing faster.

### Inferences
- **What the winners share is being the source.** Each is the organisation that owns the subject (a park service, a charity, a brand), an expert body, or a community of real observers. BuzzSkito's closest equivalent is what only it has: GTA service data, technician observations, and its own photos of local yards and pests. General pest reference rewritten from public sources sits on the aggregator side of Ray's split.
- **The one measured pace among gainers is modest.** Raptive's gainers published about one new post and updated about five a month, with visible authors and engaged readers. That comes from one food-heavy network in one update, so it is a signal, not a rule.
- **A possible opening, speculative.** Forums and YouTube losing visibility in March 2026 may free some identification searches that Reddit threads used to hold, for pages that give real first-hand identification help. No data tests this.
- **Recovery is slow.** HouseFresh took about two years, and its recovery coincided with an audience built outside Google. Avoiding a hit is far cheaper than repairing one.

### Gaps
- I found no named nature, insect, garden, wildlife or pest content site with documented 2025-2026 growth. Free tool pages show only three months, so year-long growth for peers cannot be shown here.
- I found no Search Console screenshots from a named nature or identification site.
- Amsive's appendix tables (about 400 more domains for March 2026) were not reviewed and may contain relevant names.
- SISTRIX's own May 2026 winner lists could not be read.

## 2. Which named sites lost most of their traffic in 2025-2026, and what did they share?

### Takeaway
Two patterns cover the named losses.
- **Fast, scaled publishing** of thin or machine-written pages, which rose and then fell within months: Grokipedia, the AI-platform sites in Lily Ray's study, a 50,000-page programmatic site, and the August 2026 spam cases.
- **Large general publishers and middlemen** losing to AI answers and to primary sources: People Inc. (owner of The Spruce), HubSpot's blog, consumer health publishers, and Reddit and YouTube in March 2026.

I found no 2025-2026 case of an image gallery or one-page-per-photo site being hit, and no named city or state template site with clean data.

### Inferences
- **Rise, then fall, months later.**
  - In Ray's data the fall comes 3-6 months after page growth peaks. Grokipedia's came about three months after launch.
  - BuzzSkito has added 545 pages since July 2026, 262 of them in October. If the pattern applies, the window to watch is roughly January to April 2027, just before the 2027 season.
  - The pattern is correlational, and BuzzSkito is not an AI content vendor's customer. But its guides are AI-drafted and AI-checked, so it shares the risky feature.
  - Track each weekly release cohort's impressions at 8-12 weeks.
- **Speed is not the cause; sameness is.** Grokipedia published about 6,000 pages a day; BuzzSkito published about 8 a day in October. What the losses share is many similar pages that add little to public sources, not the daily count.
- **Topic drift.** HubSpot's biggest losses were on off-topic pages. BuzzSkito, a mosquito and tick company, now also covers bed bugs, mice, spiders, snakes and wildlife. That fits Google's self-assessment warning about lots of content on many unrelated topics. Since HubSpot's cause is disputed, this is a warning, not proof.
- **Middlemen lose to sources.** Pages that restate CDC or extension-service information are on the aggregator side of the 2026 split.
- **Not seen in 2025-2026 cases:** image galleries, one page per photo, or city and state templates as a named collapse with data. The risk there rests on policy wording and Mueller's September 2026 comment (question 4), not on measured cases.

- Lily Ray's eight templates, and any sites she named, could not be read.

- HubSpot's figures are unverified at source, because Search Engine Land returned 403.

## 3. Do named sites with very large image libraries get large Google traffic, and has any 800-1,000 page site reached 30,000+ image clicks a month?

### Takeaway
The owner's belief is half true.
- **Image libraries with very large traffic do exist.** In each one, though, the picture is the product (free photos, printables) or a community answers the visitor's question (iNaturalist, BugGuide).
- **Images alone do not pull traffic.** Bugwood, an expert insect and plant photo database with about 333,000 images, shows an estimated 14,000 visits a month.
- **The 30,000 case was not found.** I found no documented case of an 800-1,000 page content site reaching 30,000 image clicks a month, and no named 2025-2026 image-traffic case at all.

**The 30,000 search**
- Searches for 2025-2026 image-traffic case studies and niche-site Search Console image reports turned up only guides and vendor claims without data. The one real report was an anecdote, from February 2025, of image traffic dropping — [SER, Feb 2025](https://seroundtable.com/google-image-search-down-ranking-ai-images-38892.html).
- One vendor puts optimising publishers at 3-8% of traffic from visual search, with no data behind it **[D]** — [polytraffic](https://polytraffic.pages.dev/articles/image-seo-visual-search-traffic.html).
- The highest 2024-2026 owner report found on Oct 30 was about 800 image clicks a month, at an unnamed site **[A]** — [suganthan.com](https://suganthan.com/blog/gsc-mcp-image-seo-tools/).

### Inferences
- **The "100,000 images" belief, tested.** It holds where the image is the product or a crowd answers questions. It fails for Bugwood, which has more than three times that many expert photos. Image count does not separate the two groups; what the visitor gets does.
- **A likely reason Bugwood gets so little.** Its images are licensed for reuse and appear on many extension and education sites, and Google tends to show one or a few hosts for near-duplicate images (Oct 30 notes). BuzzSkito's photos come from the same kind of public sources (iNaturalist, Wikimedia Commons, CDC), so the same limit probably applies. This is inference, not measured.
- **BuzzSkito's photos already earn more per image than the big archives.** It gets about 2.3-2.6 image clicks per photo a month, against about 0.35 all-channel visits per image at BugGuide and about 0.04 at Bugwood. The measures differ (image clicks against all visits), but the comparison suggests better pages per photo matter more than more photos.
- **30,000 image clicks a month** would be about 16 times BuzzSkito's late-October level. Nothing found shows a site of 800-1,000 pages at that level. The best-documented image traffic is either from before 2020 or anonymous. Treat it as unproven, not impossible: summer demand for bug and bite photos could lift the figure two to three times on season alone.
- **The Bugwood finding survives a large estimate error.** Similarweb can be wrong several-fold for small sites, but even ten times its Bugwood estimate (about 140K) would not support the belief for expert image archives.

### Gaps
- No named site publishes its Google Images share.
- Unsplash's and Houzz's library sizes were not verified here.
- It is unconfirmed whether Similarweb's bugwood.org figure includes the images.bugwood.org subdomain.

## 4. What publishing pace did growing sites use, and what did Google say in 2025-2026 about fast publishing, AI content and image pages?

### Takeaway
No source gives a safe number of new pages a week.
- **The fastest publishers rose and then fell within months:** Grokipedia at about 6,000 pages a day, the AI-platform sites, and a 50,000-page programmatic site.
- **The one measured pace among gainers was modest:** Raptive's gainers published at least one new post and updated five a month.
- **Google's 2025-2026 position is consistent.** Speed and AI use are not the problem in themselves. Large amounts of unoriginal, commodity content is, and a site full of it can lose Google's trust as a whole.

### Cited Findings
**Pace**
- **Raptive:** stable or rising sites in June 2025 published at least 1 new post and made at least 5 updates a month; falling sites managed 0-1 **[B]** — [Raptive](https://raptive.com/?p=9843).
- **AI-platform sites:** pages added fast for 6-12 months, then a traffic peak 3-6 months after the content peak, then decline **[C]** — [ConfigDeck commentary on Ray](https://configdeck.pages.dev/en/article/searchenginejournal/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai).
- **Grokipedia:** about 6,000 pages a day, and traffic halved within about four months of launch **[C]** — [Ahrefs blog](https://ahrefs.com/blog/wikipedia-vs-grokipedia).
- **CreativeKidsColor:** about 80 posts in its first 7 months (about 11 a month, my arithmetic), and still growing in Sep 2026 **[B, C]** — [Niche Site Growth](https://nichesitegrowth.beehiiv.com/p/these-niche-sites-are-flying); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/creativekidscolor.com/).
- **Vendor "publish more" studies** (for example, 11+ posts a month bringing 3.5 times the traffic) appear only in secondary write-ups with no method. The same write-ups cite a dataset where low-frequency, high-quality publishing beat high-frequency, low-quality publishing by 34% **[D]** — [inblog](https://inblog.ai/blog/publishing-frequency-data) (search summary).

**Google statements, 2025-2026 [Google]**
- **Danny Sullivan, reported Apr 1, 2025** (from the author's notes, partly paraphrased):
  - Google does not care how scaled content is produced. The key test is "large amounts of unoriginal content and also no matter how it's created".
  - He said Google never claimed AI content is fine as long as it is high quality.
  - He named content made mainly to capture traffic as a warning sign — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-on-scaled-content-its-going-to-be-an-issue/543308/).
- **Gary Illyes, August 2025 interview:**
  - AI content should be human-curated, meaning someone with editorial oversight confirms it is correct before it is published. Adding a "reviewed" label to the page is not the point.
  - He does not expect Google's guidance on review to change soon.
  - If quality and accuracy hold, how the content was made technically does not matter.
  - Content very similar to what already exists is a risk — [SEJ, Aug 12, 2025](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-says-ai-generated-content-should-be-human-reviewed/553486/); [SER, Aug 11, 2025](https://www.seroundtable.com/gary-illyes-google-interview-ai-search-content-seo-39910.html).
- **Danny Sullivan, Search Central Live Toronto, Apr 21-22, 2026.** His slides asked for content that is unique, authentic and not a commodity:
  - **Unique:** a viewpoint or information others lack or cannot easily copy.
  - **Specific:** about a particular case, not generic steps.
  - **Authentic:** shows first-hand knowledge.
  - **His example:** a running store's "top 10 things to consider when buying running shoes" is commodity content. An analysis of why one customer's shoes collapsed after 400 miles is not — [SER, Apr 23, 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-commodity-content-41200.html).
- **John Mueller, Bluesky, reported Sep 7, 2026**, about a site that combined many attributes into every possible pairing:
  - Programmatic SEO like this often produces a spam, borderline-spam or low-quality site.
  - Google's systems may have "lost faith in your site providing good value to users based on the old pages".
  - Making many pages is easy; giving real value is hard. Fixing it takes time and significant effort — [SER](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-lose-faith-42032.html).
- **Older statements still cited:**
  - Mueller (Mar 2024): a publishing pattern alone does not make content spam. Google said in 2017 that releasing 100,000 pages at once is not an SEO problem — [SER](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-publishing-content-in-bulk-spam-37077.html).
  - Mueller (Apr 2023): no SEO reason to hold back thousands of good pages — [SER](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-delay-release-of-thousands-of-pages-35200.html).
- **Image pages:** Mueller (Aug 2025) said Google reads a gallery page as one page with many images, and that an image deserves its own page only if it has unique text (Oct 30 notes) — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-unique-image-landing-pages-can-help-boost-search-visibility/553282/).
- **AI images:**
  - I found no 2025-2026 statement by Mueller.
  - In February 2024 he compared AI images to stock photos used for decoration, and said originals matter where readers expect authenticity (search result only) — [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/beyond-seo-john-mueller-on-ai-generated-images-stock-photography/504793/).
  - A secondary claim that Illyes said AI images carry no penalty is not in SEJ's account of the interview, so it is unverified.

### Inferences
- **No safe number, but a clear pattern.** What fails is many pages anyone could produce from public sources, published faster than they can be made distinctive. BuzzSkito published about 60 new pages a week in October. That is far above every documented gainer here (about 1 to 11 posts a month) and far below the collapse cases (thousands a day). The 50-new-URLs-a-day cap does not by itself make a release safe.
- **Sullivan's running-shoe example maps directly onto BuzzSkito.** "Ticks in Ohio" built from CDC data is the commodity version. A page on what the technician found when sampling GTA yards in June is not, and only a pest-control company can write it.
- **Weak pages can cost the money pages.** Mueller's September 2026 comment means a set of weak pages can lower trust in the whole site, including the service pages that earn revenue.
- **The current AI-check setup does not fit Illyes's description.** Human curation matches the Nov 3 audit's top item: 0 of 255 guides carry a human review. Guides checked only by a second AI pass do not meet it.

### Gaps
- No Google statement in 2025-2026 sets a number or rate.
- No named site has published both its pace and Search Console data showing safe, fast growth in 2025-2026.
- A reported October 2025 Sullivan post on human oversight of AI content could not be traced to its source.
