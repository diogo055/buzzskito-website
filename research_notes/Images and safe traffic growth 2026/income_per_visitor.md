## 1. What session RPMs do Mediavine and Journey publishers report in 2025-2026, by niche, country and month, and what happened to RPMs in 2026?

### Takeaway
No network publishes averages, so the best figures are single sites showing their own numbers. A 96%-US local blog averaged about $33-35 on full Mediavine (monthly $22-44, 2023-2025), then $54 in Q4 2025 and $34 in January 2026 on Raptive. Journey sites report far less: about $7.50-16 (travel, 27% US, Q1 2025) and about $9 (wine, 66% US, 2025). Season is the swing Mediavine itself quantifies: RPM "reliably drops by 40-60% in January and July". In 2026, US programmatic prices rose year on year (display CPM +27% in June) while publishers' ad volume fell by about a third, so per-session RPMs reported by individual sites look roughly flat to mildly down, and total revenue fell with traffic.

### Inferences
- **Journey against full Mediavine.** The only near like-for-like is the wine blog: about $9 on Journey against $12-15 in its first Raptive weeks, and those weeks were in Q4, which flatters them. Full-network US-heavy sites report $33-35 a year. Published Journey figures sit at roughly a third to three quarters of full-network figures, but country mix, niche and season confound every comparison. My planning ratio: Journey pays about **0.6-0.75x** what full Mediavine would on the same traffic. Low confidence.
- **Revised planning figure for this site on Journey** (my reasoning, low confidence). The earlier CA$20 for Nov-Dec (range CA$14-28) times 0.6-0.75 gives about **CA$12-15 per 1,000 sessions for Nov-Dec 2026 (range CA$8-20)**, about CA$6-11 in Jan-Mar 2027 and about CA$9-19 in summer 2027. Expect the first two to four weeks to read low: Danny's Journey RPM started at $1.61 on day one.
- The 2024 Journey anecdotes ($18-32) are far above the 2025 ones ($7.50-16, about $9). The sources differ in country mix and selection, and I found no explanation. Treat the 2024 figures as unrepresentative.
- **Country mix.** If Canada earns about 0.7 of the US and other countries about 0.4 (my assumption, as in the Oct 30 report), this site's mix (about 41% US, 40% Canada, 19% elsewhere) earns about 0.77 of an all-US site.
- **2026 in one line:** the price per ad rose and the number of ads fell. For a small site whose own traffic is growing, rising prices help. But the open-web price rise was small (+2.2% in Q2), and mid-2026 single-site reads (-5% to -9% YoY on Raptive, "steady decline" on Mediavine) do not show the +27% to +51% headline gains reaching blogs.

## 2. What raises income per session, and is more visitors or more value per visitor the bigger win?

### Takeaway
In the largest dataset found (Playwire, 8.8 billion sessions), revenue per session tracks ad impressions per page (r = 0.59) far more than pages per session (0.27), and not at all session duration (-0.03). Mediavine's own measured levers are video (Universal Player: +34% average RPM for sites without video, 2021), its Optimized Ad Experience (+7% RPM, Jul-Sep 2026) and leaving the adhesion close button off (+7% adhesion revenue). On one Mediavine site the Universal Player, adhesion and in-content ads each made about a quarter of revenue. For this site, season, network tier (getting Google demand back) and country mix move value per visitor more than any page change, and the traffic plan (about 6-8x by June 2027) is the biggest multiplier of all.

### Cited Findings

**Large-sample drivers**
- Playwire 2026 report: 8.8B sessions, 28.6B pageviews, 113.6B impressions (period not stated). Correlation (Pearson r) with revenue per session: impressions per pageview 0.59, impressions per session 0.55, pageviews per session 0.27, CPM 0.22, viewability 0.15, session duration -0.03. Publishers above the median on both page depth and ad density "earn 17x more per session" than those below on both. The 80-90% viewability bracket beats 90%+ on median revenue per session. Publishers at 90%+ fill earn 5.3x the revenue per session of those under 40%. When Amazon's ad demand is active it supplies on average 20.5% of revenue. The full 26-page PDF is behind a form. **[Primary, vendor]** — [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/2026-state-of-publisher-ad-revenue-report)
- Playwire also says ad density was the strongest lever when every variable was run against revenue per session. **[Primary, vendor] (excerpt)** — [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/2026-state-of-publisher-ad-revenue-report)

**Mediavine settings with measured or claimed effects**
- Optimized Ad Experience (OAE) sets the number of in-content ads per page from viewability, using the previous three days' data; "In every tier, ad density stays below 30%." OAE sites averaged +7.0% RPM against non-OAE sites in Jul-Sep 2026 (+12.3% August, +9.7% September). Not stated whether this was a controlled test, so self-selection is possible. **[Owner]** — [Mediavine help](https://help.mediavine.com/what-is-the-optimized-ad-experience)
- Universal Player (an outstream video ad in a sticky position; no own videos needed): "your RPM should increase by an average of 34%" for publishers not running video or the Outstream Video Adhesion; about 13% for sites switching from the Outstream Video Adhesion. On mobile it replaces the Outstream Mobile Adhesion. **[Owner, old]** — [Mediavine (Jun 23, 2021)](https://www.mediavine.com/blog/universal-player-one-video-solution-to-rule-them-all/)
- Video ads "can easily add 15-30% to your overall RPM". **[Owner] (excerpt)** — [Mediavine help](https://help.mediavine.com/video-player-faq-best-practices)
- Universal Player Plus (needs the publisher's own video): one food blog's overall ad revenue +29% YoY; UVP+ CPM 38% above the standard Universal Player; video over a third of revenue. **[Owner, one site, 2026]** — [Mediavine case study](https://www.mediavine.com/case-study/how-uvp-delivered-a-29-revenue-lift/)
- Leaving the adhesion close button off raises adhesion revenue by 7% on average, up to 10%. **[Owner] (excerpt)** — [Mediavine help](https://help.mediavine.com/the-adhesion-close-button)

**One site's ad-unit split and switch-off tests**
- BikeGremlin's Mediavine revenue by unit (undated, probably 2022): Universal Player 28%, in-content 26%, adhesion 25%, sticky sidebar 16%, sidebar 3%, mobile interstitial 2%. Switching adhesion off: estimated over 30% less revenue (mobile adhesion about 20%). Mobile Universal Player off: about 20% less (estimate). Mobile in-content ads blocked: about 25% less, measured over ten days. **[Owner, one site, mostly estimates]** — [BikeGremlin](https://io.bikegremlin.com/29602/mediavine-monetization-review/)

**Pages per session and recirculation**
- Mediavine's RPM explainer: session RPM can rise when engagement improves even while per-pageview RPM falls. **[Owner] (excerpt)** — [Mediavine](https://prod.mediavine.com/blog/what-is-rpm/)
- Grow's carousel, sidebar recommendations, social, search and bookmarks together added 5.1% pageviews at The Hollywood Gossip; "longer sessions ... means more ads viewed and increased RPM per session". **[Owner] (excerpt, about late 2025)** — [Mediavine case study](https://www.mediavine.com/case-study/how-grow-me-helped-the-hollywood-gossip-make-100000-already/)
- I found no measured before/after for Grow's "What's Next" unit or for in-content related links.

**Time on page and ad refresh**
- "Ads refresh every 30 seconds while on screen"; visitors who "don't stay long ... see fewer ads, leading to a lower RPM". **[Owner]** — [Raptive (updated Mar 18, 2025)](https://raptive.com/resources/traffic-sources/)
- Mediavine refreshes an ad after more than 30 seconds in view; "The adhesion ads and sticky sidebar ad will see the biggest benefit." **[Owner, as recorded from Mediavine's help centre]** — `project_mediavine_rpm_estimate.md`

**Page speed**
- Netzwelt (German tech site, about 2020-21; Google case study): a smaller sticky top ad, lazy-loaded lower ads and fewer ad scripts gave ad revenue +18%, CPM +20-30%, page views +27%, bounce rate -50% and viewability over 75%. Better viewability of the sticky top ad offset the banner sizes removed. **[Owner, one site, old]** — [web.dev](https://web.dev/case-studies/netzwelt)
- On this site, blocking every third-party script left phone LCP unchanged at about 3.2 s; speed work was not pursued on evidence. **[Own site]** — `CLAUDE.md`, "Speed of the article template"

**Measurement traps**
- On Journey sites in 2024, GA4 counted about 60% more pageviews than Mediavine did. **[Third-party]** — [She Knows SEO](https://sheknowsseo.co/journey-by-mediavine-analysis/)
- A tracking change moved one site's Raptive "monetized sessions" from about 110% to about 80%, shifting its reported RPM by about a quarter. **[Owner]** — [This Week in Blogging](https://thisweekinblogging.com/mediavine-vs-raptive/)

**This site's starting point**
- GA4, Oct 21-23, 2026: 1.14 pages per session, 52% of sessions engaged, 64% phones, about 41% US / 40% Canada / 19% elsewhere; about 4 ad impressions per session (estimate); about 12,600 sessions a month, about 85% starting on ad pages. **[Own site]** — `project_mediavine_rpm_estimate.md`
- Since launch (Nov 2, 2026) the phone adhesion ad and the docked video player are ON, and our own bottom bars hide below 1024 px while Mediavine pins an ad. **[Own site]** — `CLAUDE.md`, Display Ads section
- Plan: about 48,000 Google visitors in May 2027 and 63,000 in June 2027; sessions run about 1.59x Google web clicks. **[Own site]** — `CLAUDE.md`, God build section; `project_mediavine_rpm_estimate.md`

**More visitors or more value per visitor?** (my arithmetic)
- Ads only, sessions on ad pages needed for **CA$5,000 a month**: at CA$10 per 1,000, 500,000; at CA$15, about 333,000; at CA$20, 250,000; at CA$30, about 167,000; at CA$46 (a 96%-US full-Mediavine site's $33 average), about 108,000.
- Plan traffic in May-June 2027 is about 76,000-100,000 sessions (48,000-63,000 Google visitors x 1.59), about 65,000-85,000 on ad pages. At the Journey summer range (CA$9-19) that is about **CA$0.6-1.6K a month from ads**; with Google demand back (about CA$15-25), about CA$1.0-2.1K.
- So: traffic is the bigger multiplier on offer (the plan is about 6-8x today's sessions), while the controllable value-per-visitor levers add perhaps +10-25% and Google re-approval perhaps +30-70%. Value per visitor decides how much traffic the goal needs; at plan traffic, display ads alone do not reach CA$5,000 a month, so the goal also depends on Amazon income and on traffic beyond the plan.
- "Mediavine pays for traffic" holds only for sessions where an ad loads. A session that is ad-blocked, filtered, a bot, or gone before the first ad renders pays nothing (Mediavine's own Apr 2026 post, section 4). Maximum sessions and page views help mainly through more ad impressions per page that people actually see.

### Gaps
- No measured before/after for Grow recirculation or "What's Next" on any Mediavine or Journey site.
- No Journey-specific evidence on OAE, video or adhesion effects, or whether Journey offers OAE.
- Playwire's correlations are across publishers, not causal, and its full report is gated.
- Speed evidence is old or from vendors (Publift and Sparteo claims seen only as excerpts were left out).

## 3. How much is a visitor from Google Images, Discover, Pinterest or an AI assistant worth, compared with a normal Google web visitor?

### Takeaway
Only Pinterest has 2025-2026 statements from a network: Mediavine's CEO session was summarised as saying Pinterest traffic is now "equally or more valuable" than Google, and Raptive says Pinterest RPMs have risen. Discover sends large volumes in some niches but has been falling, and no network has published its RPM; anecdotes call it spiky, all-mobile and weak per session. AI assistants send engaged visitors in retail data (59% more time on site) but only 0.01% to under 1% of publisher traffic, and nobody has published an ad RPM for them. Google Images stays at the Oct 30 estimate of about 0.7x a same-country web visit, probably lower once school sessions are counted (section 4).

### Cited Findings

**Google Images**
- Planning value about 0.7x a same-country Google web visit (range 0.5-1.0x), about 1.5 Canadian cents per image visit, with Google demand assumed. No network or publisher reports an RPM for image visitors. **[Own site, estimate]** — `research_notes/Google Images traffic growth plan/value_of_image_visitors.md`
- About 85% of image clicks are from the US, and about 55% look like US school computers. **[Own site]** — `CLAUDE.md`, SEO Setup (Images); `value_of_image_visitors.md`

**Google Discover**
- Raptive (updated Feb 11, 2026): Discover is "roughly 50% of Google traffic" for tech, gaming, sports and travel sites, 76% for news, 2% for food; outside food it fell 4% YoY in 2025 and rose 5% in the second half; about one billion Discover clicks a year across Raptive. One creator fell from 10 million monthly Discover clicks to under one million after the August 2025 spam update. No RPM comparison with Search. **[Owner]** — [Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/why-you-should-be-optimizing-for-google-discover/)
- Chartbeat: Discover -34.3% YoY in the year to July 2026 (-6.6% the year before); its share of pageviews fell 28.4% over two years. **[Primary, news-heavy]** — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/)
- Discover CPMs underperform because "the traffic is almost entirely mobile" and most ad stacks "are not optimised for mobile". **[Owner, vendor] (excerpt)** — [Newor Media](https://newormedia.com/blog/google-discover-for-publishers/)
- 2021, a travel blogger: Discover "viral spikes" helped exposure but not income and pulled down average Mediavine RPM. **[Owner, old] (excerpt)** — [World Travel Family](https://worldtravelfamily.com/2021-blogging-income-report/)
- Raptive case: BJPenn got 44% more Discover clicks and 56% higher RPM over 90 days YoY, credited to a strategy session; the RPM gain is not attributed to Discover visitors. **[Owner] (excerpt)** — [Raptive](https://raptive.com/blog/bjpenn_case_study/)

**Pinterest**
- Sep 2025 AMA, as summarised: Google traffic historically gave the highest RPMs, but Pinterest traffic is now equally or more valuable. ## 4. School and Chromebook traffic: do school networks block ads or count as invalid traffic? What do networks say about short, low-engagement visits?

### Takeaway
I found no statement from Mediavine, Raptive or Google that school traffic is invalid, and no study measuring ad delivery on school networks. What exists: administrators can force ad blockers onto managed Chromebooks and filter sites by category; the ad industry's invalid-traffic rules exclude legitimate gateways from data-centre filtering; and Mediavine now says openly that sessions where no ad can serve dilute RPM. So the likely cost of school visits is "no ad served, no income", not a penalty. That has to be measured on this site.

### Cited Findings
- Districts can force-install uBlock Origin on managed Chromebooks from the Google Admin console (Device Management > Chrome > User Settings > force-installed apps). **[Third-party, about 2020] (excerpt)** — [DeployHappiness](https://deployhappiness.com/deploying-ublock-origin-for-chrome-and-configuring-a-whitelist/)
- A student: "the schools have decided to block ad-blockers from the Chromebooks". Anecdotal; other students report the opposite. **[Unsourced] (excerpt)** — [PCPartPicker forum](https://pcpartpicker.com/forums/topic/319805-getting-ad-blocker-on-school-chromebook)
- Chrome 138 disabled uBlock Origin (Manifest V2) for consumers. Effect on admin-installed extensions not confirmed. **[Third-party] (excerpt)** — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/how-to-bring-back-ublock-origin-in-chrome-whether-youre-re-enabling-or-installing-it)
- School filters such as Securly, GoGuardian and Lightspeed can block websites by category from a database. No source said whether an advertising category is blocked by default. **[Third-party] (excerpt)** — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_filtering_in_schools)
- MRC general invalid traffic includes "known data-center traffic", traffic "detected by IP Address to originate in a data-center" and "determined to be a consistent source of non-human traffic". It excludes "routing artifacts of legitimate users" and "virtual machine legitimate browsing", with an exception for "servers known to be acting as a legitimate access point for users, e.g. a corporate gateway". **[Third-party summary of MRC rules, 2017]** — [Pixalate](https://www.pixalate.com/blog/mrc-definitions-givt-invalid-traffic-data-center-traffic)
- Third-party AdSense guides describe Google flagging repeated ad clicks from one IP address; none mentions schools. **[Third-party] (excerpt)** — [Newor Media](https://newormedia.com/blog/invalid-traffic-on-adsense/)
- Mediavine, Apr 20, 2026: standard RPM divides revenue by all sessions, including "the bot traffic, the ad-blocked sessions, the visits where no ad could ever have served"; it recommends measuring "monetizable sessions only". No shares or definitions given. **[Owner]** — [Mediavine](https://www.mediavine.com/blog/your-rpm-isnt-lying-to-you-but-it-might-not-be-telling-the-whole-truth/)
- Mediavine help: "Invalid traffic can cause RPM to plummet"; warning signs include traffic from lower-rate countries to pages that do not fit (its example is India), a sudden jump in US traffic, and a surge in desktop readers on a mostly-mobile site. Suspected malicious traffic goes to publishers@mediavine.com. **[Owner]** — [Mediavine help](https://help.mediavine.com/why-did-my-earnings-stay-flat-or-decrease-when-my-traffic-went-up)
- Short stays "see fewer ads", and ads refresh every 30 seconds on screen. **[Owner]** — [Raptive](https://raptive.com/resources/traffic-sources/)
- Session duration correlates -0.03 with revenue per session, against 0.59 for impressions per pageview. **[Primary, vendor]** — [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/2026-state-of-publisher-ad-revenue-report)
- Journey's policy defines invalid traffic as impressions or clicks that artificially inflate traffic and earnings, e.g. bots and paid traffic services; traffic must come from human interest; clicking your own ads can mean suspension. **[Owner] (excerpt; both pages returned 403)** — [Journey Publisher Policies](https://journeymv.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/34832302083483-Journey-Publisher-Policies); [Journey IVT page](https://journeymv.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/38347087850267-Understanding-Invalid-Traffic-IVT)
- This site already returns 403 to Singapore and China visitors without a crawler user agent (about 20% of GA4 sessions, 2-4 s each), as invalid traffic for an ad account. **[Own site]** — `CLAUDE.md`, Bot block

### Inferences
- School visitors are people who arrived from real Google searches, so by every rule found they are not invalid traffic. Two real risks remain: (1) **no monetization**: a forced ad blocker or a filter that blocks ad domains means no ad serves, while GA4 still counts the session; (2) **click patterns**: many students behind one school IP clicking ads could look like concentrated clicking. The second is unlikely at this site's size, but it is a reason never to invite clicks near ads.
- If cloud filters route school traffic through vendor servers, those IPs could look like data-centre traffic. MRC rules exempt known legitimate gateways, but whether each exchange's fraud vendor knows every school filter's addresses is unknown.
- Short visits: duration itself barely matters once impressions per page are counted (Playwire). What matters is whether the first-screen ad and the adhesion load before the reader leaves.
- **Effect on the image-visitor value:** if a large share of school sessions serve no ads, the Oct 30 figure of about 1.5 cents per image visit is too high. Until measured, plan on about 1 cent (my adjustment).
- **How to measure without touching the site:** after a full month, compare Mediavine RPM or impressions per session for image-heavy pages on US weekdays 8 a.m.-3 p.m. against evenings and weekends, alongside GA4 sessions for the same pages and hours, plus the share of Chrome OS by hour in GA4. Also check whether the Journey dashboard shows monetized sessions.

### Gaps
- No ad-network, Google, Mediavine or Raptive guidance on school or managed-device traffic.
- No data on what share of US K-12 networks block ad domains or force ad blockers.
- Whether Journey's exchanges treat school cloud-filter IPs as data-centre traffic: unknown.
- Whether school-hour traffic is ever treated as child-directed on a general-audience site: no source found.

## 5. Mediavine's 2025-2026 rules and announcements that matter here (AI content, traffic quality, invalid traffic, Set-Up Period, minimum traffic, pay)

### Takeaway
The AI rule has not changed since March 2024: Mediavine does not monetize "low-quality, mass-produced, unedited or undisclosed AI content that is scraped from other websites", checks with third-party tools plus a human Marketplace Quality team, and says it will act on vetted sites that slip. The 2026 changes are about access: full Mediavine by ad revenue (about $5,000 a year) from Jan 15, 2026, and Journey from 1,000 premium sessions a month (US, UK, Canada, Australia), with Grow optional, from Aug 2026.
