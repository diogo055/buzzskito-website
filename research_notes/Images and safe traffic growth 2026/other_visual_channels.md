## 1. Pinterest in 2026: outbound traffic now vs 2022-2024, whether pest/garden pins get traffic, what it takes, Pinterest's own statements

### Takeaway
Pinterest is growing as an app (661M users, Q3 2026) and says outbound clicks "grew faster than users", but it publishes no organic-publisher click numbers. Its product direction (Pinterest Assistant, "decide without leaving Pinterest", AI shopping) points at keeping users on Pinterest. Publisher evidence for 2025-2026 is thin and mostly anecdotal: one measured lifestyle blog where Pinterest overtook search, set against many 2026 forum reports of sudden 80-90% account-level drops with no explanation. I found no traffic data at all for pest or bug pins. It is a slow (months to years), labour-heavy channel whose licence fit is good only for public-domain/CC0 photos.

### Cited Findings
**Pinterest's own statements (company-reported)**
- Q3 2026 (released Oct 29, 2026): 661 million global monthly active users, up 10%. US & Canada: 107 million, up only 4% from 103 million. US & Canada ARPU $8.38, up 10%. Revenue $1,174M, up 12%. CEO Bill Ready: "Our differentiated AI-driven visual search and shopping experiences are making Pinterest increasingly valuable". The release says nothing about outbound clicks or traffic to websites — [Pinterest Q3 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001506293/000150629326000151/q3-26xpressrelease.htm)
- Q3 2026 earnings call (Nov 2026), Bill Ready: "engagement is deepening as searches, boards created, and outbound clicks all grew faster than users in Q3" (no rate given). Also: "Users conduct more than 80 billion searches on Pinterest each month... The vast majority are visual, and more than half are commercial." "Pinterest Assistant is now in general availability in the U.S." "approximately 80% of searches using Pinterest Assistant are commercial in nature." With Assistant, "users can research, compare, and decide without leaving Pinterest." "Gen Z now represents 57% of our users"; "Two-thirds of Gen Z users say they come to Pinterest to shop." Visual Search Ads are "designed to drive measurable outbound action". Ready: the first ad product built around "clicks to advertisers" launched "about three years ago". The call does not mention creators or publishers — [Motley Fool transcript, Q3 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/02/pinterest-pins-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- Q2 2026: 640M MAU (+11%); US & Canada 106M (+4%); US & Canada ARPU $8.30. CEO: "AI is at the heart of our momentum and is a clear accelerant for our business." No click figures — [Pinterest Q2 2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001506293/000150629326000102/q2-26xpressrelease.htm)
- Q3 2025: outbound clicks *to advertisers* reportedly up 40% year over year. These are paid advertiser clicks, not organic publisher traffic (via search summary, not checked against the transcript) — [TipRanks, Q3 2025 call highlights](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/pinterests-q3-2025-earnings-call-highlights-growth)
- Gen AI labels (Apr 30, 2025): image Pins its systems detect as AI-generated or AI-modified get an "AI modified" label in close-up. Pinterest also ran a "see fewer" experiment for AI-prone categories such as beauty and art. Detection is admitted to be imperfect, so there is an appeals route — [Pinterest Newsroom](https://newsroom.pinterest.com/news/introducing-gen-ai-labels)
- Idea Pins were merged into one Pin format in 2023, and that format can carry external links — [Pinterest Create blog](https://create.pinterest.com/blog/new-pin-format-update)
- Image specs (Pinterest's ads best-practice page, undated): "We recommend using a 2:3 aspect ratio, or 1000 x 1500 pixels." "Most people use Pinterest on mobile devices." Titles can run to 100 characters, and "The first 40 are most likely to show"; descriptions can run to 800. The page says nothing about posting frequency or Rich Pins — [Pinterest Business: Creative best practices](https://business.pinterest.com/creative-best-practices/)

- Older benchmarks, labelled by date (2013-2016 era): Chartbeat put Pinterest under 1% of publisher referrals and SimpleReach just over 2%. 79% of Pinterest referrals went to the top 10 publishers on SimpleReach's platform. **2026 drop reports (anecdotal)**

**What it takes (vendor research, with dates)**
- Tailwind "2025" benchmark (published Nov 19, 2024; about 1 million Pins): the top 1% of Pins drove over half of impressions and clicks, and the bottom 80% of fresh Pins drove under 10%. Over 90% of website traffic came from newly created ("fresh") Pins rather than saves (via search summary) — [Tailwind benchmark](https://t_pr6104-www.tailwindapp.com/pinterest-marketing/research/2025-benchmark-study-part-1)

Other Tailwind posts give a 2-5 Pins a day "sweet spot". Pinterest's own advice is cited secondhand as 5-25 fresh Pins a day, and I could not confirm that on a Pinterest page (via search summary) — [Tailwind: how many pins](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/how-many-pins-should-you-create)
- GenSumo (third party, 173,000+ Pins, 2025-early 2026, method unverified): static Pins average 4.8 outbound clicks per 1,000 impressions and video Pins 1.6. Pins build slowly, plateau, then decline over a long tail — [GenSumo content mix](https://gensumo.pages.dev/research/pinterest-content-mix-report/); [GenSumo pin lifespan](https://gensumo.pages.dev/research/pinterest-pin-lifespan-report/)
- Consistent Pinterest traffic "can take several months" — [SocialBee](https://socialbee.com/blog/how-to-use-pinterest-for-blogging/)

**Pest, bug and garden fit**
- Pest boards exist, but none comes with traffic data: e.g. "100 Bugs and Garden Pest Control ideas" and "74 Residential and Commercial Pest Control ideas" — [Pinterest board](https://www.pinterest.com/cottagefarmstead/bugs-and-garden-pest-control/); [Pinterest board](https://www.pinterest.com/no1pestbri/residential-and-commercial-pest-control/)
- In our own Oct 30 teardown of 16 pest image searches, "no stock library or Pinterest page reaches the top 15" of Google Images — [Oct 30 report](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/reports/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan.md)

### Inferences
- **Traffic now vs 2022-2024:** the evidence cannot put a number on the change for publishers. Pinterest's "outbound clicks grew faster than users" is a platform total that probably leans on paid clicks to advertisers (the metric it monetises). Its strategy (Assistant, "decide without leaving Pinterest", AI Restyle) pushes the other way for organic publishers. The forum pattern of unexplained 80-90% account-level drops since spring 2026 is many anecdotes, not a measurement.
- **Visitor type:** mostly phone users, mostly young (Gen Z 57%) and mostly outside North America (US & Canada is 107M of 661M, about 16%). English pins on US pest topics would probably draw US women and young adults on phones. For Mediavine that is a "school-free" phone audience, unlike the ~55% of Google Images clicks the Oct 30 report traced to US school computers. One blogger says Pinterest traffic "pays a lot more"; that is one site, in a lifestyle niche.
- **Scale, illustrative only:** at GenSumo's 4.8 outbound clicks per 1,000 static-pin impressions, an account reaching 100,000 impressions a month would send about 480 visits a month, and one at 500,000 would send about 2,400. At the ~CA$20 per 1,000 sessions estimated for Nov-Dec (Oct 30 report), that is about CA$10-50 a month, after months of steady pinning. I found no pest account's impression level to anchor these figures.
- **Pest fit:** unknown. Pinterest is a planning and shopping platform ("more than half [of searches] are commercial"), and pest ID is a reactive, in-the-moment need. Garden-pest and "natural pest control" ideas fit Pinterest's habits better than "what bit me" pages. That is reasoning, not data.
- **Risk to Google:** I found no mechanism by which pinning our own photos hurts Google rankings. Pinterest pages also do not currently compete in Google Images for these pest searches (Oct 30 teardown).

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 dataset compares publisher outbound clicks from Pinterest with 2022-2024. No Mediavine or Raptive figure for Pinterest-referred sessions or RPM was found. Reddit and the Mediavine/Raptive Facebook groups, where owners post these numbers, were not tried (Reddit refuses the tools).
- No traffic data for pest, bug, bite or home-pest pins, and no Pinterest Trends data for pest terms.
- Whether Pinterest's 2026 statement on outbound clicks covers organic clicks to non-advertisers is not stated.
- I could not attribute or verify a newsletter-reported claim that a Pinterest ad representative said traffic to ad-running sites was being throttled. Pinterest has not confirmed it.
- Rich Pins (article) in 2026: not checked on a Pinterest help page.

## 2. Google Discover in 2026: what changed, which sites get it, image requirements, E-E-A-T and freshness, swings, and whether a seasonal pest site could get it

### Takeaway
Discover is now a phone-only, news-heavy feed: desktop Discover was withdrawn by September 2026, AI summaries and YouTube take part of the feed, and the February 2026 Discover core update (US English first) favours sites based in the user's country and with topic-level expertise. A Canadian site writing for US readers is on the wrong side of that change in US Discover, though seasonal Canadian tick/mosquito stories could plausibly surface for Canadian users if the update reaches Canada. The technical gate is mostly open already (max-image-preview:large is set). The prize is unpredictable and cannot be worked without the clickbait Google now penalises.

### Cited Findings
- **Eligibility and images (Google docs):** content is eligible automatically if indexed and within policy; "No special tags or structured data are required"; eligibility is "not a guarantee." The images should be at least 1,200 px wide, more than 300,000 pixels in total, and 16:9. Large previews are "Enabled by the max-image-preview:large setting, or by using AMP." Name the preferred image with og:image or primary-image markup. Avoid logos and text-heavy images. If you crop, keep the key detail in the landscape crop and name that crop in og:image — [Google Search Central: Discover](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- **Policies (Google docs):** avoid clickbait, misleading previews and sensationalism that targets "morbid curiosity, titillation, or outrage." "Discover uses SafeSearch and also filters content that might seem shocking or unexpected." Violations can bring Discover manual actions. Sports, health, entertainment and lifestyle content "appear regularly"; older content can appear "if it remains helpful and relevant" — [Google Search Central: Discover](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- **Swings (Google docs):** "Traffic from Discover is less predictable or dependable when compared to keyword-driven search visits," and it should be treated "as supplemental." Changes can come from shifting interests, content types, Search updates or Discover changes unrelated to quality — [Google Search Central: Discover](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover)
- **February 2026 Discover core update (Google, Feb 5, 2026):** Google's first core update for Discover alone. Its goals are "Showing users more locally relevant content from websites based in their country," "Reducing sensational content and clickbait," and more in-depth, original, timely content from sites with expertise. "our systems are designed to identify expertise on a topic-by-topic basis": a local news site with a gardening section could have gardening expertise, while a movie review site with one gardening article likely would not. "We're releasing this update to English language users in the US" and Google "will expand it to all countries and languages in the months ahead." "many sites may see no change at all" — [Google Search Central blog](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/02/discover-core-update)
- The update finished Feb 27, 2026, after 22 days. NewzDash data, pre (Jan 25-31) vs post (Feb 8-14): the international share of the US Discover score fell from 8.52% to 7.04%, and the US share rose from 88.86% to 89.94%. The Sun lost about two thirds, The Independent about half and Reuters about a fifth. California local articles in the Top 100 went from 10 to 16. The winners were "not always the biggest brands" but sites with a clear topical focus. Barry Schwartz says the update did not move organic ranking volatility. NewzDash calls its findings "directional" — [SEO-Kreativ, Mar 7, 2026, updated Oct 30](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-discover-core-update-february-2026-completed-what-the-newzdash-data-reveals/)
- Search Engine Roundtable says the update "may impact the traffic of non-US websites that publish news for a US audience." Google's docs also gained "Provide an overall great page experience" — [Search Engine Roundtable, Feb 5, 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-february-2026-discover-core-update-40887.html)
- **Expansion beyond the US:** a source written after the rollout said it was still limited to US English users, with no date for other countries. I found nothing later (via search summary) — [Priority Pixels](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/insights/googles-february-2026-discover-core-update-has-finished-rolling-out/)
- **Desktop:** Discover on desktop began rolling out in May 2025 — [Search Engine Roundtable, May 2025](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-discover-on-desktop-live-39434.html). By Sep 17, 2026 it could no longer be found. Damien Andell said Google "shut down Google Discover on desktop in all the countries where it was working." There is no Google statement — [Search Engine Roundtable, Sep 17, 2026](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-discover-on-desktop-not-found-42103.html)
- **AI summaries and other feed changes:** AI summaries in Discover began rolling out in July 2025 in the US Google app — [TechCrunch, Jul 15, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/15/google-discover-adds-ai-summaries-threatening-publishers-with-further-traffic-declines). Marfeel (vendor, undated) says 51% of Discover entries across the US, Brazil and Mexico are AI summaries. Of those, 77% lead to an inline YouTube play and 23% link to a publisher (via search summary) — [Marfeel](https://www.marfeel.com/blog/google-discover-s-shift-to-ai-youtube-and-x-what-publishers-are-losing). On Oct 26, 2026, a user reported Discover testing disabled clicks on sources in summaries, and SER could not reproduce it (via search summary) — [Search Engine Roundtable](https://seroundtable.com/google-discover-disabling-clicks-42369.html)
- **Who gets Discover traffic:** for news publishers, NewzDash puts Discover at 67.51% of Google traffic in late 2025. Search was 27.42%, down from 51.10% in 2023. These are shares of Google mobile traffic, not volumes (via search summary) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/news-publishers-lose-half-their-google-search-traffic-in-two-years/). John Shehata says roughly 75% by Q2 2026, and that a strong search foundation is "a primary factor in maintaining stable visibility" (via search summary) — [NewzDash](https://www.newzdash.com/guide/14-predictions-publishers-rebuild-phase-news-industry)
- **Non-news examples (vendor anecdotes):** a 2024 leaky-faucet guide got 50,000 Discover visits during an early-2026 cold snap; a gardening site "lived in Discover feeds" on topical authority and saves — [ClickRank (vendor)](https://www.clickrank.ai/google-discover-ranking-factors/). An SEO site that studies Discover reports "little to nothing" in its own Discover report — [SEO-Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-discover-core-update-february-2026-completed-what-the-newzdash-data-reveals/)
- **This site:** `max-image-preview: 'large'` is set site-wide — [app/layout.tsx line 80](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/app/layout.tsx). The photo ledger holds 829 photos, of which 745 are at least 1,200 px wide and 432 are at least 1,200 px wide and landscape (≥1.3:1). Only 15 are near 16:9 already (my count) — [data/god-build/photos.json](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/data/god-build/photos.json). As of Oct 30, about 96 pages lacked a real share image — [Oct 30 report](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/reports/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan.md)

### Inferences
- **Can a seasonal pest site get Discover?** Possibly in Canada for timely local stories ("blacklegged ticks out early in Ontario"), and only once Google applies the country-local update there. That fits "timely content" and the "based in their country" goal. In US Discover, a Canadian site writing for US readers is the case Google's change down-weights: the international share fell, and SER warned non-US sites with US audiences. So the large US information library is a poor Discover bet.
- **Visitor type:** phones only now (desktop withdrawn), in the Google app or Chrome on mobile, so it is not school-computer traffic. Discover visits are bursty, which suits Mediavine RPM on the day but not planning.
- **Requirements for us:** cheap. The flag is already set. Each article needs an og:image of at least 1,200 px, cropped to 16:9 with the subject kept in frame. Avoid text-heavy cards as the og:image and graphic bite close-ups as the lead (SafeSearch and "shocking" filters). Write headlines without sensationalism. Topic-level expertise favours mosquito and tick content, the business's real trade, over the long tail of adjacent pests.
- **Safety:** Discover optimisation does not touch web rankings (the Discover update left organic volatility alone). Sensational headlines and previews can bring a Discover manual action and conflict with the Feb 2026 update, so the safe play is not to chase it.

### Gaps
- No Discover traffic data for any pest, insect, garden-pest or home-problem site, measured or anecdotal.
- Whether the Feb 2026 update has reached Canada or other countries since about May 2026 is unconfirmed. Google's status dashboard was not checked.
- The "Follow" button for publishers in Discover (reported by trackers for Sep 2025) was not verified from Google. Nor were Discover's 2026 share of AI summaries for Canada or the size of typical traffic swings for non-news sites.
- BuzzSkito's own Discover report in Search Console was not pulled here (an owner or GSC step).

## 3. Bing Images, Microsoft Copilot and MSN: 2025-2026 data on image traffic, IndexNow, Bing's image guidelines

### Takeaway
Bing holds about 9% of US search. Microsoft gives publishers citation counts for Copilot, but no clicks and no image-traffic figures, and I found no 2025-2026 data on Bing Images clicks to publishers. The cheapest real lever here is not images: Bing's IndexNow has been refusing buzzskito.ca, most likely because the site's Bing ownership lapsed (project note, Sep 14), and ChatGPT and Copilot reportedly ground on Bing's index. MSN syndication is invitation-only.

### Cited Findings
- StatCounter, United States, all platforms, June 2026: Bing 8.73%, Yahoo 2.55%, DuckDuckGo 1.53%; Google's host share 85.9%. A third-party report citing StatCounter gives Bing 8.99% of US search referrals in Aug 2026, up from 7.95% in Aug 2025 (via search summary) — [StatCounter US](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/united-states-of-america/)
- Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance report (public preview, Feb 2026): counts citations in Copilot, Bing's AI summaries and "select partner integrations", by grounding query and page. It shows no clicks and does not cover ChatGPT (via search summary) — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/bing-webmaster-tools-testing-new-ai-performance-report-468039); [Search Influence](https://www.searchinfluence.com/blog/bing-ai-performance-report-copilot-citations/)
- A single Arabic-language article reports four new preview features in the AI Performance report on June 16, 2026, and a "new image search experience" on May 21, 2026. Unconfirmed (via search summary) — [MacMyths](https://macmyths.com/microsoft-bing-2026/)
- Advanced Web Ranking (Gianluca Fiorelli, Aug 27, 2026), claims without data: "ChatGPT and Copilot both lean on Bing's index for grounding"; "NOARCHIVE now prevents your content from being used in Copilot responses and grounding"; Bing Webmaster Tools offers image reporting that Search Console lacks; the Webmaster Guidelines were rewritten in 2025, with AI-citation guidance in Oct 2025. Recommended steps: register in Bing Webmaster Tools, submit image sitemaps, use IndexNow and add schema — [AWR: Visual search beyond Google](https://www.advancedwebranking.com/seo/visual-search-beyond-google)
- MSN Partner Hub is invitation-only and needs an invite code from Microsoft, according to a Microsoft Q&A moderator — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5850858/how-can-i-apply-for-msn-partner-hub-program). Partners earn through ad revenue sharing — [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-US/msn/partner-hub/msn-monetization-options-for-msn-partners). A vendor says the feed is now branded "Copilot Discover" (vendor claim) — [VideoNest](https://videonest.co/blog/videonest-official-microsoft-partner-copilot-discover/)
- Microsoft's Publisher Content Marketplace, a separate AI-licensing track, launched with seven publishers in Feb 2026, and Microsoft is opening the pilot to more partners (via search summary) — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/microsoft/microsoft-publisher-content-marketplace-announcement); [Digiday Q&A](https://digiday.com/media/qa-nikhil-kolar-vp-microsoft-ai-scales-its-click-to-sign-ai-content-marketplace)
- **This site (point-in-time project note, Sep 14, 2026; may be stale):** Bing's IndexNow endpoints return 403 `UserForbiddedToAccessSite` for buzzskito.ca, since at least Sep 10. The likely cause is lapsed GSC-imported ownership in Bing Webmaster Tools, as the site has no native Bing verification (no BingSiteAuth.xml, no msvalidate meta tag) — [project memory note](C:/Users/buzzs/.claude/projects/C--Users-buzzs-buzzskito-website/memory/reference_bing_indexnow_healthy.md)
- I could not read Bing's Webmaster Guidelines page: the fetch returned only a title — [Bing Webmaster help](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/webmasters-guidelines-30fba23a)

### Inferences
- **Size:** for US visitors, Bing's ceiling is roughly a tenth of Google's (8.7% vs about 86% share). Bing Images is a slice of that, so even a strong Bing image presence would add a small fraction of the ~1,530 Google image clicks a month (Oct 30 report).
- **Visitor type:** Bing's audience is generally thought to skew to Windows desktops (Edge and Windows search). No verified 2026 desktop share was found, and US school Chromebooks default to Google, so Bing image visits are probably not the school-computer kind. That is my inference.
- **Best lever:** re-establishing native Bing verification and a working IndexNow is low effort and no risk. If the AWR claim holds, it also matters for ChatGPT and Copilot visibility. The fix belongs to the owner and Bing Webmaster Tools, not to new content.
- **MSN/Copilot Discover:** not reachable without an invitation, and syndicating full articles to MSN would compete with our own pages for ad revenue. Low priority.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 data on Bing Images or Bing Visual Search clicks to publishers, and no Bing image-specific guidance read first-hand.
- BuzzSkito's own Bing traffic (GA4 referrals from bing.com and bing.com/images, and the Bing Webmaster Tools image report) was not pulled.
- The current IndexNow status may have changed since the Sep 14 note.

## 4. AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Mode): do they show publishers' images with links, and do those images send clicks?

### Takeaway
Google AI Mode shows images that each link to a source, Perplexity licenses Getty images with credit links, and ChatGPT's image groups have been reported to show images without source links. No source anywhere measures clicks from images in AI answers. AI assistants as a whole send under 1% of publisher pageviews, so this is a visibility surface, not a traffic channel to build for.

### Cited Findings
- Google AI Mode visual results (Sep 2025): "Each image has a link, so you can click out and learn more when something catches your eye." With an uploaded photo, you can "get a rich, comprehensive response with links to dive deeper." "Lens precisely identifies each object in the image. Using our query fan-out technique, AI Mode then issues multiple queries about the image as a whole and the objects within the image" (company-reported, via search summary of Google's blog) — [Google: AI Mode visual exploration, Sep 2025](https://blog.google/products/search/search-ai-updates-september-2025/); [Google: AI Mode multimodal search](https://blog.google/products/search/ai-mode-multimodal-search/)
- Perplexity and Getty Images signed a multi-year licensing deal (Oct 31, 2025) to show Getty images across Perplexity's AI search, with "image credit with link to source" (via search summary) — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/410356/perplexity-and-getty-images-sign-global-image-lice.html)
- ChatGPT: a user request on OpenAI's forum says image-group results can display copyrighted images "without creator credit, source website attribution, or clickable origin links" (user report, not an OpenAI statement) — [OpenAI Community](https://community.openai.com/t/feature-request-image-attribution-provenance-and-source-linking-in-chatgpt-image-results/1379995)
- ChatGPT began hyperlinking brand names to their homepages on May 7, 2026. Daily OpenAI referrals to the brand sites Profound monitors "roughly doubled (60%-65%)". This is about brand links, not images (vendor) — [Profound](https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/chatgpt-referrals-branded-links)
- AI referrals stay tiny for publishers. Chartbeat (via Axios) found chatbot referrals under 1% of publisher pageviews despite growing more than 200% in a year (via search summary) — [Search Engine Journal on Chartbeat](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-decline-publishers-chartbeat/590681/). Another write-up of a later Chartbeat playbook gives 0.01% and says AI referrals declined across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot and Gemini in 2026. That conflicts in size with the first, and I could not check it (via search summary) — [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/seo/chartbeat-google-search-publisher-traffic-down-40-percent/)
- In Google's own answers (from the Oct 30 report): AI Overviews cut outbound organic clicks by 38% in a 1,065-person experiment, and AI Mode cut click-through by 18.8 points in a second experiment — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/ai-overviews-cut-organic-clicks-38-field-study-finds/573145/); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18352). Lily Ray argues that in AI search an image becomes material for the answer rather than a route to the website — [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/a-reflection-on-seo-and-ai-search)
- **This site:** robots.txt already allows OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, GPTBot and CCBot — [CLAUDE.md](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/CLAUDE.md)

### Inferences
- An image in an AI answer can send a click only when the platform links it (Google AI Mode, Perplexity's licensed images). Even then, the answer usually names the bug in the text, so the reason to click is weak for ID searches, which is the pest library's main intent.
- Perplexity buying Getty images and ChatGPT showing images without links both point the same way: assistants will meet their image needs from licensed libraries or unattributed displays, not by sending readers to small publishers.
- No action beyond what is already done (bots allowed, real photos with captions, IndexNow and Bing fixed for grounding) looks worth the effort. Treat any traffic here as a bonus.

### Gaps
- No measured click-through from images in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, AI Mode or AI Overviews, for any publisher.
- How the Gemini app shows image sources was not documented in anything found.
- Whether ChatGPT's image results now link to source pages (one blog claimed so for GPT-5.1) is unverified.

## 5. Google Lens: the Search Console "Web: Multimodal" report (added Sep 24, 2026), published numbers since, what photos Lens matches, and whether Lens results link to pages

### Takeaway
Since Sep 24 Lens traffic is measurable in Search Console, but in six weeks I found no new published site numbers beyond the two single-site readings already in the Oct 30 report. Coverage of the report is all definitions and caveats. Lens and AI Mode results do lead to web pages (Google says AI Mode images link out; Lens "visual" and "exact" matches carry page addresses). How Lens chooses matches is not documented. Lens is the visual channel that is already sending this site measurable phone traffic with no extra effort.

### Cited Findings
- The filter "includes web search results where an image was used as part of the search": Lens, Circle to Search, image uploads and Chrome's "Search this image." "Because multimodal searches mostly use images rather than text, specific text query data isn't available for this traffic." Search Engine Journal published no traffic numbers from Google or anyone else, and did not say whether earlier Web totals included this traffic — [Search Engine Journal, Sep 24, 2026](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-console-multimodal-filter/590781/)
- The report gives no breakdown by entry point (Lens vs Circle to Search vs uploads). As of Oct 9, 2026 the Search Analytics API had no multimodal search type. The Sept 2026 spam update ran Sep 24-Oct 8, which confounds any late-September change (via search summary) — [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/seo/search-console-multimodal-search-type-lens-circle-to-search/)
- A Canadian agency write-up (Oct 6, 2026) has no measured site figures and says Google has published no country breakdown. Its reasoning: "an image Google never indexed cannot be matched to anyone's camera" — [Wise Media](https://wisemedia.io/2026/10/06/search-console-multimodal-search-type-canada/)
- Published site numbers known so far, all from the Oct 30 report: SEO-Kreativ, about 0.2% of text-search impressions at a 0.5% click rate, with 89% of Lens impressions on phones; Linus Li, about 2% at about 1%; BuzzSkito, 34 Lens clicks against 117 image clicks in one week around Oct 24 — [SEO-Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/search-console-multimodal-en/); [Linus Li](https://www.linusseo.com/en/gsc-web-multimodal/); [Oct 30 report](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/reports/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan.md)

SERP-API vendor documentation shows Lens "visual matches" and "exact matches" returning each hit's page URL and source site. ### Inferences
- **What Lens matches:** no primary source says. The mechanics above suggest two routes. A reader's own phone photo of a bug is on no website, so it can only reach us through "visual matches" and the fan-out queries, where the species name and page relevance matter. A reused stock photo can reach us through "exact matches", where the famous source probably ranks first. So varied, clearly identified photos (life stages, angles, scale) on the right page are the bet, as the Oct 30 report reasoned. Its November test measures Lens clicks per page.
- **Visitor type:** mostly phones (89% on the one site with a split), which makes this school-free traffic, unlike desktop Google Images. Lens visitors arrive at the page in Web results, so they behave like ordinary web visits.
- **Effort:** nothing beyond the image work already planned. The owner's monthly Search Console export is the only way to see it (no API).

### Gaps

- No Google statement on how Lens ranks matches, or whether Lens or Circle to Search shows an AI Overview before the matches for insect photos.
- BuzzSkito's own Multimodal export (steps in the Oct 30 report) is still the missing number.

## 6. For each channel: effort, cost, safety (could it hurt Google rankings?) and fit for a photo library from iNaturalist, Wikimedia Commons, CDC and NPS

### Takeaway
Only Pinterest raises real licence questions, because it means re-posting the photos on someone else's platform. Public-domain and CC0 photos (573 of 829, 69%) are free to pin. CC BY photos need a credit in each pin. CC BY-SA photos should stay off pin graphics. None of the five channels has a documented way to hurt Google rankings if done honestly; the only Google-side risk is Discover clickbait. By expected value per hour, Lens and fixing Bing are cheap and safe, Discover is a cheap prerequisite with an unpredictable payoff, Pinterest is the only channel needing sustained effort (owner-approved posting), and AI assistants are not worth building for.

### Cited Findings
- Pinterest Terms of Service (effective Apr 30, 2025; an updated version is "effective November 12, 2026"): "You will only post User Content that you have the rights to post". Users grant Pinterest "a non-exclusive, royalty-free, transferable, sublicensable, worldwide license to use, store" and to reproduce, modify and distribute the content. Accounts that "repeatedly or seriously infringe" can be suspended. The terms contain no attribution requirement — [Pinterest Terms of Service](https://policy.pinterest.com/en/terms-of-service)
- Wikimedia's guidance on CC BY-SA and social media: give the author, licence and source (ideally with a link) in the same post as the image. A social site's terms "do not override a license". Past guidance held that some sites' terms conflict with CC conditions. The page is labelled not legal advice (via search summary) — [Meta-Wiki: CC BY-SA licenses and social media](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Legal/CC_BY-SA_licenses_and_social_media/en)
- A Geograph blog post (older, undated) said Pinterest's copying method "does not promote honouring the Creative Commons licence", and that Pinterest later began showing the contributor's name and link on the pin page (via search summary) — [Geograph blog](https://www.geograph.org.uk/blog/105)
- Our photo ledger (my count, Nov 3, 2026): 829 photos. Public domain 233 and CC0 340, so 573 public-domain or CC0. CC BY 2.0-4.0: 188. CC BY-SA 2.0-4.0: 68. By source: iNaturalist 446, Wikimedia Commons 218, CDC 161, NPS 4. 229 are portrait, the shape that suits Pinterest's 2:3 format. 310 public-domain or CC0 photos are at least 1,200 px wide and landscape, the shape Discover needs — [data/god-build/photos.json](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/data/god-build/photos.json)
- House rules already in force: composites (pairs, cards, charts) use only public-domain and CC0 photos, because CC BY-SA would pass its licence to the composite and clash with our CC BY offer. Our own diagrams are CC BY 4.0 — [Oct 30 report](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/reports/Google%20Images%20traffic%20growth%20plan.md); [CLAUDE.md](C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/CLAUDE.md)
- On the Google side: Discover policy violations bring Discover manual actions — [Google Search Central: Discover](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/google-discover). The Discover core update did not move organic ranking volatility — [SEO-Kreativ](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-discover-core-update-february-2026-completed-what-the-newzdash-data-reveals/). Google's scraping policy targets sites that exist mainly to gather other people's images "without substantial added value", which concerns our own pages, not off-site posting — [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)

### Inferences
| Channel | Effort | Cash cost | Could it hurt Google? | Licence fit for our photos | Likely size for us | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Google Lens (Multimodal) | None beyond planned photo work; owner exports monthly | Free | No | Already on our pages, credits shown | Already measurable (34 vs 117 Images clicks in one week); phone traffic | Keep measuring; favour varied, well-identified photos |

- The CDC, NPS and public-domain iNaturalist/Commons photos are the safest raw material for any off-site channel. Licences are attached per photo, so a credit line must travel with every CC BY pin. Leaving CC BY-SA off pins avoids the share-alike and Pinterest-licence conflict entirely.

Pinterest is the only one that could plausibly add thousands of visits, and only after sustained, owner-approved work, with 2026 reports of sudden reach collapses as a real risk to that investment. Discover and AI answers cannot be relied on.
