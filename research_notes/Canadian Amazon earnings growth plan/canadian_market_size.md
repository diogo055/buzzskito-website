# Canadian consumer pest control market, Amazon.ca's slice, and what one affiliate site could take

Researched Oct 30, 2026. Every number carries a grade: PRIMARY (government data, company filing or earnings call), REPUTABLE (named analyst or trade press with a known method, even if paywalled), VENDOR (market-report headline, method not visible, so low quality), UNSOURCED (no source given by the page), MINE (my own estimate or assumption). Dollars are Canadian unless marked US$. Conversions use Bank of Canada annual averages: 1.3497 (2023), 1.3698 (2024), 1.3978 (2025) ([Bank of Canada](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/exchange/annual-average-exchange-rates/), PRIMARY).

## Q1. What do Canadians spend a year on consumer pest control products?

### Takeaway
No public source gives a clean figure. My estimate is low C$180M, middle C$310M, high C$580M a year in 2025 dollars, about C$19 per household in the middle case. About two-thirds of that middle figure rests on my own assumptions, because no source covers those lines. The big headlines (US$858M to US$1.6B) are paywalled vendor numbers, and Grand View's own split says about 78% of its figure is business buyers, not consumers.

## Q2. What can be built from primary sources?

### Takeaway
No Canadian government or trade-body source reports consumer pest spending in dollars. Health Canada reports only kilograms and Statistics Canada stops at "garden supplies and services" in everything I could reach. The most useful result is a primary-to-primary ratio: Canadian consumer insecticide kilograms are about 5% to 8.4% of the US figure, which scales EPA's US user dollars to about C$200M to C$470M.

### Inferences
- MINE, arithmetic. Population ratio: 41.7M / 341.8M = 12.2%.
- MINE, arithmetic. Canadian consumer insecticide kilograms (2016): 6,229,916 × 8.6% = 535,773 kg. US (2012): 14M lb × 0.4536 = 6,350,293 kg. Ratio 8.4%.
- MINE, arithmetic. Adding rodent and other lines: Canada 6,229,916 × 10.6% = 660,371 kg; US 29M lb = 13,154,179 kg. Ratio 5.0%.
- So a Canadian uses roughly 40% to 70% of what an American uses of consumer pest active ingredient (5.0% or 8.4%, against 12.2%). That fits a colder climate and provincial lawn bans. The years differ (2012 vs 2016), EPA's "other" may not match PMRA's lines, and kilograms cannot see traps or devices, so this is a rough guide.
- MINE, arithmetic. EPA dollars scaled: US$2,650M + US$175M = US$2,825M. At 5.0% that is US$142M; at 8.4%, US$238M. At 1.3978 that gives C$198M to C$333M, in 2012 US prices. If US prices rose about 1.4× from 2012 to 2025 (MINE, from memory of the CPI, not fetched), the range is C$278M to C$466M. Together: C$198M to C$466M, which contains the C$310M middle.
- Only about 10.6% of PMRA "domestic" kilograms are the insecticides, repellents and vertebrate controls this site sells. Anyone quoting the domestic class as the pest market would be counting pool chemicals and corn gluten meal.

## Q3. How big is Amazon Canada, and what share of this category plausibly sells online and on Amazon?

### Takeaway
Amazon.ca probably sells C$35B to C$51B of goods a year, roughly 40% of Canadian online retail, but pest control is mainly a store category. I estimate Amazon.ca sells C$6M (low), C$29M (middle) or C$113M (high) of consumer pest products a year, with a separate top-down check of C$26M to C$90M. The plausible band is C$25M to C$70M, and the high case is a ceiling because it stacks three high assumptions.

### Inferences
**Amazon.ca's total (MINE, arithmetic)**

- Middle C$45B: C$59.2B / 7.0% = C$846B retail; × 13.0% = C$110B online; × 41.5% = C$45.7B. This is rough, because eMarketer's retail base differs from StatCan's.
- High C$51B: US$36.772B × 1.3978 = C$51.4B.
- If Marketplace Pulse's C$35B is third-party only, the real total is above it, which would move this toward the middle or high case. All three are large against StatCan's C$59.2B (59% to 87% of it), which fits StatCan's partial coverage of third-party sales.

**Bottom-up pest slice (MINE)**

| Case | Consumer spend | Online share | Amazon share of online | Amazon.ca pest sales a year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

- Amazon's share of online: low matches eMarketer's overall 41.5%. Middle and high assume a non-food specialty category favours Amazon, with Walmart.ca, CanadianTire.ca, HomeDepot.ca and Costco.ca competing.

**Top-down check (MINE; every percentage here is an assumption)**
- SmartScout US$1.725B × 12 = US$20.7B a year. Against Amazon's US$830B that is 2.5%; if the figure is US-only and the US is about 60% to 65% of GMV, about 4%.
- C$35B to C$45B × 2.5% to 4% = C$0.9B to C$1.8B of patio, lawn and garden on Amazon.ca. Pest control at 3% to 5% of that (MINE, no source) gives C$26M to C$90M.
- This suggests the C$29M middle may sit low, so Q7 also shows a C$60M sensitivity.

**Why this category will lag.** Chemical pest products are regulated, some are hazardous to ship, and the buyer often has an urgent need met at a nearby big-box store. The 61.8% big-box share at Spectrum and the 10% e-commerce share at Scotts are US, but they are the best primary evidence found for how this shelf sells. Amazon's sales probably grow faster than the category: Scotts' e-commerce units rose 51% in FY2025 (PRIMARY, above). A figure built for 2025 will be low for 2028.

**A compliance point for the site (MINE, not legal advice).** The Act's definition of "distribute" also covers "advertise for sale", which is a reason to keep affiliate picks to products registered in Canada.

## Q4. What share of Amazon sales comes through affiliate links?

### Takeaway
No credible estimate exists publicly. The two numbers in circulation (23% and 53%) have no source. My own rough bound is 3% to 15% of sales, which is an assumption-driven estimate. On that bound, all affiliates combined would earn only about C$6,000 to C$19,000 a month on Amazon.ca pest items in the middle case.

- UNSOURCED. "53% of all Amazon sales comes from affiliate marketing", with no year. ### Inferences
- MINE, assumption-driven. If Associates are 3% to 8% of sales and marketing, that is US$1.4B to US$3.8B of commission. At an effective 3% to 5% rate, the credited sales are US$28B (1.4 / 0.05) to US$126B (3.8 / 0.03), or 3.4% to 15% of US$830B. Credited sales are not caused sales, because the 24-hour credit pays on items people would have bought anyway.
- MINE, arithmetic. Commission available to all affiliates combined on Amazon.ca pest items, per month = slice × affiliate share × 5.2% / 12:

| Amazon.ca pest slice | 5% via affiliates | 15% via affiliates | 23% (unsourced figure) |

| Low C$5.7M | C$1,200 | C$3,700 | C$5,700 |

| High C$113M | C$24,500 | C$73,600 | C$112,900 |

- So in the middle case, CA$10,000 a month earned on pest items alone would be somewhere between 35% and 160% of what every affiliate in Canada earns on those items. Larger totals are arithmetically possible only because of unrelated cart items (Q5).

- Nothing from Amazon, and nothing specific to Canada. A real figure would need an Amazon disclosure, a litigation exhibit or clickstream data.

## Q5. How big are the adjacent categories, and what can be said about commissions on unrelated cart items?

### Takeaway
Adjacent categories are much larger than pest control (about C$3B to C$7B for the household garden lines, against about C$0.3B for pest), and because Amazon credits anything bought within 24 hours, commission is not capped by the pest market. Unrelated carts depend on who is already shopping, though, so the real limit is how many Amazon visits the site starts.

### Cited Findings
- PRIMARY. Survey of Household Spending, 2023: C$452 per household on garden supplies and services, C$204 on lawn, garden and snow-removal equipment and tools — [Statistics Canada](https://www.statcan.gc.ca/o1/en/plus/8591-backyard-harvest)
- VENDOR. Air purifiers: GMI US$294.0M (2025), residential US$191.1M, offline 48% — [GMI](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/canada-air-purifier-market). Conflict: Deep Market Insights US$1,510.9M (2024), about 5× higher — [Deep Market Insights](https://deepmarketinsights.com/vista/insights/air-purifier-market/canada)
- VENDOR. Dehumidifiers: Grand View US$69.1M (2025), US$74.1M (2026 estimate) — [Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/dehumidifier-market/canada). Conflicts, up to 12× higher: Deep Market Insights US$324.36M (2024) — [source](https://deepmarketinsights.com/vista/insights/dehumidifier-market/canada); Mobility Foresights US$530.2M (2025) — [source](https://mobilityforesights.com/product/canada-dehumidifier-market); Reed Intelligence US$834.87M, household (2024) — [source](https://reedintelligence.com/insights/household-dehumidifier-market/canada)

September examples: sprinkler 8 items, C$578 ordered, C$30.04 earned; wasp foam 6 items, C$130 ordered, C$6.18 earned. September earnings per click by topic: wildlife 46¢, steamers 38¢, rodent 22¢, flies 20¢, bed bug 15¢, cockroach 15¢, general 14¢, wasp 13¢, mosquito 10¢. Amazon hides low-volume products, and 61% of October earnings sit in a hidden "Other" row — [reports/amazon-what-sells-2026-10-30.md](file:///C:/Users/buzzs/buzzskito-website/reports/amazon-what-sells-2026-10-30.md)

### Inferences
- MINE, arithmetic, using the 16.2M households of July 2023: garden supplies and services C$452 × 16.2M = C$7.3B. Lawn, garden and snow tools C$204 × 16.2M = C$3.3B. These include services, plants and snow equipment, so they are not all shoppable affiliate spending.
- MINE, conversions: air purifiers US$294.0M × 1.3978 = C$411M, residential C$267M. Dehumidifiers US$69.1M × 1.3978 = C$97M. I use the lowest vendor figure in each because the higher ones fail a quick check. At an assumed C$250 a unit (MINE), C$97M is about 390,000 dehumidifiers, or 2.3% of households a year, while Reed's US$834.87M (C$1,144M at 1.3698) would mean about 27% of households a year.
- MINE, assumption-driven Amazon slices: air purifiers C$267M × 52% × 55% = C$76M. Dehumidifiers C$97M × 35% × 55% = C$19M. Tools C$3.3B × 12% × 55% = C$218M. With pest at C$29M, the "home, pest and garden" pool is about C$342M a year, about 12× the pest slice.
- The market-size ceiling does not apply to cart items. CA$100,000 a month is C$23.1M a year, which is 0.045% to 0.066% of Amazon.ca's C$35B to C$51B. In that narrow sense, the owner is right that there are enough buyers.
- The site's own figures show that a high-priced non-chemical item earns more: the sprinkler's C$72 an item against wasp foam's C$22, both from September. That favours devices and equipment over chemicals, and the same is suggested by earnings per click on wildlife and steamers.
- Commission on an unrelated cart is whatever each item's category pays (Q6), so a general basket likely blends to about 3% to 5% (MINE). Grocery and gift cards pay nothing.
- A site cannot steer unrelated carts. It can only start more Amazon sessions, and that income depends on Amazon continuing to credit them (Q6).

### Gaps
- The share of the site's ordered value that is pest product is unknown, because Amazon's on-screen reports hide most items. Reports > Download Reports in Associates Central lists every item ordered, and only the owner can pull it. Q7's 30% is an assumption until then.
- I found no reliable Amazon.ca category mix and no steam cleaner figure.

## Q6. What are current Amazon.ca commission rates, and has Amazon cut them or is a cut likely?

### Takeaway
A third-party copy of the Amazon.ca table, checked Oct 1, 2026, matches all five rates the site observes, and I found no report of a cut to Canada's standard table. Amazon did cut selected rates by up to 50% elsewhere in 2025-26, without announcing it, and Canada's 6% on Home is double the US 3%. A Canadian cut is a real risk, though nothing I found says one is coming.

## Q7. What share of Amazon.ca pest sales would each target require, and which look possible for one site?

### Takeaway
Market size does not rule out CA$3,000 or CA$10,000 a month, but traffic does for now: CA$10,000 needs 15.5× the site's best month of Canadian sessions, every month. CA$30,000 is very unlikely, and CA$100,000 is not credible, because it would take 20% to 405% of Amazon.ca's pest slice if all pest (80% in the middle case). **Verdict by target (MINE, judgement)**
- **CA$3,000 a month: possible.** Market size is no obstacle. As a peak-season month it looks reachable within two or three seasons. As a 12-month average it needs 4.7× the best month, all year, including winter.
- **CA$10,000 a month: a long stretch, not ruled out by market size.** It would make the site a major referrer in this category in Canada (2% to 8% of the pest slice if all pest in the middle and high cases, 40% in the low case). It would probably need adjacent categories, several seasons and no rate cut. I found no Canadian example. The listing at about US$10,600 shows that level of revenue exists for a home-niche site, in markets not limited to Canada.
- **CA$30,000 a month: very unlikely on Canadian pest traffic.** It needs 46.5× the best month, and in practice it would be a general Canadian home-shopping publisher.
- **CA$100,000 a month: not credible.** It is about 80% of the middle slice if all pest and 24% at 30% pest, and it needs about one session per Canadian household per year.

**Evidence for the owner's belief**
- Amazon.ca is very large: C$35B to C$51B. CA$100,000 a month is about 0.05% of it, and the 24-hour credit reaches all of it.
- Large vendor headlines exist (C$1.2B to C$2.2B).
- Adjacent categories are about 12× the pest slice on my estimate.
- Canada pays double the US rate on Home and Patio, Lawn and Garden.
- Online is growing fast in this shelf (Scotts: units up 51% in FY2025).

**Evidence against it**
- Consumer pest spending is only C$180M to C$580M, and about 78% of Grand View's headline is business buyers by its own split.
- The category sells through big-box stores (Spectrum 61.8%, Scotts 10% e-commerce, "underpenetrated in .com").
- Amazon's slice is C$6M to C$113M, and the commission pool for all affiliates on pest items is probably C$6,000 to C$19,000 a month in the middle case.
- 60% of Amazon.ca visits are direct and 22% come from Google, so the room for referrals is limited.
- The site earns 7¢ per Canadian session, with a best month of 9,216 sessions.
- No named Canadian affiliate site turned up at these levels in three separate searches. The largest all-category US benchmark was about US$10M a year.
- Rates were cut elsewhere in 2025-26, and the income depends on an attribution rule Amazon controls.

**What would change this view**
- Euromonitor's Canadian dollar figure.
- A paid per-product estimate of amazon.ca pest sales.
- The owner's full item report, which would replace the 30% assumption.
- Real Canadian search demand from Search Console for pest shopping terms.

### Gaps
- No named Canadian affiliate site publishes earnings at these levels, in anything I could find. Searches for Canadian income reports and for RedFlagDeals, Narcity and Postmedia returned no Amazon.ca earnings. Absence from search is not proof that none exists; a private site could earn this much without saying so.
- I found no count of competing Canadian pest affiliates, so competition is described by channel, not by number.
- I did not measure Canadian search demand for pest shopping terms, which is the most direct test of whether 143,000 Canadian sessions a month could exist. It belongs with Search Console work.
