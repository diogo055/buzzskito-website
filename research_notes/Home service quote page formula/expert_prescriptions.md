# What Hormozi, direct-response and home-service experts prescribe for quote pages

Research date: Nov 1, 2026. About 45 tool calls used. Anything unfinished is marked NOT DONE under Gaps.

**Grades (what the claim rests on):**
- [OWN TEST] = the expert's own test result, with numbers
- [OPINION] = the expert's opinion, rule of thumb or estimate
- [3RD PARTY] = someone else's report: client self-report, vendor data, reader notes of a statistic
- [UNSUPPORTED] = no evidence given, or the cited source could not be found

**How I read each source:**
- (transcript) = third-party transcript of a free episode
- (preview) = partial summary page
- (notes) = reader's notes on a book
- (listing) = episode title, show notes or chapter list only
- (snippet) = search result only; the page was not opened

Pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so check the wording against the source before quoting anything publicly. Direct quotes are kept to one. Book PDFs that appeared in search results were not opened.

## 1. What exactly does Hormozi prescribe, from free official sources?

### Takeaway
His frameworks are offer frameworks, not page formulas. His only specific local home-service page rewrite I could read is Ep 971 (May 19, 2026): a dedicated ad page with a named free offer, the town, the phone number and a form, with no navigation. It rests on his experience and estimates. I found no A/B test with numbers from him on any page, and no statement from him on whether to show price.

**B. Offer frameworks ($100M Offers). Official course topics are confirmed, but the lesson content was not readable**
- The official $100M Offers course lists lessons on The Value Equation, Offer Creation (two parts), Bonuses, Guarantees, Scarcity & Urgency, and Naming Products. The page shows no dates or lesson content — [Acquisition.com](https://www.acquisition.com/training/offers) (listing)
- Value Equation: value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort and sacrifice). [OPINION] — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-100m-offers/) (notes)
- Grand Slam Offer: define the dream outcome, list every barrier, turn each barrier into a solution, pick a delivery method for each, then trim and combine. [OPINION] — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-100m-offers/) (notes)
- Naming (MAGIC): Magnet (why this offer), Avatar (who, including a hyper-local area), Goal, Interval (timeline), Container (the bundle). The notes do not spell out the acronym, so this mapping is by section. [OPINION] — [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi) (notes)
- Guarantees: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee ("all sales final"), implied, performance-based, and stacked guarantees. [OPINION] — [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi) (notes)
- Bonuses: anchor the core price and do not discount it; add bonuses instead. Name each bonus, back it with proof, and have it remove a specific reason the buyer might fail. Total bonus value should exceed the core offer. [OPINION] — [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi) (notes)
- Scarcity: limited slots, limited bonuses, or never available again. Keep supply below demand and announce when sold out. [OPINION] — [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi) (notes)
- Urgency: rolling start groups, seasonal promotions named by season, a real signup-date countdown, time-limited bonuses, advance notice of price rises, and an opportunity that decays the later you act. [OPINION] — [Max Mednik](https://www.maxmednik.com/blog/notes-on-100m-offers-by-alex-hormozi) (notes)
- Competing on price is not sustainable; charge the highest price the market will bear once value far exceeds price. [OPINION] — [Readingraphics](https://readingraphics.com/book-summary-100m-offers/) (notes)

### Inferences
- Going by the brief, BuzzSkito's page already matches Hormozi's local example in its main shape: a free offer, with a form on the first screen. The candidates his example adds are naming the free offer more specifically, cutting navigation, and making the phone number prominent. I did not audit the live page in this research.
- The "18-point" rename is the most transferable idea and the weakest in evidence, because it has no numbers. It only works if the assessment truly has those checks.
- His example was for paid ads and an in-person sale. BuzzSkito's quote traffic and its price-later flow differ, so it cannot be copied as proof.
- Buy-X-get-Y-free fits the project's rule of paying in sprays rather than dollars (`CLAUDE.md`: about $8 marginal product cost per extra visit).
- Hormozi's real-capacity scarcity fits a one-van business with a May-September season.

## 2. What do the other experts prescribe, and how do they differ?

### Takeaway
They mostly agree on one clear offer and one clear next step. They split on length: Suby's 17-step long page, StoryBrand's simple website, Hormozi's very short local page. None of the sources I read shows a controlled test for a local service page. Most of the material I could reach was third-party summary, so this section is thinner on primary evidence than Section 1.

### Cited Findings

**Sabri Suby (Sell Like Crazy)**
- A single long-form page beats a multi-page site with fancy branding; a basic logo is enough. [OPINION] — [Summify summary of his video](https://summify.io/discover/the-ultimate-step-by-step-landing-page-guide-my-1-33-billion-secret-selling-syst-zpTsCrOvGao) (preview)
- The 17 steps in order: call out the audience; big-promise headline and sub-headline; bullets; agitate the problem; present the solution; credentials and proof; benefits; proof and statistics; Godfather Offer; bonuses; value stack; reveal the price; scarcity; guarantee; call to action; warning; P.S. Calls to action run throughout. The summary numbers some of these itself. [OPINION] — [Summify](https://summify.io/discover/the-ultimate-step-by-step-landing-page-guide-my-1-33-billion-secret-selling-syst-zpTsCrOvGao) (preview)
- Price comes after value is stacked. The example gives a free consultation a stated value ("usually $997"). [OPINION] — [Summify](https://summify.io/discover/the-ultimate-step-by-step-landing-page-guide-my-1-33-billion-secret-selling-syst-zpTsCrOvGao) (preview)
- The "$1.33 billion" in the video title is unverified. [UNSUPPORTED] — [Summify](https://summify.io/discover/the-ultimate-step-by-step-landing-page-guide-my-1-33-billion-secret-selling-syst-zpTsCrOvGao) (preview)
- A free High-Value Content Offer should not contain a pitch. The thank-you page then presents the Godfather Offer in text, video or both. [OPINION] — [Goodreads reader notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/43787527-sell-like-crazy/103748944-harrison-freemyer/5bc16e4d-31d4-4996-8c8d-8a75a0d5e9bb) (snippet)

**Russell Brunson**
- Every funnel element, from ad to sales page, carries a hook, a story and an offer. When a campaign fails, the cause traces back to one of the three. [OPINION] — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/russell-brunson-sales-funnel/); [Traffic Secrets quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/74103575) (snippet)
- Paid traffic goes to a squeeze page with one goal and no distractions: the visitor can do only one thing. [OPINION] — [Dan Silvestre notes](https://dansilvestre.com/?p=4025); [Goodreads notes](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/53504694-dotcom-secrets/123802880-rory-o-brien/dab47f54-368e-4ae6-8041-05a5d21c8941) (snippet)

**Dan Kennedy**
- Always make a specific offer, so response can be tracked. Give a reason to respond now, because the loss sits with the almost-persuaded. Tell people exactly how to respond. [OPINION] — [Hooshmand notes](https://hooshmand.net/?p=1230); [SciMark](https://scimark.substack.com/i/123806524/rule-there-will-be-a-reason-to-respond-now) (snippet)

**Donald Miller (StoryBrand)**
- Grunt test: at a glance, can the visitor say what you offer, how it improves their life, and what to do next? [OPINION] — [getAbstract](https://www.getabstract.com/en/summary/building-a-storybrand/31696) (snippet)
- Above the fold: offer, success image and a direct call to action, with stakes and a plan below. [OPINION] — [Third-party StoryBrand skill file](https://verified-skill.com/skills/jordangaston/claude-plugins/story-brand/versions/1.0.0) (snippet, weak source)
- Test call-to-action wording. [OPINION] — [Teamly](https://www.teamly.com/blog/?p=13920) (snippet)

**Eugene Schwartz (Breakthrough Advertising, 1966)**
- Prospects sit at different awareness stages, and fully unaware people are usually not worth marketing to. Market sophistication, meaning how much competitors have already promised, decides the kind of advertising that works. [OPINION] — [Optimize Smart](https://optimizesmart.com/blog/schwartz-five-stages-of-awareness-in-marketing/); [Medium](https://medium.com/@chanellesegeriusbruce/5-stages-of-market-sophistication-3f014be2026d) (snippet)

**Frank Kern**
- "Results in Advance" gives value on screen before pitching. Behavioral Dynamic Response customises messages by behaviour. Both descriptions come from promotional write-ups, and no results are documented. [UNSUPPORTED] — [Practitioner write-up](https://nl-gmb-renovation.pages.dev/research/vsl-frameworks.html); [Consultant blog](https://shallot-toucan-nahf.squarespace.com/blog/frank-kern-advanced-consulting-class-review) (snippet)

**Oli Gardner (Unbounce)**
- Attention ratio: interactive elements against conversion goals, ideally 1:1. A third-party deck using self-reported rates from Unbounce's analyser shows 10.5% at 5:1 or higher, 11.9% at 2:1-4:1, and 13.5% at 1:1. This is not a controlled test. [3RD PARTY] — [Hanapin deck](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/more-traffic-more-conversions-how-to-build-great-landing-pages/135098135) (snippet)
- Unbounce's home-improvement benchmark covers 4,372 pages and 977,200 conversions: median 2.6%, top quartile 19.5%. Home services run 53.7% above the category baseline. Pest control pages do best at 400-500 words, general home services around 300, HVAC under 200. Pages mixing click, form and phone calls to action convert at 4% vs 2.6%. No date is stated (image filenames say 2024). It is correlational data from a vendor that sells landing pages, and the page reading was contradictory on whether pest control under- or over-performs. [3RD PARTY] — [Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/industry/home-improvement/)

**Joanna Wiebe (Copyhackers)**
- Long copy often wins split tests, but that usually means long pages, not long page elements. A win for B over A does not generalise to other versions. Survey customers only with questions that help write copy. No test numbers retrieved. Posts date from 2013-14 (one last updated 2020). [OPINION, reports tests] — [Copyhackers 2013](https://copyhackers.com/2013/06/is-your-copy-suffering-from-tmi/); [Copyhackers 2014](https://copyhackers.com/2014/02/optimized-crazy-egg-home-page/) (snippet)

**Peep Laja (CXL)**
- I found nothing by Laja himself. A different author on CXL reports a B2B software form where added friction lifted conversion 20% and marketing-qualified leads 10%, and cites Dan Zarrella's finding that conversion generally falls as fields rise. [3RD PARTY] — [CXL](https://cxl.com/blog/increase-conversions-lead-gen-forms/) (snippet)

## 3. Who is the Hormozi of home services?

### Takeaway
Nobody fills that role for pages. Tommy Mello is the closest on scale, but what I could read from him is about booking calls, numbers to track and software. Operators talk about lead handling and sales more than page design. Almost all numbers on instant pricing come from vendors selling the tools.

### Cited Findings

**Tommy Mello (A1 Garage)**
- In a Dec 3, 2024 ServiceTitan recap, he says contractors waste leads they already get, knows his booking rate precisely, and has his team fight for every call. The vendor hosted this. [OPINION] — [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/webinar-recap-maximize-your-leads-with-tommy-mello)
