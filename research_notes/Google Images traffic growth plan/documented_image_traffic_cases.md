# Documented cases of traffic from Google Images and Google Lens

Research date: Oct 30, 2026.

**Grades on every number.** **[A]** primary data shown (Search Console or analytics, by the owner or their consultant). **[B]** owner's or consultant's statement, with no data or no base number shown. **[C]** third-party estimate (Similarweb, Semrush and similar). **[D]** unsourced or untraceable claim, listed only as unverified.

**Limits on this research.** The search tool refuses reddit.com. BlackHatWorld thread pages, moz.com, marketingcharts.com and searchengineland.com returned errors when opened. Those are the places most likely to hold owner screenshots, so "I found none" below means none in what I could read, not that none exist. Figures marked "search result only" could not be checked on the page itself. My tools also cannot read the contents of screenshots: where a page shows one, the number quoted comes from the page's text.

**Baseline correction.** The brief's 60-65 clicks on 35,000 impressions a day is 0.17-0.19%, or 1.7-1.9 clicks per 1,000 impressions, not about 1 per 1,000. It is about 1,900 clicks a month. Recheck it in Search Console before the figure is used in the report.

I found no named website that publicly shows a Search Console figure at that level. The evidence at that scale is anonymous and mostly old: one news publisher in 2019 (roughly 50,000 a month after a technical fix) and a group of 87 sites before 2013. Good cases are rare.

- **Third-party tools do not answer this question.** None of the Similarweb or Semrush public pages I opened splits image search from other organic traffic, so public estimates cannot rank named sites by image clicks. See section 5 for the pages.
- **The largest multi-site sample points the other way.** seoClarity found about 12% of impressions but under 1% of clicks came from Google Images across 200+ US enterprise domains, Jan 2017-Dec 2018 **[A]**. AJ Kohn's clients above show 30-45%, so site type changes the answer enormously.
- **BuzzSkito's own share:** image search plus Lens was about 150 clicks a week on Oct 24, 2026, about 8% of Google clicks (project `CLAUDE.md`). The brief's 60-65 a day is about three times that.

**Unverified claims, do not rely on these [D]:**
- One website gained 600,000 more visitors a month from image search, from unnamed surveys with no sample or date — [LinkedIn post, 2019](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/30-increase-traffic-using-google-images-rahul-bhatia).
- Sites with optimised image libraries get 20-40% of organic traffic from image search, with no source or method (search result only) — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/image-seo-2026-visual-search-optimization-guide).
- Food blogs get 40% of traffic from Images, photography sites 70%, home decor 34%, healthcare 18% more clicks, all undated with no method. I could not trace any to its origin — [SEO Sandwitch](https://seosandwitch.com/google-images-statistics/).
- BlackHatWorld posts, search result only and roughly 2014-2019: a blank one-image test site reporting 284 then 1,917 visitors from Images; an image blog at about 200 Google visits a day — [thread](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/guide-how-to-generate-massive-traffic-from-google-images.1103525/).

### Inferences

**Testing the owner's hope with arithmetic, at today's click rate of 0.18%:**

| Target image clicks a month | Multiple of today's 1,900 | Impressions a day needed | Multiple of today's 35,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10,000 | 5x | About 183,000 | 5x |
| 30,000 | 16x | About 550,000 | 16x |
| 100,000 | 53x | About 1.8M | 52x |
| 300,000 | 158x | About 5.5M | 157x |

**Evidence for:**
- Before 2013, some publishers took 20%+ of organic traffic from Images, and the worst-hit lost over 100K visits a week (Define).
- A news publisher gained roughly 50,000 a month in 2019 (my arithmetic: 334K over the 6-7 months from April to November).
- Image share reached 30-45% at some ecommerce sites, and one client's image traffic grew 66% in a year against 13% for web (Kohn).
- Lens volume is large and growing (section 3).
- The BuzzSkito baseline is days old and from the off-season for insects (section 5).

**Evidence against:**
- No named content site shows this level.
- The closest topical peers get less than the upper hope from all channels combined (section 5).
- Yield per image is low wherever it can be calculated (section 4).
- Image click rates sit far below web (section 6).
- The only controlled tests were inconclusive (section 2).
- Image visitors converted 64-88% worse in the one dataset that measured it (section 2).
- Google cut this channel by 63% in 2013 with no recovery in 11 weeks (section 2).

**My read:** 10,000 a month is a 5x step that nothing here rules out, especially in season. 100,000 or more has no precedent I could read at a site this size since 2013. "Tens of thousands" sits between the two and is unproven. None of these cases shows that publishing more images was what produced the traffic.

Getty's 85% claim implies Images once supplied most of its traffic, but it comes from one side of a dispute and has no base number.

### Gaps
- Reddit, BlackHatWorld threads, private Mediavine and Raptive groups, and X and LinkedIn screenshots were not readable. A follow-up by the owner in a logged-in browser could find named cases I could not.
- No absolute monthly Images figure exists in the sources for Dreamstime, Getty or Fat Stacks.
- I found no 2024-2026 owner report above about 800 image clicks a month, which may reflect access limits more than reality.

## 2. Which named sites or case studies deliberately tried to grow image traffic, and what happened?

### Takeaway
The largest documented gain came from removing a technical block, not from adding images. The only controlled tests were inconclusive. Large percentage gains elsewhere have no base number or a very small one. I found no documented case of a publisher multiplying image clicks by publishing many more images.

### Cited Findings

| Who, when | What they changed | What happened | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| [SearchPilot, classifieds site, Jun 2026](https://www.searchpilot.com/resources/case-studies/does-adding-descriptive-alt-text-to-listing-images-improve-seo) | Alt text on listing images became listing name plus category heading; control and variant pages | Inconclusive. Best estimate +7.3% organic sessions, 95% interval -4.2% to +19.4%. Measures all organic, not Images alone | **[A]** controlled |

| [May-Fly agency, published Mar 2026](https://www.may-fly.co.uk/alt-text-for-images-case-study/) | AI-written alt text on existing images, about £10-15 per 1,000; 8 weeks against the prior 8, or year on year; says no other major changes | Fashion store +230% daily image clicks, +33% all clicks. **Changes made by Google rather than by site owners:**
- **Feb 2018, View Image button removed:** 58 self-selected sites averaged +37%, with impressions and position roughly flat. Sources differ on whether that figure is clicks or click rate. Search result only, confirmed in summary by a trade body **[B]** — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/its-alive-image-seo-is-back-from-the-dead-298751); [CEPIC](https://www.cepic.org/post/recent-changes-to-the-google-image-search-mean-improvement-for-sites-owners).
- **Jan 2013, redesign:** -63% on average across 87 domains, near -80% for the worst, no recovery in 11 weeks **[A]** — [Define](https://www.definemg.com/how-googles-image-search-update-killed-image-seo/). PhotoShelter's headline gives -78% (headline only, not read) — [PhotoShelter](https://go.photoshelter.com/photographers/blog/new-google-images-interface-causes-78-drop-in-referral-traffic-to-your-site/).
- **Late Jan 2025, possible AI-image demotion:** one game site with AI images reported a sharp drop in image traffic while web stayed steady, with no percentages. Barry Schwartz saw no drop on his AI hero images. The two summaries I read differ on whether Kane Jamison saw it himself. Google did not confirm anything. Anecdotal **[B]** — [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-image-search-down-ranking-ai-images-38892.html).
- **Jul 14, 2026, redesign:** a personalised "For You" gallery with saved collections, on desktop, US, English, signed-in users, rolling out over the following weeks, plus image generation in AI Overviews. I found no measurement of its effect on clicks — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/14/google-images-gets-a-pinterest-like-redesign-focused-on-discovery/).

**Value of the traffic:**
- Kohn's ecommerce data: conversion 0.17% for Images against 0.47% for Google overall on one site (64% worse), and 0.24% against 2.0% on another (88% worse). He also reports a marked gap in pages per visit, with figures only in charts **[A]** — [Blind Five Year Old, 2019](https://www.blindfiveyearold.com/the-problem-with-image-search-traffic).
- An author's image traffic came mostly from searches for an actor who appeared once on her blog **[B]** — [Neil Patel, citing WordStream](https://neilpatel.com/blog/drive-traffic-using-google-image-search/).
- A forum owner with 100-200 image visitors a day said people mostly wanted to download the picture and left (search result only, dated) **[B]** — [BlackHatWorld](https://www.blackhatworld.com/seo/is-there-a-way-to-make-money-from-google-image-traffic.397117/).

### Inferences
- The pattern across these cases: big moves come from unblocking or from Google changing its interface. On-page changes give small or unmeasurable effects.
- **BuzzSkito's two October jumps may be the same one-off kind.** Project `CLAUDE.md` records that `/_next/`, which holds almost every image address, was unblocked on Oct 11, and an image sitemap launched on Oct 24. If the jump dates line up, this is a level shift from releasing blocked images, like the 2019 publisher, and not a growth rate to extrapolate. Test: compare the jump dates with those two changes, and see whether clicks flatten over the next 4-8 weeks.
- Large percentages from an unknown or tiny base should not be read as a scale effect. May-Fly's only absolute figure is 3 clicks.
- Conversion data is ecommerce, not ads. It suggests image visitors are shallower, but says nothing direct about Mediavine earnings.

### Gaps
- I found no before-and-after case for a publisher that added images in bulk. Dykstra is the closest and shows no split.
- I found no RPM or pages-per-session data for image visitors on ad-funded sites. Raptive reports RPM by traffic source, but its help page does not mention image search (search result only) — [Raptive](https://help.raptive.com/hc/en-us/articles/29169377863067-Performance-by-Traffic-Source). Image-tab visits can appear as google / organic in GA4, so they may not be separable there (search result only) — [SparkCliks](https://www.sparkcliks.com/blog/organic-clicks-vs-analytics-sessions).
- I found no controlled test measuring the Image search type on its own.

## 3. What share of all Google clicks or searches goes to image results or the Images tab, and how has that changed through 2026?

### Takeaway
The last public panel figure I could find for the Images tab is from Q2 2019, about 20% of searches, and I found no 2024-2026 panel that breaks it out. So the 2026 trend is unknown. Searches are not clicks: the one multi-site click measure found under 1% of clicks.

### Cited Findings
- **Google does not publish it.** A former Google Images product manager said the figure is not publicly disclosed **[B]** — [Neil Patel](https://neilpatel.com/blog/drive-traffic-using-google-image-search/).
- **2018 (Jumpshot):** Images' share of searches shrank, with Google web search taking that traffic by embedding more images in its results. The number is only in a chart — [SparkToro, Apr 2018](https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-jumpshot-2018-data-where-searches-happen-on-the-web-google-amazon-facebook-beyond/). A commonly repeated 22.6% is credited to Moz; I could not check it at source **[D]** — [SEO Sandwitch](https://seosandwitch.com/google-images-statistics/).
- **Q2 2019 (Jumpshot):** 20.5% of searches on Google Images, 69.4% on Google, search result only **[C]** — [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/digital/seo-110397). The panel was browser-only and is now defunct. SparkToro's own text gives only about 6% of queries and 12% of clicks for all Google properties combined — [SparkToro, Aug 2019](https://sparktoro.com/blog/less-than-half-of-google-searches-now-result-in-a-click/).
- **Clicks, 2017-2018:** about 12% of impressions and under 1% of clicks from Images, a 20x click-rate gap, 200+ US enterprise domains **[A]** — [seoClarity, 2019](https://www.seoclarity.net/blog/seo-research-real-traffic-share).
- **2024 (Datos):** almost 30% of clicks went to Google-owned properties, with Images listed but not broken out **[C]** — [SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/2024-zero-click-search-study-for-every-1000-us-google-searches-only-374-clicks-go-to-the-open-web-in-the-eu-its-360/).
- **Jan-Apr 2026 (Similarweb, US):** 68.01% of searches had no click, up from 60.45% in 2024 on a different panel, which the author calls somewhat apples to oranges. The text does not mention Images, and the Google mobile app is excluded **[C]** — [SparkToro](https://sparktoro.com/blog/in-2026-less-than-one-third-of-google-searches-still-send-a-click/).
- **Where 2026 clicks go:** about 66% open web, about 27% Google properties, about 6% paid. Images is named only as one example inside the 27% **[C]** — [Digital Applied, citing SE Roundtable](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/sparktoro-zero-click-study-2026-68-percent-seo-analysis); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-sends-23-of-queries-to-the-open-web/578724/).
- **Conflict in the open-web figure:** 232 per 1,000 searches in 2026 against 360 in 2024 per [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-sends-23-of-queries-to-the-open-web/578724/); 276 against 374 per [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/sparktoro-zero-click-study-2026-68-percent-seo-analysis). SparkToro's text gives neither. Its 2024 URL says 374 for the US.
- **images.google.com is not a measure of Google Images.** Similarweb shows 12.6M visits in Sep 2026 **[C]** — [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/images.google.com/). Old figures of 10.1% of Google traffic (from Quora) and over 1 billion daily pageviews are very dated **[D]** — [Neil Patel](https://neilpatel.com/blog/drive-traffic-using-google-image-search/).
- **Lens, Google's own figures [B]:**
  - Nearly 20 billion visual searches a month in Oct 2024 (search result only) — [Scalevise](https://scalevise.com/resources/google-lens-visual-search-image-seo/).
  - Over 100 billion in Jan-May 2025, 65% growth year on year, 1.5 billion monthly users — [Google, Jun 2025](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/google-lens-ai-visual-search/).
  - Over 25 billion a month, with data dated Apr 2025, and about 1 in 5 queries commercial (the footnote is muddled) — [Google, Jun 2026](https://business.google.com/en-all/think/search-and-video/ai-search-marketing-customer-journey-india/).
  - None of these is a click figure.
- **Search Console's new Web: multimodal filter**, Sep 24, 2026, global, covers Lens, Circle to Search, image uploads and Chrome's image search — [The Keyword](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/search-console-multimodal-search-report); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/seo-pulse-google-spam-update-image-search-data/590882/).

### Inferences
- Whatever Images' share is now, in 2026 it sits inside a bucket of about 9% of all US searches (27% of the 32% that produce a click), which also holds Maps, YouTube and AI Mode. This is my arithmetic. The bucket's exact contents are not defined in SparkToro's text.
- The panels exclude the Google app, where a lot of Lens use probably happens. That means panels probably cannot see the fastest-growing part of visual search.
- Lens volume is not evidence of clicks. A visual search can be satisfied on Google's own screen. Five weeks of multimodal data per site is the first real measure, and BuzzSkito can read its own.

### Gaps
- I found no public Images-tab share for 2024, 2025 or 2026, and no measurement of the Jul 2026 redesign's effect.
- I found no site that has published its multimodal share, and no benchmark. The only site-level remark I found: Dave Smart saw drops in impressions and clicks starting on the 10th (presumably Sep 10) on a few properties, with no figures — [SE Roundtable](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-search-console-multimodal-search-type-filter-42156.html).
- Whether the brief's 60-65 clicks a day includes multimodal should be checked. They are separate filters in Search Console.
- A 10.62% figure for Google Images in US search appeared in one search summary and I could not trace it, so it is excluded. Moz could not be opened.

## 4. Which kinds of site win at scale, and which approaches could an 800-page publisher copy?

### Takeaway
I found no study of which domains take the most Google Images results, so "who wins" rests on total-traffic estimates with no image split. The image-first sites hold hundreds of thousands to millions of items, and their yield per image is low, so volume is not something an 800-page site with about 720 licensed photos can copy.

- **Scale-free comparison:** BuzzSkito's image clicks alone are about 2.6 a month per ledger photo (1,900 / 720) or about 1.5 per sitemap image (1,900 / 1,232 at the Oct 24 launch, per `CLAUDE.md`). The table's figures are all-channel visits.
- **Dykstra's model needs bulk supply:** 20-75 images per post and 600-900 stock photos a month — [Fat Stacks, 2020](https://fatstacksblog.com/image-seo/).
- **Wallpaper owners complained in 2011** that image search showed their pictures without sending the visit (search result only) — [Digital Point](https://forums.digitalpoint.com/threads/for-all-wallpaper-picture-site-owners-get-traffic-from-google-images.2065895/). Google's later removal of View Image reduced that specific leak (section 2).
- **Image blocks in web results are common but rarely clicked.** seoClarity saw images in 24% then 34% of top-10 results, later over 55% of keywords (search result only) — [seoClarity](https://www.seoclarity.net/blog/research-study-google-images). Click results are in section 6.
- **One 2026 academic paper** found Images results held fewer authoritative sources than Search and a narrow repertoire, on climate and biodiversity topics only (search result only) — [Pearce et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/238460/8/Pearce%20et%20al.%20-%202026%20-%20From%20authority%20to%20similarity%20How%20Google%20transformed%20its%20knowledge%20infrastructure%20using%20computer%20vis.pdf).

### Inferences
- **Not copyable at 800 pages:** stock, PNG, wallpaper and coloring libraries (the image is the product, the visitor wants the file, and they need 100,000+ items) and user-generated databases and Pinterest (they need a crowd). PNG and wallpaper audiences also lean toward lower-value ad markets, which would hurt Mediavine income.
- **Partly copyable:** the Dykstra gallery format, limited by the licensed-photo supply and the no-AI rule.
- **Most copyable:** reference pages that answer a "what does X look like" search with a strong photo, as ID sites and medical atlases do. This is my judgment; none of those sites publishes an image split to prove it.
- **Crude scale check, not a forecast:** at today's 1.5-2.6 clicks per image, 30,000 clicks a month needs about 11,000-19,000 images, and 100,000 needs about 38,000-65,000. A ledger of 720 is far from that, and later images would probably target weaker searches. Better ranking or summer demand could raise the yield, so this check could be too pessimistic.
- Item counts are site claims and include non-images in places (supercoloring's are printables), so visits per item is only a rough comparison.

### Gaps
- I found no domain-share study of Google Images results. Pinterest, Wikipedia and stock-site image click volumes are not public in anything I could read. One vendor claims Pinterest has the highest organic visibility on Google, unverified **[D]** — [SEOZoom](https://www.seozoom.com/pinterest-images-seo/).
- I found no recipe blog with a published image share. A search on the leading food-blog SEO consultant turned up nothing.
- Image counts for wallpapercave and pngwing were not found.

**How Google counts** ([Search Console Help](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7042828?hl=en)):
- In the Images tab, an impression counts only when the image is scrolled into view.
- Expanding a thumbnail is not a click. A click on the expanded image, or any click that leaves Google, is.
- An impression counts once per host page URL.
- Position runs left to right, then down.
- Images in the combined results tab count whether or not they are scrolled into view.
- Counting also changed in Apr 2021 (search result only) — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-search-console-performance-report-image-impression-change-347626).

### Inferences
- BuzzSkito's 0.18% is close to the only recent whole-site figure. Page type may matter a lot on that one site (0.29% against 0.05%), which is worth checking on BuzzSkito by page type.
- Compare click rates only from Apr 28, 2026 onward, because impressions before then were unreliable. The Snippet Digital May figures overlap that error window, so its 0.14% is less firm than it looks.
- Because each host page counts one impression, adding more images to the same page may not multiply impressions for a search. More images help mainly if they reach new searches. This reading of Google's rule is mine and should be tested.
- Image packs in the Web report inflate impressions and pull its click rate down. Do not mix those numbers with Image tab numbers.
- Impressions deep in the grid are real but rarely clicked (102,657 impressions and 2 clicks at position 50), so rank probably matters more than raw impressions.

### Gaps
- I found no published multi-site click-rate benchmark for the Image search type after 2019. seoClarity's actual percentages are only in a chart.
- Ranges of 0.8-2.6% and 1.4-4.9% for image click rate appeared in a search summary and I could not trace them to a named source, so they are excluded.
- I found no click rate for the multimodal filter from any site.
