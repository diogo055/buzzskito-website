# Who holds Google Images for pest, bug and bite searches, and what those pages do

Seen Oct 30, 2026. Abbreviations used throughout: "Images tab" = `https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&gl=us&hl=en&q=...`; "web results" = same URL without `udm=2`.

## Key question 1: Search by search, who gets the top image results, and does buzzskito.ca appear?

### Takeaway
All 16 searches were captured. BuzzSkito is in the top 15 on 2 of them (chigger bites #5, baby cockroach #14) and somewhere in the first 100 on 7. It is absent from the first 100 on 9, including all five spider-name searches and tick. Its best-ranked image is usually a diagram it drew, not a photo.

### Cited Findings

**Method and limits**
- I read 44 Google pages in the built-in browser, signed out, at 1400 px desktop width, from an Ontario connection with `gl=us&hl=en`. On page load 45 Google showed its `/sorry/` bot check. I stopped using Google there, did not touch that page, and did not switch browsers. Web-result and Images-tab captures were all complete before that point. — [Google Images, chigger bites](https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&gl=us&hl=en&q=chigger+bites)
- "Position" below is the order Google lists results in the page's own code (100 results per load). Screenshots show the same order filling the shortest column first. It is not a mobile position: a 6-column desktop #5 is on row 1, while a 2-column phone #5 is on row 3. — [Google Images, chigger bites](https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&gl=us&hl=en&q=chigger+bites)

A few Canadian sites appear (bcbedbugexpert.com at #6 on bed bug bites, pestsupplycanada.ca at #6 on bugs that look like ticks), but the mix is US plus international. — [bed bug bites](https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&gl=us&hl=en&q=bed+bug+bites); [bugs that look like ticks](https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&gl=us&hl=en&q=bugs+that+look+like+ticks)

### Inferences
- `gl=us` from Ontario appears to be a fair stand-in for the US desktop view, because positions line up with Search Console's US averages on 8 of 9 queries within about 8 places.
- Singular and plural behave as separate searches here (wolf spider none, wolf spiders #89; house spider none, house spiders #26).
- Of the 6 pages that have a diagram and appeared in these snapshots, on 5 the diagram is the best-ranked image (matched by shape, confirmed by eye on chigger bites and tick vs bed bug). This is the strongest signal in my data, because it compares images on the same page, so the page's own strength is held constant. Most other patterns in these notes are not controlled that way.

### Takeaway
It is a very fragmented field: 149 different sites hold the 240 top-15 slots. Pest firms and pest-product brands are the biggest group at 36%. Stock libraries hold no top-15 slot and Pinterest holds no top-15 slot. ### Inferences
- Being a Canadian domain does not look like a barrier in Images, since UK, Australian and New Zealand firms hold US-set slots.
- Authority does not lock these grids the way it locks web results, except on species names (black widow, tick, jumping spider), where 10-12 of 15 slots are hard.
- Social slots are not a target to outrank in the usual way, but they show what shape of image readers save and share (9-box bite grids, tall cards).

### Gaps
- No strength data per domain. Ahrefs is cancelled, so "beatable-looking" rests on site type. A few classification calls could move either way by a handful of slots (for example Terro counts as a small brand, Gulf of Maine Inc. counted with pest suppliers).
- I did not classify what share of the top-100 is reachable, only the top 15 and 60.

## Key question 3: What do the top 3 pages do on 6 searches, and how does our matching page compare?

### Takeaway
The top pages are short and carry few images, often just one. Many ranking images are stock. File names and alt text are often poor. Our pages are several times longer with more images, yet sit lower, so page length and photo count are not what is holding us back. The format of the winning image is what we lack on most of these pages.

## Key question 6: What patterns do the top images share?

## Key question 7: Are competitors reusing the same public-domain photos, and who wins with originals?

### Inferences
- The project note that unique original photos are what win Google Images is not borne out as a general rule on these searches. On this sample, stock on a strong or well-matched page beats uniqueness often. That is a small sample (12 images), TinEye can miss copies, and "widely used" does not always mean bought in: Orkin's photo may be its own, copied by the scraper sites in that list. It is also not controlled for page strength.
- A practical rule from this evidence: prefer public-domain photos that few others use, and don't expect to outrank the source with a famous one. Combining them into something new (a labelled pair) sidesteps the duplicate.

### Gaps
- I can't say how Google treats a re-encoded WebP of a CDC photo compared with the original, beyond this one observation.
- I opened about 20 pages, so other winners may use CDC photos without my seeing it.

## Key question 8: What images would we need, and which searches look most winnable?

### Takeaway
A site like this can realistically take many slots on pest-firm-dominated searches, and few on species names or the top 4 of bite searches. The cheapest wins come from new image formats on existing pages, not new pages. Whether that means "massive" growth is doubtful on today's numbers: click-through in Images is very low, about 0.16% across US image search for us, unless we reach roughly the top few places.

### Inferences

**Searches ranked by how winnable they look (my judgement)**

| Tier | Searches | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A: go now | baby cockroach (#14), tick vs bed bug (#23), bugs that look like ticks (#31), house spiders (#26) | Mostly small firms with one cheap image. Classic pack or AI strip made of small sites. We are already in range |
| A-: defend and climb | chigger bites (#5) | Biggest single row: 15,266 impressions. The top 4 are health and brand sites, so climbing past them needs a stronger image, not more text |
| B: winnable with new images, further back | mouse droppings and mouse poop (#84-90), mosquito larvae (#82), flea bites on humans (#37), flea bites (#41), bed bug bites (none) | Many beatable slots, but we lack the winning format. Larvae and ticks are our own trade, so we can shoot them ourselves |
| C: hard, images scarce | spider bite (none) | Slots are beatable, but real, licensed on-skin spider bite photos are rare, and it is a medical topic |
| D: leave as long shots | black widow spider, tick, jumping spider, wolf spider, brown recluse, animal droppings head term | 7-12 of 15 slots are authority sites; jumping spider needs magazine-grade macro photography |

**What to make or find, in likely payoff order**

1. **One-image labelled photo pairs** for every "X vs Y" page, built from public-domain or CC0 photos already in the ledger, at 3:2 or 4:3, with the two names in large type. This is the format on most of the first screen of tick vs bed bug, and the pairing also makes a new image rather than a copy of a CDC photo.
2. **Tall labelled summary cards (about 800x1600, showing as 1:2)** with a real photo on top, then how to tell, size, and where you find it. Three #1 slots use this shape, and each takes 2-3 rows. Start with chigger bites, bed bug bites and mouse droppings. Real photos would set them apart from the look-alike social cards.
3. **Our own scale photos.** A phone, a US penny and quarter, a fingertip, and a plain background. MMPC shows that a watermarked 1000x1000 phone-style photo reaches #2. The technician can collect mosquito larvae and ticks on the job in season, which are both in BuzzSkito's own lane. Nymphs and droppings would need a source such as willing customers or partner firms.
4. **Real-life context shots**: droppings on a shelf or paper towel, nymphs at a baseboard. Reddit and forum photos prove plain and ordinary is enough here.
5. **Real-photo ID charts**: a single image combining that page's 14 photos into a labelled chart for /blog/animal-droppings, and a nymph species grid for baby cockroach. Credits and share-alike terms need checking for each photo used (CC BY-SA photos would pass their licence to the chart), and any composite needs its own entry in the photo ledger or the guide checks may refuse it.
6. **A 3x3 "common bug bites" grid of real photos.** The drawn and social versions recur across flea, bed bug and spider searches. Our existing six-panel diagram already ranks #5, so this is an extension of what works.
7. **On-skin bite photos with consent**, from staff, customers or reader submissions. This is the one thing bite searches want that we can't get from CDC, and it would be a lasting advantage, but it is slow and needs care on privacy and medical claims. Showing bites on darker skin looks like an open gap (one tile seen).
8. **Page fixes that cost little:** lead /blog/mouse-poop with a droppings image and give it the diagram; lead the spider bite page with a bite, not a spider; and work out why the Canadian mouse page outranks its US twin for US readers, without merging them or renaming slugs (both are project rules). A neutral title on /blog/bed-bug-bites is the owner's call, because the geo word earns Canadian clicks.

All of this upgrades existing pages. Nothing here argues for new pages, which the site's own numbers say rarely pay (`.claude/skills/buzzskito-ops/SKILL.md` rule 2).

**Can this grow massively?**
- For: only 2 of 240 top-15 slots are ours, while about 112 are held by sites of a similar type. Own diagrams already got one to #5. Moving up appears to multiply click rate about 10x between position 13 and position 2, if those rows are real. As an illustration, chigger bites at 2% would be about 305 clicks per 28 days against 29 now.
- Against: at today's 0.16%, 3 million image impressions a month would be about 4,800 clicks. Most slots we can realistically win are in the middle of the grid, where the click rate is about 0.1-0.2%. Species head terms, which likely carry the most searches, look out of reach. Image clicks probably also behave differently from web clicks on the site, which I did not measure.
- My read: solid, steady growth from format upgrades on 20-40 pages is realistic. A step-change depends on winning top-3 slots across many searches, and I see that as plausible only on pest-firm-dominated searches, not across the board. Run the new formats on a small test set first and read the positions in Search Console before scaling.

### Gaps
- I can't size each search's prize beyond our own Search Console impressions. Those only count when our image is shown, so they understate demand on searches where we rank low or not at all.
- No proof yet that a new format moves a page up: all of this comes from what ranks now, not from a test on our site. Tall cards at #1, for example, sit on strong or well-matched pages, so the format may be getting credit for the page. The only within-page evidence is our diagrams beating our photos.
- True click rate at positions 1-3 on a real grid slot is unknown, because we hold almost none. The tick and black widow rows may not be normal grid placements.
- Lens remains unmeasured (see Q5).
